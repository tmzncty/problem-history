# 2000–2002: FRESA pre-sunset preservation attempt, lapse, and later restoration

## Research question

The companion packet `1995-2002-fresa-sunset-to-report-restoration-oil-pollution.md` established that the Interagency Coordinating Committee on Oil Pollution Research (ICCOPR) biennial-report requirement actually lapsed under the Federal Reports Elimination and Sunset Act of 1995 (FRESA), that a 1999–2000 reporting cycle was absent, and that Congress made FRESA inapplicable to the requirement in 2002. That packet deliberately left the House-origin history of the 2002 restoration as source debt.

Fresh primary evidence changes the interpretation materially: **the House had already selected the ICCOPR report for preservation before the May 15, 2000 sunset occurred.** The eventual information gap therefore cannot be treated as the event that first caused Congress to recognize the report as worth preserving.

## Core finding

The recovered sequence is:

```text
1995 FRESA default-sunset architecture
        ↓
1999 deadline postponed to May 15, 2000
        ↓
March–April 2000
House Transportation & Infrastructure Committee reviews its jurisdiction
and affirmatively selects reports it considers necessary to maintain
        ↓
ICCOPR §7001(e) is explicitly on the preservation list
        ↓
April 3, 2000
H.R. 4052 passes the House
        ↓
no enacted preservation before May 15
        ↓
May 15, 2000
ICCOPR reporting requirement sunsets
        ↓
1999–2000 biennial report absent
        ↓
June 2002
House again carries ICCOPR preservation in its maritime legislation
        ↓
Nov. 25, 2002
P.L. 107-295 §322(b)(6) finally makes FRESA inapplicable to ICCOPR §7001(e)
```

This is not simply `sunset -> information loss -> Congress discovers value -> restoration`. It is a stronger and stranger Problem History case: **an institution can affirmatively judge an inherited requirement worth preserving, fail to complete the preservation through the full legislative process, allow the requirement to die under a default rule, and later enact substantially the same preservation.**

## 1. March 2000: preservation begins before sunset

H.R. 4052, the *Transportation and Infrastructure Reports Restoration Act of 2000*, was introduced March 22, 2000 by House Transportation and Infrastructure Chairman Bud Shuster and Ranking Member James Oberstar. Its purpose was expressly to preserve reporting requirements under the committee's jurisdiction before the FRESA sunset.

Primary bill source:

- H.R. 4052 (106th Congress), introduced March 22, 2000: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-106hr4052ih/pdf/BILLS-106hr4052ih.pdf

House Report 106-555 records that the committee met March 23, that the bill covered matters across all six subcommittees, and that it was unanimously ordered reported without amendment by voice vote.

Primary committee source:

- H. Rept. 106-555, *Transportation and Infrastructure Reports Restoration Act of 2000*: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-106hrpt555/pdf/CRPT-106hrpt555.pdf

**Evidence strength:** very high for the timing, committee locus, and affirmative preservation process.

## 2. Actor-level problem formulation: default sunset was about to delete reports the committee considered necessary for oversight

The April 3 House debate supplies unusually clear actor language. Rep. Steven LaTourette explained that FRESA would reduce unnecessary paperwork and agency expenditure, **but would also inadvertently delete certain reports the committee believed necessary for executing its oversight responsibilities**. H.R. 4052 was presented as the correction. He emphasized that the bill restored only 61 reports, a small share of the reports affected by FRESA.

Rep. Earl Blumenauer likewise said the committee had reviewed reports within its jurisdiction on a bipartisan basis and determined which were necessary to maintain. Rep. James Oberstar repeated that formulation.

Primary floor source:

- Congressional Record, April 3, 2000, H1642–H1643: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2000-04-03/pdf/CREC-2000-04-03-house.pdf

This gives an actor-explicit distinction:

```text
FRESA's general objective
reduce unnecessary paperwork / agency expenditure

but

committee review of particular reports
some are necessary for congressional oversight
        ↓
selective preservation before sunset
```

**Evidence strength:** very high for the House committee/floor formulation. It does not establish that every House or Senate actor shared the same rationale.

## 3. ICCOPR was already one of the reports selected as necessary to preserve

Section 5 of H.R. 4052, `Preservation of Certain Reporting Requirements Relating to the Coast Guard and Maritime Transportation`, listed seven requirements. Item 7 was:

- activities of the Interagency Coordinating Committee on Oil Pollution Research — Oil Pollution Act of 1990 §7001(e), 33 U.S.C. §2761(e).

Thus ICCOPR was not first singled out after a missing report cycle became visible. It was already inside the House committee's affirmative preservation set before May 15, 2000.

Primary sources:

- H.R. 4052 text / Congressional Record, April 3, 2000, H1641–H1642.
- H. Rept. 106-555, which describes §5 as preserving reports within the jurisdiction of the Subcommittee on Coast Guard and Maritime Transportation.

**Evidence strength:** very high for selection. **Evidence remains only package-level for motive:** the recovered material says the committee selected reports it considered necessary for oversight, but does not give an ICCOPR-specific explanation of why §7001(e) passed that test.

## 4. The committee had been reviewing the FRESA problem before H.R. 4052 was introduced

H. Rept. 106-555 states that no hearings were held specifically on H.R. 4052, but that **over the preceding year** the committee had received comments and information from federal agencies and other congressional committees about FRESA and specific reporting requirements.

The report also preserves an October 18, 1999 GSA letter concerning other reporting objects, showing that at least some item-level boundary work was already underway months before the 2000 deadline.

This supports:

```text
sunset deadline approaching
        ↓
committee / agency review and correspondence
        ↓
selective preservation list
```

It does **not** prove which agency, if any, specifically advocated preservation of ICCOPR's report. That remains source debt.

**Evidence strength:** high for the existence and duration of review; unknown for ICCOPR-specific transmission.

## 5. April 3, 2000: the House passes preservation before the deadline

The House considered H.R. 4052 under suspension of the rules on April 3, 2000 and passed it. The floor debate repeatedly describes the affected reports as reports that **would** or were **to** sunset on May 15.

This corrects a potentially misleading retrospective vocabulary in later sources that calls the 2002 action a restoration of reports `eliminated after` FRESA. For process reconstruction, the key point is that the House's first preservation decision preceded actual sunset.

Primary source:

- Congressional Record, April 3, 2000, H1641–H1643.

**Evidence strength:** very high.

## 6. The preservation attempt did not become law before sunset

The recovered sources establish House passage of H.R. 4052 but no enacted H.R. 4052 preservation before May 15, 2000. Later official sources and the 2002 enactment confirm that ICCOPR §7001(e) did in fact fall under the FRESA sunset.

The exact Senate-side disposition of H.R. 4052 is not yet recovered here and should not be invented. In particular, do not silently encode `Senate rejection`: non-enactment can result from many procedural states.

What can be said safely is:

```text
House affirmative preservation
!=
enacted preservation
```

and therefore:

```text
actor recognition of value
!=
solution implementation
```

This is a useful negative edge for Problem History.

## 7. The lapse still mattered materially

The companion packet remains correct that the legal lapse produced an observable artifact gap. Later CRS reconstruction records no ICCOPR biennial report for 1999–2000, and GAO later encountered data limitations when reconstructing oil-pollution research projects.

But the causal interpretation must now be tightened:

```text
observed information gap
!=
origin of congressional recognition that the report mattered
```

The House had already made that judgment before the gap.

The gap may still have influenced later persistence or enactment in 2002, but no recovered actor source proves that yet.

## 8. 2002 repeats the preservation object, but with a narrower maritime list

On June 4, 2002, the House maritime package again contained a section titled `Preservation of Certain Reporting Requirements`. It listed six reports, including ICCOPR §7001(e). P.L. 107-295 §322(b) ultimately enacted those six exemptions from FRESA.

The 2000 Coast Guard/maritime subsection had listed seven reports. The one present in 2000 but absent from the 2002 six-report list was the Coast Guard housing-facility leasing report under former 14 U.S.C. §475(e).

This gives a small but useful selection delta:

```text
2000 House preservation set: 7 maritime reports
2002 enacted preservation set: 6 maritime reports

stable member across both sets:
ICCOPR §7001(e)
```

The stable inclusion strengthens the evidence that ICCOPR was not a one-off conference insertion in 2002. It had survived at least two House-side selection moments separated by more than two years.

Primary sources:

- Congressional Record, April 3, 2000, H1641–H1642.
- Congressional Record, June 4, 2002, H3109.
- P.L. 107-295 §322(b), 116 Stat. 2103–2104.
- H. Rept. 107-777, conference explanation of §322.

**Evidence strength:** very high for the set comparison; unknown for why the housing-leasing report dropped out.

## 9. Methodological payoff

### A. Pre-sunset preservation must be distinguished from post-sunset restoration

The earlier companion packet used a useful object-level chain, but the new evidence requires an additional state:

```text
selected_for_preservation_before_sunset
```

A robust schema should distinguish at least:

- scheduled_for_sunset;
- reviewed_before_sunset;
- selected_for_preservation;
- preservation_passed_one_chamber;
- preservation_enacted;
- legal_requirement_lapsed;
- artifact_production_lapsed;
- later_restoration_proposed;
- later_restoration_enacted.

Collapsing these into `active / dead / restored` would erase the most important process evidence in this case.

### B. Failed solution != absent problem recognition

Candidate researcher-side fixture:

```text
problem recognition
!=
solution enactment
```

H.R. 4052 shows that a legislature can recognize the impending failure mode of its own automatic sunset mechanism and formulate a selective correction, yet fail to complete that correction before the default rule executes.

### C. Default rules can override an affirmative but incomplete selection process

Researcher shorthand only: `procedural default outruns incomplete exception-making`.

This is analytically stronger than a simple story of inattentive sunset. The House was not inattentive to ICCOPR: it selected the report for preservation. The legal default nevertheless took effect because the preservation did not become law in time.

### D. Do not infer learning from the consequence merely because restoration followed it

The chronology now provides a direct warning against post hoc reconstruction:

```text
T1: Congress/committee judges report worth preserving
T2: preservation attempt fails to become law
T3: report sunsets and artifact disappears
T4: Congress later enacts preservation
```

A historian who begins at T3–T4 could incorrectly infer that the missing artifact caused T4. T1 proves that the valuation predates the observed consequence.

## Actor formulation / later reconstruction / present analysis

### Historical actors

Directly supportable:

- House Transportation and Infrastructure actors in 2000 accepted FRESA's paperwork/cost-reduction purpose while saying it would inadvertently remove some reports necessary for oversight.
- The committee reviewed reports in its jurisdiction and selected a limited set for preservation.
- ICCOPR §7001(e) was in that selected set before the May 15 sunset.
- The House passed H.R. 4052 before the deadline.
- ICCOPR appeared again in the House's 2002 preservation set and was ultimately exempted from FRESA in P.L. 107-295.

Not directly supportable yet:

- why ICCOPR specifically was considered necessary;
- who first nominated ICCOPR for preservation;
- why H.R. 4052 did not complete enactment;
- whether the missing 1999–2000 report strengthened the case for the 2002 enactment;
- why the Coast Guard housing-leasing report fell out between the 2000 and 2002 sets.

### Later reconstruction

CRS establishes the actual 30-month sunset/restoration interval and absent 1999–2000 ICCOPR biennial report. GAO later demonstrates a downstream data-reconstruction problem.

Those later observations remain consequence evidence, not evidence for the original 2000 selection motive.

### Present analytical language

Researcher-side labels only:

- `pre-sunset selective preservation`;
- `failed exception-making before default execution`;
- `procedural default outruns incomplete exception-making`;
- `recognition/enactment separation`;
- `preservation-attempt -> lapse -> restoration`.

Do not attribute these phrases to historical actors.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not say the missing ICCOPR report caused Congress to decide the report was valuable.** The House selected it for preservation before sunset.
2. **Do not call the 2000 lapse simple congressional inattention.** The House had affirmatively acted; the unresolved question is why preservation failed to complete enactment.
3. **Do not call House passage equivalent to congressional preservation.** Bicameral enactment did not occur before the deadline.
4. **Do not infer Senate rejection.** Exact Senate disposition remains source debt.
5. **Do not infer an ICCOPR-specific oversight rationale from package-level language.** The committee said selected reports were necessary for oversight, but no recovered source yet explains item 7 individually.
6. **Do not erase the actual information gap just because pre-sunset recognition existed.** The lapse still produced a missing report cycle and later data consequences.
7. **Do not treat the 2002 list as mechanically identical to 2000.** It retained six of the seven maritime reports; one dropped out.

## Suggested repository integration

This packet should be read as a correction/refinement to `1995-2002-fresa-sunset-to-report-restoration-oil-pollution.md`. The earlier packet's factual claims about legal lapse, absent artifact, 2002 enactment, and later GAO consequence remain useful. Its shorthand `sunset -> observed information gap -> restoration` should **not** be read as a causal sequence of congressional problem recognition. The fuller process is `pre-sunset preservation attempt -> lapse -> observed gap -> later enacted preservation`.

The general Problem Episode schema should add one-chamber / incomplete-solution states so that failed preservation attempts are not lost.
