# 1995–2002: FRESA sunset to report restoration — an observed information gap

## Research question

The companion `1965-1995-report-pruning-to-default-sunset.md` establishes that the Federal Reports Elimination and Sunset Act of 1995 (FRESA) changed the solution architecture from item-by-item pruning toward default expiration for covered periodic reports. Did any report actually disappear under that mechanism and later return, allowing us to observe the consequences of sunset rather than only legislative intent?

## Finding

Yes. Section 7001(e) of the Oil Pollution Act of 1990 required the Interagency Coordinating Committee on Oil Pollution Research (ICCOPR) to report biennially on its activities and plans. FRESA's catch-all sunset terminated that reporting requirement in May 2000. The committee did not produce the 1999–2000 biennial report. Congress then expressly exempted/restored the requirement in the Maritime Transportation Security Act of 2002 (P.L. 107-295 §322(b)(6)).

This is a rare object-level chain in which the default-sunset architecture generated an observable reporting gap before Congress restored the information channel.

## 1. 1990: Congress creates a recurring observatory around interagency oil-pollution R&D

The Oil Pollution Act of 1990 established ICCOPR to coordinate a comprehensive federal oil-pollution research, technology-development, and demonstration program across agencies and with outside institutions. Section 7001(e) required a biennial report to Congress on committee activities and plans.

The report therefore belonged to a larger coordination architecture; it was not the underlying research program itself.

Primary/statutory locus:

- Oil Pollution Act of 1990, §7001, codified at 33 U.S.C. §2761.

**Evidence strength:** very high for statutory creation and reporting architecture.

## 2. 1995–2000: a general sunset mechanism reaches this specific report

FRESA §3003(a)(1) provided that covered annual, semiannual, and other regular periodic reports listed in House Document 103-7 would cease to be effective after the statutory grace period unless exempted. The repository's companion packet already documents the actor-level rationale: Congress intended the sunset to force reconsideration and affirmative preservation of reports still useful for oversight.

Later CRS reconstruction identifies ICCOPR §7001(e) as one of the requirements that actually sunset under FRESA in May 2000.

Primary source for the general mechanism:

- P.L. 104-66 §3003: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ66/html/PLAW-104publ66.htm

Later high-quality reconstruction:

- CRS R42490, *Reexamination of Agency Reporting Requirements: Annual Process Under GPRAMA*.

**Evidence strength:** very high for the general FRESA mechanism; high for identifying its application to ICCOPR through CRS plus the later restoration statute.

## 3. The legal disappearance produced an observed information-production gap

CRS records an unusually concrete consequence: because the ICCOPR reporting requirement sunset, the committee produced **no biennial report for the 1999–2000 biennium**. CRS describes an approximately 30-month interval between sunset and statutory restoration.

This is stronger than merely observing that a statutory clause disappeared. The reporting artifact itself disappeared for a cycle.

GAO later encountered the consequences while reconstructing federal oil-spill research. In GAO-11-319, GAO explained that it could not count ICCOPR research projects for 1999–2000 because the committee was not required to report progress under FRESA and did not do so. GAO therefore lacked a comparable reporting source for those years.

Sources:

- CRS R42490: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R42490.html
- GAO-11-319, *Federal Oil and Gas: Interagency Committee Needs to Better Coordinate Research on Oil Pollution Prevention and Response*: https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-11-319.pdf

**Evidence strength:** high. CRS supplies the institutional reconstruction; GAO independently demonstrates a later research/oversight consequence of the missing reporting interval.

Important boundary: GAO's later difficulty does not prove that Congress in 2002 restored the report because GAO or another actor had already experienced that exact data problem. It proves that the lapse had an observable downstream information consequence.

## 4. 2002: Congress explicitly restores/preserves the ICCOPR report

The Maritime Transportation Security Act of 2002 contains an unusually direct reversal. Section 322(b), headed `Preservation of Certain Reporting Requirements`, states that FRESA §3003(a)(1) does not apply to six enumerated reports, including:

- `Activities of Interagency Coordinating Committee on Oil Pollution Research — Section 7001(e) of the Oil Pollution Act of 1990 (33 U.S.C. 2761(e)).`

Primary source:

- P.L. 107-295 §322(b)(6), 116 Stat. 2104: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-107publ295/html/PLAW-107publ295.htm

The conference report is also explicit about the legislative transformation. H. Rept. 107-777 says §322 of the House amendment **maintains six reports which were eliminated after enactment of FRESA**, and that the conference substitute adopted the House provision (while separately terminating an Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund annual report).

Primary legislative-history source:

- H. Rept. 107-777, discussion of §322: https://www.congress.gov/committee-report/107th-congress/house-report/777

**Evidence strength:** very high for restoration and for congressional recognition that these reports had been eliminated under the earlier sunset regime.

## 5. A useful contrast inside the same 2002 section

Section 322 does something analytically valuable: it restores/preserves six reports while **terminating another** Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund annual report.

Thus the 2002 action is not well described as a generic backlash against report elimination. Historical actors were still selecting among information channels:

```text
some inherited reports
    -> preserve / restore

another oil-spill-related report
    -> terminate
```

This strengthens the existing `jurisdiction-filtered pruning` finding. Restoration after sunset did not imply abandonment of report-pruning logic; Congress continued to distinguish reports it wanted from reports it did not.

## Actor formulation / later reconstruction / present analysis

### Historical actors

Directly supportable:

- 1995 Congress created a broad sunset mechanism intended to require affirmative reconsideration of covered periodic reports.
- By 2002 Congress described six reports as having been eliminated after FRESA and enacted a provision making the FRESA sunset inapplicable to them.
- ICCOPR §7001(e) was one of those six.
- In the same statutory section Congress terminated a different oil-spill reporting requirement.

The 2002 conference explanation recovered here does **not** give an item-specific reason why ICCOPR's report was valuable enough to restore. Do not invent one.

### Later reconstruction

CRS reconstructs the ICCOPR sequence as sunset in May 2000, no 1999–2000 biennial report, and restoration in late 2002. GAO later shows that the missing report interval impeded reconstruction of projects for those years.

These are later observations of consequences, not evidence of the 2002 conferees' motive unless direct transmission material is recovered.

### Present analytical language

Candidate researcher-side labels only:

- `sunset -> observed information gap -> restoration`
- `default-expiration reversal`
- `information-channel reappearance`
- `restoration-with-selective-pruning`

Do not attribute these terms to historical actors.

## Problem-History payoff

The companion packet's abstract 1928→1995 chain can now be extended with an object-level post-sunset test:

```text
1928
mass report repeal + demand route preservation
        ↓
1945 / 1954 / 1960 / 1965
selective recurring pruning
+ committee-interest filter
        ↓
1995 FRESA
default sunset architecture
+ affirmative preservation burden
        ↓
May 2000
ICCOPR biennial-report requirement sunsets
        ↓
1999–2000 reporting cycle absent
        ↓
2002
Congress expressly restores/preserves §7001(e)
while terminating another report in the same section
        ↓
later GAO reconstruction encounters the missing-cycle data gap
```

This yields a 74-year problem/solution transformation chain from 1928 to 2002.

The strongest methodological result is that **formal disappearance can become empirically observable before restoration**. A sunset is not merely a change in statute books: in this case one expected reporting cycle was actually absent.

At the same time:

```text
observed information loss
!=
proved restoration motive
```

That distinction should remain explicit.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not say Congress restored ICCOPR because the missing 1999–2000 report caused a known oversight crisis.** No recovered 2002 actor source says that.
2. **Do not say ICCOPR research itself stopped.** The reporting requirement/report artifact disappeared; the underlying interagency research program continued.
3. **Do not treat later GAO difficulty as contemporaneous congressional evidence.** It is strong consequence evidence, not motive evidence.
4. **Do not describe 2002 as repeal of FRESA generally.** Congress made the sunset inapplicable to selected reports.
5. **Do not infer that every restored report had actually ceased production in practice.** ICCOPR is unusually useful because CRS and GAO document an actual missing cycle; other restored requirements need separate object-level checking.
6. **Do not treat restoration as rejection of pruning.** The same §322 terminated another oil-spill report.
