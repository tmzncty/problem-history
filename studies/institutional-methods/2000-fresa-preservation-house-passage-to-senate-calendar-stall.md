# 2000 FRESA preservation: House passage to Senate-calendar stall before sunset

## Research question

The companion packet `2000-2002-fresa-pre-sunset-preservation-failure-and-restoration.md` established that the House selected ICCOPR §7001(e) and other Transportation and Infrastructure reports for preservation before the May 15, 2000 FRESA sunset, passed H.R. 4052 on April 3, and nevertheless failed to enact preservation before the deadline. It deliberately left the Senate-side disposition unresolved.

Fresh official calendar evidence narrows that process gap substantially.

## New finding

The final House calendar for the 106th Congress records H.R. 4052 as:

- reported April 3, 2000 (H. Rept. 106-555);
- passed by the House under suspension of the rules on April 3;
- received in the Senate on April 4; and
- **ordered placed on the Senate calendar on April 4, 2000**.

The final calendar records no later Senate passage, committee referral, amendment, or other advancement for H.R. 4052.

Primary source:

- *Calendars of the United States House of Representatives and History of Legislation, 106th Congress, Second Session*, final history of bills and resolutions, H.R. 4052 entry, p. 137: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CCAL-106hcal-S2/pdf/CCAL-106hcal-S2.pdf

Supporting House sources:

- H. Rept. 106-555, *Transportation and Infrastructure Reports Restoration Act of 2000*: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-106hrpt555/pdf/CRPT-106hrpt555.pdf
- Congressional Record, Apr. 3, 2000, H1640–H1642: https://www.congress.gov/106/crec/2000/04/03/146/39/CREC-2000-04-03-pt1-PgH1640.pdf

**Evidence strength:** very high for House passage, Senate receipt, and placement on the Senate calendar; high for the negative statement that the final legislative history records no subsequent advancement. This does not identify why Senate floor action never occurred.

## Why this changes the process reconstruction

The previous safe statement was only:

```text
House passes preservation
    !=
enacted preservation
```

The Senate-side state can now be resolved more precisely:

```text
2000-04-03
House passes H.R. 4052
        ↓
2000-04-04
Senate receives H.R. 4052
        ↓
ordered placed directly on Senate calendar
        ↓
no later advancement recorded in final 106th-Congress history
        ↓
2000-05-15
FRESA default sunset executes
```

This is important because it rules out one tempting reconstruction: the bill did **not** disappear merely because it was referred to a Senate committee and remained buried there. The official history instead places it on the Senate calendar immediately after receipt.

At the same time, calendar placement is not passage. The evidence therefore supports a distinct intermediate state:

`ready_for_floor_consideration / calendared_but_not_enacted`

That label is researcher-side shorthand, not actor vocabulary.

## Actor formulation vs procedural reconstruction vs present analysis

### Historical actors

House actors on April 3 explicitly said FRESA had a valid paperwork-reduction purpose but would inadvertently eliminate some reports the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee regarded as necessary for oversight. H.R. 4052 selectively preserved a limited set, including ICCOPR §7001(e).

The recovered Senate calendar entry does **not** supply a Senate actor-level substantive formulation. It is procedural evidence only.

### Procedural reconstruction

The official final legislative calendar establishes that the House-passed preservation package reached the Senate, was ordered placed on the calendar the next day, and did not subsequently advance in the recorded bill history.

This is stronger than generic `non-enactment`, but weaker than any claim that senators rejected the package on the merits.

### Present analytical language

Candidate project labels only:

- `calendared-but-not-enacted preservation`;
- `recognized problem + floor-ready solution + default execution`;
- `procedural noncompletion before automatic deadline`.

Do not attribute these labels to 2000 actors.


This strengthens the existing methodological point:

```text
problem recognition
!= solution formulation
!= one-chamber passage
!= floor-ready status in second chamber
!= enactment
```

An automatic default can therefore execute even after a preservation proposal has moved substantially through the legislature.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not say the Senate rejected H.R. 4052.** No vote or actor statement of rejection is recovered.
2. **Do not say a Senate committee killed the bill.** The final calendar says it was ordered placed on the Senate calendar rather than recording committee referral.
3. **Do not infer why floor action did not occur.** Scheduling congestion, bargaining, holds, substantive opposition, competing vehicles, or other causes remain unproven.
4. **Do not call Senate calendar placement equivalent to imminent passage.** It establishes procedural availability, not probability of enactment.
5. **Do not restore the old causal story `information gap -> recognition`.** The House had already selected ICCOPR for preservation before both sunset and the missing report cycle.
6. **Do not infer Senate agreement with the House oversight rationale from receipt/calendar placement.** The Senate procedural record recovered here contains no equivalent actor formulation.

## Compact finding

> The House's April 2000 attempt to preserve ICCOPR and other Transportation and Infrastructure reports did not die in a Senate committee referral. The final official legislative calendar records H.R. 4052 as received by the Senate and ordered placed on the Senate calendar on April 4, one day after House passage. No later advancement is recorded before the May 15 FRESA sunset. The episode should therefore be modeled as `pre-sunset problem recognition -> House preservation -> second-chamber calendar placement -> procedural noncompletion -> default sunset`, while leaving the reason for Senate inaction unresolved. This further demonstrates that an automatic institutional default can execute even when a corrective solution has already been recognized, formulated, passed one chamber, and made procedurally available in the other.
