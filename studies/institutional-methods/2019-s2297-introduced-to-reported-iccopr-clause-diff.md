# 2019 S.2297 introduced → reported: ICCOPR redesign predates committee amendment

## Research question

The open ICCOPR chain had narrowed a source debt to a precise legislative-version question: were the later-added NAS implementation-guidance function and ten-year statutory update rule already present when S.2297 was introduced on July 25, 2019, or did they enter during Senate Commerce Committee work before the bill was reported on September 26?

Direct comparison of the official GPO introduced and reported texts resolves this question.

## Core finding

**Both mechanisms were already present in S.2297 as introduced on July 25, 2019.** So were the quarterly ICCOPR meeting requirement, public meeting summaries, detailed new research-plan content schema, and the older NAS/NIST advice architecture.

The September 26 reported version retains this core §429 planning/governance architecture. Therefore the committee-reporting stage cannot be treated as the origin of the ten-year cadence or the third NAS implementation-guidance function.

This moves the source problem upstream again:

```text
1990 OPA baseline
  NAS development advice + adequacy review
        ↓
2009 proposal still shows two-function NAS form
        ↓
2019-07-25 S.2297 introduced
  already contains:
  - quarterly ICCOPR meetings
  - public summaries
  - refreshed plan-content schema
  - NAS development advice
  - NAS adequacy review
  - NEW third NAS implementation/organizational guidance
  - NIST guidance
  - 10-year statutory updates
        ↓
2019-09-26 Senate-reported S.2297
  core architecture retained
        ↓
2021 enactment
```

## 1. Introduced bill already contains the complete planning/review bundle

The official introduced version of S.2297 is dated July 25, 2019. Section 429 rewrites Oil Pollution Act §7001.

Its ICCOPR membership section already requires members to meet at least quarterly and requires a public summary after each meeting.

Its research-plan clause already requires a plan within 180 days that:

- identifies current research programs across federal agencies, state and tribal governments, universities, and corporate entities;
- assesses current knowledge;
- identifies significant research gaps and technological deficiencies exposed by past spills;
- establishes national research priorities/goals;
- assesses technology applicability/effectiveness by oil class;
- estimates resources and timetables;
- summarizes response-equipment research under currents, ice cover, and ice floes.

Most importantly, the introduced NAS contract clause already contains all three functions:

1. advice/guidance during plan preparation and development;
2. adequacy assessment and report to Congress; and
3. **organizational guidance regarding implementation, including delegation of topics and research among federal agencies represented on ICCOPR.**

Immediately afterward, the introduced bill already contains the NIST advice/guidance clause and the requirement for a new plan **not later than ten years after enactment and every ten years thereafter**.

Primary source:

- S.2297, 116th Cong., Introduced in Senate, July 25, 2019, §429, official GPO HTML: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-116s2297is/html/BILLS-116s2297is.htm

**Evidence strength: very high.** This is the official introduced legislative text.

## 2. Senate-reported text retains the same core architecture

The Senate-reported version is dated September 26, 2019 and explicitly marks committee amendments. Its §429 still contains the same quarterly meetings/public summaries, research-plan schema, three NAS functions, NIST guidance, and ten-year update rule.

Primary source:

- S.2297, 116th Cong., Reported in Senate, Sept. 26, 2019, §429, official GPO HTML: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-116s2297rs/html/BILLS-116s2297rs.htm

The reported version does contain amendments elsewhere in and around §429—for example, additional language concerning use of testing facilities other than OHMSETT is visible in the reported text—but the core planning/review/update bundle at issue here is not a committee-stage invention.

**Evidence strength: very high** for version comparison of the relevant clauses.

## What this corrects

Earlier repository work correctly located the architecture no later than the Senate-reported bill, but left open whether it entered during committee work. That uncertainty can now be closed.

Do **not** write:

```text
Senate Commerce Committee added the 10-year rule / third NAS function
```

The evidence instead supports:

```text
architecture present at introduction
→ committee retains it
→ later enactment retains substantially the same design
```

This is a useful legislative-history distinction:

```text
first version recovered with mechanism X
!=
committee stage that later reports bill containing X
```

## Actor / process / present-analysis separation

### Historical actors / legislative text

Directly supportable:

- Senators Sullivan, Wicker, Cantwell, and Markey introduced S.2297 on July 25, 2019.
- The introduced bill already contained the third NAS implementation-guidance function and ten-year updates.
- It also already contained quarterly meetings/public summaries and the refreshed research-plan schema.
- Senate Commerce reported the bill with amendments on September 26, while retaining those clauses.

### What is still not established

The introduced text does **not** tell us:

- which sponsor, staff office, agency, or outside actor drafted §429;
- why exactly ten years was selected rather than ICCOPR's organizational six-year cadence;
- why actors considered the inherited two-function NAS architecture insufficient and added implementation guidance;
- whether the language came from Coast Guard/ICCOPR technical assistance or an earlier bill.

Sponsorship must not be converted into individual authorship of every clause.

### Present analytical language

Researcher-side labels only:

- `introduction-stage architecture`;
- `committee retention != committee origin`;
- `legislative-version provenance`.

## Problem-History payoff

The immediate legislative-origin question is now narrower than before. The 2019–2021 chain should be represented as:

```text
pre-2019 institutional/problem history
        ↓
2019-07-25 introduced legislative solution already bundled
        ↓
2019-09-26 committee-reported version retains bundle
        ↓
2021 enactment
```

That means the next useful evidence is **not another introduced-vs-reported comparison**. The next target must precede or explain introduction.

Highest-value candidates:

1. predecessor bills in the 115th/early 116th Congress containing the same §7001 rewrite;
2. Senate Commerce markup materials only insofar as they identify provenance or explain retention—not as the presumed origin;
3. sponsor/committee press releases and section-by-section materials from July 2019;
4. Coast Guard, ICCOPR, NOAA, or committee technical-assistance correspondence;
5. legislative drafting files or stakeholder submissions that explain `10 years` and the third NAS function.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not attribute the architecture to the September 2019 committee amendment merely because the reported bill contains it.** It predates reporting.
2. **Do not attribute every clause personally to the four sponsors.** Introduction identifies the legislative vehicle and sponsors, not clause-level authorship.
3. **Do not infer motive from textual presence.** We now know *when at latest* the mechanisms entered this bill, not why.
4. **Do not collapse retained 1990 functions and new functions.** NAS development advice and adequacy assessment are inherited; implementation guidance is the later expansion visible by July 2019.
5. **Do not call ten years a codification of ICCOPR's six-year organizational cycle.** The intervals remain different and the rationale remains unrecovered.

## Compact finding

> Official GPO versions close the introduced-versus-reported source debt for S.2297 §429. The July 25, 2019 introduced bill already required quarterly ICCOPR meetings and public summaries, a detailed new research-plan schema, all three NAS functions including organizational guidance for implementation, NIST guidance, and ten-year statutory plan updates. The September 26 Senate-reported version retains this core architecture. The ten-year cadence and third NAS function therefore did not originate in the committee-reporting stage. The remaining historical question moves upstream: who supplied or drafted this architecture before introduction, and why did they choose ten-year statutory renewal plus implementation-level NAS guidance while ICCOPR itself was operating on a six-year planning cycle?
