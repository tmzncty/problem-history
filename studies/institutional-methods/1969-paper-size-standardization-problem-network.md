# 1969: paper-size standardization becomes a cross-system coordination problem

## Why this packet exists

The existing 1921–1980 letter-paper packet correctly records the June 30, 1969 interagency meeting as an explicit reopening of paper-size standardization. A closer recovery of the meeting report sharpens what the historical actors were actually doing. The meeting was not merely a binary reconsideration of `8 x 10.5 Government paper` versus `8.5 x 11 commercial paper`. It assembled a heterogeneous network of government offices, standards bodies, printing/publishing actors, paper and envelope interests, data-processing suppliers, microfilm, direct mail, retail, and printing-equipment interests, and explicitly treated their standards activities as interrelated.

This matters for Problem History because the object of concern had expanded. The 1920s economy problem could be acted on by reducing varieties and choosing a Government size. By 1969, paper size sat inside multiple information-production and information-handling systems whose standards could interact. The recovered actor formulation therefore supports a stronger transition from a local material-economy solution to a coordination problem among interdependent standards regimes.

## Recovered actor-level findings

### 1. The relevant actor field was much wider than GSA/NBS

The meeting report identifies Dr. Robert B. Hobbs, Deputy Technical Director of the Government Printing Office, in his role as chairman of the Technical Subcommittee of the Paper Specification Standards Committee of the Joint Committee on Printing. Harold F. Drury, Director of the Commerce Department's Printing & Publishing Division, described his office's standardization interest; the report notes that his work connected the Government with more than 37,000 printers and publishers.

The meeting also included representatives of numerous Federal agencies and a wide set of trade/technical organizations. The report specifically names:

- American Paper Institute;
- Data Processing Supplies Association;
- Direct Mail Advertising Association;
- Envelope Manufacturers Association;
- Graphic Arts Marketing & Information Service;
- National Microfilm Association;
- National Paper Trade Association;
- National Printing Equipment Association;
- National Retail Merchants Association;
- Printing Industries of America;
- United States of America Standards Institute participants.

**Evidence strength:** very high. These are directly enumerated in the contemporaneous meeting report / attendance material.

### 2. Historical actors explicitly linked paper size to methods of communicating information

The report says that, throughout the discussion, participants identified and discussed different methods of communicating information in relation to paper. This is stronger than a retrospective claim that later technologies happened to interact with paper sizes. The actors themselves were treating paper-size standardization as connected to an information-communication system.

**Evidence strength:** very high for the general actor formulation; not sufficient by itself to assign a specific causal weight to photocopying, data processing, microfilm, filing, or any other single technology.

### 3. Interdependence among standards activities was itself identified as a problem

The report calls the interrelationship among the standards activities of the many participating groups an important fact identified in discussion. It records general agreement that groups working on standards related in some way to paper size should establish active communication and a relationship with the Committee on Standardizing Paper Sizes.

This is direct evidence that coordination among standards-producing groups was not merely our present reconstruction. The participants themselves recognized that standards work in one domain could not safely be treated as isolated from adjacent domains.

**Evidence strength:** very high.

### 4. The meeting did not yet constitute a settled replacement decision

The conference reviewed possible functional areas and revised a grouping of end products. Chairman Bernard J. Taymans closed by identifying further developmental and fact-finding work: uniform terminology and definitions still had to be developed, functional groupings and end-product classifications still had to be refined, and future comments/recommendations were to rest on factual analysis and documentation. He also treated the establishment of a communication channel among groups doing paper-size-related standardization as a main accomplishment of the meeting.

Therefore the 1969 event should be coded as **problem structuring / coordination / fact finding**, not as an adoption event for 8.5 x 11.

**Evidence strength:** very high.

## Problem transformation

The fuller chain is now more precise:

```text
1920s
many sizes / material and production waste
        ↓
choose a smaller Government correspondence standard
        ↓
standard becomes durable infrastructure
        ↓
1969
paper size is embedded in multiple standards activities
across printing, publishing, data processing, microfilm,
envelopes, direct mail, equipment, retail, etc.
        ↓
problem structuring:
terminology + functional grouping + end-product classification
+ communication among standards groups
        ↓
1978 JCP decision
bounded migration of stationery/correspondence to 8.5 x 11
```

The strongest new observation is:

```text
legacy divergence
        !=
only a two-size comparison

legacy divergence
        +
interdependent standards regimes
        =
a broader coordination object by 1969
```

A provisional researcher-side label is **`standards-network problem expansion`**. This is analytical vocabulary, not a phrase used by the 1969 participants.

## Historical actors / later reconstruction / present analysis

### Historical actors

Directly supportable claims:

- the 1969 meeting assembled government, standards, industrial, equipment, data-processing, microfilm, paper, envelope, direct-mail, retail, printing, and publishing interests;
- participants discussed methods of communicating information in relation to paper;
- participants explicitly recognized interrelationships among standards activities;
- they agreed on the need for active communication among groups whose standards related to paper size;
- the work was still in a fact-finding and classificatory stage, with terminology, functional grouping, and end-product classifications needing further development.

### Later reconstruction

Later accounts may describe the 1969 meeting as part of the road to the 1978/1980 switch. That chronological placement is useful, but it must not be allowed to turn the 1969 meeting into an already-decided 8.5 x 11 campaign.

### Present analysis

The following are project-level constructs only:

- `standards-network problem expansion`;
- `compatibility boundary expansion`;
- treating the meeting as a transition from a single-artifact economy problem toward coordination of interdependent standards regimes.

They are defensible summaries of the recovered structure, not historical quotations.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not make photocopying the demonstrated cause of the 1978 decision from this source.** The report supports a broad communication/standards ecology, not a single-technology explanation.
2. **Attendance is not causation.** The presence of the National Microfilm Association or Data Processing Supplies Association proves relevance/interest, not that microfilm or data processing drove the eventual size change.
3. **Coordination is not consensus on a size.** General agreement concerned communication among standards activities; it does not prove agreement on 8.5 x 11, A4, or another end state.
4. **Problem structuring is not adoption.** The chairman explicitly left terminology, classifications, fact finding, and documented recommendations as future work.
5. **Do not read 1978 backward into 1969.** The later JCP conformity decision does not establish that the 1969 meeting was convened to ratify that outcome.

## What to change in the parent 1921–1980 packet

The parent packet's 1969 node should be read more narrowly and more richly:

- retain `explicit reconsideration`;
- add that the meeting framed paper size within multiple methods of communicating information and interrelated standards activities;
- classify the event as `problem structuring / coordination / fact finding`, not `replacement choice`;
- keep specific technology causation (photocopiers, filing equipment, automated reproduction) as source debt unless separately demonstrated.

## Remaining uncertainties

- What proposal or administrative trigger caused NARS/GSA and NBS to convene the June 1969 meeting?
- What were the revised functional groups and end-product classes mentioned in the report?
- Which participants subsequently submitted factual analyses or recommendations?
- Did a follow-on committee report rank competing sizes or quantify transition costs?
- When does the first actor-explicit statement appear that compatibility with commercial 8.5 x 11 outweighed the old Government-size economy?
