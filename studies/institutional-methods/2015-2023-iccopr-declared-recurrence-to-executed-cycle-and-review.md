# ICCOPR 2015–2023: from declared recurrence to executed cycle — and the re-problematization of cycle quality

## Why this packet exists

The preceding ICCOPR packet identified an important methodological distinction: a solution can exist, be current, or possess a mechanism intended to detect its own obsolescence. The FY2015–2021 Oil Pollution Research and Technology Plan (OPRTP) declared a six-year update cycle. That declaration by itself did not establish that recurrence became institutional practice.

This packet tests the declared update rule against the next cycle and then asks whether successful recurrence ended the problem.

## New findings

### 1. The six-year recurrence was actually executed

The National Academies' 2023 retrospective reconstructs the sequence explicitly. After the 1997 plan went unrevised for more than a decade, ICCOPR member agencies, following Deepwater Horizon, recognized both the need to update it and to commit to regular six-year updates. The FY2015–2021 plan embodied that commitment. A successor FY2022–2027 OPRTP was publicly released in January 2022.

The Coast Guard's current ICCOPR document page independently lists the FY2022–2027 OPRTP and describes its purpose as providing current assessments of research needs and priorities. The plan organizes recurring identification of knowledge gaps and priorities and connects the planning document to biennial reporting.

This is enough to upgrade the earlier status:

`declared recurrence -> executed recurrence`

The claim should remain narrow: one successful successor cycle demonstrates execution of the update rule, not indefinite durability.

### 2. The second cycle was not merely a voluntary continuation: Congress re-specified the planning obligation in 2021

The FY2022–2027 cycle sits inside a changed legal architecture. The 2021 NDAA again directed ICCOPR to develop a research plan and required National Academies advice/review. The codified statute now requires an updated research plan not later than ten years after January 1, 2021, and every ten years thereafter.

This matters because the six-year cadence and the statutory cadence are not identical. The historical sequence should therefore not be flattened into a single stable "six-year legal requirement." The evidence instead supports at least three layers:

- ICCOPR's post-Deepwater-Horizon six-year planning commitment;
- actual publication of the FY2022–2027 successor plan on that approximate cycle;
- a 2021 statutory architecture requiring plan updates at a ten-year interval while also imposing a National Academies review role.

Analytically:

`organizational update rule != statutory minimum/update rule`

Do not describe the six-year cycle as though Congress simply codified it.

### 3. Successful recurrence exposed a second-order problem: the quality of the recurrence process

The National Academies' 2023 review is especially useful because it does not treat publication of the successor plan as proof that the planning mechanism is adequate.

The review found that preparation and development of the FY2022–2027 plan occurred mostly during the final year of the six-year cycle. In the Academies' assessment, this compressed schedule limited comprehensive review of ongoing studies and identification of emerging research needs. It recommended beginning preparation earlier — for example, around year three — and moving toward a more continuous process for collecting and updating research needs and progress.

The review also identified implementation obstacles: ICCOPR had little dedicated funding, and without sustained funding or an implementation strategy the OPRTP risked functioning as a list of research needs rather than an implementable research plan.

This creates a useful transformation:

`stale plan -> recurring plan -> recurrence-process quality problem`

The object of problematization has moved. In 2009–2011, a central problem was that the old plan had remained stale. By 2023 a successor plan existed on the intended cycle, but reviewers could now ask whether the cycle's information-gathering, priority-setting, stakeholder engagement, metrics, funding, and implementation machinery were adequate.

### 4. Recurrence is therefore not a terminal solution state

A repository-level state model should distinguish at least:

`update rule declared`

`successor artifact produced`

`cadence repeated`

`development process adequate`

`implementation capacity adequate`

`outcomes measured`

The 2022 successor artifact moves ICCOPR beyond the first state. The 2023 National Academies review simultaneously demonstrates that later states remain independently contestable.

## Actor language vs later reconstruction vs researcher shorthand

### Historical actors / institutions

High confidence:

- ICCOPR agencies after Deepwater Horizon recognized the need for regular six-year plan updates (as reconstructed by the congressionally commissioned National Academies review from ICCOPR's planning history).
- The FY2022–2027 OPRTP states that subsequent revisions are intended to provide current assessments and that ICCOPR intends to update the plan every six years.
- Congress in the 2021 NDAA imposed a renewed research-plan requirement and National Academies review architecture; the codified provision establishes a ten-year statutory update interval.
- The National Academies in 2023 explicitly criticized the concentration of plan development in the final year of the six-year cycle and recommended a more continuous, earlier-starting process.

### Later conceptual reconstruction

The National Academies' 2023 account is a retrospective synthesis. It is strong evidence for the sequence and for the review committee's own 2023 problem formulation, but statements about what ICCOPR actors in 2010–2015 "recognized" should preferably be paired with contemporaneous ICCOPR material when available.

### Researcher shorthand

The following are analytical labels, not actor vocabulary:

- `declared recurrence -> executed recurrence`
- `organizational update rule != statutory update rule`
- `recurrence-process quality problem`
- `solution self-renewal architecture`

## Evidence strength

### High

1. FY2022–2027 successor plan exists and was released in January 2022.
2. The 2015–2021 plan's six-year recurrence commitment was followed by a successor plan covering 2022–2027.
3. Current federal law specifies a ten-year research-plan update interval beginning from January 1, 2021.
4. The 2023 National Academies review explicitly identifies late-cycle plan development, inadequate time, limited funding, and implementation strategy as obstacles.

### Medium-high

5. The six-year organizational cadence can be treated as having been executed once. One repetition is evidence of execution, but not enough to infer indefinite institutional durability.

### Not established

6. That the 2022 plan was produced solely because of the six-year commitment. The 2021 NDAA also directly required a new plan.
7. That Congress intended to replace or reject ICCOPR's six-year practice by specifying a ten-year statutory update requirement.
8. That the six-year cycle will continue after FY2027.
9. That the National Academies' 2023 recommendations have all been implemented.

## Hindsight risks

### Do not turn successful publication into successful implementation

The existence of the FY2022–2027 plan establishes artifact recurrence. It does not establish that priority research was funded, executed, or evaluated.

### Do not conflate organizational and statutory cadence

The six-year cycle emerged in ICCOPR's post-Deepwater-Horizon planning practice. The post-2021 statute contains a ten-year update requirement. Treating them as one rule erases a meaningful institutional layering.

### Do not narrate the 2023 critique backward

The National Academies' concern that plan preparation was concentrated in the last year is a 2023 evaluation of the 2022 cycle. It should not be projected backward as the reason ICCOPR adopted the six-year rule in 2015.

### Do not infer durable institutionalization from one repetition

A successor artifact proves that the declared recurrence was executed at least once. Stronger language such as "self-sustaining institutional cycle" requires later cycles or evidence that the cadence survived changes in personnel, statute, resources, and external shocks.

## Remaining uncertainties / source debt

1. Recover contemporaneous 2010–2015 ICCOPR meeting records or planning correspondence that document the decision to adopt exactly a six-year cycle, rather than relying on the National Academies' later reconstruction.
2. Trace the legislative history of the 2021 NDAA provision to determine why Congress chose a ten-year statutory update interval while ICCOPR was operating on a six-year plan cycle.
3. Test implementation of the 2023 National Academies recommendations against FY2024–FY2026 ICCOPR quarterly meetings and biennial reports.
4. Watch the next plan transition after FY2027. A third plan cycle would provide much stronger evidence for durable recurrence.

## Next candidate

Highest-value next step: reconstruct the legislative genealogy of the 2021 NDAA amendment to 33 U.S.C. §2761. The key historical question is not merely that Congress required another plan, but why the enacted architecture combined a new plan, external National Academies review, and a ten-year statutory update interval while ICCOPR's own planning practice used six years. That could reveal whether Congress was codifying, correcting, or independently redesigning the solution's self-renewal mechanism.
