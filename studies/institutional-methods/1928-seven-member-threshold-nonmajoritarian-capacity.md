# 1928: the seven-member information threshold had non-majoritarian capacity, but its original purpose remains unproved

## Research question

The current branch has already recovered the 1928 report-pruning package as a combined institutional redesign: Congress repealed 128 routine reporting requirements while adding a demand route under which the House Committee on Expenditures in the Executive Departments, or any seven of its members, and the corresponding Senate committee, or any five of its members, could require information within committee jurisdiction. A separate packet also notes that the newly consolidated House committee had 21 members, making seven exactly one third of the committee.

That arithmetic leaves a sharper question: **what did the seven-member threshold actually permit in the 70th Congress, and how far may that structural effect be distinguished from claims about what the 1928 actors intended?**

This packet resolves the first question for the House and deliberately leaves the second open.

## Core finding

At the time the 1928 statute was enacted, the House Committee on Expenditures in the Executive Departments had 21 members: 13 majority-party members and 8 minority-party members. The statutory threshold of seven therefore did not merely fall below a committee majority. It was also small enough that a request could, as a matter of institutional arithmetic, be made by members drawn wholly from the minority side of the committee.

```text
House committee membership in 1928: 21
majority-party members:             13
minority-party members:              8
statutory request threshold:         7

7 < committee majority (11)
7 < minority membership (8)
```

The D.C. Circuit explicitly recovered these numbers in *Maloney v. Murphy*, 984 F.3d 50 (D.C. Cir. 2020), citing David Canon et al., *Committees in the U.S. Congress, 1789–1946*. The court described §2954 as empowering not only the full committee but also a smaller, non-majority group of members. This is a later judicial characterization of the statute's structure, not evidence of 1928 actor intent.

Official House institutional history independently confirms that the committee was fixed at 21 members when the eleven departmental expenditures committees were consolidated on December 5, 1927. The same history records that the committee's jurisdiction was enlarged on March 17, 1928 to include independent establishments and commissions.

The present-day codification, 5 U.S.C. §2954, independently confirms the seven-member House and five-member Senate thresholds and traces them to §2 of the Act of May 29, 1928, ch. 901, 45 Stat. 996.

**Evidence strength:**

- **very high** that the House committee had 21 members and the statute set a seven-member threshold;
- **high** for the 13/8 partisan composition, because the D.C. Circuit supplies a specific scholarly source and the arithmetic is straightforward;
- **very high** that seven members could structurally act without a committee majority;
- **high** that seven members could structurally be drawn entirely from the eight-member minority contingent;
- **unproved** that enabling minority-party requests was the contemporaneously stated reason Congress selected seven.

## Historical actor claim vs later reconstruction vs present analysis

### Historical/legal facts recoverable without inference

- Congress created the seven-member House and five-member Senate request thresholds in the 1928 statute.
- The House recipient committee had been consolidated at 21 members in December 1927.
- Its jurisdiction was enlarged in March 1928.
- In the 70th Congress, the House committee's partisan composition was 13 majority / 8 minority according to the historical membership data recovered in later scholarship and cited by the D.C. Circuit.

### Later judicial reconstruction

The D.C. Circuit calls the empowered group a smaller, "non-majority" group and uses the 13/8 composition to explain the statute's institutional operation. That characterization is useful evidence about legal structure and later interpretation, but it is not a quotation from the 1928 committee report or floor debate.

### Present analytical shorthand

The following formulations are researcher language only:

- `non-majoritarian request capacity`;
- `threshold effect`;
- `minority-capable demand route`;
- `structural affordance != demonstrated legislative purpose`.

Do not attribute these terms to the 70th Congress.

## Why this advances the 1928 Problem Episode

The earlier packet could only observe:

```text
21-member committee
→ seven-member request threshold
→ 7/21 = one third
```

That left the threshold as an unexplained numerical clue. The partisan composition adds a historically relevant institutional fact:

```text
13 majority + 8 minority = 21
request threshold = 7
therefore a full committee majority was unnecessary
and even seven members from the minority contingent could satisfy the text
```

This is stronger than merely observing that seven is one third. It identifies a concrete institutional capability created by the statute.

But it also sharpens the hindsight boundary. There are at least three propositions that must not be collapsed:

1. **textual rule:** any seven House committee members could request information;
2. **structural effect:** seven was below both the 11-member committee majority and the eight-member minority contingent;
3. **historical purpose:** Congress chose seven in order to create a minority-party information right.

The first two are now strongly supported. The third remains source debt.

## Problem-History method payoff

This case suggests separating three fields whenever a historical institutional threshold is encountered:

```text
formal_threshold
observed_membership_structure
actor_stated_threshold_rationale
```

and, where useful:

```text
researcher_reconstructed_capabilities
```

A threshold's institutional consequences can often be established from rules plus membership data even when the actors left no explanation for why they selected that number. That reconstructed capability is historically meaningful, but it should not be silently converted into an actor motive.

For Problem History this is a useful general warning: **solution architecture may have demonstrable affordances whose original problem formulation is not yet recoverable.** A later actor may then reinterpret or mobilize that affordance as if it had always been the central purpose.

## Relation to later reinterpretation

Later legal and political discussion commonly describes 5 U.S.C. §2954 as the "Seven Member Rule" and emphasizes its usefulness to groups smaller than a committee majority. That later framing is part of the provision's reception history. It should be studied as a possible **reformulation of what the 1928 device was for**, not projected backward as the original 1928 problem statement.

A future 1928→2020+ chain could therefore distinguish:

```text
1928 enacted threshold
→ original contemporaneous rationale still partly unknown
→ durable statutory affordance
→ later minority/non-majority interpretation and use
```

This is potentially a long problem-transformation chain, but the transmission and reinterpretation stages require their own evidence.

## Senate asymmetry remains open

The Senate side must not be inferred from the House numbers. The statute used a different threshold—five members—and the relevant Senate committee had a different institutional history and membership structure. Until the 70th Congress Senate committee roster and partisan composition are recovered from an authoritative source, do not call the five-member rule minority-capable or assume it followed the same ratio as the House rule.

This asymmetry is itself useful: `7 House / 5 Senate` may encode chamber-specific committee structure rather than a single abstract fraction. That is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not write that Congress created a "minority right" in 1928 unless a contemporaneous source says so.** The structure permitted a seven-member group that could consist entirely of minority-party members; purpose is a separate claim.
2. **Do not treat the D.C. Circuit's 2020 formulation as 1928 actor vocabulary.** It is a later judicial reconstruction.
3. **Do not assume seven was selected because it equals one third of 21.** The numerical relation is real; the design rationale is not yet recovered.
4. **Do not generalize the House result to the Senate five-member threshold.** Recover the Senate roster first.
5. **Do not equate non-majoritarian with bipartisan or partisan.** "Any seven" could be seven majority members, seven minority members, or a mixed group.
