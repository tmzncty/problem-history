# 1928: House seven-member and Senate five-member information-request thresholds were structurally asymmetric

## Research question

The current branch has established that section 2 of the Act of May 29, 1928 (45 Stat. 996; later 5 U.S.C. §2954) replaced part of the information access lost through repeal of 128 routine reporting mandates with a demand route. It also established that the House Committee on Expenditures in the Executive Departments had 21 members in 1928, including 8 minority-party members, so the statutory threshold of any seven House committee members was structurally capable of being used without majority-party participation.

A remaining source debt was whether the Senate's parallel threshold of five members had the same structure. It did not.

## Core finding

The Congressional Record for December 1927 gives the complete Seventieth Congress Senate Committee on Expenditures in the Executive Departments roster:

- Frederic M. Sackett (chairman)
- Frederick Hale
- Henry W. Keyes
- Guy D. Goff
- Claude A. Swanson
- Thomas F. Bayard Jr.
- Harry B. Hawes

The committee therefore had **7 members**. The first four were Republicans and the latter three Democrats. Section 2 of the 1928 act allowed an information demand by the Senate committee or **any five members thereof**.

This produces a sharply different structural capacity from the House side:

```text
HOUSE (1928)
committee size: 21
majority-party members: 13
minority-party members: 8
statutory request threshold: 7

7 < committee majority (11)
7 <= minority contingent (8)
=> a request could structurally be made by a non-majority group
=> a request could structurally be made entirely by minority-party members

SENATE (1928)
committee size: 7
Republicans: 4
Democrats: 3
statutory request threshold: 5

5 > committee majority (4)
5 > minority contingent (3)
=> the five-member route required more than a bare committee majority
=> neither party contingent could satisfy the threshold alone
=> any five-member request necessarily required cross-party membership
```

This means the paired statutory language `any seven` / `any five` did **not** create equivalent member-group powers in the two chambers at enactment.

## Primary evidence

### Senate roster

The Congressional Record of December 1927 records the Senate standing committee assignments for the Seventieth Congress and lists the Committee on Expenditures in the Executive Departments as:

> Messrs. Sackett (chairman), Hale, Keyes, Goff, Swanson, Bayard, and Hawes.

Source:

- *Congressional Record*, 70th Cong., 1st sess., December 1927, p. 481, GovInfo PDF: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1928-pt1-v69/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1928-pt1-v69-7.pdf

**Evidence strength: very high.** This is a contemporaneous official congressional source and gives the full committee roster.

### Statutory threshold

Section 2 of the Act of May 29, 1928 required executive departments and independent establishments to furnish information on request of the relevant House committee or any seven members thereof, or the relevant Senate committee or any five members thereof. The current codification traces 5 U.S.C. §2954 directly to this provision.

Sources:

- Act of May 29, 1928, ch. 901, §2, 45 Stat. 996.
- 5 U.S.C. §2954, Historical and Revision Notes, derivation from May 29, 1928, ch. 901, §2, 45 Stat. 996: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section2954

**Evidence strength: very high.** Statutory text / official codification.

## 1. The Senate threshold was not a minority-member analogue of the House threshold

Modern discussion often describes §2954 as the House "seven-member rule" and Senate "rule of five" together. That is textually understandable, but historically it can conceal a major structural difference at enactment.

On the House side, seven members were fewer than both the 11 members needed for a majority of a 21-member committee and the 8-member minority contingent. A seven-member group could therefore be a true committee minority and could be composed entirely of minority-party members.

On the Senate side, five members out of seven were **more than a bare committee majority**. With a 4 Republican / 3 Democratic composition, neither party contingent could independently assemble five members. A five-member demand necessarily crossed the party divide.

Thus:

```text
same statutory form: "any N members"
!=
same institutional affordance
```

The meaning of a numerical threshold depends on the contemporaneous committee's size and composition.

## 2. This corrects a tempting hindsight projection

The present-day statute is frequently litigated and discussed as a congressional minority-information right. The D.C. Circuit in *Maloney v. Murphy* (2020), for example, emphasized that §2954 empowers a smaller, non-majority group and specifically recovered the 1928 House committee's 21-member, 13-majority/8-minority composition.

That description fits the **House threshold's 1928 structural effect**. It should not be projected automatically onto the Senate threshold.

For the Senate in 1928:

```text
five-member statutory group
=
5/7 of committee
=
supermajority-sized member group
=
necessarily bipartisan under the recorded 4/3 composition
```

The word `bipartisan` here describes a mathematical consequence of the recorded party composition, not an actor-stated legislative purpose.

Source for later legal reconstruction:

- *Maloney v. Murphy*, 984 F.3d 50 (D.C. Cir. 2020), discussing the House committee's 1928 composition and the non-majority capacity of the seven-member route.

**Evidence strength: high** for the later judicial interpretation; it is not contemporaneous evidence of 1928 intent.

## 3. Historical actors, later reconstruction, and present analysis

### Historical / legally explicit

Directly established by contemporaneous official evidence:

- the Senate committee had seven named members in December 1927;
- the 1928 statute authorized requests by any five Senate committee members;
- the House threshold was any seven members.

Party affiliations of the seven Senate members are independently established by congressional biographical records and the Seventieth Congress party record.

### Later reconstruction

- Modern courts characterize §2954 as unusual because it permits member groups smaller than the full committee to demand executive information.
- *Maloney* specifically reconstructs the House side as non-majoritarian.

This later vocabulary must not be attributed to the Seventieth Congress unless a contemporaneous source uses equivalent reasoning.

### Present analytical shorthand

The following are researcher labels:

- `threshold asymmetry`;
- `minority-capable House route`;
- `cross-party-required Senate route`;
- `institutional affordance`;
- `threshold / membership interaction`.

They summarize observable institutional consequences. They do not establish why Congress selected seven and five.

## 4. Problem-History payoff: thresholds are relational, not self-explanatory numbers

This case strengthens a general method rule for institutional Problem Episodes:

```text
formal threshold
+
contemporaneous body size
+
party / faction composition
=
actual structural capacity
```

A threshold should therefore not be coded merely as `N = 5` or `N = 7`. The episode should preserve at least:

- `threshold_n`;
- `eligible_body_size`;
- `ordinary_majority_n`;
- `majority_contingent_n`;
- `minority_contingent_n`;
- `single_minority_can_trigger`;
- `single_majority_can_trigger`;
- `cross_faction_required_given_composition`;
- `actor_stated_rationale_known`.

For 1928:

| Chamber | Body | Size | Party split | Request threshold | Minority alone? | Majority party alone? | Cross-party required? |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|---|
| House | Expenditures in Executive Departments | 21 | 13 / 8 | 7 | yes | yes | no |
| Senate | Expenditures in Executive Departments | 7 | 4 / 3 | 5 | no | no | yes |

The table records structural capacity under the 1928 membership, not legislative purpose.

## 5. Relation to the larger 1928 episode

The branch now supports a more granular sequence:

```text
inherited mandatory-report stock
→ Bureau of Efficiency item review
→ proposed mass repeal
→ concern over continued congressional information access
→ House-added / legislatively developed demand safeguard
→ two chamber-specific member thresholds
→ thresholds operate differently because recipient committees differ in size/composition
```

This is important because the substitute information route was not a single homogeneous institutional mechanism. Its House and Senate branches had materially different activation structures from the moment of enactment.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not call the entire 1928 statute a minority-party information-right design.** The House route was structurally minority-capable; the Senate route was not.
2. **Do not infer intent from arithmetic.** `7 of 21` and `5 of 7` are established institutional relationships; why Congress chose those numbers remains unresolved.
3. **Do not equate non-majority with minority party.** On the House side the threshold could be satisfied by a non-majority group and, given the eight-member minority contingent, by minority-party members alone. These are related but distinct propositions.
4. **Do not call the Senate threshold bipartisan by statutory text.** The statute said `any five`; cross-party participation follows from the particular 4/3 committee composition in 1928.
5. **Do not assume committee composition was immutable.** The structural effect of fixed statutory thresholds can change when committee size or party allocation changes.

## Remaining uncertainties

- No contemporaneous explanation has yet been recovered for why Congress selected exactly seven House members and five Senate members.
- The bill genealogy still needs to identify the first textual appearance of each threshold: Bureau draft, introduced bill, House amendment/report, Senate amendment/report, or conference/enrollment.
- It remains unknown whether Senate actors consciously chose five because the committee had seven members, because five corresponded to a conventional working group/quorum practice, or for another reason.
- The exact party composition should be cross-checked against the Official Congressional Directory for the relevant session, although the named roster plus congressional party records already makes the 4/3 split highly secure.
