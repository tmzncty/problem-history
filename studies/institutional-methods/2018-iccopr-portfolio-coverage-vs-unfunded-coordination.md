# 2018 ICCOPR: measurable priority coverage without a funded execution mandate

## Research question and non-duplication

Existing packets establish the 2015 six-year plan, its 2022 successor, and the 2023 National Academies critique of funding/implementation. This packet adds **contemporaneous 2018 congressional questioning and quantified interim implementation claims**, rather than another retrospective claim that ICCOPR lacked resources.

## New primary evidence (April 17, 2018)

At the Senate Commerce Committee hearing on the nomination of Vice Admiral Karl L. Schultz as Coast Guard Commandant, written questions (Q9–Q15) explicitly challenged the adequacy of funding and implementation of ICCOPR's FY2015–2021 plan. Schultz's submitted answers stated:

- ICCOPR **itself was not funded and did not undertake research projects**. Its 15 member agencies funded their own projects through their own appropriations; ICCOPR coordinated information and strategic direction (Q10, Q12–Q13).
- The 2015 plan contained **25 Standing Research Areas and 150 priorities**. The Coast Guard's reported 2016–2017 inventory found member agencies conducting research in **92% of the areas**, with projects addressing **some aspect of 61% of the priorities** (Q11).
- For FY2018–2019 ICCOPR intended to analyze gaps in the first two years' coverage (Q11).
- Neither the Coast Guard nor ICCOPR had completed an exhaustive estimate of adequate investment specifically for tar-sands response technology (Q9).
- When asked whether ICCOPR would meet all goals by FY2021, the answer emphasized that **ICCOPR did not itself meet research goals**; member agencies used the plan as strategic direction (Q12).
- When asked about National Laboratory projects, the answer distinguished ICCOPR's convening from projects contracted by its members (Q14–Q15).

Primary source: *Nomination of Vice Admiral Karl L. Schultz*, hearing before Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation, 115th Congress, 2nd Session, **April 17, 2018**, S. Hrg. 115-772, written questions and answers, especially pp. 50–55. https://www.congress.gov/115/chrg/CHRG-115shrg39949/CHRG-115shrg39949.pdf (text around lines 2046–2116 in web extraction).

## Historical actors' questions vs our reconstruction

**Actor-explicit:** senators asked how much funding ICCOPR needed, whether interagency agreements could improve it, how many priorities were being addressed, and whether all research goals could be met. Schultz's answers explicitly distinguished ICCOPR's coordinating role from the member agencies' funding/execution roles. These are contemporaneous claims from a nominee's written responses, not independent audited performance outcomes.

**Researcher reconstruction:** the 2015 planning reform created a shared priority vocabulary and a way to measure **portfolio alignment**, but the 2018 exchange exposes a boundary between **coordination/coverage** and **funded delivery**:

`plan with 150 priorities -> mapping member projects to priorities -> 61% some-aspect coverage -> no ICCOPR project appropriation or direct delivery mandate`

The 92% and 61% numbers measure different denominators. Neither measures completion, effectiveness, expenditure adequacy, or causal impact of ICCOPR.

**Later perspective:** the National Academies' 2023 criticism of insufficient implementation strategy and sustained funding can be compared to this 2018 exchange, but **must not be projected back as the 2018 actors' full diagnosis**, nor treated as evidence that the 2018 hearing caused the 2019–2021 statutory changes.

## Evidence grades

- **A**: the hearing's date, the questions posed, and the Coast Guard nominee's stated organizational/funding distinction.
- **A for attribution; B for underlying reality**: reported 92%/61% research portfolio coverage, absent a separately audited project-by-project inventory.
- **Not established**: 61% of priorities achieved; 39% had no relevant work at all (the criterion was only 'some aspect'); adequacy of funding; direct legislative transmission to S.2297; an ICCOPR-specific congressional appropriation.
