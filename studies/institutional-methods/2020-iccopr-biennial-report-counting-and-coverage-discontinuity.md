# 2020 ICCOPR biennial report: project-count ambiguity and loss of comparable priority coverage

## Delta / question
The existing PR #41 packet `2018-iccopr-original-biennial-report-inventory-vs-crosswalk.md` recovered the FY2016–17 report (316 active projects, >250 publications, 92% of 25 standing research areas, 61% of 150 priorities) and explicitly deferred comparison with FY2018–19 until definitions were checked. The **May 14, 2020** original FY2018–19 report is now available and exposes a new, source-internal comparability problem.

**Primary source:** ICCOPR / U.S. Coast Guard, *Interagency Coordinating Committee on Oil Pollution Research: FY 2018–2019 Activities*, Report to Congress, May 14, 2020, https://www.dco.uscg.mil/Portals/9/ICCOPR%20Biennial%20Report%20to%20Congress%20FY2018-2019_Approved%20%28Signed%29.pdf

## Source-anchored observations
1. **Different counts within the same report.** The signed transmittal and Executive Summary state **150** oil-pollution research projects during FY2018–19 and **more than 60** publications (cover letter; Executive Summary, p. 1). But the detailed “Member Research and Technology Initiatives” passage describes an analysis of **more than 139 projects** (p. 6), without explaining the relation of that analytical subset to the 150. This is not proof of contradiction: `>139` can include 150, and the populations or counting stages may differ. It is a **documentary ambiguity**, not a verified data error.
2. **Activity, not completion.** The note attached to the p. 6 analysis defines included projects as initiated, ongoing, and/or completed during FY2018–19. This repeats the broad temporal inclusion of the prior report. It still does not prove equal counting, deduplication, or reporting coverage across cycles.
3. **Priority-denominator continuity without published coverage fraction.** The Executive Summary retains the 2015 plan's **25 standing research areas and 150 priorities** (p. 1), but unlike the FY2016–17 report it does **not** supply a headline 92%-/61%-style coverage calculation for FY2018–19. Thus `316 → 150` cannot be interpreted as decline in research capacity, and `61% → ?` cannot be filled from the project count.
4. **Reporting-object continuity.** Appendix B is labeled “Fiscal Year 2018 and 2019 Research Projects” (table of contents, p. i; body p. 6), so an inventory survives even as the earlier headline priority-coverage indicator is not repeated. This contrasts with the FY2016–17 report's body/appendix letter mismatch already documented in PR #41.
5. **Actor-stated carryover.** The Executive Summary explicitly says that tracking/monitoring, interagency research, public communication and research-to-operations initiatives were introduced in the FY2016–17 report and remained in progress (p. 1). This supports institutional continuity of the **stated agenda**, not success in solving measurement problems.
6. **Another actor problem, separately documented.** The FY2018–19 report describes the January 2019 GAO-19-31 recommendations: more systematic consultation with nonfederal stakeholders and review of whether committee membership should expand (p. 6). Do not merge this membership/stakeholder diagnosis with the separate project-priority mapping problem.

## Actor / later reconstruction / hindsight
- **Contemporary actors (2020 report):** 150 projects and >60 publications; >139 analyzed projects; ongoing tracking and research-to-operations initiatives; 25 areas / 150 priorities; GAO recommendations as described by ICCOPR.
- **Source-critical reconstruction:** The report provides two differently phrased project counts and does not explicitly reconcile them. The earlier 61% priority coverage is not reproduced in the new executive summary or project-analysis passage.
- **Present-day analytical question:** Did the measurement regime shift from priority coverage toward a project inventory, or was a coverage crosswalk still made internally but omitted from publication? This is a question, **not** an established institutional change or actor motive.

## Evidence strength and limitations
- **High:** official dated report, explicit numbers, footnote inclusion window, appendix designation, carryover initiatives.
- **Medium:** observation of non-repetition of the earlier headline coverage percentages in the principal reporting sections; this does not prove that no internal calculations existed.
- **Not established:** exact 139/150 reconciliation; same sampling/deduplication between cycles; changes in total federal oil-pollution research activity; changes in research-priority coverage; causal relation to 2019–2021 legal amendments.
