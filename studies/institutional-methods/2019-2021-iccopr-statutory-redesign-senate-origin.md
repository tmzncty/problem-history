# 2019–2021 ICCOPR statutory redesign: Senate-origin evidence for external review and ten-year updates

## Why this packet exists

The existing ICCOPR chain reaches a 2021 statutory architecture that combined a new research plan, National Academy of Sciences review/advice, NIST guidance, and ten-year statutory updates. The remaining source debt was actor/process-level: did this architecture arise only in the final 2020/2021 enactment, and can its immediate legislative origin be located?

Fresh primary evidence narrows that question substantially. The core architecture already appears in the Senate Commerce Committee's reported version of **S. 2297, Coast Guard Authorization Act of 2019**, reported September 26, 2019. This predates both final NDAA enactment and publication of the FY2022–2027 OPRTP.

## New finding

### 1. The core architecture was already present in the Senate-reported bill in September 2019

Section 429 of S. 2297, as reported by the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation on September 26, 2019, proposed a broad rewrite of Oil Pollution Act §7001 (33 U.S.C. §2761).

The bill required ICCOPR to submit a new research plan within 180 days. The plan had to:

- identify current research programs across Federal agencies, State and tribal governments, universities, and corporate entities;
- assess the current state of knowledge on prevention, response, mitigation, and environmental effects;
- identify significant research gaps and technological deficiencies exposed by past spills;
- establish national research priorities and goals;
- assess technologies across classes of oil;
- estimate resources and timetables; and
- summarize research on equipment in currents, ice cover, and ice floes.

The same section required the Chair to contract with the National Academy of Sciences to:

1. advise on preparation and development of the plan;
2. assess the adequacy of the submitted plan and report that assessment to Congress; and
3. provide organizational guidance for implementation, including allocation of topics/research among participating Federal agencies.

It separately required NIST advice on quality assurance and measurement standards.

Finally, the bill required an updated research plan **ten years after enactment and every ten years thereafter**.

Primary source:

- S. 2297, 116th Congress, reported in Senate Sept. 26, 2019, §429, pp. 209–220 (especially pp. 213–216): https://www.congress.gov/116/bills/s2297/BILLS-116s2297rs.pdf

**Evidence strength: very high.** This is the reported legislative text itself.

## 2. This changes the chronology of the ten-year rule

The existing packet correctly distinguishes ICCOPR's six-year organizational planning cadence from the later ten-year statutory cadence. The new evidence adds a timing constraint:

```text
post-Deepwater-Horizon ICCOPR practice
    -> six-year organizational planning commitment
    -> FY2015–2021 plan

by Sept. 2019
    -> Senate-reported S. 2297 already proposes:
       new plan + NAS development/adequacy/implementation review
       + NIST guidance + ten-year statutory updates

Jan. 2021
    -> substantially the same architecture enacted in P.L. 116-283 §8304

2022
    -> FY2022–2027 successor OPRTP produced

2023
    -> National Academies evaluates plan/process/implementation
```

Therefore the ten-year cadence cannot be explained as a congressional reaction to problems first observed in the FY2022–2027 planning cycle. It was already legislative text in 2019.

Likewise, the National Academies review role was not invented after the 2022 plan existed. Congress's Senate-side proposal had already designed an **external review layer around the planning process**.

## 3. The statutory design was broader than a cadence change

The 2019 bill did not merely say `update every ten years`. It bundled several institutional controls:

- a legislatively specified content schema for the plan;
- external scientific advice during plan development;
- external adequacy assessment after submission;
- external organizational guidance for implementation;
- NIST quality/measurement advice;
- recurring statutory update obligation;
- quarterly ICCOPR meetings and public summaries;
- expanded/explicit research topics and testing provisions elsewhere in §429.

This matters because `six years vs ten years` is only one visible parameter. The legislative object was a broader redesign of **how planning would be produced, checked, and implemented**.

Researcher shorthand only:

`internal recurring planning -> statutorily specified planning + external review architecture`

Do not attribute that phrase to senators.

## 4. Historical actors / legislative reconstruction / present analysis

### Historical actors and institutions

Directly supportable:

- Senators Dan Sullivan, Roger Wicker, Maria Cantwell, and Edward Markey introduced S. 2297 on July 25, 2019.
- The Senate Commerce Committee reported the bill with amendments on September 26, 2019.
- The reported §429 already contained the new plan, NAS advice/assessment/implementation role, NIST guidance, and ten-year update rule.
- The final 2021 law contains substantially the same planning/review architecture, with enactment-date references updated to the Elijah E. Cummings Coast Guard Authorization Act of 2020.

### Later reconstruction

The 2023 National Academies review is useful for evaluating how the FY2022–2027 plan was developed and for reconstructing ICCOPR's six-year organizational cadence, but it is not needed to establish the 2019 legislative origin of the external-review/ten-year architecture.

## 5. Methodological payoff

The case suggests that a Problem Episode schema should distinguish at least two mechanisms that are easy to collapse:

```text
update rule
    = when/how a solution must be renewed

review rule
    = who evaluates the renewed solution and by what authority
```

ICCOPR's history now contains both. Its organizational six-year cadence is an update rule. The 2019 Senate proposal adds a statutory ten-year update rule **and** a National Academies review/advice rule.

A third variable is also visible:

```text
content rule
    = what the renewed plan must contain
```

S. 2297 specified research inventory, knowledge assessment, gaps, priorities, technology applicability, resources/timetables, and environmental-condition testing. A plan can therefore be recurrent without being externally reviewed, and externally reviewed without sharing the same cadence as the organization's own planning cycle.

## 6. Hindsight risks

1. **Do not say Congress chose ten years because the 2022 plan-development process was too compressed.** The ten-year language was already in the Senate-reported bill in September 2019.
2. **Do not say the 2021 statute simply codified ICCOPR's six-year cycle.** It did not; the Senate text already specified ten years.
3. **Do not infer the reason for choosing exactly ten years from the bill text.** The text proves the choice and architecture, not the actor rationale for the interval.
4. **Do not infer that National Academies review was a response to the 2023 review's own findings.** The review mandate predates the review.
5. **Do not treat S. 2297 reported text as final law.** It is strong evidence of legislative origin/process; enactment occurred later through P.L. 116-283.
6. **Do not infer that all four sponsors personally authored or endorsed every design detail.** Sponsorship and committee-reported text identify the legislative vehicle and institutional locus, not individual drafting responsibility.

## 7. What remains unknown

The highest-value unresolved question has now narrowed considerably:

**Why did Senate actors choose this particular bundle, especially a ten-year statutory cadence and mandatory National Academy review, while ICCOPR itself was operating on a six-year planning cycle?**

The reported bill text establishes *where and when* the architecture appears, but not the rationale for each design choice.

Priority source targets:

1. the Senate Commerce Committee report accompanying S. 2297, if recoverable, especially its explanation of §429;
2. introduced-vs-reported diff for S. 2297 to determine whether the planning/review architecture was present on July 25 or added/changed in committee;
3. markup transcript, amendments, sponsor statements, or committee press material around §429;
4. Coast Guard / ICCOPR technical-assistance correspondence to Senate Commerce;
5. predecessor bills from the 115th/116th Congress that may reveal transmission of the language;
6. House/Senate conference evolution into P.L. 116-283 §8304.

## Compact finding

> The 2021 ICCOPR statutory redesign did not first appear at final NDAA enactment and cannot be read as a reaction to the later FY2022–2027 planning experience. Senate-reported S. 2297 already contained the core architecture on September 26, 2019: a new content-specified research plan, mandatory National Academy of Sciences advice during development, external adequacy assessment and implementation guidance, NIST quality/measurement advice, and ten-year statutory updates. This moves the source problem upstream. The unresolved historical question is no longer when the architecture appeared, but why Senate actors selected this particular external-review and ten-year-update design while ICCOPR's own organizational planning practice used a six-year cycle.
