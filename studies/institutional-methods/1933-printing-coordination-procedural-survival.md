# 1933 printing coordination: organizational abolition without procedural extinction

## Why this advances the existing episode

The repository already establishes that the Permanent Conference on Printing (PCP), organized in 1921, was included in the Federal Coordinating Service abolished in 1933. The unresolved question was whether its printing-specific functions transferred to the Treasury Procurement Division or simply disappeared.

New evidence does **not** justify a direct PCP → Procurement Division transfer edge. It instead supports a different and methodologically useful result: several PCP-produced standards and economies survived the organization that created them. This separates **organizational survival**, **function transfer**, and **procedural/standard survival**.

## New findings

### 1. The Procurement Division inherited only specified Federal Coordinating Service functions

NARA's Record Group 137 administrative history states that the Federal Coordinating Service was abolished by EO 6166 and that **most functions** transferred to the Treasury Procurement Division. But its more precise description identifies the transferred functions as those relating to disposal of seized/surplus property and procurement, warehousing, and distribution of property, exercised through named bodies such as the Federal Traffic Board, Federal Specifications Board, Federal Standard Stock Catalog Board, Federal Real Estate Board, and Interdepartmental Board of Contracts and Adjustments.

The Treasury Secretary's FY1934 annual report gives essentially the same enumerated transfer. The Permanent Conference on Printing is not named.

**Evidence strength:** high for the proposition that the documented transfer instrument did not simply transfer the Federal Coordinating Service wholesale; medium for the negative proposition that no printing-specific PCP function transferred elsewhere, because omission from these lists is not exhaustive proof of non-transfer.

Sources:
- NARA, RG 137, Records of the Federal Supply Service: https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/137.html
- Annual Report of the Secretary of the Treasury, FY1934 (FRASER): https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/annual-report-secretary-treasury-state-finances-194/annual-report-secretary-treasury-state-finances-fiscal-year-ended-june-30-1934-5580/fulltext
- GAO, 17 Comp. Gen. 700 (1938), reproducing the 1933/1934 transfer structure: https://www.gao.gov/products/90983-1

### 2. PCP-generated arrangements survived PCP itself

The Government Printing Office's official centennial history, *100 GPO Years, 1861–1961*, retrospectively states that the PCP was abolished by the June 10, 1933 executive order. It then lists durable results of the conference: direct printing-and-binding appropriations rather than the former allotment plan; standardized letterhead stock and size; 172 standardized forms for common departmental use; envelope varieties reduced to four standards; publication sizes reduced from more than fifty to eight standards; and other procedural corrections.

Most importantly, the GPO history says that the conference's recommendations produced immediate savings and that **those savings were still being extended in 1961**.

This is retrospective institutional testimony, not a 1933 actor statement, and the savings claim is not independently audited here. But it is strong evidence that GPO itself understood the conference's standards and procedures as surviving the conference's organizational death for decades.

Source:
- U.S. Government Printing Office, *100 GPO Years, 1861–1961*, p. 126: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-GP3-4938ab5a455054f8d34d9c153eba2bf1/pdf/GOVPUB-GP3-4938ab5a455054f8d34d9c153eba2bf1.pdf

### 3. Pre-abolition sources show what kind of durable objects the conference produced

The 1931 *Standards Yearbook* describes standardization of permanent forms as a principal PCP function and says systematic standardization was being used to eliminate duplication. The 1932 volume records continued work on paper stocks, standard stock books/tablets, and publication-format recommendations. These are not merely recurring meetings; they are standards, forms, specifications, and routines capable of persisting without the coordinating body that originated them.

Sources:
- NBS, *Standards Yearbook 1931*: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/MP/nbsmiscellaneouspub119.pdf
- NBS, *Standards Yearbook 1932*: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/MP/nbsmiscellaneouspub133.pdf

## Actor / reconstruction / researcher separation

### Historical actors

By 1931–1932, PCP participants and contemporary federal publications treated standardization, elimination of duplication, paper/form specifications, and printing economy as active administrative tasks. In 1933 the Public Printer stated that the conference itself had been abolished with the Federal Coordinating Service.

Nothing found in this pass shows a 1933 actor saying that the Procurement Division inherited the PCP as such, or that abolition meant the printing-coordination problem had been solved.

### Later institutional reconstruction

NARA and the Treasury's administrative histories reconstruct the specific functions transferred into Procurement Division. The 1961 GPO centennial retrospectively attributes durable standards and continuing savings to the abolished PCP.

### Present-day analytical reconstruction

A useful candidate distinction is:

```text
organization survival
    != authority survival
    != function transfer
    != standard/procedure survival
    != problem-formulation survival
```

A candidate label for the newly evidenced mechanism is **procedural sediment survival**: an organization can disappear while standards, forms, specifications, and routines it produced remain embedded in later practice.

This is our analytical vocabulary, not the actors' term.

## Consequence for the existing 1921–1968 chain

The episode should no longer be represented only as:

```text
1921 coordination organization
  -> 1933 organizational abolition
  -> uncertain successor
```

A better evidence-sensitive representation is:

```text
1921 PCP created
  -> standards / forms / routines accumulated
  -> 1933 PCP organizationally abolished
       |-> selected Federal Coordinating Service procurement functions transferred to Procurement Division
       |-> PCP-specific transfer remains unproven
       `-> PCP-produced standards/procedures continue to generate claimed economies afterward
  -> 1953 report-printing waiver permanentized
  -> 1961 GPO still describes PCP recommendations as yielding continuing savings
  -> 1968 printing-waiver authority codified
```

This now supplies a 40-year organizational/procedural chain and, when combined with the 1921–1968 statutory path already documented in the repository, strengthens the broader multi-decade transformation analysis without inventing a transfer edge.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not infer transfer from functional similarity.** Procurement Division dealt with procurement and standardization, but that alone does not prove it inherited PCP printing functions.
2. **Do not infer extinction from abolition.** PCP's legal/organizational death did not erase standards already embedded in forms, sizes, specifications, or GPO practice.
3. **Do not treat the 1961 savings statement as a contemporaneous 1933 motive.** It is later institutional memory.
4. **Do not turn persistence into intentional preservation.** A procedure may survive because it is already embedded, even without an actor consciously deciding to preserve the original institution's program.
5. **Do not equate procedural survival with problem-formulation survival.** The rule can persist after actors stop articulating the original problem in the same terms.

## Remaining uncertainties

- Whether a Treasury, GPO, Budget Bureau, or Joint Committee on Printing instrument explicitly reassigned any PCP printing-coordination duties after October 1933.
- Which PCP standards remained formally binding versus merely customary after abolition.
- Whether the 1961 claim of continuing savings can be reconstructed quantitatively from GPO cost or requisition records.
- Whether the 1935 GPO Style Manual preserves explicit PCP-approved rules while dropping the institution's name; this would be a useful object-level continuity test.
