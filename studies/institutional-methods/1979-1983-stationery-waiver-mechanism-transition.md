# 1979–1983: stationery waivers change from legacy-size exception to general standards exception

## Why this follow-up matters

The open paper-size research already established two facts: FPMR Temporary Regulation B-5 allowed post-1979 waivers for continued 8 × 10.5-inch stationery, and the 1983 permanent rule canceled all pre-existing GSA stationery waivers while retaining a new §101-11.603-15 `Waivers` section. The missing question was what the new waiver mechanism actually required.

The full 1983 text now closes that rule-level edge. It shows that permanentization did not merely preserve the old waiver procedure under a new section number. The object and administrative route of the exception changed.

## Primary evidence

### 1. 1979 B-5: waiver is specifically an exception to the old-size cutoff

FPMR Temporary Regulation B-5, published October 2, 1979, prohibited agencies after December 31, 1979 from printing or procuring printing of 8 × 10.5-inch stationery or its multiple sizes unless NARS approved the action in writing. Waiver requests were addressed to GSA/NARS.

Source:

- 44 FR 56699 (Oct. 2, 1979), FPMR Temporary Regulation B-5, `Stationery Standards`: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1979-10-02/pdf/FR-1979-10-02.pdf

**Evidence strength:** very high / direct rule text.

The actor-level exception object is narrow and transition-specific:

```text
normal rule after 1979-12-31:
no new 8 × 10.5 stationery printing/procurement
        ↓
exception:
written NARS approval
```

### 2. 1983 permanent rule: old waivers are canceled, but waiver-making survives

FPMR Amendment B-52, published July 6, 1983, explicitly canceled all GSA stationery waivers issued before the final rule while establishing a permanent §101-11.603-15.

The recovered full text of §101-11.603-15 states that an agency seeking a waiver **of the regulation** must submit a written request to the Director, Office of Information Systems, Office of Information Resources Management. The request must contain:

- a statement of justification;
- pertinent specifications; and
- where appropriate, scaled samples showing designs.

Source:

- 48 FR 31033–31037 (July 6, 1983), especially §101-11.603-15, GovInfo: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1983-07-06/pdf/FR-1983-07-06.pdf

**Evidence strength:** very high / direct final-rule text.

This resolves an uncertainty in the companion `1983-stationery-permanent-rule-waiver-reset-and-standard-hierarchy.md`: the permanent waiver section was not merely a heading whose criteria remained unknown.

## New finding: exception capacity survived, but the exception object broadened

The transition is now more precise than `old waivers canceled → new waivers possible`.

```text
1979 temporary regime
waiver object = continued production/procurement
of legacy 8 × 10.5 stationery
        ↓
1983 permanentization
all inherited GSA stationery waivers canceled
        ↓
new permanent §101-11.603-15
waiver object = the stationery regulation generally
        ↓
request must document justification + specifications
(+ design samples where appropriate)
```

Thus permanentization changed at least three dimensions:

1. **exception state:** inherited waivers were reset;
2. **exception scope:** the waiver mechanism was no longer textually limited to continued old-size production;
3. **exception administration:** requests went through the Office of Information Systems / Office of Information Resources Management under the permanent stationery-management regime.

The present-day analytical description `exception-mechanism migration` is useful here, but it is not historical actor vocabulary.

## Actor / later reconstruction / present analysis

### Historical actors

Directly supportable:

- 1979 NARS/GSA treated waiver as a gate for post-cutoff use of the old stationery size.
- 1983 GSA canceled inherited stationery waivers.
- 1983 GSA nevertheless retained a written waiver procedure for the permanent regulation.
- the new procedure required justification and technical/design documentation.

### Later reconstruction

Later shorthand such as “the Federal Government switched to Letter size in 1980” misses both the transition-era waiver gate and the later permanent exception machinery.

### Present analytical language

Candidate terms only:

- `exception-state reset` — already identified in the companion packet;
- `exception-mechanism migration` — exception-making survives while its scope/administrative route changes;
- `exception-capacity survival` — concrete exceptions disappear without eliminating the institutional capacity to authorize deviations.

Do not promote these to general schema categories from this case alone.

## Methodological payoff

For standards transitions, recording only `waiver allowed: yes/no` is insufficient. A more useful evidence model asks:

- What exactly can be waived?
- Who approves the waiver?
- What evidence must the applicant provide?
- Are inherited waivers grandfathered, canceled, or re-reviewed?
- Does permanentization preserve the same exception object, or create a broader/narrower one?

This case shows:

```text
exception capacity continuity
!=
exception-state continuity
!=
exception-scope continuity
!=
approving-office continuity
```

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not say all pre-1983 waivers concerned 8 × 10.5 paper.** The final rule says all GSA stationery waivers were canceled; the actual population has not been recovered.
2. **Do not say agencies automatically received replacement waivers.** The new rule requires a written request with justification and supporting specifications.
3. **Do not infer the substantive approval threshold from the submission requirements.** The recovered text tells applicants what to submit, but does not state a quantified or exhaustive merits test.
4. **Do not infer practical nonconformity from the existence of a waiver procedure.** Exception capacity is not evidence that waivers were frequently granted.
5. **Do not treat the 1983 general waiver section as proof that old-size stationery remained an ordinary permissible exception.** An actual post-1983 request/grant is still needed for that claim.

## Compact finding

> The 1983 permanent stationery rule did not simply abolish exceptions created during the 8 × 10.5 → 8.5 × 11 transition. It canceled inherited GSA stationery waivers while creating a new written waiver procedure for the permanent regulation generally. Requests now had to state justification and provide pertinent specifications and, where appropriate, scaled design samples. The historical transition is therefore not `waivers end`, but `legacy exception state reset + exception capacity survives under a broader permanent administrative mechanism`. What remains missing is actual waiver practice: which agencies reapplied, what deviations they sought, and which requests GSA granted.
