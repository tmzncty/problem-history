# 2002–2009: ICCOPR report restoration did not settle the underlying research-coordination problem

## Research question

The companion FRESA packets trace ICCOPR §7001(e) through pre-sunset preservation, legal lapse, a missing biennial report, and statutory restoration in 2002. What happened after the reporting channel returned? Did restoration of the report also amount to restoration or settlement of the underlying oil-pollution R&D coordination problem?

Fresh 2009 congressional hearing evidence gives a useful negative answer. Historical actors could simultaneously treat the restored biennial reporting channel as functioning, defend real interagency communication/coordination, and reopen the broader research program as inadequate, under-resourced, stale, or in need of structural redesign.

This packet therefore adds a new layer distinction to the existing FRESA genealogy:

```text
reporting-channel restoration
!=
substantive program restoration
!=
problem settlement
```

## 1. 2002 restored the reporting requirement, not the whole 1990 settlement

Existing companion work establishes that P.L. 107-295 §322(b)(6) made the FRESA sunset inapplicable to Oil Pollution Act §7001(e), restoring/preserving ICCOPR's biennial activities report after the 2000 lapse.

That action should remain coded narrowly. It repaired one statutory information channel. It did not reenact Title VII wholesale, provide a new research plan, restore every earlier funding authority, or prove that the research-coordination architecture was substantively adequate.

Primary source already bound in companion packet:

- P.L. 107-295 §322(b)(6), 116 Stat. 2104: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-107publ295/html/PLAW-107publ295.htm

**Evidence strength:** very high for restoration of §7001(e); no inference from this statute alone about program effectiveness.

## 2. 2009 actors reopened the substantive problem while acknowledging the reporting channel

On June 4, 2009 the House Science and Technology Subcommittee on Energy and Environment held *A New Direction for Federal Oil Spill Research and Development*. The hearing record reconstructs the 1990 program and notes that ICCOPR had produced plans in 1992 and 1997, but that the research plan had not been updated since 1997. The hearing background also states that technological advances had been modest and reproduces ICCOPR's own 1997 conclusion that most technology and information gaps identified in 1990 remained, attributing this to inadequate appropriations for oil-pollution technology programs.

Primary source:

- House Science and Technology Committee, *A New Direction for Federal Oil Spill Research and Development*, June 4, 2009, Serial No. 111-29: https://www.congress.gov/111/chrg/CHRG-111hhrg49820/CHRG-111hhrg49820.pdf (especially pp. 7–14).

**Evidence strength:** very high for the 2009 committee's problem framing; the quoted 1997 gap assessment is also historical ICCOPR evidence as reproduced in the official hearing record.

The committee's proposed answer was not merely “make sure the biennial report exists.” The bill under discussion sought to reorient the interagency program toward emerging threats and make the interagency structure more efficient, with a new assessment, plan, public-comment process, grants, and annual reporting architecture.

Thus by 2009 the problem object had expanded/reopened beyond the information channel restored in 2002.

## 3. The 2009 hearing contains an important actor disagreement: coordination could work while the program still looked inadequate

The hearing should not be flattened into a simple “ICCOPR failed” story.

Chairman Brian Baird asked directly about the status of the coordinating committee. Rear Admiral James Watson answered that the committee did meet, that research activity and communication continued, and that a biennial report compiled the agencies' activities for Congress. He said avoiding duplication and maintaining dialogue were occurring, while also saying the committee could be taken to another level and noting that some earlier grant/funding authorities had ended or depended on appropriations.

Later, EPA's Albert Venosa defended the working-level network: overlapping participants met monthly through the National Response Team Science and Technology Committee, communicated about research, collaborated and coordinated. But he also conceded: “Perhaps we haven’t been as good about reporting to Congress as much as we should.” Watson likewise described the system as working reasonably well in feeding responder needs into research, while identifying weaker organization/incentives for private-sector participation.

Primary source:

- same 2009 hearing, pp. 40–47, especially Watson/Venosa exchange.

**Evidence strength:** very high for actor-level disagreement/qualification.

This means the evidence does **not** support:

```text
2002 restoration
→ ICCOPR still failed in every sense
→ 2009 replacement
```

A more faithful reconstruction is:

```text
working-level communication / some coordination survives
+
biennial reporting exists again
+
old research plan remains stale
+
resources / authorities / incentives are constrained
+
Congress reopens whether the program structure is adequate
```

## 4. 2009 actors distinguished reporting from doing

This episode is especially useful because the actors themselves expose different layers.

Venosa's formulation allows both:

- interagency communication/collaboration/coordination is happening; and
- reporting to Congress could be better.

Watson allows both:

- the biennial report is compiled and submitted; and
- the committee could be taken “to another level,” with earlier grants/trust-fund research activity no longer available in the same way.

House actors, meanwhile, proposed a redesigned committee/program because the broader R&D results and structure were not considered adequate for emerging threats.

Therefore:

```text
coordination practice
!=
reporting performance
!=
research funding/capacity
!=
plan currency
!=
technology outcome
```

This is stronger than the earlier general warning `report restoration != program restoration` because the distinctions are visible inside one contemporaneous hearing.

## 5. Problem transformation after the FRESA episode

The ICCOPR sub-chain can now be extended:

```text
1990
OPA creates interagency R&D coordination program
+ plan + biennial congressional reporting
        ↓
1997
updated plan still reports many 1990 gaps remaining
        ↓
1995–2000 FRESA process
reporting requirement scheduled for sunset
        ↓
2000
House preselects §7001(e) for preservation
but bicameral preservation is not completed
        ↓
legal lapse + missing report cycle
        ↓
2002
§7001(e) reporting channel restored
        ↓
2009
biennial reporting exists and working-level coordination is defended
BUT
plan has not been updated since 1997
resources/authorities are constrained
Congress considers reorienting the program and streamlining structure
```

The important transformation is that a repaired accountability channel does not close the substantive problem. The same institution can move from an **information-channel preservation problem** back into a **program-capability / coordination-design problem**.

Researcher-side label only: `layer-specific restoration with substantive re-problematization`.

## 6. Historical actors / later reconstruction / present analysis

### Historical actors

Directly supportable:

- 2002 Congress restored/preserved the ICCOPR biennial reporting requirement.
- In 2009 House actors treated the federal oil-spill R&D program as needing a new direction, citing stale planning, modest advances, new threats, and interagency-structure concerns.
- Watson and Venosa nevertheless defended real interagency communication/coordination and described biennial reporting as operating.
- Venosa separately acknowledged room for improvement in reporting to Congress.
- Witnesses identified resource, authority, incentive, and research-translation issues rather than presenting one single failure mechanism.

### Later reconstruction

GAO-11-319 (2011) later found limited ICCOPR action to foster communication among member agencies between 1997 and 2009, irregular meetings before the late 2000s, inconsistent representation, and a need for stronger coordination and planning. This is valuable later evaluation, but should not overwrite the more heterogeneous 2009 actor field.

- GAO-11-319, March 25, 2011: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-11-319

## 7. Methodological payoff

A restoration event should be typed by **what layer is restored**.

For institutional information systems, distinguish at least:

- legal reporting requirement;
- actual report production;
- underlying program/capability;
- coordinating organization/practice;
- planning artifact and update cycle;
- funding/authority;
- observed technical outcomes.

A restored report can coexist with a stale plan, weak resources, disputed coordination quality, or unsolved technical gaps. Conversely, actors can defend substantive coordination while conceding weak congressional reporting.

Therefore:

```text
restoration at layer X
!=
return to the prior institutional state as a whole
```

This complements the repository's earlier `information-layer decomposition` work on production/submission/printing/distribution/retention.

## 8. Hindsight risks

1. **Do not say the 2002 restoration fixed ICCOPR.** It restored a reporting requirement.
2. **Do not say the 2009 hearing proves ICCOPR was simply nonfunctional.** Watson and Venosa explicitly described functioning communication, collaboration, coordination, and biennial reporting.
3. **Do not use the 2011 GAO assessment as if it were the unanimous 2009 actor view.** It is later evaluation and is more critical than some hearing witnesses.
4. **Do not infer that the missing 2000 report caused the 2009 reauthorization effort.** The 2009 problem framing centers broader R&D capability, planning, resources, new threats, and structure.
5. **Do not equate stale planning with no research.** Agencies continued research; the issue was the age/adequacy of the comprehensive plan and wider program performance.
6. **Do not treat proposed 2009 redesign as enacted replacement.** This packet concerns problem re-formulation and proposed solution architecture, not final enactment.

## Compact finding

> The 2002 restoration of ICCOPR §7001(e) repaired a statutory reporting channel, not the oil-pollution R&D program as a whole. By 2009 Congress was again problematizing the program's stale research plan, limited resources, emerging threats, and interagency structure. Yet witnesses simultaneously defended functioning interagency communication and coordination and confirmed that biennial reports were again being submitted, while acknowledging reporting could improve. The episode therefore supplies a clean layer distinction: `reporting-channel restoration != substantive capability restoration != problem settlement`. A restored accountability artifact can coexist with renewed contest over the underlying program, and historical actors may disagree about which layer is actually failing.
