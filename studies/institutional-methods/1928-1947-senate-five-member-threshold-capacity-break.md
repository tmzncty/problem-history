# 1928–1947: the Senate five-member information threshold changes capacity without changing text

## Research question

When did the 1928 Senate `any five members` information-demand rule first cease to be a 5-of-7, cross-party-required mechanism and become a non-majoritarian / minority-party-capable mechanism?

## New finding

The break can now be located at the opening of the 80th Congress in January 1947, when the Legislative Reorganization Act of 1946 was implemented.

In 1928 the Senate Committee on Expenditures in the Executive Departments had seven members (4 Republicans, 3 Democrats). Under §2 of the 1928 reporting-repeal act, an information demand required any five members. Thus five exceeded both party contingents and the four-member bare committee majority: under that contemporaneous composition, cross-party participation was mathematically required.

The Legislative Reorganization Act of 1946 reorganized the Senate committee system, reducing 33 standing committees to 15 and assigning the reorganized standing committees broader, expressly defined jurisdictions and oversight functions. Contemporary and near-contemporary accounts describe the ordinary Senate standing committees created under the Act as 13-member committees (Appropriations was the exception).

Most importantly, the Congressional Record for January 17, 1947 supplies an actor-level roster for this specific committee. Senator Millard Tydings read its membership on the floor: seven Republicans (George D. Aiken, Homer Ferguson, Bourke B. Hickenlooper, John W. Bricker, Edward J. Thye, Joseph R. McCarthy, Irving M. Ives) and six Democrats (John L. McClellan, James O. Eastland, Clyde R. Hoey, Glen H. Taylor, A. Willis Robertson, Herbert R. O'Conor). Senator Aiken shortly afterward explicitly referred to the standing committee as having 13 members.

Therefore, by January 17, 1947:

`committee size = 13`

`majority contingent = 7 Republicans`

`minority contingent = 6 Democrats`

`1928 statutory threshold = 5`

Consequences:

- `5 < bare committee majority (7)`;
- `5 <= minority contingent (6)`;
- either party contingent contained enough members to satisfy the statutory threshold by itself.

The capacity transition is therefore bounded much more tightly than the previous 1949–1950 observation:

`1928: 5-of-7; cross-party participation required`

→ **`January 1947: 5-of-13; non-majority and minority-party-alone triggering structurally possible`**

The statutory number `five` did not need to change. The surrounding institution did.

## Actor language vs later reconstruction

### Actor-explicit

The 1946 reformers described the Legislative Reorganization Act as a reorganization and consolidation of Congress's committee machinery, with enlarged/defined jurisdictions, professional staff, and stronger oversight. In the January 17, 1947 debate, Tydings argued that the newly constituted Committee on Expenditures should perform investigation work assigned to it by the Reorganization Act rather than have that work placed in a separate special committee. He explicitly described the committee as 13 members and read its 7–6 partisan composition.

### Researcher reconstruction

The propositions `non-majority-capable`, `minority-party-alone-capable`, and `capacity break` are analytical descriptions obtained by combining the unchanged five-member statutory threshold with the contemporaneous 13-member, 7–6 roster. They are not recovered 1946/1947 actor vocabulary.

### Not established

There is currently no evidence that Congress enlarged/reconstituted this committee **in order to alter the effect of the 1928 five-member rule**, or that legislators in 1946–1947 noticed this consequence. Do not write the committee reorganization as an intentional amendment of the minority-information mechanism.

## Evidence

### Primary / near-primary

1. **Congressional Record, Senate, January 17, 1947, pp. 395–396.** Tydings reads the full membership of the Committee on Expenditures in the Executive Departments: seven Republicans and six Democrats. Aiken then refers expressly to the committee's thirteen members. This is the strongest evidence for the actual 80th Congress composition.
   - GovInfo: `GPO-CRECB-1947-pt1`, January 17, 1947, especially pp. 395–396.
   - Evidence strength: **A**.

2. **Legislative Reorganization Act of 1946, Pub. L. 79-601, 60 Stat. 812 (Aug. 2, 1946).** Reorganized the congressional committee system and supplied the jurisdictional framework implemented in the 80th Congress.
   - Evidence strength: **A** for the legal reorganization; do not infer from the statute alone the 1928-rule capacity consequence.

3. **1928 Act, ch. 901, §2, 45 Stat. 996 (May 29, 1928)** (later codified in the lineage of 5 U.S.C. §2954). Supplies the unchanged Senate threshold of any five members.
   - Evidence strength: **A**.

### Institutional / high-quality secondary

4. **U.S. House History, Art & Archives, “The Legislative Reorganization Act of 1946.”** Notes that the Act took effect with the 80th Congress and reduced Senate standing committees from 33 to 15 while modernizing committee machinery.
   - Evidence strength: **B+**.

5. **National Archives, Guide to Senate Records, ch. 11.** Describes the post-1946 jurisdiction of the Senate Committee on Expenditures and its 80th–82nd Congress records; useful for the institutional transition and archival targets.
   - Evidence strength: **B+**.

## Why this matters for Problem History

This closes an important methodological gap in the earlier `capacity drift` packet. The relevant state change was not merely visible by 1949–1950; it can now be associated with a concrete institutional transformation at the beginning of 1947.

A useful object model is:

`stable legal rule`
+
`recipient institution at t1`
→ `capability A`

`same legal rule`
+
`recipient institution at t2`
→ `capability B`

This is a distinct form of solution history: **capability mutation without textual amendment**.

It also warns against treating later legal descriptions of §2954 as if the rule had possessed the same practical structure continuously since 1928. In the Senate, the same five-member text was initially 5-of-7 and cross-party-required; after the 1946 reorganization was implemented, it was 5-of-13 and minority-party-alone-capable.

## Hindsight risks

- Do **not** call the 1928 Senate provision a minority right at enactment: in the recovered 4–3, seven-member committee, five members necessarily crossed party lines.
- Do **not** say the 1946 Act intentionally converted §2 into a minority right unless actor-level evidence is found.
- Do **not** project modern §2954 litigation vocabulary backward as contemporaneous terminology.
- `Minority-party-alone-capable` describes structural possibility, not demonstrated historical use.

## Next candidate

The highest-value next step is no longer simply filling every intervening roster. If §2 is not unique, this becomes a general Problem History mechanism: institutional reorganization can silently rewrite the effective capacity of older legal solutions without amending their text.
