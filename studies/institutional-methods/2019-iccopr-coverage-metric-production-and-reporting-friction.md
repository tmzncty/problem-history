# 2018–2019 ICCOPR: priority coverage as a constructed reporting measure

## Question / non-duplication

The existing 2018 hearing packet records 92% of 25 Standing Research Areas (SRAs) and approximately 61% of 150 priorities as having some research coverage, while explicitly warning that coverage is not completion. The missing link was how ICCOPR actually gathered and classified member-agency projects. A contemporaneous ICCOPR meeting record now supplies the *workflow* and its documented friction, without yet supplying the original project-to-priority crosswalk.

## Primary document

U.S. Coast Guard / ICCOPR, **FY 2019 Second Quarter ICCOPR Meeting Minutes**, meeting **12 March 2019**, memorandum dated **19 April 2019** (digitally signed 24 April 2019), pp. 7–10, particularly §§6(a), 7(b). https://www.dco.uscg.mil/Portals/9/Users/051/63/563/ICCOPR%20FY19%20Q2%20Minutes%20Final.pdf

This is a contemporary institutional meeting record, not an independent audit or the original FY2016–17 biennial report.

## New evidence

1. **Metric repeated, scope clarified (§6(a), p. 7).** The minutes say that in FY2016–17 ICCOPR member agencies conducted research in 92% of 25 SRAs and activities addressed aspects of *approximately* 61% of 150 priorities. They explicitly note that priorities vary from broad to targeted. The minutes add a conditional estimate: if academic research were included, the nexus would be closer to 100%. This is a speculative scope expansion, not a measured revised rate.
2. **Tracking infrastructure absent (§6(a), p. 7).** NOAA's Steve Lehmann stated that there was **no single database** for tracking research activities; some consortia had attempted to link activities to research classes/SRAs. This is actor-level evidence that a seemingly precise coverage statistic was produced in a fragmented information environment.
3. **Report assembly documented (§7(b), pp. 9–10).** Executive Director Kirsten Trego described a core group defining a report framework and data-request form, issuing member-agency data calls, and reviewing drafts; the Executive Director then compiled/analyzed data, assembled project-title/hyperlink appendices, and coordinated approvals.
4. **Classification and review were labor-intensive (§7(b), pp. 9–10).** The meeting recorded inconsistent synopsis lengths, requests for out-of-scope material, longer-than-anticipated agency approvals, and extra work to research or verify project-to-SRA mappings. Members preferred different summary granularity (BSEE longer descriptions, EPA OEM shorter reports with links). The spreadsheet and data-call process were to be revised for the next cycle.
5. **Operationalization distinct from compilation (p. 4).** Lundgren observed in a separate discussion of Gulf research information that earlier efforts often compiled material without getting it operationalized. This concerns the research-to-practice problem, **not** a demonstrated defect in the 61% calculation.

## Problem-History separation

**Historical actors' problem:** In 2019 ICCOPR participants themselves identified missing unified tracking, heterogeneous agency inputs, classification verification, review/approval delays, and the tension between detailed evidence and a concise report. They chose to retain the report framework while improving collection tools and scheduling.

**Archival reconstruction:** The 2018 Senate answers and 2019 ICCOPR minutes independently attest the same approximate 92%/61% *reported* coverage and identify a concrete administrative workflow behind its publication. The meeting record does not independently validate the underlying crosswalk or tell us whether 61% is a count of unique priorities, a weighted measure, or another calculation.

**Present analytical vocabulary only:** `metric-production chain`, `coverage ≠ achievement`, `classification labor`, and `reporting observability gap`. These are not quotations or purposes attributed to Congress or ICCOPR.

## Evidence grading

- **A**: existence/date of meeting; actor-attributed observations about missing database, data calls, synopsis heterogeneity, review delays, and mapping verification.
- **A for attribution / B for measurement validity**: repeated 92%/61% figures; original source-level denominator, deduplication and project-priority mapping remain unverified.
- **Not established**: completion of 61% of priorities; inclusion of all academic/industry research; a 100% measured coverage rate; whether the reporting difficulties caused 2019 legislation or the 2022 plan design.

## Why it changes the episode

The existing chain was `2015 priority plan → 2018 coverage claim → 2023 external critique`. We can now insert an observed mechanism:

`distributed member-agency projects → data calls and uneven synopses → manual project-to-SRA mapping / verification → interagency approval → reported coverage statistic`.

This demonstrates that *knowing whether the research agenda is being addressed* became a practical information-production problem for the coordinating body. The 2019 meeting records active attempts to improve that process, not simply a failure diagnosed by later observers.
