# 1990–2021: ICCOPR review baseline correction and later expansion

## Research question

Which parts of the National Academy / research-plan architecture in the 2021 amendment to Oil Pollution Act §7001 were inherited from 1990, and which were genuinely added later?

## Finding

Clause-level comparison of the original 1990 statute with the 2021 amendment requires a correction to an earlier repository inference. The original Oil Pollution Act of 1990 required two National Academy of Sciences functions: (1) advice and guidance in preparation/development of the research plan, and (2) assessment of the submitted plan's adequacy with a report to Congress. It did **not** contain the later third function—organizational guidance regarding implementation, including allocation/delegation of research topics among federal agencies—and it did **not** contain a recurring ten-year statutory update rule.

The 2021 amendment therefore did not invent external scientific review, but it did expand that review from plan formation + adequacy assessment into implementation/organizational guidance, while also adding a statutory recurring update rule.

## 1. 1990 baseline: two NAS functions, no statutory recurrence

The enacted text of Public Law 101-380, §7001(b), required ICCOPR to submit an implementation plan within 180 days. The plan had to identify agency roles and responsibilities, assess knowledge, identify research gaps, establish priorities/goals, estimate resources/timetables, and identify regional research needs.

The NAS contract clause required the Academy to:

1. provide advice and guidance in preparation and development of the plan; and
2. assess the adequacy of the submitted plan and report its conclusions to Congress.

The original text contains neither a third NAS implementation-guidance function nor a ten-year plan-update cadence.

Primary text:

- Public Law 101-380, Aug. 18, 1990, 104 Stat. 559–560, §7001: https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=2001723X.TXT
- Contemporary/older compilation preserving the same pre-2021 wording: https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/coast/oil-spill/forms/files/opa90.pdf

**Evidence strength:** very high. This is enacted statutory text.

## 2. The original external review mechanism actually operated

The 1990 NAS clause was not merely dormant statutory capability at the outset. The National Academies' 2023 retrospective records that ICCOPR produced the first R&T Plan in 1992 and, as required by OPA 90, engaged NAS/NRC to review it. The resulting 1993 review made substantive recommendations; the 1997 update responded to those recommendations to the extent ICCOPR considered practicable.

Source:

- NASEM, *Review of ICCOPR's 2022-2027 Oil Pollution Research and Technology Plan* (2023), ch. 1: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26780/chapter/3

**Evidence strength:** high as later institutional reconstruction, corroborating operation of the statutory review mechanism. For actor-level claims about the 1993 review itself, recover the 1993 NRC report directly in a later pass.

## 3. 2009 evidence preserves only the original two-function review form

A 2009 Senate legislative text concerning oil-pollution research again specified NAS participation as (1) advice/guidance in preparation and development of the plan and (2) adequacy assessment/report to Congress. It did not include the later implementation/organizational-guidance function in that NAS clause.

Primary source:

- Congressional Record, Mar. 24, 2009, S3685: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2009-03-24/pdf/CREC-2009-03-24.pdf

**Evidence strength:** high for the content of this 2009 proposal. Do not treat an unenacted proposal as the operative law.

## 4. 2021 enacted architecture adds two separable mechanisms

The 2021 amendment to 33 U.S.C. §2761 requires NAS to perform three functions:

1. advice/guidance during plan preparation and development;
2. adequacy assessment after submission, reported to Congress; and
3. organizational guidance regarding implementation, including delegation of topics/research among represented federal agencies.

It separately adds a recurring statutory update rule: a new research plan not later than ten years after Jan. 1, 2021, and every ten years thereafter.

Primary / official sources:

- GovInfo current statutory text and amendment notes: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2022-title33/html/USCODE-2022-title33-chap40.htm
- National Academies OCGA reproduction of P.L. 116-283 / Coast Guard Authorization Act text: https://www.nationalacademies.org/units/NRCEO-NRC%20EO-23-P-592/legislatively-directed-activities/william-m-mac-thornberry-national-defense-authorization-act-for-fiscal-year-2021

**Evidence strength:** very high for enacted architecture.

## Correction to earlier reconstruction

An earlier repository packet correctly recognized continuity in external review but overstated that the implementation/organizational-guidance function was already part of the 1990 baseline. The original 1990 statute shows otherwise.

Correct transformation:

```text
1990 statutory review architecture
  - plan-development advice
  - post-submission adequacy assessment
        ↓ actually exercised in 1992–1993
1997 plan responds to review
        ↓ long plan-update gap
2009 proposal still shows two-function NAS review form
        ↓
2021 enacted architecture
  - retains development advice
  - retains adequacy assessment
  - ADDS implementation / organizational guidance
  - ADDS recurring 10-year statutory update rule
```

This is more precise than either of two misleading stories:

```text
"external review was invented in 2021"        [false]
"all three NAS functions were already in 1990" [false]
```

## Actor formulations vs later reconstruction vs present analysis

### Historical actors / enacted rules

The 1990 Congress specified plan-development advice and adequacy assessment. The 2021 enacted statute specifies an additional implementation/organizational-guidance role and recurring ten-year updates.

### Later reconstruction

NASEM's 2023 report reconstructs the sequence of 1992 plan → 1993 NAS review → 1997 update → long gap → post-Deepwater Horizon six-year ICCOPR practice → 2022 plan. This is valuable evidence about institutional operation but should not be substituted for the wording of the 1990 statute when identifying statutory origin.

### Present analytical shorthand

Researcher-side labels only:

- `review-scope expansion`
- `formation review -> adequacy review -> implementation guidance`
- `update rule != review rule`
- `statutory capability != observed exercise`

Do not attribute these phrases to historical actors.

## Method payoff

This correction strengthens a general source-control rule for Problem History:

```text
later statute contains mechanism X
!=
later statute originated mechanism X
```

But the converse matters too:

```text
later reconstruction describes mechanism X as part of a long-lived statute
!=
mechanism X existed in every historical version of that statute
```

For amended statutes, institutional genealogy requires versioned clause comparison. A current-code citation or a later institutional retrospective can silently collapse later amendments into the apparent historical baseline.

## Hindsight risks

1. **Do not read current 33 U.S.C. §2761 backward into 1990.** Current text incorporates the 2021 amendment.
2. **Do not say NAS implementation guidance existed in OPA 90 as enacted.** The original §7001(b)(2) contains only preparation/development advice and adequacy assessment.
3. **Do not say external scientific review was invented in 2021.** The 1990 two-function review requirement was real and was exercised on the 1992 plan.
4. **Do not call the ten-year rule an original OPA 90 cadence.** It is present in the post-2021 statute, not the 1990 enacted text.
5. **Do not infer motive from clause appearance alone.** This packet identifies what changed, not yet why Congress selected implementation guidance or ten years.
6. **Do not treat 2009 proposal language as enacted continuity.** It is useful evidence of a proposed solution form, not itself the operative legal baseline.
