# 1990–2021 ICCOPR: external review was original architecture; 2019 added implementation guidance and recurrence

## Why this packet exists

The preceding ICCOPR packet located the ten-year update rule and a National Academy review role in Senate-reported S. 2297 (2019). A closer source-control pass changes the interpretation materially: **National Academy participation in plan development and adequacy review was not a new 2019 invention. It was already part of Oil Pollution Act §7001 in 1990.**

The genuine 2019 redesign therefore needs to be stated more narrowly and more precisely. Senate-reported S. 2297 retained/reinstated the older advice-and-adequacy architecture, while adding at least two important layers: National Academy organizational guidance for implementation and a recurring ten-year statutory plan-update rule. It also specified quarterly ICCOPR meetings/public summaries and a detailed updated plan-content schema.

This is a useful correction because otherwise a later amendment can be mistaken for the origin of an institutional mechanism that it actually inherited.

## 1. 1990 baseline: NAS advice and adequacy assessment were already statutory

The pre-2021 text of 33 U.S.C. §2761, derived from Oil Pollution Act of 1990 §7001, required the ICCOPR chair to contract with the National Academy of Sciences to:

1. provide advice and guidance in preparation and development of the research plan; and
2. assess the adequacy of the submitted plan and report the assessment to Congress.

The same original architecture separately required NIST advice and guidance on quality assurance and standards measurements.

Sources:

- Historical 33 U.S.C. §2761 text preserving the pre-2021 architecture: https://chanrobles.com/usa/uslaws/codes/title33/33usc2761.php
- National Academies, *Review of ICCOPR's 2022–2027 Oil Pollution Research and Technology Plan* (2023), retrospective statement that OPA 90 directs National Academies review/advice: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26780/chapter/2

**Evidence strength:** very high for statutory architecture; the National Academies source is later reconstruction but independently confirms the institutional genealogy.

Therefore this sequence is wrong:

`internal planning -> 2019 invention of external scientific review`

The safer baseline is:

`1990 plan requirement + external NAS development advice + external adequacy assessment + NIST standards advice`

## 2. 2009–2010 evidence shows external review remained part of reform proposals before 2019

This continuity is visible well before S. 2297. In March 2009 Senate legislative text concerning oil-pollution R&D again specified National Academy participation in preparation/development of the research plan and assessment of its adequacy. After Deepwater Horizon, House Science and Technology work on H.R. 2693/H.R. 5716 likewise treated effective coordinated oil-spill research as an active institutional problem.

Sources:

- Congressional Record, Mar. 24, 2009, S3685: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2009-03-24/pdf/CREC-2009-03-24-pt1-PgS3659.pdf
- House Science and Technology, 111th Congress activity summary on oil-pollution R&D and Deepwater Horizon: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-111hrpt698/html/CRPT-111hrpt698.htm

**Evidence strength:** high for continuity of the external-review solution form in legislative proposals; these proposals do not by themselves establish enactment.

## 3. 2019 Senate-reported S. 2297: what was actually new or expanded

Section 429 of Senate-reported S. 2297 rewrote §7001. It required a new research plan with a detailed content schema: inventory current research, assess the state of knowledge, identify research gaps and deficiencies exposed by past spills, establish national priorities/goals, assess technology applicability, estimate resources/timetables, and summarize research under difficult environmental conditions.

For National Academy participation, the bill contained three functions:

1. advice/guidance in plan preparation and development;
2. adequacy assessment and report to Congress; and
3. **organizational guidance regarding implementation, including allocation of topics/research among participating Federal agencies.**

The first two are inherited from the 1990 architecture. The third is the important expansion visible in the 2019 text.

S. 2297 also added a recurring rule: **ten years after enactment and every ten years thereafter**, ICCOPR would submit an updated research plan. The reported bill additionally required quarterly committee meetings and public summaries.

Primary source:

- S. 2297, 116th Congress, Senate-reported Sept. 26, 2019, §429, especially pp. 208–219: https://www.congress.gov/116/bills/s2297/BILLS-116s2297rs.pdf

**Evidence strength:** very high.

The corrected transformation is therefore:

```text
1990
plan-content requirement
+ NAS development advice
+ NAS adequacy assessment
+ NIST standards advice
        ↓
long period of plan staleness / later coordination critique
        ↓
2015 ICCOPR organizational six-year recurrence
        ↓
2019 Senate-reported redesign
retains older NAS advice + adequacy review
BUT adds/expands:
- NAS implementation/organizational guidance
- detailed refreshed content schema
- quarterly meetings/public summaries
- recurring ten-year statutory update rule
        ↓
2021 enactment
```

## 4. Problem-History payoff: distinguish mechanism origin from mechanism expansion

This source correction yields a general method warning:

`amendment contains mechanism X != amendment originated mechanism X`

For a later redesign, compare at least:

- inherited mechanism;
- retained mechanism;
- expanded mechanism;
- genuinely new mechanism;
- changed cadence;
- changed actor/authority;
- changed object/content schema.

In this case, `external review` is too coarse a label. The historically useful decomposition is:

```text
review during development        [already 1990]
post-submission adequacy review  [already 1990]
implementation guidance          [added/expanded in 2019 text]
recurring statutory update rule  [added in 2019 text]
```

Researcher-side labels only:

- `review-function decomposition`
- `mechanism inheritance vs expansion`
- `review architecture layering`

## 5. Historical actors / later reconstruction / present analysis

### Historical actors / statutes

High confidence:

- OPA 90 already made National Academy advice during plan preparation and adequacy assessment part of the statutory planning architecture.
- S. 2297 in 2019 retained those functions and added National Academy organizational guidance for implementation.
- S. 2297 also added a ten-year recurring update rule and quarterly meeting/public-summary requirements.

### Later reconstruction

The National Academies' 2023 review accurately states that OPA 90 directs National Academies involvement. It is useful confirmation but should not replace the underlying statutory genealogy.

### Present analysis

The phrase `external review layer` remains usable only if decomposed. Treating all NAS participation as a 2019 novelty would erase thirty years of institutional continuity.

## 6. Hindsight risks

1. **Do not say 2019 invented National Academy review of ICCOPR plans.** Advice during development and adequacy assessment date to OPA 90.
2. **Do not say nothing changed in 2019.** Organizational/implementation guidance and recurring ten-year updates are substantive additions to the architecture visible in S. 2297.
3. **Do not collapse all National Academy functions into one binary review variable.** Development advice, adequacy assessment, and implementation guidance are different functions.
4. **Do not infer that the ten-year rule replaced ICCOPR's six-year organizational practice.** The two cadences can coexist; actor rationale for choosing ten years remains unrecovered.
5. **Do not infer that statutory review was continuously executed merely because it existed in law.** Actual National Academy reviews/engagement must be checked artifact by artifact.

## 8. Remaining uncertainty / next candidates

The highest-value source debt changes again:

1. clause-diff **1990 §7001 vs 2009/2010 proposals vs S. 2297 introduced vs S. 2297 reported vs P.L. 116-283 §8304**;
2. determine exactly when the third NAS function—organizational guidance for implementation—first appears;
3. determine exactly when the ten-year update rule first appears;
4. recover committee/markup or technical-assistance evidence explaining those additions;
5. reconstruct whether the original 1990 NAS advice/adequacy mandate was actually executed for the 1992 and 1997 plans, and whether non-use/staleness of that review architecture contributed to later redesign.

The last question is especially important: a statutory review mechanism may exist yet become dormant. If so, the 2019–2021 episode may be partly a history of **reactivating and expanding an old solution**, not simply inventing a new one.

## Compact finding

> A closer statutory baseline changes the interpretation of the 2019–2021 ICCOPR redesign. National Academy advice during research-plan development and post-submission adequacy assessment were already required by Oil Pollution Act §7001 in 1990; they were not invented in S. 2297. The Senate-reported 2019 bill retained those inherited review functions while adding organizational guidance for implementation, a detailed refreshed plan-content schema, quarterly meeting/public-summary requirements, and a recurring ten-year statutory update rule. The correct Problem History transformation is therefore not `internal planning -> external review`, but `old external-review architecture -> retained and expanded review/implementation architecture + recurring statutory renewal`. This adds a source-control rule: before calling a feature of a later reform new, diff it against the original institutional baseline.
