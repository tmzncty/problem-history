# M0 Calibration — Researcher Oracle

> **Do not show this file to test Agents before they answer.**
>
> This oracle records expected distinctions and likely failure modes. It is a calibration aid, not a final historical synthesis. Pairwise relation labels below are deliberately provisional where the five-source packet is too thin to support a strong identity verdict.

## Executive finding

The five cases do **not** by themselves demonstrate one continuous 73-year problem called “Can machines think?” They demonstrate a historically related **problem field** in which targets, stakes, standards of success, institutional locations, and acceptable answer forms change substantially.

The correct calibration outcome is therefore not a pretty linear lineage. A good Agent should recover at least the following transformations while preserving uncertainty:

```text
1950 Turing
ambiguous ordinary-language question
→ explicit operational replacement through imitation-game performance

1955 Dartmouth
research-program conjecture
→ decomposable engineering agenda under “artificial intelligence”

1972 Lighthill
field-internal technical ambitions
→ external evaluation of coherence, practical success, and research support

1980 Searle
program performance / AI claims
→ philosophical sufficiency question about intentionality and understanding

2023 GPT-4
large-model development
→ capability evaluation + reliability + alignment + safety + predictable scaling + deployment
```

The arrows above mean **candidate historical transformations to investigate**, not settled `continuous` edges.

# MI-01 oracle — Turing, 1950

## Actor-explicit problem

High confidence:

- Turing explicitly begins with “Can machines think?”
- He also explicitly says that questions about the imitation game replace the original question.
- Later he calls the original wording too meaningless/imprecise to deserve direct discussion while continuing to engage objections surrounding machine intelligence.

This is a particularly valuable calibration case because the source itself distinguishes **problem topic** from **operative formulation**. An Agent that returns only “Turing asks whether machines can think” has missed a central actor-side methodological move.

## Reconstructed actor problem

Medium-to-high confidence, if carefully phrased:

- How can an ambiguous dispute about machine thinking be converted into a more discriminating, operationally discussable performance question?

This reconstruction is licensed because Turing explicitly narrates the replacement. It should not be inflated into present formulations such as machine consciousness, AGI alignment, or LLM sentience.

## Presuppositions

Explicit / strongly supported:

- ordinary definitions of “machine” and “think” are inadequate for the proposed investigation;
- a structured comparison through linguistic interaction can make at least part of the controversy more precise;
- digital computers and learning machines are appropriate objects for the discussion.

Do not infer:

- that Turing equates imitation-game success with consciousness;
- that he supplied a timeless definition of intelligence;
- that every later benchmark is a direct descendant of his exact problem.

## Evidence strength

**High.** Published original article with stable DOI and archival offprints. The key transformation is actor-explicit, not a later historian's inference.

## Hindsight risks

- calling every later behavioral benchmark a “Turing Test” in the same problem sense;
- silently replacing “thinking” with consciousness or sentience;
- treating a later textbook slogan as more authoritative than Turing's own explicit question replacement.

# MI-02 oracle — Dartmouth proposal, 1955

## Actor-explicit problem / programme

High confidence:

- the actors propose a summer study explicitly named “artificial intelligence”;
- they state a conjecture about describing aspects of learning/intelligence precisely enough for machine simulation;
- they enumerate research tasks involving language, abstraction, problem solving, self-improvement, neural nets, complexity, and creativity/randomness.

The operative form is therefore not merely a yes/no question about whether machines think. It is a **programme-building and engineering decomposition**: what aspects of intelligence can be specified, simulated, and experimentally advanced, and how?

## Reconstructed actor layer

Medium confidence:

- The proposal is attempting to make a heterogeneous cluster of machine-intelligence research tractable as a shared programme and social/intellectual project.

That wording is a researcher reconstruction. It must remain distinct from the proposal's explicit conjecture and task list.

## Presuppositions

Strongly supported:

- at least some features called learning/intelligence are susceptible to precise description;
- machine simulation is an appropriate route for investigating them;
- the programme can be decomposed into technical subproblems.

Do not upgrade the proposal's conjecture into a demonstrated fact.

## Evidence strength

**High for actor wording; medium-to-high for programme interpretation.** The accessible text is a 2006 open-access reproduction of a 31 August 1955 proposal, with the mediation explicitly documented and archival copies identified. The reproduction is reliable for calibration but a publication-level final project should still record archival or facsimile provenance for quotation-critical claims.

## Hindsight risks

- “Dartmouth = absolute birth of every AI problem.” Roberto Cordeschi's historical review explicitly examines important pre-Dartmouth topics and controversies; institutional naming/consolidation is not proof that the underlying problem field began ex nihilo.
- treating the later success of AI as proof that the 1955 conjecture was already established;
- projecting today's definition of AI backward into every 1955 participant's usage.

# MI-01 → MI-02 provisional relation

## Continuity evidence

- overlapping concern with machine capacities associated with intelligence;
- close chronology and a broader research milieu of digital computation, learning machines, automata, and machine intelligence;
- both permit machine performance to count as empirical evidence rather than treating the topic as purely metaphysical.

## Discontinuity evidence

- Turing explicitly concentrates on replacing an ambiguous question with an imitation-game criterion; Dartmouth proposes a research programme and decomposes engineering tasks;
- institutional stakes differ: journal argument vs collaborative programme formation;
- answer form differs: Turing's operative question admits performance predictions/objections; Dartmouth's proposal invites research progress across subprojects.

## Oracle verdict

`transformed_successor` is a plausible hypothesis, **not a calibration requirement**. `undetermined` is acceptable if an Agent explains that this five-source packet lacks the direct uptake/citation evidence needed to establish a stronger historical lineage.

**Automatic `continuous` is a failure.** Shared vocabulary/topic alone is insufficient.

# MI-03 oracle — Lighthill, 1972/1973

## Actor-explicit problem

High confidence from the introduction:

- the Science Research Council has to decide how to treat increasing AI research-support requests;
- Lighthill has been commissioned to provide a general view useful for policy formation and decision taking.

This means the report itself occupies an **institutional evaluation episode**: Is the heterogeneous field labelled AI coherent/successful/promising enough, in the relevant senses, to justify forms of research support?

Inside that review Lighthill also diagnoses technical problems such as combinatorial explosion and the difficulty of general-purpose approaches.

## Important separation

Do not collapse:

```text
technical criticism of broad AI methods
=
claim that no machine could ever be intelligent
=
policy conclusion about research support
```

They are different claims.

Agar's 2020 historical reinterpretation is valuable here because it emphasizes Lighthill's broader view that strong research should be connected with practical problem solving. That is a **later scholarly reconstruction**, not actor wording to be inserted silently into the 1972 report.

## Evidence strength

**High for report framing and technical criticisms.** The University of Edinburgh AIAI-hosted scan provides the report text; bibliographic control identifies 1972 submission/report and 1973 symposium publication. **High-quality later reconstruction:** Jon Agar, *BJHS* 2020, DOI `10.1017/S0007087420000230`.

## Hindsight risks

- saying Lighthill asked “Can machines think?”;
- saying he proved “AI is impossible”;
- using “AI winter” as if it were the report's own actor category;
- explaining the report only through later funding consequences and ignoring the question it was commissioned to answer.

# MI-02 → MI-03 provisional relation

## Continuity evidence

- MI-03 explicitly evaluates a field now institutionally recognized under the “AI” label;
- many of the ambitions under review descend from the sort of general programme articulated at Dartmouth;
- technical generality, problem solving, learning, and machine performance remain part of the object field.

## Discontinuity evidence

- the primary actor role shifts from proposers trying to create a research programme to an external assessor judging a mature-enough field for resource allocation;
- stakes shift toward research policy, coherence, practical success, and support;
- negative evaluation of a programme is not simply another answer to the programme's original technical subquestions.

## Oracle verdict

`transformed_successor` or `displaced` may be defensible depending on the exact episode boundary. The packet does not force one. The Agent should at minimum notice the **problem responsibility and institutional stake shift**.

# MI-04 oracle — Searle, 1980

## Actor-explicit problem

High confidence:

- Searle explicitly targets the proposition that program instantiation by itself is sufficient for intentionality;
- his abstract makes brain causal features and program sufficiency central;
- the Chinese Room argument is deployed to separate formal program execution from the intentionality/understanding he regards as relevant.

The safe actor-level reconstruction is therefore about **sufficiency for intentionality / understanding**, not generic machine capability.

## What not to project

- present LLM consciousness or sentience disputes;
- a claim that no artificial physical system could ever have intentionality;
- a claim that no computer program can produce useful or human-like outputs.

The paper's argument is narrower and more structured than those slogans.

## Evidence strength

**High.** Published journal article with Cambridge metadata and DOI `10.1017/S0140525X00005756`. The relevant proposition appears in the article abstract.

## Hindsight risks

- retrofitting GPT/LLM terminology;
- treating “Chinese Room” as a complete substitute for Searle's explicit propositions;
- turning an argument about sufficient conditions for intentionality into a benchmark-performance dispute.

# MI-03 → MI-04 provisional relation

## Continuity evidence

- both intervene in disputes grouped under the historical label AI;
- both question broad claims made around computer intelligence;
- both may draw on examples of machine language/problem-solving claims.

## Discontinuity evidence

- institutional locus changes sharply: commissioned research-policy evaluation vs philosophy-of-mind argument;
- primary target changes from coherence/performance/practical promise of a research field to the sufficiency of program instantiation for intentionality;
- criteria of success differ: empirical/research-program usefulness vs philosophical explanation/constitution of understanding.

## Oracle verdict

`undetermined` is the preferred conservative verdict from this packet alone. A stronger `revived`, `transformed_successor`, or `analogy_only` claim would require richer direct-interlocutor and citation evidence.

An Agent that declares simple continuity because both criticize “AI” fails the calibration.

# MI-05 oracle — GPT-4 release, 2023

## Actor-explicit problems

High confidence for OpenAI's own framing:

- capability measurement on professional/academic benchmarks;
- model limitations and reliability;
- factuality and steerability;
- adversarial evaluation, safety, alignment, monitoring, and mitigation;
- predictable scaling and preparation for future capabilities;
- staged deployment through product/API channels.

The source does **not** need to ask whether GPT-4 “really thinks” in order to constitute a Problem Episode. Its central problem structure is much more operational and institutional: what the model can do, how reliably, how its future capabilities can be anticipated, what risks accompany those capabilities, and how deployment should be managed.

## Researcher-analytic temptation

A present researcher may legitimately ask:

> How did the historical machine-intelligence problem become a capability-evaluation and safety-governance problem?

But that is a **researcher_analytic** formulation. It cannot be inserted into the 2023 source as OpenAI's own historical question unless direct evidence supports it.

Likewise “Can GPT-4 think?” is not licensed as actor wording by this packet.

## Evidence strength

**High for OpenAI's actor framing; limited for field-wide generalization.** The official release is dated 14 March 2023 and links the technical report. It is one actor's self-description and must be counterbalanced by independent researchers, competitors, regulators, users, and critics before defining a 2023-wide episode.

## Hindsight risks

- benchmark performance = understanding;
- benchmark performance = consciousness;
- safety/alignment = philosophy of mind;
- treating OpenAI as the sole actor representing “AI in 2023”;
- turning earlier AI history into a teleological prehistory of large language models.

# Later reconstruction vs actor statement

The calibration should force explicit tagging of these common later narratives:

## “Dartmouth was the birth of AI”

This is a useful historiographic shorthand for naming/institutional consolidation, not evidence that earlier work had no relevant antecedents. Cordeschi 2007 explicitly surveys pre-Dartmouth topics and controversies. Store it as later historical reconstruction unless a specific actor uses equivalent language.

Source: Roberto Cordeschi, “AI Turns Fifty: Revisiting Its Origins,” *Applied Artificial Intelligence* 21, nos. 4–5 (2007): 259–279, DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/08839510701252304.

## “The Lighthill report caused the AI winter”

This is a later causal/history-of-the-field claim. Agar 2020 notes that the report has often been held to be a major cause of the later slowdown, while reinterpreting the report through Lighthill's views on research and practical applications. Neither claim should be substituted for the report's own 1972 commission/problem framing.

Source: Jon Agar, “What is science for? The Lighthill report on artificial intelligence reinterpreted,” *British Journal for the History of Science* 53, no. 3 (2020): 289–310, DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007087420000230.

## “GPT-4 answers Turing/Searle”

This is a present analytic proposition, not something established by the GPT-4 release. To support a historical relation, collect explicit 2022–2024 actor texts that invoke Turing, Searle, understanding, intentionality, or consciousness and then test whether those actors are actually reopening the older problem or merely borrowing its vocabulary.

# Golden failure expectations

Treat the following as calibration failures unless the Agent supplies unusually strong independent evidence:

1. **Perennial-question compression:** “All five ask whether machines can think.”
2. **Topic = problem:** any appearance of machine/intelligence/AI is treated as identity evidence sufficient by itself.
3. **Turing flattening:** the Agent quotes the opening question but misses the actor-explicit replacement.
4. **Dartmouth teleology:** the 1955 conjecture is written as a fact proven by later AI successes.
5. **Birth myth hardening:** “AI began at Dartmouth” is treated as an uncontested ontological beginning rather than a historiographic/institutional claim.
6. **Lighthill sloganization:** “AI is impossible” replaces the actual commission, heterogeneous category analysis, technical criticisms, and research-support stakes.
7. **Retrospective vocabulary:** “AI winter” is attributed to Lighthill's 1972 actor formulation without primary evidence.
8. **Searle overreach:** a sufficient-condition argument about intentionality becomes “machines can never be intelligent in any sense.”
9. **Benchmark ontology:** GPT-4 benchmark performance is promoted into actor claims about consciousness, thought, or intentionality.
10. **Single-actor field collapse:** OpenAI's 2023 release is treated as the entire AI field's question structure.
11. **Causation = identity:** influence between texts is used as sufficient proof that the problem itself is continuous.
12. **No counterevidence:** the Agent supplies only a continuity story and never tests discontinuity.

# What evidence should be collected next

This five-source packet is deliberately narrow. Before promoting it into an M2 pilot, collect evidence that can test or break the candidate edges:

### 1950 → 1955

- direct citations or recollections by Dartmouth participants concerning Turing's 1950 paper;
- 1950–1956 discussions of “machine intelligence,” cybernetics, automata, learning machines, and heuristic programming;
- evidence showing whether the actors themselves recognized a shared problem or only adjacent research programmes.

### 1955 → 1972

- UK AI grant applications and Science Research Council records;
- actor replies to Lighthill;
- field definitions in British laboratories before the review;
- evidence on what counted as successful AI before and after the report.

### 1972 → 1980

- Searle's named AI interlocutors and the specific claims he understood as “strong AI”;
- direct uptake of machine-understanding claims in late-1970s NLP/AI;
- evidence distinguishing philosophical intentionality from research-policy disputes.

### 1980 → 2023

- explicit Searle/Turing/understanding references in LLM-era research and product discourse;
- benchmark papers vs consciousness/understanding papers as separate source populations;
- institutional evidence for the rise of alignment, red-teaming, scaling prediction, and deployment governance as distinct problem responsibilities.

If these materials reveal multiple branching episodes rather than one chain, the correct model is `split`, `merged`, `analogy_only`, or several unrelated/overlapping lineages — not a forced linear evolution.
