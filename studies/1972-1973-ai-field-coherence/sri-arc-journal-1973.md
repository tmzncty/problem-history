# SRI ARC Journal: Contemporary Reception and Circulation Evidence, 1973

> **Status:** focused source note.
>
> **Purpose:** preserve a contemporaneous U.S. AI-community report of the Lighthill television controversy and its IJCAI circulation without promoting that reception evidence into consensus or causal impact.

## Source identity

**SRI ARC Journal**, archive object titled `SRI ARC Journal : 21207-21212; 1973-12-26-1973-12-29`, preserved by the Computer History Museum.

Direct archive PDF:

- https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2016/09/102726041-05-01-acc.pdf

The relevant section is headed `Report on the BBC TV Lighthill Debate`.

**Source type:** contemporaneous technical-community periodical / reception source.

**Evidence strength:** medium-high to high for what this SRI community source reported in late 1973; not an official BBC production log and not a substitute for the underlying television recording or conference records.

---

## New actor-world evidence

The journal reports that a colour videotape of the BBC debate, converted for American television standards, was obtained from the BBC and shown **five times at IJCAI**, with Donald Michie introducing the screenings.

It also reports the following chronology:

- a roughly two-and-a-half-hour debate filmed at the Royal Institution in London on **4 July 1973**;
- an edited roughly eighty-minute version aired on **BBC2 on 30 August 1973**;
- participants included James Lighthill, Donald Michie, John McCarthy, and Richard Gregory, with George Porter moderating.

These are paraphrases of the contemporary report, not independently verified BBC metadata.

## What this establishes

At minimum, the source supports that by late 1973:

1. the controversy had become a portable media object;
2. the programme was deliberately transferred into the U.S. professional AI community;
3. IJCAI audiences encountered it repeatedly rather than through one isolated showing;
4. Michie participated in presenting the screenings;
5. at least one contemporary U.S. AI-community source treated the debate as professionally salient enough to report in detail.

This is stronger evidence for **circulation and uptake** than later statements that the programme simply became famous.

## What this does not establish

The source does not prove:

- that IJCAI attendees agreed with Michie or McCarthy;
- that attendees rejected Lighthill;
- that the tape changed U.S. AI research priorities;
- that the broadcast caused British funding reductions;
- that all viewers interpreted the debate as the same problem;
- that the SRI reporter's chronology is necessarily superior to official BBC or Royal Institution records not yet inspected.

The safe inference is therefore:

```text
repeated professional circulation
    -> historical uptake / attention evidence
    != consensus
    != problem identity
    != causal impact
```

# Chronology conflict

This source is especially valuable because its dates conflict with later/current metadata already found elsewhere.

Current evidence includes:

| Source | Claim | Evidential role |
|---|---|---|
| SRI ARC Journal, late 1973 | filmed/debated 4 Jul 1973; BBC2 broadcast 30 Aug 1973 | contemporaneous reception report |
| University of Edinburgh AIAI archive | labels archive page `BBC TV - June 1973` | later institutional preservation metadata |
| later historical accounts | some give other event dates | secondary chronology leads |

Do not collapse these into one canonical date.

Future source work should distinguish:

- live-event date;
- filming date;
- broadcast date;
- archive-catalogue date/label;
- U.S. tape-transfer date;
- IJCAI screening dates.

The **30 August 1973 broadcast date** is currently supported both by this contemporary SRI report and by later scholarship, but it still deserves direct BBC confirmation before being frozen as canonical metadata.

# Problem History value

## 1. Audience change can alter problem stakes

The British SRC controversy begins in a public-funding and field-evaluation context. By the time the tape is shown at IJCAI, the audience is an international professional AI community.

Even if the same recording is shown, the historical function is different:

```text
British policy controversy
        -> televised public controversy
        -> international disciplinary reception
```

A stable recording therefore does not guarantee a stable Problem Episode.

## 2. Circulation is its own evidential category

This source recommends preserving a distinction between:

- existence;
- publication/broadcast;
- circulation;
- uptake/attention;
- endorsement;
- institutional consequence.

The Lighthill tape currently reaches the fourth category. Strong evidence for the fifth or sixth still needs direct records.

## 3. Contemporary reception is not actor formulation

The SRI reporter's characterization tells us how one contemporary professional community source framed the controversy. It does not replace the formulations of Lighthill, Michie, McCarthy, Gregory, SRC officials, or individual viewers.

This makes the source useful for **historical recognition / reception evidence** while keeping formulation provenance intact.

# Hindsight risks

## H1 — `five screenings = consensus`

Repeated showings prove availability and interest, not agreement.

## H2 — `international attention = causal importance`

Attention is a necessary lead for impact research, not a causal verdict.

## H3 — reception report = official chronology

The SRI journal is contemporary and detailed, but it is still not the BBC production record.

## H4 — one audience reaction = U.S. AI opinion

Any reaction described by the reporter must remain local to that reporter/audience unless independently corroborated.

## H5 — later `AI winter` narrative

Do not use the tape's circulation as retrospective proof that participants already understood themselves to be entering an `AI winter`.

# Provisional verdict

The SRI ARC Journal supplies **reliable new contemporaneous evidence that the Lighthill controversy underwent rapid professional circulation beyond Britain in 1973**.

That is a genuine historical extension of the episode's evidence chain, but it should be coded conservatively:

```text
strong evidence: circulation / professional attention
medium evidence: reported production chronology
not established: consensus / policy causation / continuous problem identity
```

Its most useful methodological contribution is to prevent a common collapse:

```text
widely circulated
!= widely accepted
!= historically causal
```
