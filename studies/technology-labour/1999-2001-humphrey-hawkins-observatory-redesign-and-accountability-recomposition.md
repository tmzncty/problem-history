# 1999–2001: Humphrey–Hawkins observatory redesign and accountability recomposition

Status: **M2 implementation / disappearance test / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet extends:

- `1994-2000-humphrey-hawkins-monetary-ranges-to-reporting-afterlife.md`;
- `2000-humphrey-hawkins-selective-reenactment-and-accountability-split.md`.

The preceding work established a narrow disappearance event:

```text
1978 §108 / Federal Reserve Act §2A
semiannual reporting
+ money / credit growth ranges
+ relation to wider employment / price / production goals
+ explanations of range revisions / deviations

        ↓ functional hollowing

1994
ranges formally survive
but FOMC recasts them as long-run benchmarks

        ↓ legal expiry

May–Jun 2000
old periodic-reporting requirement expires
FOMC stops establishing ranges

        ↓ selective reconstruction

Dec 2000
Congress restores semiannual monetary-policy reporting / hearings
but does not restore mandatory monetary-growth ranges
```

Fresh evidence now answers a second question:

> Did the 2000 redesign simply weaken Federal Reserve accountability when it removed the monetary ranges, or did it recompose accountability across different mechanisms?

The answer is materially more complex than either `weakened` or `strengthened`.

By February 1999 the FOMC itself had explicitly defended the semiannual reports and hearings as useful for communication and public / congressional accountability. By 30 June 2000 Senator Phil Gramm publicly described a **cross-institutional consensus among Senate Banking, House Banking, and the Federal Reserve Board** to preserve reporting while, for the first time, writing a formal Fed-chair testimony procedure into the Federal Reserve Act. On 25 July House actors described the hearing as a central oversight and democratic-dialogue institution even though the old statutory requirement had lapsed. In February 2001, after enactment, Gramm and Sarbanes described the new settlement as a deliberate bipartisan redesign: the report’s focus changed, permanent semiannual oversight survived, the Chair’s personal appearance became formally specified, and the routine hearing burden fell from four appearances to two unless the other chamber requested a follow-up.

Combined with the already recovered deletion of mandatory monetary ranges, the strongest reconstruction is therefore:

```text
quantitative intermediate-target accountability
        ↓ removed

broad narrative / economic observatory
        ↓ retained

personal Chair appearance requirement
        ↓ more explicitly formalized

routine hearing frequency
        ↓ reduced / rationalized
```

Researcher-side shorthand in this packet uses **`accountability recomposition`** and **`oversight-form substitution`**. These are analytical descriptions, not historical actor vocabulary and not schema proposals.

## 1. Source control

### 1.1 FOMC minutes, 2–3 Feb 1999

Primary / contemporaneous institutional record:

- *Federal Reserve Bulletin*, June 1999, p. 426;
- reproduced in the Board of Governors, *Annual Report, 1999*;
- FRASER full text:
  https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/annual-report-board-governors-federal-reserve-system-117/1999-2452/fulltext

Relevant passage is under **`Sunset Legislation Relating to Humphrey-Hawkins Reports`**.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct collective FOMC minute**.

The Committee discussed the Federal Reports Elimination and Sunset Act of 1995 and agreed that the semiannual reports and associated congressional hearings had been useful and should continue. The recorded reasons are especially important for this packet:

- they gave the FOMC an effective means to explain policy;
- they allowed it to communicate its views on varied issues;
- they enhanced accountability to the public and Congress.

This is a pre-sunset, actor-explicit statement of what part of the inherited Humphrey–Hawkins architecture the FOMC valued.

Boundary:

This minute concerns the reports / hearings as an institution. It does **not** say every reporting subcomponent—especially the monetary-growth ranges—remained useful.

That distinction becomes central in 2000.

### 1.2 FOMC transcript, 27–28 Jun 2000

Federal Reserve official transcript:

https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/FOMC20000628meeting.pdf

Relevant pages: approximately transcript pp. 81–90.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct internal deliberation**.

Already established in the companion packet:

- Greenspan says the FOMC has not used the statutory monetary ranges to guide policy for years;
- money / credit aggregates can remain informationally useful even if mandatory ranges are not;
- he expects likely renewal legislation to preserve semiannual reporting but omit annual ranges;
- Broaddus agrees operational importance has declined but argues the ranges still supplied a quantitative institutional-accountability / long-run-anchor function.

Use in this packet:

The June transcript gives the Fed-side **functional decomposition** immediately before Congress publicly announces a negotiated oversight architecture.

Boundary:

Greenspan’s anticipation of the later design does not establish that he or the Federal Reserve authored the legislative language.

### 1.3 Senate Banking Committee press release, 30 Jun 2000

Primary committee communication:

https://www.banking.senate.gov/themes/banking/press_archive/prel00/0629mpol.htm

Title: **`GRAMM SETS DATE FOR MONETARY POLICY REPORT`**.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct contemporaneous congressional actor statement**.

Gramm says scheduling Greenspan’s 20 July hearing became possible because a consensus had been reached among:

- members of the Senate Banking Committee;
- members of the House banking committee;
- the Federal Reserve Board of Governors.

He characterizes the agreed structure as, for the first time, placing in the Federal Reserve Act a formal procedure for the Federal Reserve Chair to testify before Congress on the conduct of monetary policy.

He contrasts this with the lapsed Humphrey–Hawkins provisions, which he describes as requiring the Federal Reserve to `consult` with the committees.

His stated reason is oversight: the requirement would ensure Congress receives the information it needs to oversee the Federal Reserve.

This is a major genealogy advance because the redesign is no longer visible only at final enactment in December. A core institutional agreement existed by **30 June 2000**, two days after the FOMC transcript discussion.

Boundary:

`cross-institutional consensus on a core testimony/reporting architecture`

is not yet the same as:

`proof that all three institutions agreed on every clause, including deletion of monetary ranges`.

The precise proposal / draft text behind the June 30 announcement remains unrecovered.

### 1.4 Senate Banking Committee press release, 5 Jul 2000

Primary committee communication:

https://www.banking.senate.gov/themes/banking/press_archive/prel00/0705mrk.htm

Evidence strength: **high / direct contemporaneous legislative-process evidence**.

The Committee announced a 13 July markup of legislation providing semiannual reports to Congress by the Federal Reserve Chair, described as replacing the lapsed Humphrey–Hawkins provisions.

Use:

This establishes that by 5 July a concrete legislative object existed at least at committee-working level, rather than only an abstract consensus.

Boundary:

The release does not provide the complete text or a stable bill number in the retrieved source. A subsequent 13 July action release does not clearly identify what happened to this Fed-report item.

Therefore do **not** infer:

- that this specific text was approved on 13 July;
- that it was identical to H.R. 5640 / final §2B;
- that it already contained every final deletion or hearing-frequency rule.

The July 13 committee business record / markup text is a high-value source debt.

### 1.5 House Banking and Financial Services hearing, 25 Jul 2000

Hearing:

- *Conduct of Monetary Policy: Report of the Federal Reserve Board pursuant to the Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1978, P.L. 95-523 and the State of the Economy*;
- House Committee on Banking and Financial Services;
- 106th Congress, 2d Session;
- 25 Jul 2000;
- Serial No. 106-68;
- GPO publication.

Searchable reproduction currently used:

https://www.whenthefedspeaks.com/doc/testimony_20000725_chair_conduct_of_monetary_policy_report_of

Evidence strength: **high / direct contemporaneous hearing reproduction, but official GPO page-image binding remains source debt**.

Key actor formulations:

#### Chairman James Leach

Leach states that Greenspan’s appearance is no longer legally required, yet says there may be no more important congressional oversight responsibility for the Banking Committee than the semiannual review of Federal Reserve monetary policy.

He also says he has been working closely with Senator Gramm on legislation to renew the reporting requirement and expects the process to be completed before adjournment.

This provides a House-side transmission statement independent of Gramm’s Senate explanation.

#### Ranking member John LaFalce

LaFalce thanks Greenspan for continuing the report during the temporary statutory lapse and argues that an independent central bank requires appropriate public oversight.

He interprets the Humphrey–Hawkins process as creating dialogue between the Federal Reserve and the public through elected representatives.

He urges Senate action on a House-passed measure that would renew the Humphrey–Hawkins report together with other reporting requirements.

#### Representative Carolyn Maloney

Maloney emphasizes that the Chair’s appearances give Congress and the outside world a limited but important opportunity to understand Federal Reserve thinking and future plans, and supports reauthorization.

Use:

These House actors strengthen the case that the surviving object was not merely an inert reporting form. They understood the hearing / report as an oversight, communication, and public-dialogue institution.

Boundary:

These statements do **not** show Leach, LaFalce, or Maloney endorsing the specific deletion of monetary-growth ranges. Keep their oversight rationale separate from Gramm’s later December indicator-obsolescence rationale.

### 1.6 Senator Gramm floor statement, 7 Dec 2000

Already established in the companion packet.

Congressional Record, Senate, 7 Dec 2000, p. S11681:

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2000-12-07/pdf/CREC-2000-12-07.pdf

Evidence strength: **very high / direct contemporaneous principal legislative-manager statement**.

Gramm describes the final package as:

- renewing Federal Reserve monetary-policy reporting / appearances;
- eliminating older required indicators;
- naming money-supply measures as an example of indicators he considered no longer useful.

This actor statement supplies the selective-deletion rationale missing from statutory omission alone.

### 1.7 Public Law 106-569, 27 Dec 2000, §1003

Official law:

https://www.congress.gov/106/plaws/publ569/PLAW-106publ569.pdf

Relevant pages: 114 Stat. 3028–3029.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct enacted primary evidence**.

The law:

- retains the first sentence of Federal Reserve Act §2A;
- strikes everything after that first sentence;
- creates new §2B;
- requires the Chair to appear at semiannual hearings;
- establishes an alternating primary-committee structure, with the other chamber’s committee able to request a follow-up appearance;
- requires a written monetary-policy report addressing broad economic developments and prospects;
- keeps employment, unemployment, production, investment, real income, productivity, exchange rates, trade/payments, and prices inside the observatory;
- does **not** restore annual money / credit growth ranges;
- does **not** restore the old range-revision / deviation explanation mechanism.

### 1.8 First hearing under the new structure, 13 Feb 2001

Official GPO / GovInfo hearing PDF:

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-107shrg75191/pdf/CHRG-107shrg75191.pdf

Citation:

- *Federal Reserve’s First Monetary Policy Report for 2001*;
- Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs;
- S. Hrg. 107-132;
- 13 Feb 2001;
- printed pp. 1–2.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct immediate post-enactment actor retrospective**.

Important technical boundary:

The official PDF text was directly retrieved, but automated screenshot retrieval timed out in this pass. Therefore the relevant pages are **official-text verified but not newly image-bound** here.

#### Gramm

Gramm calls the hearing the first under the new reporting arrangement.

He says the old Humphrey–Hawkins process required reporting on many factors that, in his view, were less relevant in 2001 than in the 1970s. He says a bipartisan agreement **changed the focus of the report**.

He also explicitly states that the new structure normally reduces Greenspan’s routine appearances from **four hearings a year to two**, while leaving the non-primary chamber able to request an additional appearance.

This is important because the redesign simultaneously strengthens and reduces different accountability dimensions.

#### Sarbanes

Sarbanes describes the end-of-2000 legislation as giving **permanent reauthorization** to the semiannual monetary report, notes that the Federal Reserve supported it, and says the reports and public testimony perform a **critical oversight function** that is being carried forward without interruption.

This is an immediate bipartisan counterweight to any claim that Gramm’s focus-change language meant oversight itself was being abandoned.

### 1.9 Later scholarly / institutional controls

#### Wischnewsky, Jansen, and Neuenkirch (2021)

Arina Wischnewsky, David-Jan Jansen, and Matthias Neuenkirch, “Financial stability and the Fed: Evidence from congressional hearings,” *Economic Inquiry* 59(3):1192–1214.

https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ecin.12977

Use: **later high-quality scholarship / not actor definition**.

The article reconstructs the development of congressional monetary-policy oversight and uses Humphrey–Hawkins hearings as a long-run communication corpus. It is useful for confirming that the hearing process became a durable informational institution, but it must not replace 1999–2001 actor statements when defining the redesign problem.

#### McElravey (1989)

John N. McElravey, “Communication and the Humphrey-Hawkins process,” Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, *Economic Commentary*, April 1989.

https://www.fedinprint.org/item/fedcec/29197

Use: **contemporaneous institutional analysis / secondary to official records**.

Its explicit concern with using the Humphrey–Hawkins process to communicate Federal Reserve objectives is useful context showing that the communication function did not suddenly appear at sunset.

## 2. Fresh finding A: the FOMC wanted the observatory preserved before the range debate reached its endpoint

The February 1999 FOMC minute matters because it predates:

- the May 2000 sunset;
- the June 2000 Greenspan / Broaddus range debate;
- the December selective reenactment.

At that point the FOMC collectively identifies three positive functions of the semiannual process:

```text
policy explanation
+
communication of views
+
accountability to Congress / public
```

This makes it unsafe to tell a simple story in which the Federal Reserve merely wanted to escape Humphrey–Hawkins accountability and Congress later rescued a weakened remnant.

At least at the collective FOMC level in February 1999:

> **the semiannual report / hearing institution itself is actor-valued and continuation is affirmatively desired.**

But this does not establish that the FOMC values every old quantitative requirement.

The next year shows the opposite for the ranges.

Therefore:

```text
support for observatory continuation
!=
support for every instrument embedded in the observatory
```

## 3. Fresh finding B: the 2000 debate is an internal decomposition of “accountability”

Combining February 1999 with June 2000 makes the Fed-side sequence much sharper:

```text
Feb 1999 collective FOMC:
reports + hearings useful
because communication + accountability

        ↓

Jun 2000 Greenspan:
monetary ranges not credible policy guides;
likely reporting renewal should not require them

Jun 2000 Broaddus:
ranges operationally weak
BUT still a quantitative accountability / anchor device
```

So `accountability` is not a single mechanism in the historical material.

At least three layers are separable:

1. **narrative accountability** — explain policy and economic reasoning;
2. **personal / dialogic accountability** — Chair appears and answers Congress;
3. **quantitative intermediate-target accountability** — publish ranges and later explain revision / deviation.

The inherited Humphrey–Hawkins apparatus bundled all three imperfectly.

By 2000 historical actors were deciding which should survive together.

## 4. Fresh finding C: by 30 June the redesign had a cross-institutional negotiated core

The 30 June Gramm statement is a substantial advance beyond the June 28 FOMC transcript.

Greenspan’s transcript says a particular renewal structure is the likely outcome.

Two days later Gramm says that a consensus has actually been reached among:

```text
Senate Banking participants
+
House banking participants
+
Federal Reserve Board of Governors
```

The agreed core is not described merely as `continue the old report`.

Gramm instead emphasizes a new legal form:

> write a formal Fed-chair testimony procedure into the Federal Reserve Act.

This suggests the genealogy has at least the following shape:

```text
Feb 1999
FOMC wants reports/hearings continued

        ↓ negotiation not yet recovered

28 Jun 2000
Greenspan expects reports-without-ranges outcome

        ↓

30 Jun 2000
Gramm announces House + Senate + Fed consensus
on formal Chair-testimony / reporting procedure

        ↓

5 Jul 2000
committee markup announcement for replacement legislation

        ↓

Jul–Dec 2000
House / Senate legislative completion

        ↓

27 Dec 2000
PL 106-569 §1003
```

This is much stronger than a final-statute-only genealogy.

But the missing negotiation record remains important: the present evidence shows convergence, not who proposed which clause.

## 5. Fresh finding D: House actors define the surviving institution through oversight and democratic dialogue

The 25 July House hearing is useful because the old legal requirement is already gone.

That creates a natural test:

> if the statute disappears temporarily, which parts of the practice do actors voluntarily keep, and why?

Leach, LaFalce, and Maloney do not describe the hearing as a ceremonial leftover.

Their formulations converge on:

- congressional oversight;
- public accountability of an independent central bank;
- communication of Federal Reserve reasoning;
- elected-representative mediation between the Fed and public;
- continuity during the legal lapse.

This is strong evidence for **practical afterlife with actor-recognized function**.

It also blocks a misleading inference:

```text
old statutory §2A expires
→ accountability institution disappears
```

Instead:

```text
old statutory architecture expires
BUT
actors voluntarily preserve the hearing practice
because they still recognize an oversight / dialogue problem
```

That practice is then re-statutized in December.

## 6. Fresh finding E: the new law simultaneously strengthens and weakens different oversight dimensions

This is the packet’s main method result.

If accountability were placed on one line from `weak` to `strong`, the 2000 change would be contradictory.

### Dimension 1: personal appearance

According to Gramm’s June 30 formulation, the new design for the first time places a formal Chair-testimony procedure in the Federal Reserve Act.

Direction:

```text
more explicit / more personalized statutory accountability
```

### Dimension 2: narrative economic observatory

New §2B preserves a broad report on monetary policy and employment / production / income / productivity / prices / external conditions.

Direction:

```text
substantial continuity / statutory reconstruction
```

### Dimension 3: quantitative monetary-range accountability

Annual money / credit ranges and later revision / deviation explanations are not restored.

Direction:

```text
mechanism disappearance
```

### Dimension 4: routine hearing burden

Gramm’s February 2001 explanation says the redesign normally reduces the Chair’s routine appearances from four hearings a year to two, while allowing the other chamber to request a follow-up.

Direction:

```text
routine burden reduced
+ optional duplication retained
```

Therefore the historical result is not faithfully captured by:

```text
accountability weakened
```

or:

```text
accountability strengthened
```

The evidence supports:

```text
accountability recomposed across mechanisms
```

This is researcher shorthand only.

## 7. Fresh finding F: Gramm and Sarbanes give complementary immediate post-enactment relation judgments

The 13 February 2001 hearing is especially valuable because both statements come only weeks after Public Law 106-569.

### Gramm’s relation judgment

He emphasizes **difference**:

- old reporting factors had become less relevant in his view;
- bipartisan agreement changed the report’s focus;
- hearing frequency was rationalized from four to two routine Chair appearances.

### Sarbanes’s relation judgment

He emphasizes **continuity**:

- the legislation permanently reauthorized the semiannual report;
- Fed support existed;
- public testimony’s critical oversight function continues without interruption.

These are not mutually exclusive once the historical object is decomposed.

They are evidence that the immediate actors themselves saw both:

```text
functional continuity
+
institutional redesign
```

This is preferable to forcing one speaker into the role of `correct continuity judgment` and the other into `wrong discontinuity judgment`.

## 8. Problem-history reconstruction

The relevant problem is not simply `how to make the Fed report to Congress`.

By 1999–2001 at least four actor-recognized difficulties are entangled:

### A. Public / congressional accountability of an independent central bank

Actors: FOMC collectively; Leach; LaFalce; Sarbanes; Gramm.

Answer candidates:

- recurring reports;
- Chair testimony;
- congressional questioning;
- public explanation.

### B. Communication of policy reasoning and economic outlook

Actors: FOMC collectively; House members; later scholarship confirms the process became a durable communication channel.

Answer candidates:

- narrative report;
- testimony;
- discussion of broad economic indicators.

### C. Whether monetary aggregates remain credible mandatory policy targets

Actors: Greenspan; Broaddus; Gramm.

Answer candidates:

- keep ranges;
- remove ranges but keep measurement;
- replace quantitative anchor with another formal anchor;
- rely on broader long-run inflation discussion.

### D. How much repetitive testimony should be required

Actor: Gramm especially, with the enacted alternating-committee procedure.

Answer candidate:

- one primary hearing per semiannual cycle;
- optional follow-up by the other chamber.

The 2000 statute is therefore a negotiated solution to **multiple partially separable accountability problems**, not simply a continuation or repeal of one old problem.

## 9. Relation judgment

Do not freeze a whole-object relation as `continuous` or `disappeared`.

The evidence is dimension-specific:

```text
old broad monetary-policy observatory
→ selectively reconstructed

old money-growth-range mechanism
→ disappears

public / congressional accountability problem
→ remains highly askable

formal personal-appearance procedure
→ becomes more explicit

routine duplicate hearing burden
→ reduced
```

For the full 1978 → 2000 oversight architecture, `reformulated` is currently a strong candidate.

For the monetary-range submechanism, `disappeared` is much stronger and already supported by the companion packet.

For the broader Congress–Fed oversight problem, disappearance would be false.

## 12. Hindsight risks

### 12.1 Do not narrate a simple Fed escape from oversight

The February 1999 FOMC minute directly shows collective support for continuing the reports/hearings because of communication and accountability value.

That does not mean the Fed wanted the entire old statute unchanged.

### 12.2 Do not narrate a simple congressional tightening

The final design formalizes Chair testimony but removes quantitative range machinery and reduces routine appearances.

`formal testimony requirement` cannot stand in for the whole accountability architecture.

### 12.3 Do not make monetary-aggregate obsolescence objective by fiat

Greenspan and Gramm offer actor judgments about declining usefulness.

Broaddus preserves a contemporaneous counterargument about accountability value.

Later historians / economists must keep that disagreement visible.

### 12.4 Do not infer causal drafting transmission from temporal proximity

June 28 Fed discussion → June 30 Gramm announcement is strikingly close.

But without draft correspondence / committee staff records:

```text
close chronology + matching architecture
!= proven transmission path
```

### 12.5 Do not equate hearing count with accountability strength

Four routine hearings can contain less useful information than two formally structured hearings; two hearings can also reduce opportunities for questioning.

The sources establish institutional redesign, not a single objective accountability score.

### 12.6 Do not let post-2000 scholarship define the 2000 actor problem

Wischnewsky et al. and later central-bank-communication research are useful for historical significance and persistence.

The 1999–2001 problem definition should remain anchored in FOMC, committee, hearing, floor, and statutory sources.

The current best judgment is therefore not `Humphrey–Hawkins accountability disappeared` and not `the old process simply continued`.

It is:

> **the range-centered quantitative accountability mechanism disappeared inside a deliberately preserved and partially re-formalized Congress–Fed observatory; actors simultaneously strengthened the formal personal-testimony channel, preserved narrative oversight, and reduced repetitive hearing burden.**

That is a genuine problem-history transformation because the actors were not merely editing administrative form. They were deciding **what kind of answerability an independent central bank should owe Congress once an inherited quantitative control object had lost credibility**.
