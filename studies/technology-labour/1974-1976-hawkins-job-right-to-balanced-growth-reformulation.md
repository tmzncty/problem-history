# 1974–76: from enforceable job right to full-employment-and-balanced-growth governance

Status: **M2 source packet / draft-genealogy and enforceability identity test / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet fills a source debt left by:

- `1946-1978-full-employment-revival-and-redefinition.md`;
- `1945-1946-s380-to-employment-act-reformulation.md`.

The merged 1946–78 packet established that the enacted 1978 Humphrey–Hawkins Act explicitly strengthened and supplemented the Employment Act of 1946, but it deliberately did **not** freeze the 1974–76 internal genealogy. That debt matters because the campaign itself changed enough that `Humphrey–Hawkins` cannot safely be treated as one stable historical problem.

The narrow question here is:

> What changed between Augustus Hawkins's 1974 Equal Opportunity and Full Employment proposal and the 1976 Full Employment and Balanced Growth substitute, and which changes alter the problem itself rather than merely its legislative wording?

The strongest new source is a **10 March 1976 staff memorandum from Hawkins's own House Subcommittee on Equal Opportunities**. It explicitly compares the new 1976 substitute with previous versions, identifies features common to all versions, and separately lists additions, changes, and eliminations.

That makes this an unusually strong identity test. The actors themselves supply both:

1. a continuity claim; and
2. an itemized record of what was materially redesigned.

The best current reconstruction is:

```text
1974 H.R. 15476
employment as an individually enforceable right
+ Job Guarantee Office
+ Standby Job Corps
+ judicial appeal
+ equal-opportunity / anti-discrimination protection
+ annual Full Employment and Production Program

        ↓ Jan 1975 reintroduction as H.R. 50 / S. 50
        ↓ March 1975 major broadening
        ↓ 1975 hearings / revision

1976 substitute
employment-right language retained
+ 3% unemployment target, now within four years
+ Full Employment and Balanced Growth Plan
+ fiscal / monetary / anti-inflation coordination
+ countercyclical + structural + youth policies
+ Full Employment Office + conditional job reservoirs

but
- judicial appeal
- Job Guarantee Office
- direct imposition of policy on the Federal Reserve
- mandated National Institute for Full Employment Research
- some mandated local / advisory machinery
```

A provisional researcher-side description is:

**normative continuity + enforcement retreat + macro-governance broadening**.

That phrase is analytical, not actor vocabulary and not a schema verdict.

## 1. Why the 1974–76 interval is not a minor drafting episode

The 1978 Act can look, from a distance, like a single four-year campaign to revive the full-employment commitment of 1945–46.

The newly recovered evidence shows that this view is too coarse.

The 1974 formulation made the **individual claimant** unusually visible. Hawkins described the proposal as a Federal job-guarantee bill whose central point was a government guarantee of individual job rights. The bill's architecture included a Job Guarantee Office, a Standby Job Corps, local reservoirs of public/private employment projects, and recourse to Federal court for a person who believed that the employment right had been denied.

By March 1976, the Hawkins subcommittee could still say that all versions shared an employment-right commitment and a last-resort public-employment mechanism. But the new substitute also explicitly eliminated the judicial appeal and the Job Guarantee Office, lengthened the timetable for the unemployment target, broadened the macroeconomic planning framework, elevated inflation and balanced growth, changed the relationship to the Federal Reserve, and reorganized the observatory/administrative machinery.

So the historical relation cannot be recovered merely by tracking the phrase `full employment`.

A central methodological distinction is:

```text
right language survives
!=
right remains individually enforceable
```

and:

```text
last-resort employment survives
!=
job-guarantee mechanism survives unchanged
```

## 2. Source control

### 2.1 October 1974: Hawkins's own newsletter inserted in the Congressional Record

The official *Congressional Record* for 7 October 1974 reproduces material from Augustus Hawkins explaining H.R. 15476.

Official GovInfo object:

- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1974-pt25/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1974-pt25-6-3.pdf

The indexed official text identifies the proposal as a comprehensive Federal job-guarantee bill and explains:

- useful paid work for able and willing adults as a human right;
- a Job Guarantee Office;
- a Standby Job Corps;
- local reservoirs of public/private employment projects;
- an annual Full Employment and Production Program;
- strengthened congressional/JEC machinery;
- an institutional research component;
- Federal court appeal for a person claiming deprivation of employment rights.

Especially important for source criticism: Hawkins says the proposal deliberately emphasized its **human aspects** as a strategic move to secure referral to the House Education and Labor Committee, while acknowledging that fiscal, monetary, price-stability, balance-of-payments, and business-promotion policies also belonged to the proposal.

Evidence strength: **high / direct actor material reproduced in an official contemporaneous legislative record**.

Boundary: the official indexed text was recoverable in this pass, but the large Congressional Record PDF was not image-bound page-by-page. Exact quotation should receive later page-image verification.

### 2.2 March 10, 1976: Hawkins Subcommittee staff memorandum comparing versions

House Committee on Education and Labor, Subcommittee on Equal Opportunities, Augustus F. Hawkins, chairman, staff memorandum dated 10 March 1976:

> The New “Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1976” in comparison with its previous versions.

Ford Presidential Library / NARA scan:

- https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/library/document/0014/1075807.pdf

This is the highest-value source in the packet because it is not a later historian's comparison. It is a contemporaneous staff-produced, actor-proximate version genealogy.

The relevant scanned pages were checked against the page images in this research pass.

Evidence strength: **very high / contemporaneous actor-proximate comparative primary evidence, image-level checked**.

Important boundary:

- a staff memorandum is not the statute and not proof that every sponsor accepted every characterization;

### 2.3 1976 Senate hearings on the revised bill

*Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1976: Hearings Before the Subcommittee on Equal Opportunities / Senate committees on S.50 and related proposals*.

FRASER full-text source object:

- https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/full-employment-balanced-growth-act-1976-1087/fulltext

The reproduced 1976 bill and testimony confirm that the revised architecture contains:

- a right/goal of useful paid employment;
- a 3-percent unemployment goal within no more than four years;
- a Full Employment and Balanced Growth Plan;
- fiscal, monetary, anti-inflation, countercyclical, structural, and youth-employment policy;
- a Full Employment Office and employment-project reservoirs for people who cannot otherwise obtain work.

Evidence strength: **high / direct contemporaneous hearing and reproduced bill text**.

Boundary: FRASER full text is OCR-first. Freeze-level quotations should be checked against scanned pages.

### 2.4 1975 intermediate evidence

The 1975 stage should not be skipped simply because the 1976 staff memo summarizes it.

Useful primary leads include:

- H.R.50 / S.50 hearings beginning February 1975;
- Hawkins statements that eight hearings heard dozens of witnesses;
- a June 1975 Congressional Record insertion from the Coalition of Black Trade Unionists describing the National Purposes Budget as the central mechanism and treating residual public jobs as smaller if macro policy succeeds.

These are useful for reconstructing how supporters outside the bill-drafting staff understood the problem, but they must not be promoted into Hawkins's own formulation without separate evidence.

Evidence strength: **medium-high as contemporaneous reception / stakeholder formulation; not bill-author identity**.

### 2.5 1977 equal-opportunity criticism as a reception check

A September 1977 Joint Economic Committee hearing records women's testimony that the earlier title, *Equal Opportunity and Full Employment Act*, and provisions ensuring equity had been weakened or deleted as `balanced growth` gained prominence.

FRASER source object:

- https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/

This is useful because it shows that the title and equity changes were recognized by contemporaneous movement actors as substantively important, not just by later historians.

Evidence strength: **high as contemporaneous witness interpretation / contestation**.

Boundary: witness interpretation is not legislative intent and should remain actor-specific.

### 2.6 Scholarly controls

Use these only as historiography / source maps:

- Bernard E. Anderson, “Robert Browne and Full Employment” (2008), on Browne's role in the early Hawkins bill and the later weakening of the job-guarantee formulation;
- Helen Ginsburg, “Historical Amnesia: The Humphrey-Hawkins Act, Full Employment and Employment as a Right” (2012), on the enforceable-right architecture of the early bill and later retreat;

These sources help identify archives and interpret later stakes; they must not overwrite the contemporaneous version genealogy.

## 3. 1974: the visible problem is an individual employment right plus macro responsibility

### 3.1 The individual claimant is unusually explicit

Hawkins's 1974 public explanation does not treat full employment only as an aggregate unemployment rate.

The proposal's central human object is a person who is:

- able to work;
- willing to work;
- seeking work;
- unable to obtain useful paid employment through ordinary channels.

The proposed response is not limited to increasing aggregate demand. The architecture includes a direct institutional path by which such a person could ultimately reach publicly supported work.

The strongest feature for Problem History is the availability of **judicial appeal**. A person claiming deprivation of the employment right could seek review in Federal court.

That makes the governed failure more specific than:

```text
national unemployment rate is too high
```

It includes:

```text
an identifiable person has not received the employment opportunity
that the statutory right says should be available
```

This matters because later versions can preserve the noun `right` while removing the mechanism that makes the individual claimant visible and enforceable.

### 3.2 The job guarantee is not merely a public-works proposal

The 1974 design combines:

- ordinary private employment as preferred placement;
- public/private project reservoirs;
- a Standby Job Corps;
- a Job Guarantee Office;
- a broader annual national economic/employment program.

The answer space is therefore layered:

```text
private / ordinary employment first
        ↓ if unavailable
project-reservoir or standby employment
        ↓
individual enforcement machinery
```

This is more specific than a generic claim that `government should create jobs`.

### 3.3 Hawkins explicitly reveals strategic public framing

The 1974 newsletter contains an unusually valuable methodological warning. Hawkins says the sponsors chose to emphasize the **human** aspects of the proposal strategically in order to secure referral to Education and Labor, even though the bill also involved broad macroeconomic policy.

This gives a real-source fixture:

```text
public emphasis / committee-referral strategy
!=
full problem specification
```

A historian who inferred the entire bill's problem only from its human-right rhetoric would miss fiscal, monetary, price, external-balance, and business-policy components that Hawkins himself says were also present.

Conversely, a historian who reads later macroeconomic planning language backwards into 1974 would miss the unusually strong individual enforcement architecture.

## 4. January–March 1975: reintroduction and broadening

The March 1976 Hawkins staff memorandum supplies the direct genealogy:

```text
Aug 1974 first version
        ↓
Jan 1975 identical reintroduction as H.R.50 / S.50
        ↓
March 20, 1975 House Subcommittee Print
“major broadening”
        ↓
new 1976 substitute
```

This chronology is important because it blocks a common compression:

```text
1974 Hawkins bill
→ 1978 Humphrey–Hawkins Act
```

There was an internal reformulation before the bill even reached its better-known 1976 form.

The 1975 hearings also expose a wider problem field. Supporters describe a National Purposes Budget that coordinates national goals and resources, with the ordinary Federal budget as only one component. At least some labor supporters understood residual public employment as a backstop that should shrink if macro policy successfully generated enough ordinary jobs.

This is an important actor plurality point:

```text
same bill coalition
!=
one problem formulation
```

The bill author, Black trade unionists, macroeconomic planners, civil-rights actors, and later inflation critics can share support for `full employment` while attaching different causal models and answer priorities to it.

## 5. March 1976: the Hawkins staff itself separates continuity from redesign

The 10 March 1976 memorandum is particularly useful because it first lists **features common to all versions**, then separately enumerates **new, changed, and eliminated features**.

### 5.1 What Hawkins staff says remains common

The memorandum says all versions share, among other things:

1. a statutory employment-right commitment for adults able, willing, and seeking work;
2. restoration of full employment / production / purchasing power in the Employment Act policy framework;
3. an annual Presidential first-resort general economic program;
4. a supplemental / last-resort Department of Labor employment mechanism;
5. protection for people excluded by sex, age, race, color, religion, or national origin;
6. explicit attention to inflation;
7. a stronger congressional/JEC role.

This is direct actor-side continuity evidence.

It supports a real genealogy and warns against treating the 1976 bill as a wholly unrelated proposal.

But the same memo immediately prevents `continuous` from becoming the default identity verdict.

### 5.2 New features broaden the governed field

The 1976 substitute adds or elevates:

- `balanced growth` as a named goal;
- a comprehensive anti-inflation policy tied to fiscal and monetary policy;
- countercyclical policy including state/local assistance;
- financial provisions for depressed regions and inner cities;
- youth employment programs;
- regulatory / budgeting review;
- new state transmission / hearing processes;
- a national advisory committee;
- stronger integration of JEC and congressional budget machinery.

The problem field therefore becomes less reducible to:

```text
person without a job
→ guarantee a job
```

and more explicitly includes:

```text
aggregate employment
+ inflation
+ fiscal and monetary policy
+ countercyclical stabilization
+ regional distress
+ structural disadvantage
+ youth employment
+ planning / budget coordination
```

That is a substantive broadening of causal and institutional scope.

### 5.3 Changed features alter targets and observatory design

The staff memorandum records multiple changes:

- the short title changes from *Equal Opportunity and Full Employment Act* to *Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1976*;
- the first-resort national program is renamed and reorganized;
- high-priority areas are consolidated rather than given the earlier specific target structure;
- the 3-percent unemployment target is extended to four years;
- last-resort jobs are redistributed according to applicants' needs;
- the annual manpower report gains an affirmative-action assessment;
- the employment-right wording is modified;
- a **Full Employment Office** replaces the **Job Guarantee Office** and relies on the Employment Service / CETA infrastructure;
- the Federal Reserve reports independently on support for Plan goals rather than simply being placed under the earlier mandate.

These are not all equal in Problem History terms.

A title change alone does not prove a new problem.

But changes to:

- time horizon;
- target specification;
- responsible institution;
- observatory;
- relation to independent monetary authority;
- route from claimant to job;

can alter the problem specification materially.

### 5.4 Eliminated features narrow the enforceability architecture

The staff memorandum explicitly lists elimination of:

- Federal judicial appeal by an individual claiming deprivation of employment rights;
- direct imposition of full-employment policy on the Federal Reserve and other independent agencies;
- a mandated National Institute for Full Employment Research;
- some mandatory Manpower Report content / renaming;
- annual JEC full-employment conferences;
- mandatory use of local CETA Planning Councils as advisory boards for project reservoirs.

The first deletion is especially important.

It yields a strong method fixture:

```text
normative right continuity
!=
remedy continuity
```

and more specifically:

```text
an actor can say the “right” remains common to all versions
while the individual judicial remedy has disappeared
```

That is a much more precise statement than `the job guarantee was watered down`.

## 6. The 1976 revised bill: a layered employment regime rather than the 1974 enforcement model

The 1976 hearings confirm that the revised proposal still treats access to useful paid employment as a national right / goal and still preserves a last-resort employment mechanism.

But the route is now layered inside a broader national plan:

```text
fiscal / monetary / anti-inflation policy
+ full-employment and balanced-growth planning
+ countercyclical measures
+ structural / regional / youth measures
        ↓
ordinary employment opportunities
        ↓ if still unavailable
Full Employment Office / project reservoirs
```

The 1976 unemployment target is also a governance object in its own right: reaching 3 percent is no longer framed as an immediate or very short fixed deadline, but as a target to be reached as promptly as possible and within four years.

Thus:

```text
same quantitative target
!=
same timetable / same obligation
```

This difference matters because the success criterion has changed.

### 6.1 “Employer of last resort” requires version control

Later discussion often compresses multiple versions into the phrase `employer of last resort` or `job guarantee`.

That vocabulary is too blunt for this repository.

At least three distinct mechanisms must be separated:

```text
1974:
individual right + Job Guarantee Office + Standby Job Corps + judicial appeal

1976:
right language + Full Employment Office + conditional project reservoirs
without judicial appeal

1978 enacted:
conditional reservoir authority, still further constrained and dependent on authorization / trigger conditions
```

A historian can reasonably group all three under a broad `last-resort employment` family.

But Problem History should not silently convert family resemblance into identity.

## 7. Equal opportunity does not simply disappear; it is reweighted and contested

The 1976 staff memo says protection against exclusion by sex, age, race, color, religion, or national origin remains common to the versions.

Therefore the short-title change from `Equal Opportunity` to `Balanced Growth` cannot by itself support a claim that equal opportunity vanished.

Yet the title shift and deletion / consolidation of earlier machinery were contemporaneously contested.

In 1977, women witnesses before the Joint Economic Committee argued that the earlier bill's explicit equity provisions had been weakened as equal opportunity moved out of the title and toward the margins of the measure. They also rejected the assumption that aggregate full employment would automatically end discrimination and called for targeted training and counseling.

This produces a useful actor-level disagreement:

```text
Hawkins staff:
anti-discrimination protection remains a common feature

movement witnesses:
equity has been substantively weakened / marginalized
```

Do not normalize one into the other.

They may be talking about different dimensions:

- formal non-discrimination language;
- visibility / priority;
- enforcement;
- targeted institutions;
- distributive outcomes.

This is another case where `survival` is dimensional.

## 8. Pairwise identity test: 1974 → 1976

### 8.1 Strong genealogy

Genealogy is not in doubt.

The Hawkins subcommittee explicitly states:

- the first version appeared in 1974;
- it was identically reintroduced in January 1975;
- a March 1975 print substantially broadened it;
- the 1976 substitute retained, added, changed, and eliminated specified features.

So `unrelated convergence` is not plausible.

### 8.2 Strong normative continuity

Continuity is also real in several dimensions:

- useful paid employment remains a central national commitment;
- ordinary employment remains preferred;
- government retains a last-resort employment role;
- discrimination / excluded groups remain named;
- the Employment Act of 1946 remains the statutory base to be strengthened.

### 8.3 Material changes in problem-defining dimensions

But identity weakens in other dimensions:

| Dimension | 1974 | 1976 |
|---|---|---|
| visible harmed subject | identifiable jobless claimant | claimant remains, but increasingly nested inside national planning and program categories |
| employment right | strong human-right framing | retained in statutory language |
| enforceability | Federal judicial appeal | appeal eliminated |
| direct guarantee institution | Job Guarantee Office / Standby Job Corps | Full Employment Office / project reservoirs via existing employment/CETA machinery |
| central title emphasis | Equal Opportunity + Full Employment | Full Employment + Balanced Growth |
| unemployment target | earlier tighter formulations / strong guarantee orientation | 3% within no more than four years |
| inflation | present but less dominant in public human-right framing | comprehensive anti-inflation policy becomes explicit architecture |
| monetary authority | stronger direct policy imposition | Federal Reserve reports independently on support for goals |
| observatory | guarantee / research / employment architecture | planning, budget, JEC, state, advisory, fiscal/monetary architecture broadened |
| answer space | individual guarantee + projects + national economic policy | macro stabilization + structural policy + regional/youth measures + residual jobs |

### 8.4 Current relation candidate

Do **not** freeze yet.

Candidates:

- `reformulated` — strongest if normative target + direct genealogy outweigh enforcement redesign;
- `transformed_successor` — plausible if individual enforceability / causal architecture / institutional responsibility are treated as defining;
- a compound relation may be needed if the schema can represent dimensional continuity and transformation separately.

For now the safest statement is:

```text
1974 → 1976
strong direct genealogy
+ normative continuity
+ explicit actor-recognized common features
+ explicit actor-recognized elimination of enforcement machinery
+ broadening into macro/structural/anti-inflation governance

=> identity remains non-trivial and should not be labeled continuous by default
```

## 9. Method contribution

This packet adds unusually clean real-source fixtures.

### 9.1 Same campaign does not mean one stable Problem Episode

```text
same sponsor
+ same bill family
+ same headline goal
!=
same problem specification
```

### 9.2 Normative survival is not institutional survival

```text
right survives in statutory language
!=
right remains judicially enforceable
```

### 9.3 Mechanism families need version control

```text
last-resort employment survives
!=
Job Guarantee Office survives
!=
individual guarantee survives unchanged
```

### 9.4 Broadening can coexist with narrowing

The 1976 bill broadens:

- inflation policy;
- balanced growth;
- monetary/fiscal coordination;
- regional policy;
- youth policy;
- planning and budget integration.

At the same time it narrows:

- individual judicial remedy;
- direct guarantee machinery;
- direct command over independent agencies;
- some mandated local/research institutions.

Therefore:

```text
problem broadening
!=
more expansive remedy in every dimension
```

### 9.5 Actor-asserted continuity can coexist with actor-documented transformation

The same March 1976 memorandum says:

- these features are common to all versions;
- these other features were added, changed, or eliminated.

That is exactly the kind of historical evidence the repository needs.

The actor does not force us to choose between `all same` and `all different`.

### 9.6 Strategic framing can be an actor-explicit source of historical distortion

Hawkins's 1974 explanation says the human emphasis was partly strategic for committee referral.

Therefore:

```text
historical public framing
can be strategic metadata about institutional venue
rather than a complete causal/problem decomposition
```

This is important well beyond this episode.

## 10. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1: treating `Humphrey–Hawkins` as one bill from 1974 to 1978

Wrong because the campaign includes materially different versions, and Hawkins staff explicitly documents the changes.

### Risk 2: reading the 1978 inflation / balanced-growth architecture backward into 1974

The early proposal had broader macro elements, but its public and institutional architecture made the individual employment right much more visible and enforceable.

### Risk 3: treating continued `right` language as proof of continued legal enforceability

The judicial remedy was explicitly eliminated.

### Risk 4: treating `employer of last resort` as a version-invariant mechanism

It can refer to very different institutional paths.

### Risk 5: inferring disappearance of equal opportunity from the short title alone

The 1976 staff memo says anti-discrimination protections remain; later witnesses nevertheless say equity has been marginalized. Both pieces are needed.

### Risk 6: treating Hawkins staff as the whole coalition

Labor, civil-rights, women's groups, Federal Reserve actors, Carter administration economists, and opponents define the problem differently.

### Risk 7: turning modern `job guarantee` debates into the historical actor problem

Modern policy vocabulary can illuminate comparison but should not overwrite the specific 1974 offices, remedies, triggers, and planning machinery.

### Risk 8: equating legislative genealogy with identity

This packet is a counterexample: genealogy is direct and strong, while identity still requires dimension-by-dimension checking.

The newly recovered 1976 Hawkins Subcommittee memorandum makes one conclusion especially strong:

> The 1974–76 campaign is neither a simple continuous job-guarantee problem nor a clean replacement. Hawkins's own staff describes an explicit genealogy with common normative commitments while simultaneously documenting the removal of individual enforcement machinery and the addition of a much broader macroeconomic, anti-inflation, structural, regional, youth, and planning regime.

That gives the repository a concrete historical case in which:

```text
direct genealogy is strong
normative vocabulary is partly continuous
institutional mechanism changes materially
causal / policy field broadens
individual remedy narrows

therefore

not a consequence of bill lineage or shared vocabulary.
```
