# 1975–1976 Humphrey–Hawkins: the employment right survives while its remedy architecture retreats

Status: **M2 source packet / intermediate-draft remedy archaeology / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet follows directly from:

- `1974-1976-hawkins-job-right-to-balanced-growth-reformulation.md`;
- `1975-march-h50-layered-job-right-and-national-purposes-budget.md`.

Those packets established that the 20 March 1975 H.R. 50 Subcommittee Print was a layered formulation: a stronger national-planning / National Purposes Budget architecture was added **without yet removing** the individual employment-right machinery, administrative claims process, private Federal suit, Job Guarantee Office, and residual public/private job reservoirs.

The largest remaining date problem was therefore narrower:

> **When does the individually enforceable remedy retreat actually become actor-explicit, and what survives after it retreats?**

Fresh primary evidence now supplies a hard upper bound and a more precise answer.

On **10 March 1976**, the House Subcommittee on Equal Opportunities, under Augustus F. Hawkins, produced a direct comparison of the new `Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1976` with the previous versions. The comparison explicitly lists the subsection providing judicial appeals to persons who believed themselves deprived of their employment rights among the **“Previous features eliminated.”** At the same time, it explicitly lists the employment right itself and last-resort government-financed employment machinery among the **“Features common to all versions.”**

The revised 1976 S. 50 text then shows what replaced the older remedy package: an identifiable individual claimant still remains; the Full Employment Office still makes initial determinations; job reservoirs remain mandatory in specified circumstances; and **administrative appeal procedures** remain. What is gone is the earlier private Federal-court enforcement path.

The result is not adequately described by either:

```text
job right continues unchanged
```

or:

```text
job guarantee disappears
```

A safer reconstruction is:

```text
March / June 1975
employment right
+ identifiable individual claimant
+ administrative determination / appeal
+ private Federal judicial action
+ Job Guarantee Office
+ residual public/private job reservoirs

        ↓ revision completed no later than 10 Mar 1976

March 1976 substitute
employment right remains
+ identifiable individual claimant remains
+ initial administrative determination remains
+ administrative appeal remains
+ Full Employment Office
+ residual job reservoirs remain
BUT
- private judicial appeal / cause-of-action mechanism
- Job Guarantee Office as such
- some direct mandatory institutional controls

        ↓

broader full-employment + balanced-growth
+ anti-inflation + structural + countercyclical problem field
```

Researcher-side shorthand:

**normative-right continuity + claimant continuity + remedy-layer retreat + institutional reconfiguration**.

This is an analytical description, not actor vocabulary and not a schema verdict.

## 1. Repository question carried forward

The merged March-1975 packet established three things that must not be lost:

1. the March 20, 1975 `major broadening` did **not** itself abolish the Federal-court remedy;
2. Hawkins on 11 June 1975 still treated judicial relief as important to H.R. 50's effectiveness;
3. a February 1976 hostile witness was still able to quote the **March 20, 1975 version** and its district-court machinery.

The third point is important but easy to overread. A witness quoting the March 1975 text in February 1976 proves that the older version remained available and politically salient; it does **not** prove that a then-current February 1976 draft still contained the same judicial remedy.

Therefore the surviving source debt was:

```text
present in March / June 1975
        ↓
exact first deletion draft unknown
        ↓
known absent sometime in 1976
```

The new source moves the right-hand boundary to an exact actor-produced document dated **10 March 1976**.

## 2. Direct March 10, 1976 actor comparison: judicial appeal is explicitly eliminated

### Source and provenance

Gerald R. Ford Presidential Library, Loen and Leppert Files, Box 10, folder `Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act (Humphrey-Hawkins Bill)`:

- White House transmittal, 15 March 1976;
- attached House Subcommittee on Equal Opportunities comparison, **10 March 1976**;
- heading: `The New "Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1976" in comparison with its previous versions`.

Digital source:

https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/library/document/0014/1075807.pdf

The White House cover memorandum identifies the attachment as a comparison of H.R. 50 with previous versions and says H.R. 50 was scheduled for Subcommittee markup on 15 March 1976.

The comparison itself is headed by the House Subcommittee on Equal Opportunities and Augustus F. Hawkins as Chairman.

Relevant pages were checked against the scanned page images in this research pass, not only OCR/search text.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct actor-proximate draft genealogy and explicit deletion judgment**.

### What the comparison says is continuous

Under `I. Features common to all versions`, the March 10 memorandum says that all versions include:

- a right for adult Americans able, willing, and seeking work to opportunities for useful paid employment at fair compensation;
- restoration of full employment, production, and purchasing power to the Employment Act framework;
- a Presidential first-resort program for general economic stimulus;
- supplemental / last-resort Department of Labor machinery for government-financed employment opportunities through public and private employment-project reservoirs;
- protection for people excluded from employment by sex, age, race, color, religion, or national origin;
- explicit attention to inflation;
- a stronger congressional role in policies required to maintain full employment without inflation.

This is an unusually useful actor relation judgment. The drafters themselves are saying:

```text
right language
+ first-resort macro policy
+ last-resort employment machinery
```

remain part of the same legislative project across versions.

That does **not** prove full problem identity, but it strongly constrains a disappearance claim.

### What the comparison says changed

The same memorandum then identifies changes including:

- `Equal Opportunity and Full Employment Act` → `Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1976`;
- National Purposes Budget / Full Employment and Production Program → `Full Employment and Balanced Growth Plan`;
- a longer timetable for reaching the 3 percent unemployment goal;
- last-resort jobs distributed on the basis of applicants' needs;
- `Job Guarantee Office` → `Full Employment Office`, using U.S. Employment Service and CETA facilities as arranged by the Secretary of Labor;
- the Federal Reserve shifted toward an independent reporting relation rather than direct imposition of the Act's policy.

These are not only lexical changes. They change responsibility, institutional location, targeting, and the relationship between macro governance and individual claimant machinery.

### The crucial explicit deletion

Under `IV. Previous features eliminated`, the very first item is:

> the subsection providing judicial appeals for persons who felt deprived of their employment rights.

Other eliminated features include:

- direct imposition of the Act's full-employment policies on the Federal Reserve System and other independent agencies;
- a mandated National Institute for Full Employment Research;
- some mandated Manpower Report contents and nomenclature;
- annual JEC full-employment conferences;
- mandatory use of local CETA Planning Councils in developing the project reservoirs.

The key historiographic value is that the Subcommittee does **not** say the employment right is eliminated. It says the **judicial-appeal subsection** is eliminated while simultaneously listing the employment right and last-resort machinery as common features.

Therefore the most precise actor-grounded statement is:

```text
by 10 Mar 1976:
private judicial enforcement has been removed
while the statutory right language and residual employment machinery remain
```

This is materially narrower—and more useful—than saying simply that the `job guarantee` vanished.

## 3. Date bound: what is known and what remains unknown

The current source chain permits a stronger date bound but not yet an exact drafting date.

### Known present

By the March 20, 1975 print and the 11 June 1975 Hawkins/UAW evidence already recovered in the preceding packet:

- an individual could be administratively recognized as a claimant;
- Section 5(g) supplied a `willing and able` presumption;
- Section 5(h) supplied a Federal judicial action after administrative procedures;
- Hawkins said sponsors regarded the provision as important to the effectiveness of the bill.

Official Congressional Record source:

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt14/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt14-4-3.pdf

### Known absent

By the Hawkins Subcommittee's **10 March 1976** comparison, the judicial-appeal subsection is explicitly on the eliminated-features list.

### Not yet known

The precise first draft in which the provision disappeared is still unrecovered.

Do **not** infer that the deletion happened after the February 16, 1976 Boston hearing merely because Lawrence Sherman quoted the March 1975 version there. He explicitly keyed his quotation to the older March 20 version.

The strongest current interval is therefore:

```text
11 Jun 1975: definitely present in the operative H.R.50 discussion

        ↓ exact revision node still unknown

10 Mar 1976: definitely eliminated in the Hawkins Subcommittee comparison
```

Method fixture:

```text
explicit deletion date bound
!=
exact first-draft date
```

## 4. The 1976 claimant does not disappear: the remedy is decomposed

The Hawkins comparison tells us that judicial appeal is gone, but to understand the problem transformation we need to ask what happens to the claimant after that deletion.

The revised S. 50 text, printed by the Senate Committee on Labor and Public Welfare on **22 March 1976** and reprinted in the May 20, 21, and 25 Senate Banking hearings, provides a direct answer.

Official committee-print metadata:

https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CPRT-94SPRT68182O

FRASER searchable hearing text:

https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/full-employment-balanced-growth-act-1976-1087/fulltext

The Senate Banking hearing contents identify the text as `S. 50, as revised by proposed substitute amendment` and as a reprint of the Senate Labor and Public Welfare Committee print.

Evidence strength: **high / direct official revised bill text; FRASER text is OCR-first, so frozen quotations should receive scan-page binding**.

This Senate text should not be silently assumed to be letter-for-letter identical to the March 10 House Subcommittee Print. It is used here to establish the contemporaneous revised 1976 remedy architecture, not an unproved House/Senate text identity.

### 4.1 A concrete harmed / claimant subject remains

Section 206(a) requires the President, through the Secretary of Labor, to develop policies, procedures, and programs to provide employment opportunities to adult Americans who are:

- able, willing, and seeking work;
- unable, despite serious effort, to obtain work in the general economic environment or through other provisions of the Act.

Thus the broader macro / balanced-growth formulation has **not** dissolved the unemployed person into aggregate indicators alone.

### 4.2 The Full Employment Office remains an administrative claimant-processing institution

Section 206(b) establishes a Full Employment Office in the Department of Labor.

Section 206(c) gives the Secretary a sequence that includes:

- counseling, training, and support;
- referral into private and public employment through existing placement machinery / U.S. Employment Service;
- referral into jobs generated by other employment provisions.

So the renamed Office is not merely a symbolic replacement for the Job Guarantee Office.

### 4.3 The residual employment obligation remains unusually strong

Section 206(d) says that insofar as able, willing, and seeking adults are not provided job opportunities through those routes, employment opportunities **shall be provided** by the President through reservoirs of federally operated public employment projects and Secretary-approved private nonprofit projects.

The reservoirs are phased in to achieve the statutory unemployment goal.

This blocks a simple mechanism-disappearance narrative:

```text
private suit disappears
!=
residual Federal employment mechanism disappears
```

### 4.4 Administrative appeal survives judicial appeal

Section 206(e) requires regulations covering:

- an initial determination of the job seeker's abilities;
- nondiscrimination compliance;
- priority criteria based on employment need;
- eligibility criteria for the project reservoirs;
- **administrative appeal procedures** reviewing ability, need, and eligibility determinations.

This is the most important remedy-level finding of this packet.

The historical change is not:

```text
appeal
→ no appeal
```

It is closer to:

```text
1975 architecture:
administrative determination / appeal
        +
private Federal judicial action

        ↓

1976 architecture:
administrative determination / appeal remains
        -
private Federal judicial action
```

That distinction matters because otherwise `employment right` becomes a binary variable and important legal/institutional layers disappear from the history.

## 5. A remedy stack is a better unit of comparison than the word `right`

The 1975–76 evidence suggests that the project should avoid asking only:

> Does the bill contain a right to employment?

Instead, for this case, at least five dimensions need to be separated:

```text
1. normative / statutory right language
2. identifiable individual claimant or harmed subject
3. administrative eligibility / determination process
4. administrative review / appeal
5. private judicial enforcement
6. substantive residual employment machinery
```

The numbering deliberately includes the sixth item because substantive provision and legal remedy are not the same thing.

The March 1976 formulation preserves 1, 2, 3, 4, and an important version of 6 while removing 5 and reorganizing the office that administers the field.

This produces several strong source-grounded fixtures:

```text
right language survives
!=
private cause of action survives

administrative appeal survives
!=
judicial appeal survives

claimant survives
!=
judicial claimant survives

job reservoir survives
!=
personally enforceable job guarantee survives

office rename
!=
function disappears

remedy retreat
!=
problem disappearance
```

These are researcher-side comparison rules extracted from a real historical transition. They are not actor terms.

## 6. The actor-defined problem simultaneously broadens again in 1976

The same March 10 comparison prevents an equally misleading opposite story in which the only change is legal enforceability.

The revised 1976 formulation adds or strengthens:

- balanced growth as an explicit co-object with full employment;
- comprehensive anti-inflation policies linked to fiscal and monetary policy;
- countercyclical policies and State/local countercyclical grants;
- depressed-region and inner-city measures;
- youth employment policy;
- Federal regulatory and budget-efficiency review;
- a new advisory / congressional planning structure;
- a longer four-year timetable for the 3 percent unemployment objective.

The Subcommittee itself says the Act seeks to maintain full employment **without inflation**.

Therefore the problem transformation is multidimensional:

```text
individual unable to obtain a job
still remains governable

while

the causal / policy field expands toward:
aggregate growth
+ inflation
+ countercyclical stabilization
+ structural / regional unemployment
+ youth transition
+ fiscal / monetary coordination
+ government efficiency
```

The removal of private judicial enforcement should be studied **inside** this broader re-specification, not as an isolated legal technicality.

## 7. Historical actors and later historians must remain separate

Later scholarship is useful here precisely because it demonstrates the danger of flattening the primary evidence.

### Helen Lachs Ginsburg

Helen Lachs Ginsburg, `Historical Amnesia: The Humphrey-Hawkins Act, Full Employment and Employment as a Right`, *Review of Black Political Economy* 39 (2012), pp.121–136, treats the enacted Humphrey–Hawkins Act as a much-weakened outcome and reconstructs a longer history of employment as a right.

Publisher / bibliographic source:

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1007/s12114-011-9121-3

Evidence role: **later scholarly interpretation / historiographic control, not actor formulation**.

For this repository, `weakened` is a useful high-level historical interpretation but not an adequate clause-level relation label. The March 1976 primary record lets us say more exactly *which* dimensions weaken and which remain.

### Steven Attewell

Steven Attewell, *People Must Live by Work: Direct Job Creation in America, from FDR to Reagan* (University of Pennsylvania Press, 2018), treats direct job creation as a distinct policy tradition and describes it as a significant and polarizing issue in 1970s Humphrey–Hawkins deliberations.

Publisher source:

https://www.pennpress.org/9780812295313/people-must-live-by-work/

Evidence role: **later scholarly reconstruction / literature map**.

This helps explain why the changing direct-job machinery deserves to be tracked separately from macro full-employment commitments, but it cannot substitute for the contemporaneous bill versions when determining the exact remedy transition.

### Historiographic warning

A later historian may reasonably summarize the deletion of private enforceability as the weakening or loss of a `job guarantee` in a normative/political sense.

The primary March 1976 text nevertheless requires the repository to preserve the narrower institutional facts:

```text
Federal-court remedy: removed
Job Guarantee Office label: removed / reorganized
statutory employment-right language: retained
administrative claimant process: retained
administrative appeal: retained
residual public/private employment reservoirs: retained
```

Method fixture:

```text
later policy shorthand
!=
clause-level historical identity
```

## 8. Archival leads for the cause of the retreat

The March 10 Subcommittee comparison explicitly says that, for brevity, it does **not** give the reasons for the changes.

Therefore the current evidence proves the transformation but not yet its causal authorship.

The Leon H. Keyserling Papers at the Truman Library now provide an unusually dense source map for the relevant weeks. The collection description identifies a dedicated Humphrey–Hawkins Bill File and lists materials including:

- H.R. 50 / S. 50 committee and subcommittee prints;
- `Comments on Possible Action Programs Under H.R. 50`, 11 March 1976, prepared for Hawkins's March 12 press conference;
- Keyserling testimony / press materials on H.R. 50 and S. 50;
- March 22, 1976 S. 50 committee print;
- later 1976 amendments and staff materials;
- Library of Congress study material on the bill.

Finding aid:

https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/personal-papers/leon-h-keyserling-papers

Evidence strength: **high as archival source map; zero by itself for causal attribution**.

Because Keyserling worked on the legislation, these files are high-priority. But:

```text
archive proximity
+ known policy involvement
!=
proof that Keyserling proposed the judicial-remedy deletion
```

The same caution applies to later claims about Humphrey, AFL-CIO, Hawkins staff, executive agencies, or congressional committee pressure. The responsible actor / bargaining mechanism behind the deletion remains open until draft markups, correspondence, memoranda, or direct testimony is recovered.

## 10. Current relation judgment

Do **not** freeze a schema edge yet.

The new evidence makes simple `continuous` harder to defend because an important legal remedy and named guarantee institution are explicitly removed while the overall policy field broadens substantially.

But it also makes a total `transformed_successor` / disappearance reading too strong if it implies that the individual employment right, claimant, and residual employment-provision mechanism vanish. They do not.

The strongest current description is:

```text
20 Mar 1975 formulation
        ↓
by 10 Mar 1976 substitute

strong direct genealogy
+ actor-declared common normative target
+ claimant continuity
+ residual employment-mechanism continuity
BUT
private judicial enforcement removed
+ administrative institution reorganized
+ policy / causal field broadened again
```

`reformulated` is therefore a strong candidate for the whole relation, while a more fine-grained model may eventually need to represent separate transformations of:

- normative commitment;
- claimant / harmed subject;
- enforcement remedy;
- responsible institution;
- first-resort macro policy;
- last-resort direct-employment mechanism.

This case is evidence that one flat relation may hide different directions of change across dimensions.

## 13. Provisional update to the longer problem chain

The larger 1945→1978 full-employment genealogy can now be made more precise without asserting unchanged identity:

```text
1945 S.380
full-employment assurance
+ aggregate prospective calculation
+ residual Federal responsibility

        ↓ 1946 compromise

continuing Federal responsibility
+ Economic Report / observatory
- explicit full-employment assurance / aggregate-gap closure rule

        ↓ 1974 Hawkins

explicit employment right revived
+ individual claimant
+ judicial enforceability
+ job guarantee machinery
+ macro planning

        ↓ March 1975 additive broadening

right + judicial enforceability still present
+ National Purposes Budget / national priorities
+ direct jobs as residual backstop

        ↓ by 10 March 1976

right remains
+ claimant / administrative review remains
+ residual job reservoirs remain
- private judicial enforcement
+ balanced-growth / inflation / structural field expands

        ↓ 1978 compromise remains to be remedy-diffed
```

The value of this chain is precisely that different layers can disappear, return, or transform at different times. `Full employment`, `employment right`, `Federal responsibility`, `job guarantee`, `administrative appeal`, and `public job reservoir` should not be treated as interchangeable names for one perennial problem.
