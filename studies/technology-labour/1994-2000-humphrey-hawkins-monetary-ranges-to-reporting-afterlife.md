# 1994–2000: from Humphrey–Hawkins monetary ranges to reporting afterlife

Status: **M2 implementation/disappearance test / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet extends:

- `1979-1983-humphrey-hawkins-goals-to-forecasts-implementation-drift.md`;
- `1981-1982-humphrey-hawkins-required-path-to-benchmark.md`;
- `1946-1978-full-employment-revival-and-redefinition.md`.

The earlier packets recovered a gradual weakening of the President-side Humphrey–Hawkins numerical-goal architecture:

```text
1979
short-term values already function as forecasts
+ medium-term path still closes on statutory goals

1980
authorized timetable deferral
+ revised path still closes on formally deferred goal

January 1981
medium-term closure path breaks under Carter

February 1982
Reagan CEA explicitly calls annual goals
“benchmarks of economic progress”

1983 JEC
openly disputes goal → forecast / benchmark substitution
```

The remaining forward question was whether the Federal Reserve side of the 1978 observatory experienced a comparable hollowing, and whether the eventual disappearance of a reporting mechanism can be dated without falsely claiming that the broader employment/price-stability problem disappeared.

Fresh primary evidence gives a unusually strong sequence:

```text
1978 section 108 architecture
mandatory semiannual reports
+ announced monetary/credit growth ranges
+ relation to employment/inflation/economic goals
+ explanation of revisions/deviations

        ↓

July 1994 FOMC
ranges formally retained under Humphrey–Hawkins
BUT
recast as long-run benchmarks rather than guides to current policy

        ↓

1998 Fed actor confirmation
ranges are still statutorily reported
but do not describe the policy the Fed intends to implement that year

        ↓

15 May 2000
periodic reporting requirement sunsets

        ↓

June/July 2000
FOMC stops setting money/debt ranges
because legal requirement has expired
and says they have not been useful policy benchmarks for years

        ↓

25 July 2000
Congress/Fed nevertheless continue the familiar semiannual hearing/report practice
without the old legal requirement

        ↓

27 Dec 2000, Public Law 106-569
old section 2A after its first sentence is struck
+ new section 2B restores statutory semiannual hearings/reports
+ broad economic observatory survives
- mandatory monetary/credit growth ranges do not return
- old revision/deviation explanation mechanism does not return
```

The key present-day analytical distinction is therefore:

> **the monetary-range accountability mechanism can disappear while the broader statutory objectives and the congressional monetary-policy observatory survive.**

Researcher-side shorthand used below includes `instrumental hollowing`, `observatory afterlife`, and `selective procedural survival`. These are not actor vocabulary and are not proposed schema relations.

## 1. Source control

### 1.1 Public Law 95-523, 27 Oct 1978, section 108

Official *Statutes at Large* PDF / Congress.gov:

- https://www.congress.gov/95/statute/STATUTE-92/STATUTE-92-Pg1887.pdf

Relevant printed pages:

- 92 Stat. 1897–1898.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct enacted primary evidence**.

Use:

This supplies the baseline architecture that 1994–2000 modifies:

- February and July written Federal Reserve reports;
- objectives/plans for ranges of growth or diminution of monetary and credit aggregates;
- explicit relation of those plans to employment, unemployment, production, investment, real income, productivity, trade/payments and prices;
- relation to Presidential/Congressional short-term goals;
- consultation with congressional committees;
- if ranges are revised or missed because of changing conditions, explanation of the reasons for revisions/deviations.

Important chronology boundary:

The famous first sentence of Federal Reserve Act section 2A—long-run growth of monetary/credit aggregates commensurate with production potential in order to promote maximum employment, stable prices, and moderate long-term interest rates—predates the 1978 Humphrey–Hawkins reporting amendment. Do not attribute the origin of that first sentence to Public Law 95-523.

The 1978 Act changed the reporting/accountability architecture around it.

### 1.2 FOMC presentation material, 5–6 Jul 1994

Federal Reserve official PDF:

- https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomc19940706material.pdf

Evidence strength: **very high / direct internal actor-proximate primary evidence**.

Relevant image-audited presentation page:

- presentation p. 5 (PDF page around the July monetary-range discussion).

The staff lays out an explicit alternative:

- in an era of unstable money demand, current ranges could be treated less as a guide to current policy;
- instead, they could function as a long-run benchmark for money growth consistent with price stability and normal velocity behavior;
- the staff explicitly notes that this was **not the intent of the Humphrey–Hawkins Act**.

This is unusually valuable because the historical actor itself distinguishes:

```text
statutory / original-intent function
!=
proposed contemporary operational function
```

Boundary:

A staff presentation is not itself the FOMC's final decision. It proves that the reinterpretation was explicitly formulated inside the policy process and that its divergence from perceived statutory intent was recognized.

### 1.3 FOMC minutes, 5–6 Jul 1994

Federal Reserve Bulletin / FRASER full text:

- https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-bulletin-62/october-1994-20869/fulltext

FRASER FOMC minutes scan:

- https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/docs/historical/FOMC/meetingdocuments/19940706min.pdf

Evidence strength: **very high / direct collective FOMC primary evidence**.

Use:

The minutes show the Committee actually retained/reaffirmed its ranges while observing that the heavily reduced ranges could be viewed as **long-run benchmarks** for monetary growth consistent with maximum sustainable economic expansion in a noninflationary environment, conditional on more normal velocity behavior.

The Committee simultaneously recognized persistent velocity uncertainty and the need to watch a broad range of other financial/economic indicators.

Therefore:

```text
staff proposal of benchmark reinterpretation
→ collective minutes adopt benchmark description
```

is a stronger edge than staff discussion alone.

Boundary:

The minutes do not say the statutory reporting requirement disappeared in 1994. The ranges remain formally reported. This is a change in operational meaning under continuing formal obligation.

### 1.5 Laurence H. Meyer, Federal Reserve speech, 1998

Federal Reserve actor evidence (source map from Federal Reserve materials; exact speech should be page-bound in a later source pass).

Evidence strength: **high / direct later actor confirmation**, pending tighter page binding.

Use:

By 1998 Meyer describes the money-growth ranges as something the Fed is required by statute to set but not as a statement of the policy it intends to implement over the coming year; rather, they correspond to a longer-run price-stability / normal-velocity benchmark.

### 1.8 Monetary Policy Report to the Congress, 20 Jul 2000

Official Federal Reserve PDF:

- https://www.federalreserve.gov/boarddocs/hh/2000/july/fullreport.pdf

Evidence strength: **very high / direct official Board/FOMC primary evidence**.

The relevant report page has been image-audited in this pass.

Key finding:

At the June 2000 meeting the FOMC did **not** establish money/debt growth ranges for 2000/2001 because the legal requirement to establish and announce them had expired. The report adds an actor-side causal judgment: uncertainties in money/debt velocities meant the ranges had not provided useful benchmarks for monetary policy for many years.

But money and credit did not vanish as observable variables; the FOMC still said their behavior remained useful for gauging economic/financial conditions and continued discussing them.

This is a particularly clean fixture:

```text
mandatory policy-target/reporting role disappears
!=
variable disappears from observation
```

### 1.10 Public Law 106-569, 27 Dec 2000, section 1003

Official GovInfo PDF:

- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-106publ569/pdf/PLAW-106publ569.pdf

Relevant printed pages:

- 114 Stat. 3028–3029.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct enacted primary evidence**.

The relevant pages were image-audited in this pass.

Section 1003 does two analytically distinct things:

1. **Repeal / selective retention:** Federal Reserve Act section 2A is amended by striking everything after the first sentence.
2. **New observatory:** a new section 2B requires the Chair to appear at semiannual congressional hearings and requires a written report discussing monetary policy plus past/prospective developments in employment, unemployment, production, investment, real income, productivity, exchange rates, international trade/payments, and prices.

What is not restored in new section 2B:

- mandatory objectives/plans for money/credit aggregate growth ranges;
- mandatory next-year aggregate ranges;
- the old clause requiring explanations for revisions/deviations from those announced range objectives/plans.

Important boundary:

This law does **not** erase the entire Humphrey–Hawkins / Employment Act problem field. It preserves a broad reporting observatory, and elsewhere in Public Law 106-569 Congress also preserves the Employment Act of 1946 section 3 report from the general sunset mechanism.

The correct disappearance object here is narrower: the **section-2A monetary-aggregate range accountability machinery**.

## 2. Baseline: the 1978 Fed architecture contains more than “dual mandate” language

A present-day summary of Humphrey–Hawkins often collapses the Federal Reserve provisions into a generic statement that monetary policy should consider employment and inflation.

For Problem History purposes that loses the governing mechanism.

The 1978 section 108 architecture tied together at least four layers:

```text
A. broad economic observatory
employment / unemployment / production / investment / income /
productivity / trade / prices

B. monetary-policy intermediate object
ranges of growth or diminution of monetary and credit aggregates

C. cross-institution relation
those objectives/plans must be related to Presidential/Congressional short-term goals

D. accountability for path changes
deviation/revision may be justified by changing conditions
BUT the Board must explain the reasons in later consultation/reporting
```

Thus the law never required mechanical achievement of a fixed money-growth number under all circumstances.

But neither were ranges merely optional descriptive statistics.

They were a required announced object inside an accountability/reporting chain.

This matters because the later history does not move directly from:

```text
binding mechanical money rule
→ discretion
```

The starting point was already conditional/discretionary.

The more precise question is:

> When does a required intermediate object cease to function as a meaningful guide/accountability device even before the legal requirement itself disappears?

The 1994 material supplies a unusually explicit answer candidate.

## 3. July 1994: actor-recognized divergence between legal-intent function and operational function

The FOMC staff presentation is unusually valuable because it does not merely say that velocity has become unstable.

It formulates a change in **what the required range is for**.

Under money-demand uncertainty, the Committee might stop treating current ranges primarily as guides to current policy and instead use them as a long-run benchmark consistent with price stability and normal velocity behavior.

Then comes the crucial source-critical phrase: the presentation explicitly says this was **not the intent of the Humphrey–Hawkins Act**.

This creates a high-quality actor-proximate distinction:

```text
formal object retained:
annual M2/M3 ranges

historically perceived original function:
public guidance/accountability concerning current monetary policy

new proposed function:
long-run price-stability benchmark under hypothetical normal velocity
```

The historical change is not lexical.

The same numeric ranges can remain on the page while their operational semantics change.

Candidate method fixture:

```text
same required number
!=
same policy function
```

Another:

```text
formal compliance
can coexist with
actor-recognized functional departure from perceived statutory intent
```

Do not overstate this from the staff paper alone. The staff says `might`.

The next source matters because the Committee itself moves into the same language.

---

## 4. The FOMC collectively retains the ranges but calls them long-run benchmarks

The July 1994 minutes show that the Committee:

- reaffirmed the existing 1994 M2/M3 ranges;
- adopted provisional 1995 ranges;
- recognized continuing velocity uncertainty;
- expected monetary aggregates to be evaluated alongside a broad set of other indicators;
- said the heavily reduced ranges **could be viewed as long-run benchmarks** consistent with maximum sustainable noninflationary expansion if velocity eventually behaved more normally.

This is not statutory disappearance.

It is closer to **instrumental hollowing under formal survival**:

```text
legal reporting slot survives
+ range survives
+ vote survives
+ publication survives

but

range loses much of its status as a near-term policy guide
```

`instrumental hollowing` is researcher shorthand only.

The actor wording itself is `long-run benchmarks` plus uncertainty about velocity.

The distinction is especially useful because it resembles the 1982 Executive `benchmarks of economic progress` shift while operating on a different object:

```text
1982 Executive side:
annual macro goals → benchmarks

1994 Fed side:
required monetary growth ranges → long-run benchmarks
```

Do **not** infer a direct causal transmission between these two episodes without evidence.

For method testing, however, they independently demonstrate the same logical possibility:

```text
benchmarkization
can preserve measurement/reporting
while weakening correction/guidance force
```

## 5. 1994 is not a disappearance date

Several facts block a false disappearance claim.

First, the FOMC explicitly acts **in keeping with** Humphrey–Hawkins reporting requirements.

Second, it continues to establish ranges.

Third, the ranges remain published and voted.

Fourth, monetary aggregates remain part of the information set.

Therefore do not write:

```text
1994: monetary targets disappear
```

Safer:

```text
1994: required monetary ranges remain formally alive
while their operational policy-guide role is reinterpreted downward
```

This yields a useful disappearance-boundary rule:

```text
function weakens
!=
formal obligation disappears
```

and the inverse:

```text
formal obligation survives
!=
original function survives
```

## 6. By the late 1990s, the benchmark interpretation has become durable

The 1998 Meyer material is useful as a persistence check.

The Fed still has to provide statutory ranges, but Meyer distinguishes that obligation from the actual year-ahead policy stance.

The ranges instead express a long-run monetary-growth environment associated with price stability under more normal velocity behavior.

This means the 1994 shift should not be reduced to an isolated meeting-specific wording choice.

A careful genealogy is now:

```text
1994 staff explicitly recognizes reinterpretation
        ↓
1994 FOMC collective minutes adopt long-run benchmark description
        ↓
late 1990s Fed actor explanations treat ranges as statutory reports
without treating them as annual operating-policy commitments
```

Still, this is not yet legal disappearance.

The law and report slot remain.

## 7. 1999: Congress anticipates legal sunset

The 14 January 1999 Senate Banking Committee statement is important because it gives a contemporaneous congressional view before expiration.

Chairman Gramm says the semiannual reports are scheduled to be discontinued after 1999 as a result of Public Law 104-66.

He also says he would consider renewal if committee members want the reports retained.

This creates a useful separation between two historical questions:

```text
Is the old requirement legally scheduled to expire?
YES, actor-explicit.

Does Congress therefore regard the oversight practice as valueless?
NO, at least not uniformly; renewal remains an active option.
```

Thus:

```text
statutory sunset
!=
institutional consensus that the practice should disappear
```

The general sunset statute fixes 15 May 2000 as the termination date for listed periodic reports.

## 8. June/July 2000: legal range requirement expires and the FOMC immediately stops producing ranges

The July 20 Monetary Policy Report supplies perhaps the cleanest disappearance evidence in the whole chain.

The FOMC does not set money/debt ranges for 2000 and 2001.

The report gives two distinct reasons:

1. **legal:** the requirement to establish and announce ranges has expired;
2. **epistemic/operational:** velocity instability means the ranges have not supplied useful monetary-policy benchmarks for years.

This is a rare case where legal disappearance and prior functional hollowing line up explicitly.

The chronology is therefore not:

```text
law repealed
→ then actors decide ranges are useless
```

It is closer to:

```text
1990s functional degradation / reinterpretation
→ 2000 legal requirement expires
→ actors stop reproducing the range mechanism
```

That difference matters for causal history.

The sunset supplied an opportunity to cease a mechanism whose operational role had already been deeply reduced.

But the report also says money and credit remain useful for gauging economic and financial conditions and continues to discuss them.

So:

```text
mandatory target/reporting mechanism disappears
!=
measurement object disappears
```

and:

```text
policy-guide role disappears
!=
indicator role disappears
```

## 9. July 2000: practice survives after law lapses

Five days after the report date, the House Banking Committee holds the familiar semiannual monetary-policy hearing.

The hearing is methodologically valuable because actors openly acknowledge the legal discontinuity.

Chairman Leach says Greenspan's appearance is no longer legally required but treats the semiannual review as a major oversight responsibility.

Other members explicitly thank Greenspan for continuing the Humphrey–Hawkins report despite the temporary lapse and call for statutory renewal.

Thus, for several months, the field contains:

```text
old statutory requirement: absent

report: survives voluntarily/institutionally

hearing: survives by congressional/Fed practice

“Humphrey–Hawkins” label: survives

money-growth ranges: no longer supplied
```

This is a unusually clear fixture against simple legalism:

```text
statutory expiry
!=
immediate practice disappearance
```

But it is equally a fixture against institutional-continuity shortcuts:

```text
practice continues
!=
old legal architecture continues
```

The combination may be called **observatory afterlife** for present analytical convenience.

Again, this is researcher vocabulary only.

## 10. December 2000: Congress restores the observatory without restoring the monetary-range machinery

Public Law 106-569 is the decisive identity test.

Section 1003(a) instructs that Federal Reserve Act section 2A be amended by striking **all after the first sentence**.

That means the enduring first-sentence monetary-policy objectives remain.

But the Humphrey–Hawkins reporting machinery appended after that sentence is removed.

Section 1003(b) then creates a new section 2B.

The new structure requires:

- semiannual Chair appearances before Congress;
- discussion of the Board/FOMC's monetary-policy efforts, activities, objectives and plans;
- a written report;
- attention to employment, unemployment, production, investment, real income, productivity, exchange rates, international trade/payments, and prices.

This clearly restores/stabilizes the **oversight observatory**.

But it does not recreate the old intermediate-target architecture.

Missing are the specific requirements to announce:

- current-year money/credit aggregate ranges;
- following-year ranges;
- the relation of those range plans to Presidential/Congressional short-term goals;
- later explanations for deviations/revisions from the announced range path.

So the legal transition is not well represented as:

```text
Humphrey–Hawkins expires
→ Humphrey–Hawkins restored
```

A more accurate clause-level reconstruction is:

```text
1978 section 2A reporting architecture
broad observatory
+ monetary-range intermediate target
+ cross-institution goal relation
+ revision/deviation explanation

        ↓ 15 May 2000 lapse

brief voluntary institutional continuation
without range obligation

        ↓ 27 Dec 2000

new section 2B
broad observatory
+ statutory semiannual appearance/report
- mandatory monetary-range intermediate target
- old deviation explanation architecture
```

This is **selective procedural survival**, not full restoration.

## 11. What disappeared—and what did not

### 11.1 Strong disappearance candidate

By the end of 2000, there is strong evidence for disappearance of this specific object:

> **the Humphrey–Hawkins / section-2A requirement that the Fed announce monetary/credit aggregate growth ranges as a recurring intermediate policy/accountability object and explain revisions/deviations through the statutory reporting process.**

The sequence is unusually well supported:

1. formal requirement exists;
2. operational meaning weakens;
3. actors explicitly describe it as a long-run benchmark rather than a current-policy guide;
4. legal requirement expires;
5. FOMC stops setting the ranges;
6. replacement statute restores oversight but omits the range mechanism.

That is much stronger than inferring disappearance merely because a term becomes less common.

### 11.2 What did **not** disappear

Do not generalize the disappearance claim to:

- maximum-employment / price-stability objectives;
- congressional monetary-policy oversight;
- semiannual reporting practice;
- observation of money/credit variables;
- employment/unemployment as part of the Fed reporting field;
- the Employment Act / Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act problem field as a whole.

Those objects have different survival histories.

In particular, section 2A's first sentence remains after December 2000.

New section 2B again mandates a broad economic report including employment and unemployment.

So:

```text
one accountability mechanism disappears
!=
full-employment problem disappears
```

## 12. A useful three-layer disappearance model

This episode suggests separating at least three layers when testing historical disappearance.

### Layer A — operational function

Does the device still guide current action?

In this case:

```text
1994 onward:
monetary ranges increasingly NO
```

### Layer B — formal/statutory obligation

Must the actor still produce the device?

In this case:

```text
through early 2000:
YES

from May 2000:
NO
```

### Layer C — institutional observatory / successor practice

Does the surrounding reporting/oversight institution survive?

In this case:

```text
July 2000:
YES by practice despite lapse

Dec 2000 onward:
YES again by new statutory section 2B
```

This yields a sequence that a flat `continuous/disappeared` flag would miss:

```text
functional hollowing
→ legal expiry
→ practical afterlife
→ selective statutory reconstruction
```

Possible method fixture:

```text
problem mechanism can disappear by layers
```

Do not promote this to schema until tested against unrelated cases.

## 13. Relation candidates

### 13.1 1978 Fed-reporting architecture → 1994 benchmark regime

Strong genealogy is obvious.

Candidate:

- `reformulated` at the functional/instrument level;
- not disappearance yet.

Why not `continuous` without qualification:

- the required ranges no longer carry the same current-policy-guide meaning;
- staff explicitly recognizes a departure from perceived Humphrey–Hawkins intent.

Why not `transformed_successor` yet:

- same legal requirement, same report, same range object, same institutional route remain.

### 13.2 1994 benchmark regime → May/July 2000 lapse

At the **monetary-range mechanism** level:

- strong disappearance candidate.

At the **broader monetary-policy observatory** level:

- continuity / institutional afterlife.

This is exactly why a single episode-wide relation may be misleading.

### 13.3 old section 2A reporting machinery → new section 2B

Strong legal/institutional genealogy.

Candidate description:

- `reformulated` / selective successor at the observatory level;
- non-continuity / disappearance of monetary-range accountability at the instrument layer.

Do not freeze one flat relation without deciding whether schema relations are allowed to target problem dimensions / mechanisms rather than whole episodes.

## 15. Hindsight risks

### 15.1 Do not read modern “dual mandate” shorthand backward

The 1978 Fed provisions were not merely a two-word mandate statement.

They created a specific information/reporting chain involving monetary/credit aggregate ranges and explanations of deviations.

### 15.2 Do not call the old ranges a rigid monetary rule

The 1978 statute itself allowed non-achievement under changing conditions, provided reasons for revisions/deviations were explained.

The historical change concerns the role/accountability significance of the ranges, not a transition from perfect mechanical binding to discretion.

### 15.3 Do not turn 1994 staff language into a legal ruling

`This was not the intent of the Humphrey-Hawkins Act` is an actor-proximate staff judgment about perceived intent.

It is excellent evidence that actors recognized a functional mismatch.

It is not a court judgment that the Committee was violating the statute.

### 15.4 Do not conflate legal sunset with causal policy change

The ranges had already lost much operational significance before May 2000.

The 1995 sunset law did not create velocity instability.

It changed the legal requirement at the end of a longer functional process.

### 15.5 Do not call July 2000 “no Humphrey–Hawkins” in every sense

The report/hearing tradition continued, and actors still used the inherited label.

What lapsed was the old reporting requirement; what stopped immediately was the range-setting mechanism.

### 15.6 Do not call December 2000 simple restoration

Congress deliberately or at least textually reconstituted a different statutory reporting architecture.

New section 2B restores semiannual oversight and broad economic reporting but not the old monetary-range / deviation machinery.

### 15.7 Do not infer disappearance of employment concern

The replacement report explicitly continues to include employment and unemployment.

The strong disappearance finding is mechanism-specific.

## 19. Provisional reconstruction

The strongest current reconstruction of the Federal Reserve side is:

```text
1978
Humphrey–Hawkins reporting architecture:
macro observatory
+ mandatory monetary/credit aggregate ranges
+ goal relation
+ deviation/revision explanation

        ↓

1980s–early 1990s
money-demand/velocity instability erodes operational usefulness
[needs deeper intermediate packet before fine-grained dating]

        ↓

July 1994
formal range obligation remains
but FOMC adopts long-run-benchmark interpretation;
staff explicitly recognizes mismatch with perceived HH intent

        ↓

late 1990s
benchmark / statutory-compliance function persists
without strong current-policy-guide function

        ↓

15 May 2000
old periodic reporting requirement sunsets

        ↓

June–July 2000
FOMC stops establishing ranges;
Fed says legal requirement expired and ranges had not been useful policy benchmarks for years

        ↓

July 2000
report/hearing survives by institutional practice despite legal lapse

        ↓

27 Dec 2000
Congress restores semiannual statutory monetary-policy observatory in new §2B
while omitting the old mandatory monetary-range / deviation mechanism
```

The best current Problem History judgment is therefore dimension-specific:

> **The broader employment/price-stability/monetary-policy observatory does not disappear in 2000. A particular Humphrey–Hawkins intermediate-target accountability mechanism does. Its disappearance is preceded by years of functional hollowing and followed by selective procedural reconstruction.**

No whole-episode `continuous / reformulated / transformed_successor / disappeared` relation is frozen from this packet alone.
