# 1999–2000: Humphrey–Hawkins preservation proposal to selective redesign

Status: **M2 implementation / disappearance genealogy / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet extends:

- `1994-2000-humphrey-hawkins-monetary-ranges-to-reporting-afterlife.md`;
- `2000-humphrey-hawkins-selective-reenactment-and-accountability-split.md`;
- `1999-2001-humphrey-hawkins-observatory-redesign-and-accountability-recomposition.md`.

The preceding work established that by 2000 Congress ultimately restored the semiannual monetary-policy observatory while not restoring the old money/credit growth ranges. It also recovered a June 2000 cross-institutional consensus around formal Chair testimony, but left the concrete legislative object behind the July markup unresolved.

This packet closes an important part of that gap.

The House-passed **H.R. 3046, Banking and Housing Agency Accountability Preservation Act**, was a direct preservation route: section 2 would exempt reports required by **Federal Reserve Act §2A (12 U.S.C. 225a)** from the 1995 automatic sunset. In October 1999 House actors framed the problem as an overbroad sunset threatening useful oversight reports and answered it by selectively preserving the existing statutory reporting architecture.

By July 2000, however, the Senate Banking Committee was no longer merely considering the House text. Congressional schedules identify a **committee print of a substitute amendment to H.R. 3046**, and the Senate floor authorization for the 13 July markup describes that object as **“providing for semiannual Federal reserve testimony before Congress.”** In other words, the same legislative vehicle visibly moved from:

```text
preserve old §2A reporting requirement from sunset
```

toward:

```text
replace lapsed Humphrey–Hawkins reporting provisions
with a redesigned semiannual report / testimony architecture
```

The exact July 13 committee print has not yet been recovered, so it is not safe to claim that its clauses already matched final §2B or already deleted every monetary-range provision in final form. But the change in legislative object is now directly evidenced rather than inferred only from the December statute.

Researcher-side shorthand used below—**`preservation-to-redesign shift`** and **`sunset review as a selection event`**—is analytical vocabulary, not actor language and not a schema proposal.

## 1. Source control

### 1.1 H.R. 3046, introduced 7 Oct 1999 and passed by House 19 Oct 1999

Official GPO bill record, introduced version:

https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-106hr3046ih

Official GPO House-passed / engrossed version:

https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-106hr3046eh

Official text:

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-106hr3046eh/html/BILLS-106hr3046eh.htm

Relevant location:

- §2, especially item (38): Federal Reserve Act §2A, 12 U.S.C. 225a.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct legislative proposal and House-passed text**.

The bill title itself states its purpose as preserving limited Federal agency reporting requirements “to facilitate congressional oversight and public accountability.” Section 2 says the automatic-sunset provision of the Federal Reports Elimination and Sunset Act of 1995 shall not apply to reports required under a list of provisions. The list includes:

- Employment Act of 1946 §3, 15 U.S.C. 1022;
- Federal Reserve Act §2A, 12 U.S.C. 225a;
- other banking, housing, consumer, and agency reporting provisions.

The House-passed bill does **not** rewrite §2A’s monetary-policy reporting architecture. Its operative move is exemption from sunset.

Therefore the strongest legal description of the 1999 route is:

```text
existing §2A reporting architecture
        +
automatic sunset threat
        ↓
exempt §2A report from sunset
        ↓
preserve inherited architecture
```

Boundary:

`the House voted to preserve the §2A reporting requirement`

is stronger than:

`every House actor affirmatively endorsed every subcomponent of §2A, including monetary-growth ranges as a useful policy guide`.

The latter is not established by the bill alone.

### 1.2 House floor debate, 19 Oct 1999

Official Congressional Record:

https://www.congress.gov/106/crec/1999/10/19/145/142/CREC-1999-10-19-house.pdf

Relevant pages: H10233–H10235 (PDF pages around 52–53 in the full-day file).

Evidence strength: **very high / direct contemporaneous House actor formulations**.

#### Representative Sue Kelly

Kelly describes H.R. 3046 as a bipartisan attempt to exempt useful reports from the impending sunset. She identifies the semiannual Humphrey–Hawkins reports as the best-known example and says congressional review of Federal Reserve monetary policy is an ongoing oversight obligation.

Her formulation is particularly useful because she simultaneously endorses report pruning in principle and criticizes the 1995 law’s implementation as too blunt: she says the sunset process used a “meat axe” where a “scalpel” would have been more appropriate.

She explains that Chairman Leach directed committee staff to review the 1993 Clerk’s report, that staff initially identified roughly 270 reports connected to Banking Committee jurisdiction, and that agencies were contacted for input. Many reports, she says, should sunset; the bill preserves a smaller group regarded as worth retaining, including the Fed’s semiannual Humphrey–Hawkins report.

Actor problem structure:

```text
there are genuinely obsolete / wasteful reports
        +
automatic sunset is too indiscriminate
        +
some reports are necessary for oversight
        ↓
review the list
        ↓
preserve selected existing requirements
```

This is not an anti-sunset position in principle. It is a **selective-preservation** formulation.

#### Representative Paul Kanjorski

Kanjorski describes the bill as reauthorizing a limited set of reports still pertinent to current conditions and names the President’s Economic Report, the CEA annual report, and the Fed’s semiannual Humphrey–Hawkins report among the most important.

He also says the Federal Reserve had assured Congress that, even if H.R. 3046 did not become law before the deadline, it would continue to treat the Humphrey–Hawkins requirements as if they remained law. His reason for valuing the reports is congressional oversight and the information needed for Congress to act.

Use:

This is actor evidence that **practice-after-sunset** was already contemplated as a bridge while Congress tried to preserve the statutory requirement.

Boundary:

It is evidence about the Fed’s communicated intention as reported by Kanjorski; it is not a substitute for recovering the underlying Fed correspondence.

### 1.7 Final statute, Public Law 106-569, 27 Dec 2000

Official statute:

https://www.congress.gov/106/plaws/publ569/PLAW-106publ569.pdf

Relevant: §1003, 114 Stat. 3028–3029.

Evidence strength: **very high / enacted primary source**.

As established in preceding packets, the law:

- leaves the first sentence of Federal Reserve Act §2A;
- strikes the remainder of §2A;
- creates new §2B;
- statutorily requires semiannual Chair appearances and written Monetary Policy Reports;
- preserves broad macroeconomic reporting variables;
- does not restore mandatory money / credit aggregate growth ranges or the old range-deviation explanation machinery.

The fresh contribution of this packet is that this final selective reconstruction now has a visible **1999 preservation predecessor** and a **July 2000 substitute-amendment transition object**.

### 1.8 Later research control: Edward Nelson, 2026

Edward Nelson, “The Practice of U.S. Monetary Policy Independence from Martin to Greenspan,” *Finance and Economics Discussion Series* 2026-014, Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, 23 Feb 2026.

https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026014pap.pdf

Relevant: pp. 47–48 and note 51.

Use: **later high-quality institutional scholarship / not historical actor evidence**.

Nelson makes two useful legal-historical corrections:

1. the monetary-growth-range requirement ceased because the Humphrey–Hawkins reporting clause had already lapsed earlier in 2000; the December 2000 statute should not be described as the act that newly “repealed” the range requirement;
2. the December amendment made the Chair’s MPR-associated congressional testimony a statutory requirement for the first time; the 1979–2000 testimony practice had been conventional rather than expressly compelled by the 1978 act.

This later reconstruction supports careful legal wording in this packet, but it does not define what 1999–2000 actors thought the problem was.

## 2. Fresh finding A: the 1999 House answer was preservation, not redesign

The House-passed H.R. 3046 makes the pre-sunset answer unusually clear.

Its operative relation to Federal Reserve Act §2A is not:

```text
rewrite
narrow
replace
modernize
```

but:

```text
sunset provision shall not apply
```

That is a preservation architecture.

The House debate confirms the same orientation. Actors say the 1995 sunset mechanism is too indiscriminate, review the Clerk’s list, solicit agency input, identify reports that should die, and then exempt a subset considered useful.

So the actor-recognized problem can be reconstructed with high confidence as:

> **How can Congress prevent an automatic, overbroad sunset mechanism from eliminating reporting requirements still needed for banking/housing oversight and public accountability?**

Formulation type: `reconstructed_actor`.

Support:

- H.R. 3046 title and operative text;
- Kelly’s report-review / meat-axe-versus-scalpel explanation;
- Kanjorski’s oversight rationale;
- bipartisan sponsorship and House passage.

This should not be rewritten as a 1999 debate over whether monetary-growth ranges were good economics. The recovered House materials do not establish that as the problem being asked.

## 3. Fresh finding B: by July 2000 the same legislative vehicle had become a redesign object

The strongest new point is not simply that a new bill later appeared.

The official July schedule says the Senate Banking Committee was considering:

```text
committee print
of a substitute amendment
TO H.R. 3046
```

and the July 13 Senate record characterizes the substitute as:

```text
providing for semiannual Federal reserve testimony before Congress
```

The July 5 committee announcement describes its purpose as **replacing the lapsed Humphrey–Hawkins reporting provisions**.

This establishes a real transformation in the legislative answer space:

```text
1999 answer:
keep the inherited reporting law alive

        ↓ sunset actually occurs + practice continues + negotiations proceed

mid-2000 answer under consideration:
construct a replacement reporting / testimony arrangement
```

This is stronger than inferring redesign from the final statute alone.

It also means H.R. 3046 is historically interesting even though the House-passed version was not enacted in its original form. A failed/stalled proposal can still be a direct predecessor in problem genealogy.

## 4. Fresh finding C: `renew Humphrey–Hawkins` had become semantically underdetermined

By July 2000 several actors can use continuity language around the same institution while referring to materially different legislative routes.

House actors still emphasize renewal / continuation of the report and H.R. 3046’s broad report-preservation purpose.

The Senate Banking Committee, meanwhile, describes a substitute amendment to H.R. 3046 that will provide semiannual Fed testimony and replace lapsed provisions.

By December, Gramm explicitly justifies retaining reporting while dropping money-supply indicators he regards as outdated.

Therefore:

```text
“renew the Humphrey–Hawkins report”
!=
restore the old §2A mechanism clause-for-clause
```

The same institutional label can bridge:

```text
preservation
→ replacement
→ selective reconstruction
```

without actors necessarily treating that lexical continuity as a contradiction.

This is a high-value identity fixture because lexical / institutional continuity can conceal mechanism-level discontinuity.

## 5. Fresh finding D: sunset functioned as a forced review gate, not a substantive verdict by itself

The 1999 House debate is unusually useful for disappearance methodology.

Kelly explicitly supports eliminating outdated or wasteful reporting requirements while criticizing indiscriminate sunset. Committee staff reviewed hundreds of candidate reports, eliminated nonperiodic / already-expired / defunct cases, contacted agencies, and separated reports to die from reports to preserve.

That means:

```text
statutory sunset mechanism
!=
actor judgment that every sunset object is obsolete
```

The sunset created a **review obligation / selection event**.

For Federal Reserve reporting, that review process did not yield a single answer over time:

```text
1999:
preserve inherited §2A

2000:
replace / redesign reporting and testimony architecture
```

So the institutional event is better understood as opening an answer-space review than automatically declaring the old problem or old instrument dead.

Researcher shorthand:

> `sunset review as a selection event`

Do not promote this to a general method rule until tested outside this episode.

## 7. Identity / relation judgment

### 7.1 Is October 1999 H.R. 3046 the same problem as the July 2000 substitute?

Evidence for continuity:

- same legislative vehicle;
- same broad object: Fed monetary-policy reporting / congressional oversight;
- same committees and overlapping House/Senate actors;
- continuity rhetoric: renew, replace lapsed provisions, preserve oversight;
- practice continues during legal lapse.

Evidence for discontinuity / reformulation:

- House 1999 solution preserves existing §2A wholesale from sunset;
- July 2000 Senate process uses a substitute amendment;
- Senate committee language centers semiannual Chair reporting/testimony rather than simple exemption;
- final settlement does not restore the old monetary-range architecture and makes testimony expressly statutory.

Provisional verdict:

```yaml
relation: reformulated
confidence: medium
freeze: false
```

Reason not to freeze:

The exact July 13 committee-print text is still missing. We can prove that the legislative answer object changed, but cannot yet measure the full clause-level distance at that intermediate node.

### 7.2 Why not `continuous`?

Because continuity of oversight purpose does not erase the shift from preservation to substitute redesign.

### 7.3 Why not immediately `transformed_successor`?

Because the broad object, responsibility relation, institutional observatory, and many actors remain strongly continuous. The strongest discontinuity so far is mechanism-level, not evidence that the entire oversight problem has been replaced by an unrelated problem.

## 10. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1 — treating H.R. 3046 preservation as an endorsement of monetarism

The House voted to keep §2A reporting from sunset. That does not prove members separately evaluated and endorsed the policy usefulness of monetary-growth ranges.

### Risk 2 — treating legal sunset as substantive obsolescence

House actors explicitly distinguish useful from obsolete reports and treat Humphrey–Hawkins reporting as useful. Sunset is a statutory event, not itself a historical-actor verdict on the object’s value.

### Risk 3 — reading the final §2B backward into the July committee print

The July object is demonstrably a substitute centered on semiannual testimony, but the exact text remains missing.

### Risk 4 — assuming July 13 markup approval from scheduling evidence

The markup was scheduled. The Committee’s public action release does not list H.R. 3046 among approved items. Do not silently turn agenda into vote outcome.

### Risk 5 — assigning authorship to the Federal Reserve

June 30 consensus includes the Fed Board; Greenspan anticipated the architecture on June 28. Neither proves the Fed drafted the substitute amendment.

### Risk 6 — overclaiming LaFalce’s bill identification

H.R. 3046 is the high-confidence match for his “House-passed bill,” but the quoted sentence does not name it.

### Risk 7 — saying PL 106-569 “repealed” the range requirement

The range/report requirement had already lapsed earlier in 2000. The stronger description is that the December reconstruction **did not restore** that component.
