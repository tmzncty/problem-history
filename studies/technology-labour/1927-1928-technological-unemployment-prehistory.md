# 1927–1928: before the federal technological-unemployment inquiry

> **Status:** source packet / prehistory supplement to `1928-1937-technological-unemployment.md`.
>
> **Purpose:** move the recoverable chronology one step earlier without claiming a single birth date for the problem or the phrase.
>
> **Boundary:** `technology / labour` remains a present researcher retrieval umbrella. This note distinguishes worker displacement, a newly isolated technological-unemployment causal category, and later federal institutionalization.

## Executive finding

The existing Episode B packet correctly establishes that a substantial U.S. institutional debate over technological unemployment predates the October 1929 crash. New evidence pushes the recoverable actor-side sequence earlier than Senate Resolution 219 (3 May 1928).

Two nodes matter:

1. **James J. Davis, September 1927:** the U.S. Secretary of Labor publicly formulates "the problem of the worker displaced by machinery." His object is the worker caught in rapid mechanization and occupational displacement; the formulation does not require the later stable label `technological unemployment`.
2. **Sumner H. Slichter, February 1928:** in "The Price of Industrial Progress," Slichter explicitly separates cyclical from technological causes, calls unemployment created by technical progress a "new kind of unemployment problem," and argues that if business-cycle control improves, technological unemployment becomes a permanent policy problem.

The strongest current chronology is therefore:

```text
1927 Davis
worker displaced by machinery
+ rapid mechanization
+ adjustment problem
        ↓ not proven lexical identity
Feb. 1928 Slichter
technical progress isolated as a causal unemployment category
+ "technological" unemployment
+ permanent-policy framing
        ↓ direct public circulation
21 Feb. 1928 Congressional Record
Slichter article inserted into Senate proceedings
        ↓
May 1928 S. Res. 219
        ↓
Dec. 1928–Feb. 1929 Senate unemployment hearings
        ↓
1929–1937 institutional research / measurement / policy field
```

This is **not** a claim that Davis caused Slichter, Slichter caused S. Res. 219, or that 1927/1928 is the absolute origin. Those transmission edges remain undetermined.

## 1. James J. Davis, 1927: displacement before the stable label

### Source identity

James J. Davis, "The Problem of the Worker Displaced by Machinery," *Monthly Labor Review* 25, no. 3 (September 1927), 32–37.

Independent bibliographic confirmation:
- Cambridge bibliography records Davis 1927, title, journal, volume/issue and pp. 32–34/37.
- A later public reproduction identifies the text as a speech by the U.S. Secretary of Labor published in the September 1927 *Monthly Labor Review*.

### Actor-side formulation

Davis opens from rapid gains in mechanical productivity and asks what machinery is doing **for** and **to** workers. His examples include automatic glass production replacing skilled glass blowers. The problem object is therefore not an abstract future "end of work." It is visible occupational displacement during rapid mechanization.

Safe researcher paraphrase:

> When mechanization sharply reduces the labor and skill required for particular processes, what happens to the workers displaced during the transition, and how should society assist adjustment without rejecting technical progress?

This is a reconstruction, not Davis's exact wording.

### Evidence strength

- **High:** article identity, date, author/office, title, journal.
- **Medium-high:** content recovered through later reproduction; before frozen quotation, bind exact wording to the original *Monthly Labor Review* page image.

### Method significance

Davis is a clean warning against lexical birth stories:

```text
problem is actor-explicit
before
later stable category label is necessarily fixed
```

A search for the phrase `technological unemployment` alone would undercount this episode.

## 2. Slichter, February 1928: causal isolation and permanent-policy framing

### Source identity

Sumner H. Slichter, "The Price of Industrial Progress," *The New Republic* 53, no. 688 (8 February 1928), 316–18.

A contemporaneous reproduction appears in the *Congressional Record*, Senate, 21 February 1928, p. 3313 ff. The Congress.gov searchable scan preserves the relevant passage.

### Actor-explicit claims

Slichter distinguishes two principal causes of current unemployment: cyclical and technological. He argues that the present situation is not primarily cyclical and describes a "new kind of unemployment problem" created by technical progress.

His historical claim is especially useful: the phenomenon had existed before, but cyclical fluctuations had obscured it. If business-cycle stabilization improved, he argued, technological unemployment would remain and therefore require a permanent policy.

He then reformulates the question as one of **absorption / effective demand**: why is community demand not large enough to create jobs for workers displaced by technical progress? His answer space considers wage/price adjustment and monetary expansion rather than opposition to machinery.

### Problem structure

| field | Slichter 1928 |
|---|---|
| object | unemployment attributable to technical progress |
| contrast class | cyclical unemployment |
| novelty claim | newly visible / newly isolated problem, not a newly existing phenomenon |
| mechanism | labor displacement plus insufficient demand to absorb displaced workers |
| stakes | persistent unemployment even under improved cycle stabilization |
| answer space | permanent policy; demand/monetary and price-wage mechanisms |
| rejected shortcut | ordinary business revival alone is sufficient |

### Evidence strength

- **High:** date, article identity, congressional reproduction, actor-explicit distinction and permanent-policy claim.
- **Medium-high:** exact New Republic pagination/title from later scholarship; original magazine container should still be archived if convenient.

## 3. Why this changes Episode B

The current Episode B begins institutionally with S. Res. 219 (May 1928) and correctly rejects a Depression-created problem story. This supplement shows a prior transformation already underway:

```text
occupational displacement by machinery
        ↓
technical progress isolated as
a distinct causal unemployment category
        ↓
permanent policy problem
        ↓
federal investigation / classification
```

The transition is substantive. Davis's formulation centers the **displaced worker and occupation**. Slichter centers the **causal decomposition of unemployment and economy-wide absorption**. The later Senate investigation contains both individual-transition and aggregate-policy concerns.

Therefore the safe relation is not `same problem`, but:

```text
shared problem field
+ increasingly explicit causal classification
+ changing answer space
```

## 4. Historical actor / later reconstruction / present researcher

### Historical actors

- Davis: rapid mechanization visibly displaces workers and skills; adjustment is a public problem.
- Slichter: technical progress should be analytically separated from cyclical causes; technological unemployment may persist and needs permanent policy.

### Later reconstruction

Recent histories of technological unemployment often describe 1928 as a terminological turning point and connect Slichter to the emergence of the phrase. This is useful for source mapping, but it does not prove an absolute first use.

High-quality later controls:
- Ioannis Katselidis, Angelos Vouldis, Panayotis G. Michaelides, "Sumner Slichter and Emil Lederer on technological unemployment," *International Journal of Social Economics* 38.6 (2011): 537–556.
- Emy Kim and Mark Solovey, "'The Machine Takes Our Jobs Away': The problem of technological unemployment in the work of Chicago sociologist William F. Ogburn," *Journal of the History of the Behavioral Sciences* 59.4 (2023): 363–379.

## 5. Hindsight risks

### Phrase-first false emergence

Do not infer:

```text
first recovered phrase "technological unemployment"
=
first historical problem
```

Davis 1927 already gives an explicit machinery-displacement problem without requiring that label.

### Later-category backfill

Do not rewrite every earlier machinery controversy as `technological unemployment`. The later category isolates unemployment causation in a way that earlier Machinery Question formulations need not share.

### Novelty-claim literalism

Slichter can call this a "new kind" of unemployment problem while also saying the phenomenon had always existed. The historian must preserve both levels:

```text
new visibility / causal isolation / policy category
!=
new underlying phenomenon
```

### Institutional-causation overreach

Chronological proximity among Slichter's February article, its Senate circulation, and S. Res. 219 in May does not by itself prove a direct drafting or causal chain.
