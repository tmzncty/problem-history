# 1946–78: full-employment revival, structural redefinition, and the Humphrey–Hawkins identity test

Status: **M2 forward source packet / revival-vs-reformulation test / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet continues:

- `1945-1946-s380-to-employment-act-reformulation.md`;
- `1945-s380-interagency-problem-decomposition.md`;
- and the postwar `full-employment → manpower` bridge already developed elsewhere in this pilot.

The narrow question is not whether Humphrey–Hawkins was politically descended from the Employment Act of 1946. That genealogy is unusually strong and actor-explicit.

The narrower Problem History question is:

> When actors in the 1970s explicitly set out to **strengthen, supplement, fulfill, or make more specific** the Employment Act of 1946, did they reopen the 1945 full-employment problem, continue the 1946 settlement, or construct a substantially different problem under inherited statutory machinery?

The evidence supports a mixed answer that should not yet be collapsed into one schema edge.

The 1978 Act deliberately reactivated several things that had been weakened or deleted in the 1946 compromise:

- the word **full** employment;
- a national goal framed as fulfillment of a **right to full opportunities for useful paid employment**;
- explicit numerical unemployment goals and timetables;
- an expectation that the President, Congress, and Federal Reserve expose their policy plans against those goals.

But the governed problem had also changed substantially. By 1978 full employment was legislatively coupled to:

- inflation and price stability;
- productivity growth;
- balanced growth and the Federal budget;
- international trade and competitiveness;
- structural unemployment by region and labor-force group;
- racial and other forms of discrimination;
- youth employment;
- training, counseling, placement, and targeted employment measures;
- explicit Federal Reserve reporting and coordination.

And the strongest earlier employer-of-last-resort version of the 1970s proposal had itself been weakened before enactment.

A provisional researcher-side description is therefore:

**statutory revival of full-employment language and explicit goal machinery inside a transformed unemployment–inflation–structural-policy problem field.**

That phrase is analytical. It is not actor vocabulary and it is not a frozen relation verdict.

---

## 1. Why this is a high-value identity test

The repo already has a documented 1945–46 transformation:

```text
Jan 1945 S.380
right / entitlement language
+ Federal duty to assure sufficient opportunities
+ anticipatory full-employment aggregate calculation
+ residual Federal investment / expenditure closure rule

        ↓ conference transformation

Feb 1946 Employment Act
continuing Federal policy and responsibility
+ maximum employment / production / purchasing power
+ Economic Report + CEA observatory
- no statutory “full employment” assurance
- no explicit aggregate-gap closure rule
```

The 1978 statute then says, in its own text, that one of its purposes is to **“strengthen and supplement”** the purposes and policies of the Employment Act of 1946.

That creates direct genealogy rather than a researcher-imposed lineage.

But direct genealogy does not decide identity.

The difficult relation is:

```text
1945 stronger full-employment formulation
        ↓
1946 negotiated observatory / responsibility settlement
        ↓
1970s actors explicitly complain that 1946 is insufficient
        ↓
1978 reintroduces “full”, a right-goal, numerical targets,
structural employment programs, anti-inflation policy,
and Federal Reserve reporting
```

Possible readings include:

- `revived` — a suppressed full-employment commitment is deliberately reopened;
- `reformulated` — the 1946 settlement is made more explicit under new causal assumptions and instruments;
- `transformed_successor` — the 1970s unemployment/inflation/structural-disadvantage problem is too different to call the same problem;
- a compound relation for which one schema edge is too lossy.

Do not choose among these by vocabulary alone.

## 3. The enacted 1978 Act is an explicit repair / extension of 1946, not merely a later resemblance

The strongest genealogy evidence is in the statute itself.

Section 101 states that one purpose of Title I is to:

> strengthen and supplement the purposes and policies of the Employment Act of 1946.

The long title likewise describes a federal responsibility to use practicable programs and policies to promote full employment, production, real income, balanced growth, productivity, national priorities, and reasonable price stability, and requires explicit short- and medium-term goals and better coordination of economic policymaking.

This gives a high-confidence relation at the **statutory genealogy** level:

```text
Employment Act of 1946
        ↓ explicit amendment / supplementation
Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1978
```

But that is not yet a Problem Episode identity relation.

Research rule:

```text
explicit statutory amendment
!=
problem continuity
```

## 4. A lexical / normative revival is real: “full” and the employment-right goal return

The 1946 conference settlement had removed the Senate formulation’s stronger `full employment` assurance vocabulary.

In 1978 Congress explicitly amended the declaration of policy to include promotion of **full employment**, and Section 102(b) established as a national goal fulfillment of the right to full opportunities for useful paid employment at fair compensation for those able, willing, and seeking to work.

This is not merely a historian noticing similar words.

The legislature itself intentionally inserted them into the inherited 1946 statutory framework.

There is therefore strong evidence for **lexical and normative reactivation**.

But compare the mechanisms.

### 4.1 1945 S.380

The earlier S.380 logic was primarily aggregate and sequential:

```text
forecast labor force
→ estimate full-employment production / expenditure requirement
→ estimate private + State/local + Federal expenditure
→ identify aggregate deficiency
→ Federal investment / expenditure fills the residual requirement
```

Its “right” did not mean an individual could simply demand assignment to a government job.

### 4.2 1978 enacted Act

The 1978 statute instead combines:

```text
right-goal
+ explicit unemployment targets
+ yearly short/medium-term goals
+ general macro policy
+ structural employment policy
+ training / counseling / placement
+ conditional employment-project reservoirs
+ inflation policy
+ Federal Reserve reporting
```

So:

```text
revived right vocabulary
!= revived 1945 closure mechanism
```

This is a particularly useful identity fixture because lexical revival and mechanism discontinuity occur together.

## 5. The governed object is no longer just an aggregate employment gap

Public Law 95-523 begins with a multi-problem diagnosis.

Congress finds prolonged:

- unemployment **and underemployment**;
- idle productive resources;
- high inflation;
- inadequate productivity growth.

It further says high unemployment itself may contribute to inflation through skill loss, underused capital, productivity effects, unit labor costs, and supply effects.

It then states that aggregate fiscal and monetary policies alone have been unable to achieve the combined objectives and must be supplemented by other measures.

This is historically important.

The problem is no longer represented simply as:

```text
insufficient aggregate expenditure
→ insufficient total employment
```

The enacted representation is closer to:

```text
aggregate demand / monetary-fiscal policy
        +
structural unemployment
        +
skill / training barriers
        +
regional disadvantage
        +
discrimination
        +
productivity / bottlenecks
        +
inflation
        +
institutional policy coordination
        ↓
full-employment-and-balanced-growth problem field
```

This does not mean every actor accepted this causal picture. Section 9 below shows direct contemporaneous disagreement.

## 6. Structural unemployment is not a side note: the statute explicitly differentiates groups, regions, and barriers

The 1978 Act says effective policy should include measures designed specifically to reduce:

- cyclical unemployment;
- structural unemployment in regions;
- structural unemployment among particular labor-force groups.

It also links increased job opportunity and full employment to reducing discrimination based on sex, age, race, color, religion, national origin, handicap, and other improper factors.

The National Employment Conference provision singles out:

- structural unemployment;
- disadvantaged youth;
- incentives for private employers to hire the hard-core unemployed.

Title II then develops regional / structural employment policies and youth employment policies.

This is a major problem-structure change relative to treating employment only as a single national aggregate.

### Actor-level confirmation: Secretary of Labor Ray Marshall

In the May 1978 Senate hearings Marshall describes the route to the unemployment target as coordination of monetary and fiscal policy with **structural policies** aimed at barriers facing specific groups, explicitly naming inadequate education/training and discrimination.

He presents coordination in two senses:

1. coordination among macroeconomic actors;
2. coordination between macroeconomic and structural policies.

That is direct actor evidence for the 1978 problem formulation.

It is not a later researcher importing “structural policy” into the record.

Research rule:

```text
same national unemployment rate
!= same employment problem
```

if the relevant actors now treat group-specific barriers and distributive composition as causally indispensable.

## 7. Inflation is inserted into the problem, not merely added as an unrelated goal

This is one of the clearest transformations.

### 7.1 Proxmire: simultaneous employment and price-stability targets

Opening the May 8 Senate hearings, Chairman William Proxmire says 32 years of experience with the 1946 Act make it time to state economic goals more specifically and coordinate policy more consistently.

He then argues that Congress should make explicit that full employment and price stability can and should be pursued together, proposing a numerical inflation goal alongside unemployment targets.

### 7.2 Muriel Humphrey: 1946 is insufficiently specific and silent on inflation

Senator Muriel Humphrey’s prepared testimony gives an explicit relation judgment:

- Humphrey–Hawkins **amends** the Employment Act of 1946;
- makes its employment / production commitment more specific;
- builds on 30 years of experience to articulate a **new strategy**;
- recognizes inflation as a major national problem and gives reasonable price stability high statutory priority;
- notes that the 1946 Act had been silent on inflation as such.

This is a valuable actor formula because it simultaneously asserts continuity and novelty.

In shorthand:

```text
inherited commitment
+ explicit claim of amendment
+ “new strategy”
+ new co-problem (inflation)
```

That is exactly the kind of evidence that should resist a binary continuous/broken verdict.

### 7.3 Final statute

The enacted law establishes interim numerical goals for both:

- unemployment: no more than 3% for persons aged 20+ and 4% for persons aged 16+ within the statutory timetable;
- inflation: no more than 3% within the corresponding timetable.

The statute also says anti-inflation policy should be designed not to impede achievement of the unemployment timetable.

After the interim inflation target, the statute sets a further goal of zero inflation by 1988, subject to the same proviso protecting unemployment goals.

The presence of two quantified targets changes the observatory itself.

Policy is now evaluated against a vector rather than a single aggregate employment deficiency.

## 8. The Federal Reserve becomes an explicit part of the observatory and responsibility architecture

Section 108 amends the Federal Reserve Act.

The Board of Governors must report to Congress twice yearly on:

- recent economic developments;
- planned ranges for monetary and credit aggregates;
- employment / unemployment;
- production;
- investment;
- real income;
- productivity;
- trade and payments;
- prices;
- and the relation between monetary plans and the President’s short-term goals.

The Board retains room to revise or deviate when conditions change, but must explain deviations.

This is not present in the 1945 S.380 problem architecture in the same institutional form.

The 1978 problem therefore includes a distinct **cross-institution coordination / accountability problem**:

```text
President’s goals
↔ Congress
↔ Federal Reserve plans
```

This is one reason not to describe 1978 simply as restoration of a 1945 fiscal closure rule.

## 9. Contemporaneous actors did not agree on the causal model

This is one of the highest-value methodological results of the packet.

### 9.1 Proponent / Administration formulation: coordination + structural measures

Secretary Marshall argues that achieving the unemployment goal requires coordinated monetary/fiscal measures plus structural policies targeted at group-specific barriers.

CEA chairman Schultze likewise treats the simultaneous reduction of unemployment and inflation as difficult but feasible enough to pursue, especially with structural measures.

### 9.2 Senator Garn: the bill identifies the wrong cause

Senator Jake Garn’s opposition statement accepts unemployment reduction, balanced growth, and inflation control as desirable goals but attacks the problem diagnosis.

He says Humphrey–Hawkins assumes that a major cause of economic problems, especially unemployment, is inadequate federal economic-policy planning and coordination.

Garn’s competing causal account is almost the inverse:

```text
Federal intervention / deficit policy / capital drain / market distortion
→ unemployment and economic weakness
```

rather than:

```text
insufficient coordination / structural correction
→ persistent unemployment
```

This is direct actor disagreement over the **causal object**, not merely partisan disagreement over a preferred instrument.

For Problem History:

```text
same stated goal
!= same problem
```

if the actors disagree over what produces the unwanted condition.

### 9.3 Federal Reserve governor Partee: earlier bill and later bill are materially different

Governor J. Charles Partee, speaking for the Federal Reserve Board, says the 1978 versions contain substantial improvements over the earlier bill on which he testified in 1976.

He particularly welcomes:

- greater emphasis on inflation;
- removal of provisions that would have required the Federal Government to become employer of last resort;
- revised wage / program design for special employment measures.

This is crucial relation evidence **inside the same Humphrey–Hawkins campaign**.

The campaign cannot be treated as one stable Problem Episode from 1974/75 through October 1978.

## 10. The final employment-project “reservoir” is conditional, not a simple employer-of-last-resort guarantee

Public Law 95-523 still retains a section titled:

`Job Training, Counseling and Reservoirs of Employment Projects`.

But the enacted architecture matters.

The Secretary of Labor is first to use existing authority for:

- counseling and training;
- placement into private and public jobs;
- supportive services;
- flexible / part-time options where appropriate.

Only when people cannot, in the President’s judgment, be provided private jobs or jobs under existing programs may the President establish public / nonprofit employment-project reservoirs **as may be authorized by law**.

New programs cannot begin earlier than two years after enactment and require a Presidential finding to Congress that other means are not yielding enough jobs to meet the goals.

They are also targeted toward high-unemployment areas and structurally unemployed people and designed not to draw workers out of private employment.

Thus:

```text
reservoir language survives
!= automatic individual federal job guarantee
!= 1975/76 employer-of-last-resort architecture survives intact
```

Partee’s testimony is consistent with this distinction: the Board understood the strongest employer-of-last-resort feature to have been deleted even though a conditional reservoir mechanism remained.

This is a useful warning against counting section names rather than reading trigger conditions and authorization structure.

## 11. Contemporaries explicitly disagreed over whether Humphrey–Hawkins actually repaired 1946

### 11.1 A pro-bill House formulation: 1946 lacks mechanisms

During the March 16, 1978 House debate, a supporter argues that the Employment Act of 1946 does not adequately address contemporary employment problems because it lacks mechanisms by which the Federal Government can guarantee job opportunities.

The speaker characterizes Humphrey–Hawkins as strengthening 1946 through:

- a meaningful-job commitment;
- an interim 4% unemployment target;
- annual Presidential reporting of policies and programs to reach the goal.

This is direct contemporaneous **repair language**.

Source-control debt: the indexed segment recovers the passage but the speaker heading lies outside the recovered excerpt; identify and page-bind the speaker before using the passage as a named quotation in a frozen episode.

### 11.2 Rep. Stewart McKinney: it is only another statement of failed goals

Five days earlier, Rep. Stewart B. McKinney offers almost the opposite identity judgment.

He says the wording resembles the Employment Act of 1946 and argues that neither the earlier Act nor the 1978 proposal itself creates the specific programs needed to produce jobs.

For him, the resemblance to 1946 is evidence **against** efficacy: restating an old goal does not solve unemployment.

This yields a strong identity fixture:

```text
same predecessor relation

proponent:
1946 goal + new mechanisms / targets = meaningful strengthening

opponent:
1946-like language + no adequate direct program = empty repetition
```

The identity and significance of continuity are themselves contested by historical actors.

## 12. The 1970s “right to employment” is also a civil-rights / distribution problem

The enacted Act states that increased job opportunity and full employment would contribute to eliminating discrimination by sex, age, race, color, religion, national origin, handicap, and other improper factors.

Its structural sections treat disadvantaged youth and group-specific labor-market barriers as policy objects.

This matters for relation testing against 1945.

A researcher could easily see the recurring phrase:

`able, willing, and seeking to work`

and infer continuity.

But in the 1970s, actors are using full-employment policy inside a field transformed by:

- civil-rights law;
- persistent racial unemployment gaps;
- urban / regional structural unemployment;
- youth unemployment;
- the policy category of disadvantaged workers;
- manpower / training institutions built since the early 1960s.

The same lexical right is therefore attached to a different distributive map of unemployment.

A future frozen episode should explicitly compare whether `who counts as the harmed subject?` has changed between 1945 and the 1970s.

## 13. The anti-inflation transformation is not a researcher invention

The final Act contains an entire `Overcoming Inflation` section.

Congress finds that sole dependence on fiscal or monetary policy to fight inflation can worsen both inflation and unemployment and calls for coordinated macro policy plus targeted structural anti-inflation measures.

The enumerated answer space includes:

- monitoring sectoral inflation and bottlenecks;
- alleviating shortages of goods, services, labor, and capital;
- commodity / material stockpiles;
- productivity cooperation between labor and management;
- competition / antitrust / small-business measures;
- modifying inflationary regulation;
- exports / international competitiveness.

So inflation is not simply a constraint saying “do not spend too much.”

It becomes a governed field with its own structural diagnoses and interventions.

Modern scholarship by Goutsmedt is useful here as historiographic control: it reconstructs how Phillips-curve expertise and political pressure contributed to inserting an explicit numerical inflation target and making inflation/unemployment balance central to the final law.

But the packet does not need that modern literature to prove that inflation became part of the actor-level problem: the 1978 statute and hearings show that directly.

## 14. Pairwise identity table

| Dimension | 1945 S.380 | 1946 enacted settlement | 1978 enacted Humphrey–Hawkins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| normative target | continuing full employment / sufficient opportunities | maximum employment, production, purchasing power | full employment + balanced growth + price stability + other goals |
| right language | explicit employment right / entitlement language | stripped / weakened | national goal of fulfilling right to full opportunities for useful paid work |
| measurement | full-employment labor force / GNP / aggregate expenditure requirement | Economic Report observatory, no explicit statutory closure calculation | short- and medium-term numerical goals for unemployment, inflation, production, income, productivity, etc. |
| causal center | prospective aggregate expenditure deficiency | broad conditions under which employment / production / purchasing power are promoted | aggregate + structural unemployment + discrimination + skills + productivity + inflation + coordination |
| Federal residual duty | explicit residual investment / expenditure | no explicit closure rule | practicable means; conditional structural / employment reservoirs; no automatic aggregate fiscal closure |
| individual public job guarantee | no simple individual assignment guarantee | no | strongest earlier employer-of-last-resort form deleted; conditional reservoirs remain |
| Federal Reserve | not the same explicit reporting relation | not integrated into the Act’s observatory in 1946 form | statutory reporting / coordination against economic goals |
| inflation | not coequal statutory target | not explicit in 1946 policy declaration | explicit major problem + numerical target + structural anti-inflation policy |
| distributive / structural groups | much less differentiated in the bill’s aggregate architecture | limited in the statute | regions, structural unemployment, youth, disadvantaged groups, discrimination explicitly governed |
| observatory | National Production and Employment Budget | Economic Report + CEA | expanded Economic Report + numerical goals + Fed reporting + congressional review |

This table strongly argues against `same words = same problem`.

It also argues against the opposite error, `different context = no continuity`: the amendment relation, inherited observatory, statutory language, and actor claims of repair are too strong to call the cases unrelated.

## 15. Candidate relation judgments — deliberately not frozen

### 15.1 1946 Employment Act → 1978 Act

Continuity evidence:

- explicit amendment and `strengthen and supplement` language;
- retained continuing Federal policy / responsibility architecture;
- retained Economic Report / CEA machinery;
- explicit actor statements that 1978 makes 1946 commitments more specific;
- many 1978 mechanisms are designed as expansions of the 1946 framework.

Discontinuity evidence:

- reactivation of `full` and right-goal language;
- numerical unemployment targets;
- inflation becomes a major explicitly quantified problem;
- structural unemployment and group / regional barriers enter the core causal model;
- Federal Reserve reporting becomes part of the institutional observatory;
- structural employment and anti-inflation programs alter the answer space.

Provisional relation:

**`reformulated` or compound `reformulated + partial revival` candidate.**

Do not freeze yet.

### 15.2 1945 S.380 → 1978 Act

Continuity / revival evidence:

- full-employment vocabulary;
- right / entitlement-like employment language;
- stronger Federal responsibility than the minimalist reading of 1946;
- annual anticipatory goals and explicit measurement;
- actors explicitly narrated 1970s legislation as fulfilling a promise weakened in the 1946 compromise.

Discontinuity evidence:

- 1945 aggregate expenditure-gap closure is not restored as the 1978 governing mechanism;
- 1978 includes structural unemployment, racial / group inequality, training, regional policy, productivity, inflation, trade, and Federal Reserve coordination;
- the 1970s campaign itself moved away from a stronger employer-of-last-resort model before enactment;
- `right` operates in a different institutional and causal field.

Provisional relation:

**`revived` is a plausible candidate only if defined as revival of a suppressed normative / problem commitment, not restoration of the same mechanism. `transformed_successor` also remains plausible for the full problem specification.**

The current schema may be too one-dimensional to express both facts cleanly.

## 16. Method fixtures added by this case

### Fixture A — explicit amendment does not settle problem identity

```text
Act B explicitly amends Act A
!=
Problem B is continuous with Problem A
```

### Fixture B — lexical revival can coexist with causal transformation

```text
“full employment” disappears in 1946
then reappears in 1978
!=
1945 problem simply returns
```

### Fixture C — a revived right need not revive its old mechanism

```text
right / full-opportunity language returns
!=
residual fiscal closure rule returns
!=
automatic government job guarantee
```

### Fixture D — the same campaign can contain multiple Problem Episodes

```text
1974/75 stronger employer-of-last-resort proposal
→ 1978 conditional reservoir / balanced-goal statute
```

The political campaign is continuous; the problem architecture is not necessarily stable.

### Fixture E — adding a constraint can create a new co-problem

Inflation is not merely an external veto on employment policy by 1978. It receives:

- a numerical target;
- a causal diagnosis;
- a dedicated policy section;
- structural interventions;
- Federal Reserve accountability.

So:

```text
new policy constraint
can become
new governed problem dimension
```

### Fixture F — actor disagreement about the predecessor relation is evidence

Proponents say 1978 strengthens / fulfills / specifies 1946.

McKinney says it repeats 1946-like aspirations without adequate programs.

The disagreement should be preserved, not normalized into one retrospective judgment.

### Fixture G — target continuity can mask subject change

The aggregate phrase `employment opportunities` survives, but the 1978 problem explicitly differentiates:

- regions;
- youth;
- structurally unemployed workers;
- disadvantaged groups;
- discrimination categories.

Thus:

```text
same aggregate target
!= same harmed subject / distributive problem
```

## 17. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1 — “Humphrey–Hawkins simply restored S.380”

Too strong.

There is real normative revival, but the causal model, policy tools, institutional architecture, and co-problems differ substantially.

### Risk 2 — “Humphrey–Hawkins merely amended the 1946 Act, so this is one continuous problem”

Statutory genealogy is not enough.

The new law deliberately changes the observatory and problem dimensions.

### Risk 3 — “1978 created the modern dual mandate exactly as we now understand it”

Avoid projecting the later settled language of Federal Reserve doctrine backward.

The 1978 actors were negotiating concrete relations among unemployment targets, inflation targets, monetary aggregates, structural policy, and Fed independence.

### Risk 4 — “Phillips curve explains the whole Act”

Goutsmedt demonstrates its importance in the inflation debate, but the Act also emerges from civil-rights / racial employment concerns, national-planning projects, structural unemployment policy, productivity worries, congressional-budget reforms, and institutional coordination.

### Risk 5 — “right to work” means an enforceable individual job guarantee

Do not infer this from the long title or Section 102(b).

The enacted reservoir machinery is conditional and depends on existing / further legal authority; the strongest employer-of-last-resort provisions were explicitly understood by the Federal Reserve as having been removed from earlier versions.

### Risk 6 — “reservoir projects prove employer of last resort survived unchanged”

Wrong for the same reason.

Trigger conditions, timing, authorization, targeting, and priority for private / existing-program jobs matter.

### Risk 7 — “inflation was always part of the same full-employment problem”

The 1946 Act’s statutory formulation did not make inflation an explicit coequal target in the 1978 sense. Muriel Humphrey and Partee both treat this as a significant change.

### Risk 8 — “1978 is just watered-down legislation and therefore methodologically uninteresting”

The weakening narrative may be politically meaningful, but Problem History should identify **which problem dimensions changed**:

- employer-of-last-resort logic;
- inflation target;
- structural policy;
- numerical goals;
- Fed accountability;
- conditional reservoir architecture.

## 20. What remains uncertain

1. **The exact 1974–76 draft sequence.**
   The record strongly indicates earlier employer-of-last-resort / job-guarantee provisions, but a clause-level sequence from Hawkins’s early Equal Opportunity and Full Employment bill through the 1976 Humphrey–Hawkins text is still needed.

2. **When the right changes legal / practical meaning.**
   The language survives into 1978 as a national goal, while enforcement architecture changes. The project needs bill text and committee reports to decide when this transition occurred.

3. **Which negotiations produced the conditional reservoir structure.**
   Partee identifies removal of the employer-of-last-resort feature as an improvement, but this alone does not prove the Federal Reserve caused the deletion.

4. **How the Carter Administration changed the problem.**
   Marshall and Schultze support the bill while emphasizing structural measures and feasibility. Drafting / negotiation documents are needed to distinguish endorsement from causal authorship.

5. **How central racial equality was across actors.**
   The final statute contains antidiscrimination and structural provisions, but the Congressional Black Caucus / Hawkins problem formulation should be reconstructed from named primary documents rather than inferred from later scholarship alone.

6. **Whether 1978 should be `revived` in schema terms.**
   The case may reveal that `revived` needs dimension-level metadata: what exactly revives—lexicon, normative commitment, causal model, instrument, observatory, or institutional lineage?

## 21. Next highest-value primary-source targets

1. **Hawkins’s 1974/75 Equal Opportunity and Full Employment Act text and hearings** — recover the original job-right / Job Guarantee Office / Stand-By Job Corps / judicial-enforcement architecture directly.

2. **1976 Humphrey–Hawkins bill and hearings** — identify exactly what Partee means by earlier employer-of-last-resort provisions and compare them clause by clause to 1978.

3. **1977 Carter–Hawkins–Humphrey negotiation records** — determine when price stability, revised targets, and conditional job reservoirs entered the compromise and who advocated each change.

4. **1978 committee reports and conference materials** — distinguish the House, Senate, and final formulations and identify whether Congress described deletions as compromises, clarifications, or changes of purpose.

5. **Named Congressional Black Caucus statements / Hawkins speeches** — reconstruct the racial-distribution / equal-opportunity problem from direct actors.

6. **Federal Reserve 1976 testimony** — pair with Partee’s 1978 statement to recover the Board’s own before/after relation judgment.

7. **First Economic Reports and Fed reports under the new Act (1979–81)** — implementation test: did the new observatory actually govern policy as the statute imagined, or did the problem lose askability / priority immediately after enactment?

The last target is especially valuable because it can turn the study from legislative genealogy into a disappearance / implementation test.

## 22. Provisional synthesis

The evidence now supports a stronger and more precise statement than either “Humphrey–Hawkins restored full employment” or “Humphrey–Hawkins was only a weakened symbolic amendment.”

The 1978 Act is an actor-explicit attempt to repair and extend the 1946 statutory settlement. It reactivates full-employment and employment-right language and makes economic objectives numerically observable. But it does so after three decades in which the relevant problem field has accumulated manpower institutions, civil-rights claims, structural unemployment, regional and youth policy, inflation, productivity concerns, budget institutions, and a more explicit Federal Reserve role.

The resulting historical object is therefore simultaneously genealogical and transformed:

```text
1946 machinery is inherited
+ some suppressed 1945 normative language is revived
+ 1970s policy instruments and causal objects are added
+ the strongest early-1970s job-guarantee mechanism is weakened before enactment
```

The most defensible present conclusion is:

> **What revives in 1978 is not a complete 1945 problem package. A full-employment commitment is deliberately reopened inside a new unemployment–inflation–structural-policy architecture.**

That is enough to justify further relation testing, but not yet enough to freeze a single `revived`, `reformulated`, or `transformed_successor` edge.
