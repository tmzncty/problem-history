# 1944–45: from the Patton investment-gap amendment to the National Production and Employment Budget

Status: **M2 source packet / actor-explicit reformulation test / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet deepens `1944-reconversion-job-transfer-to-full-employment.md` at the point where the reconversion problem became a continuing full-employment planning problem.

The key question is narrower than “where did the Employment Act come from?”:

> When the War Contracts Subcommittee took James G. Patton’s August 1944 proposal as a starting point, did it simply carry the same full-employment problem forward, or did the governed object, required knowledge, responsible institution, and answer space change before the first Full Employment Bill appeared?

The evidence is unusually strong because the December 1944 War Contracts Subcommittee report **explicitly narrates the transformation itself**. It says that the Patton proposal was circulated to executive agencies, that many objected to its fixed `$40,000,000,000` capital-investment figure and other features, that staff were instructed to draft a new measure, and that **“an entirely new bill was prepared.”**

The result is a high-value Problem History fixture:

```text
explicit legislative genealogy
+ same stated end (“full employment”)
+ same congressional workstream

!=

same problem specification
```

The August proposal asks how to close a **capital-investment shortfall against a fixed annual target**. The December draft asks how to estimate a **full-employment volume of gross national product**, compare it with prospective economy-wide investment and expenditure, and correct a prospective **National Budget deficiency** through a broad policy programme and, residually, Federal expenditure.

A provisional researcher-side description is **problem re-specification through measure substitution**: the policy goal remains recognizably related, but the variable treated as the governing deficit changes. This is an analytical label only, not actor vocabulary and not a schema proposal.

## 1. Source control: the bill genealogy is more complicated than later retrospectives imply

### 1.1 S.1823 → S.1893 → S.2061

A contemporaneous September 1944 *Monthly Catalog* entry for the Senate hearings states:

- S.1893 **superseded S.1823**;
- S.2061 was the later bill for a national programme of war mobilization and post-war adjustment;
- the hearings on 3–4 August covered S.1730, S.1893, and S.2061.

Source:

- U.S. Government Publications Monthly Catalog, September 1944, entry for `War mobilization and post-war adjustment` and `Mobilization and demobilization problems`:
  <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-GP3-5adf8afb496eee8ba46ed35077c558c8/pdf/GOVPUB-GP3-5adf8afb496eee8ba46ed35077c558c8.pdf>

The War Contracts Subcommittee’s own year-end legislative table makes the lineage still clearer:

```text
S.1730
+
S.1893 (superseding S.1823)
        ↓
new / amended S.2061
        ↓
S.2051 based in part on S.1730 + S.1893 + S.2061
        ↓
Public Law 458, 3 Oct 1944
```

Official Congressional Record indexed text:

- `CONGRESSIONAL RECORD—SENATE`, 1944, p. 9761:
  <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt7/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt7-15.pdf>

Evidence strength: **very high / contemporaneous official legislative genealogy**.

### 1.2 The Patton proposal itself has two contemporaneous bill references—and that is not a contradiction to erase

On **8 August 1944**, Senator Murray formally told the Senate:

- he was sending to the desk an amendment suggested by James G. Patton;
- it was an amendment **to S.2061**;
- he did not intend to offer it on the floor immediately because the Committee on Military Affairs had not studied it.

In the National Farmers Union release that Murray then inserted in the Record, however, the proposal is described as an amendment to the **Kilgore bill, S.1893**.

Official Congressional Record indexed text, p. 6757:

- <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt5/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt5-14.pdf>

This is best handled as a traceable legislative transition, not normalized away:

```text
NFU proposal vocabulary:
Patton amendment to Kilgore S.1893

formal Senate filing on 8 Aug:
Patton amendment suggested to S.2061
```

Given that S.2061 had just been reported as a new Murray–Kilgore measure built out of the previous reconversion workstream, both references are historically intelligible.

**Do not silently rewrite either one.**

### 1.3 A later official retrospective compresses the genealogy

The Joint Economic Committee’s 1966 official history calls the Patton proposal an amendment added to **S.1823**.

Source:

- *Twentieth Anniversary of the Employment Act of 1946: An Economic Symposium*, 1966, pp. 133–134:
  <https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/twentieth-anniversary-employment-act-1946-economic-symposium-1199/fulltext>

That retrospective is useful for the longer Employment Act genealogy, but the 1944 contemporaneous record is more precise for bill identity.

Fixture:

```text
later official institutional history
!= exact contemporaneous legislative numbering
```

and:

```text
retrospective genealogy compression
!= primary-source identity
```

This matters because a project that tracks problems through institutional transmission must not let later summary narratives collapse intermediate legislative objects.

## 2. August 1944: Patton specifies full employment as a fixed capital-investment gap

The strongest source is Murray’s 8 August Congressional Record insertion of the National Farmers Union proposal.

Official indexed text:

- `CONGRESSIONAL RECORD—SENATE`, 8 Aug 1944, p. 6757 ff.:
  <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt5/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt5-14.pdf>

Murray summarizes the amendment as authorizing Government investment and expenditure equal to the amount by which prospective private investment and construction falls short of an annual **$40 billion** level said to be required for full employment.

Patton’s release then supplies the proposed mechanism.

### 2.1 The problem variable

The crucial measured gap is:

```text
$40 billion required annual capital / construction expenditure
-
prospective private + State/local investment
=
shortfall to be filled
```

Patton ties the `$40 billion` figure to a projected `$200 billion` annual production volume and roughly `$170 billion` national income.

The historical problem is therefore not merely “unemployment exists.” It is made governable through a **numerical investment threshold**.

### 2.2 The knowledge-producing institution

Section 103(a) directs the **Joint Committee on Internal Revenue Taxation** annually to estimate:

- prospective savings;
- prospective investment by private business, groups, individuals, and State/local governments.

This matters because the later full-employment bill does not merely preserve the same calculation and move it to another office. The required object of estimation changes.

### 2.3 The answer sequence

The amendment lays out a staged correction mechanism:

1. estimate prospective non-Federal investment;
2. calculate the gap below `$40 billion`;
3. Reconstruction Finance Corporation offers loans to private business up to that amount;
4. if loans plus expected private/State/local expenditure still fall short, authorize Federal appropriations for a national works programme;
5. review expenditures quarterly and revise the public works rate upward or downward.

The logic is strikingly specific:

```text
fixed investment target
→ measure investment gap
→ try RFC credit
→ fill residual with national works
```

Evidence strength: **very high / direct contemporaneous proposal inserted in the Congressional Record**.

Boundary:

- this was a proposed amendment, not enacted policy;
- Murray explicitly said the committee had not studied it and that he did not intend immediate floor action;
- the text demonstrates Patton/NFU’s formulation, not Senate consensus.

## 3. December 1944: the subcommittee explicitly says it replaced the proposal with an “entirely new bill”

The key source is the War Contracts Subcommittee’s year-end report, printed into the Congressional Record in December 1944.

Official indexed text begins around pp. 9755–9759:

- <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt7/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1944-pt7-15.pdf>

The report itself reconstructs the chain:

1. Patton submitted the `$40 billion` capital-investment proposal in August;
2. no action was taken on it;
3. the chairman had it printed as an amendment to pending mobilization/reconversion legislation;
4. copies were sent to executive agencies and departments for comment;
5. several agencies expressed sympathy with the objective but objected to the fixed `$40 billion` figure and other features;
6. the staff was instructed to study the proposal and draft a new measure meeting the objections;
7. **an entirely new bill was prepared**, titled the `Full Employment Act of 1945`.

This is one of the strongest possible forms of reformulation evidence available to the project.

It is not:

```text
historian notices that two bills look different
```

It is:

```text
historical actor says:
proposal A was criticized
→ staff was told to redesign it
→ an entirely new bill resulted
```

Therefore the relation is explicit genealogy **plus actor-explicit redesign**.

Fixture:

```text
direct descent
!= unchanged problem specification
```

and:

```text
actor-explicit “entirely new bill”
can coexist with
actor-explicit use of an earlier proposal as the starting point
```

The choice between `same problem` and `totally unrelated problem` is therefore false.

## 4. What changes: from a fixed investment threshold to an economy-wide full-employment budget

The December report explains the new object before reproducing the draft bill.

### 4.1 The target is no longer a fixed $40 billion of capital investment

The subcommittee now says the Government should calculate:

- the level of **gross national product** to aim at;
- the total investment **and other expenditure** by consumers, business, State/local government, and Federal Government needed to provide jobs for everyone;
- the prospective total of those expenditures.

The deficit becomes:

```text
required economy-wide expenditure at full-employment production
-
prospective economy-wide expenditure
=
prospective National Budget deficiency
```

This is not just a changed numerical estimate.

The variable that defines the problem changes from:

```text
capital investment below a fixed dollar floor
```

to:

```text
aggregate expenditure below an endogenously estimated
full-employment volume of production
```

### 4.2 The target itself becomes conditional rather than fixed

The December draft requires estimates of:

- the number of jobs needed to assure continuing full employment;
- the gross national product required to provide that number of jobs;
- the investment and other expenditure required to achieve that GNP;
- prospective investment and expenditure.

By January 1945, S.380 makes this dynamism even more explicit: Murray’s accompanying questions and answers say the bill deliberately avoids a preconceived fixed number of jobs, because the employment requirement depends on population, labor-force participation, military service, hours worked, and similar changing factors.

Primary/full-text source for S.380 and Murray’s January 22 presentation:

- *National Policy and Program for Continuing Full Employment* / Congressional Record material, FRASER:
  <https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/national-policy-program-continuing-full-employment-3592/fulltext>

Thus:

```text
Patton:
known normative scalar = $40b investment

December / January:
full-employment requirement must itself be estimated
from labor force + prices + production + aggregate expenditure
```

This is a change in **knowledge requirement**, not merely answer choice.

## 5. The policy answer space broadens beyond investment loans and public works

Patton’s proposal is not pure public-works spending; it prioritizes private investment and RFC credit before Federal works. But the set of policy levers is still organized around filling an investment/construction gap.

The December bill instead says the first response to a prospective National Budget deficiency should be a programme to encourage increased **non-Federal investment and other expenditure**. It explicitly allows policy across:

- banking and currency;
- monopoly and competition;
- wages and working conditions;
- foreign trade and investment;
- agriculture;
- taxation;
- social security;
- natural-resource development;
- other fields affecting non-Federal investment and expenditure.

Only insofar as these measures are insufficient does the bill call for Federal investment and expenditure to close the remaining gap.

The problem-answer relation therefore changes from:

```text
investment shortfall
→ credit
→ public works residual
```

to:

```text
prospective economy-wide expenditure deficiency
→ coordinated non-Federal / regulatory / fiscal policy programme
→ Federal investment and expenditure residual
```

This supports:

```text
same ultimate objective
!= same answer space
```

and also:

```text
broader instrument set
can indicate a broader causal model,
not merely more tools for the same problem
```

## 6. Responsibility and observatory position change

### 6.1 Patton proposal

The main analytical responsibility sits with the **Joint Committee on Internal Revenue Taxation**.

The Director of War Mobilization then uses the finding to notify the RFC, organize national works, and review expenditure rates.

### 6.2 December draft / January S.380

The full-employment calculation is reorganized around a **National Production and Employment Budget** transmitted by the **President**.

The December report says the Budget is to be prepared by the Bureau of the Budget under presidential direction. The draft also creates a congressional Joint Committee on the Budget to review the programme.

By S.380 the President must estimate:

- labor-force size;
- full-employment production volume;
- required and prospective aggregate investment/expenditure;
- the expected deficiency or excess;
- policies to prevent unemployment or inflationary dislocation.

This is a different administrative vantage point:

```text
Patton:
tax/investment committee detects capital gap

→

Full Employment Bill:
executive-wide macro appraisal detects
employment-linked national expenditure gap
```

The change of responsible institution therefore corresponds to a change in what has to be known.

Fixture:

```text
responsibility transfer
may be constitutive of problem reformulation
when the required observatory changes
```

It should not be reduced to a neutral organizational reshuffle.

---

## 7. The December report also changes the normative object: a continuing right-to-work responsibility

The year-end report says the fundamental premise of the proposed bill is that every American able and willing to work has a right to a useful and remunerative job, and that Government responsibility is required to make this meaningful through continuing full employment.

It then explains the National Budget as the mechanism for making that responsibility operational.

This does not mean the Patton proposal lacked a strong normative commitment to full employment. Patton explicitly says the Nation should use its strength to achieve and maintain full employment.

The difference is more precise:

```text
August:
full employment attached to a fixed investment programme

December:
full employment becomes an explicit continuing governmental responsibility
for which an economy-wide appraisal procedure is constructed
```

By January 1945 the declaration is revised again, placing free competitive enterprise and private investment first while retaining Federal responsibility to assure sufficient employment opportunities and to supply Federal expenditure if necessary.

Therefore the right/responsibility language itself has a drafting history and should not be flattened into one timeless “full-employment doctrine.”

## 8. This is a rare actor-grounded case of problem re-specification through criticism

The most useful Problem History abstraction is not “Patton invented the Employment Act” or “the Employment Act was Keynesian.”

The source chain permits a more precise reconstruction:

```text
Aug 1944 Patton/NFU proposal
fixed $40b investment threshold
+ savings/investment forecast
+ RFC loans
+ national works residual

        ↓ proposal circulated

executive-agency criticism
especially fixed $40b and other shortcomings

        ↓ actor-explicit redrafting instruction

Dec 1944 Full Employment Bill draft
jobs + full-employment GNP
+ total consumer/business/State/local/Federal expenditure
+ National Budget deficiency
+ broad non-Federal policy programme
+ Federal expenditure residual

        ↓ comments / revision

22 Jan 1945 S.380
more explicit dynamic labor-force / production / expenditure appraisal
+ President-centered National Production and Employment Budget
```

The genealogical relation is extremely strong.

The identity relation is not.

The transformed features include at least:

| Dimension | August Patton proposal | December/January full-employment formulation |
|---|---|---|
| immediate quantified object | capital/investment shortfall | aggregate expenditure / production shortfall |
| target | fixed `$40b` annual investment | estimated full-employment GNP / volume of production |
| population link | implicit through full-employment claim | explicit labor-force/job estimate |
| knowledge producer | Joint Committee on Internal Revenue Taxation | President/Bureau of Budget + congressional budget review |
| private response | RFC loans | wide policy programme affecting non-Federal expenditure |
| Federal residual | national works | Federal investment/expenditure, broader than works |
| time logic | annual investment floor, quarterly review | recurring anticipatory National Budget |
| central causal emphasis | insufficient productive investment | insufficient aggregate economy-wide expenditure relative to full-employment production |

This table is a **researcher comparison** of actor texts. It is not a claim that the actors used these exact category labels.

## 9. Relation judgment

### 9.1 What can be frozen with high confidence

**Historical transmission / genealogy:** very strong.

The December report itself states:

```text
Patton proposal
→ agency comments
→ staff study / redrafting instruction
→ entirely new Full Employment Bill
```

No inferential bridge is needed for the fact of transmission.

### 9.2 What should not be frozen as simple continuity

Simple `continuous` problem identity is weak because the following all change:

- measured deficit;
- target variable;
- required data;
- causal representation;
- responsible institution;
- policy answer space;
- relationship between private and Federal action.

A future schema-level relation such as `reformulated` or `transformed_successor` would fit better than `continuous`, but this packet does **not** create or freeze such a relation.

### 9.3 What should not be called rupture

It would also be wrong to treat the Full Employment Bill as unrelated to the Patton proposal.

The direct actor account makes the lineage explicit.

The correct lesson is:

```text
problem genealogy
can be strong precisely where
problem identity is weak
```

## 11. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1: treating “full employment” as one stable problem because the phrase persists

The phrase is continuous across Patton, the December draft, S.380, and later legislation.

But the problem-defining deficit moves from fixed capital investment to a dynamic economy-wide production/expenditure relation.

```text
same slogan
!= same governed object
```

### Risk 2: reading the August proposal backward as merely an early version of modern aggregate-demand management

The Patton amendment is specific about savings, capital/construction investment, RFC lending, and a fixed `$40b` floor.

Do not erase those features because later macroeconomic language looks more familiar.

### Risk 3: treating the December draft as if it simply increased the Patton amount or broadened spending

The subcommittee itself says an **entirely new bill** was drafted after criticism.

The relevant difference is conceptual as well as instrumental.

### Risk 4: using the 1966 JEC shorthand to identify the exact August bill object

The 1966 history says the Patton amendment was added to S.1823.

The contemporaneous record shows S.1823 had been superseded by S.1893 and that Murray formally submitted the amendment to S.2061 on 8 August.

Prefer contemporaneous legislative control for exact bill identity.

### Risk 5: assuming agency sympathy with the objective means agreement on the formulation

The December report explicitly says agencies objected to the fixed figure and other features.

```text
shared policy goal
!= shared problem model
```

### Risk 6: turning “entirely new bill” into total conceptual rupture

The new measure was produced through review of the Patton proposal and responds to criticisms of it.

`entirely new bill` is evidence of redesign, not evidence of no genealogy.

### Risk 7: calling the sequence simply “Keynesianization”

That may be a useful historiographic question, but it is not an adequate actor-level description.

The primary-source chain can be described without importing a doctrinal label:

```text
fixed investment-gap programme
→ economy-wide expenditure / employment budget
```

Only separate intellectual-history evidence should establish Keynes/Hansen transmission.

## 13. Remaining uncertainties

1. **Executive-agency comments**
   - The December report confirms their existence and summarizes objections.
   - The individual Agriculture, Interior, Labor, Budget Bureau, FEA, OPA, and other letters should be recovered directly.
   - These could reveal *why* the fixed `$40b` measure was rejected and whether agencies proposed alternative problem models.

2. **Draft-by-draft sequence between August and 18 December**
   - Bailey describes an informal drafting group and multiple drafts.
   - Direct War Contracts Subcommittee staff files / Gross papers / Murray papers would be needed for a source-complete drafting genealogy.

3. **December draft → January S.380 exact diff**
   - The January text is available.
   - A mechanical clause-level comparison should identify precisely what changed after the December public draft and comments.

4. **Intellectual transmission**
   - The primary sources prove legislative redrafting.
   - They do not alone prove which elements derived from Keynes, Hansen, the National Resources Planning Board, National Farmers Union economists, Bureau of the Budget staff, or other channels.

5. **Why the knowledge producer moved**
   - The texts show Joint Committee on Internal Revenue Taxation → President/Bureau of Budget.
   - The internal argument for this institutional move remains to be recovered directly.

6. **Implementation genealogy after 1946**
   - Later Council of Economic Advisers / Economic Report machinery should not be treated as straightforward implementation of the December 1944 National Budget until the 1945–46 legislative deletions and substitutions are compared.

## 15. Provisional conclusion

The August–December 1944 sequence supplies unusually strong evidence for a distinction central to Problem History.

The actors themselves preserve both sides of the relation:

- **genealogy:** the Patton proposal was circulated, criticized, studied, and used as the immediate precursor in the drafting process;
- **transformation:** the subcommittee says the result was an `entirely new bill`, and the text replaces a fixed investment-gap calculation with an economy-wide labor-force / production / expenditure budget.

The strongest current relation judgment is therefore:

```text
Patton August 1944 proposal
        ↓ direct actor-explicit transmission
Full Employment Bill December 1944

relation:
strong genealogy
+ material re-specification

not:
simple continuity

not:
unrelated rupture
```

This is a particularly good repository fixture because it demonstrates that **direct descent is not evidence of problem identity**. In this case, the direct descent is exactly what lets us observe the problem being deliberately redesigned.
