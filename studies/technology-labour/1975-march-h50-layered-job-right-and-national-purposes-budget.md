# March 1975 H.R. 50: a layered job-right + National Purposes Budget formulation

Status: **M2 source packet / intermediate-draft reconstruction / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet fills the largest remaining gap in `1974-1976-hawkins-job-right-to-balanced-growth-reformulation.md`: the Hawkins Subcommittee staff later described the 20 March 1975 Subcommittee Print of H.R. 50 as a **major broadening**, but the earlier packet did not yet recover enough of that intermediate version to say what the broadening actually did.

The new evidence changes the shape of the 1974–76 sequence.

The March 1975 version should **not** be represented as a simple halfway point in which an individual job-right problem was already being replaced by macroeconomic planning. Multiple contemporaneous sources show that the March print instead layered a substantially broadened national-planning / National Purposes Budget architecture **on top of** still-strong job-right, administrative-remedy, judicial-appeal, employer-of-last-resort, Job Guarantee Office, and Standby Job Corps machinery.

A safer provisional reconstruction is therefore:

```text
1974 H.R.15476
individual employment right
+ job-guarantee administration
+ judicial appeal
+ annual national employment / production planning

        ↓ Jan 1975 identical H.R.50 / S.50

20 Mar 1975 Subcommittee Print
“major broadening”
= rights/remedy architecture still present
+ much thicker Full Employment and National Purposes Budget
+ explicit 3% unemployment / 18-month target
+ national-goals / resource-allocation framework
+ Federal-budget / Federal-Reserve consistency
+ public/private project reservoirs as residual backstop

        ↓ 1975–early 1976 revision

1976 substitute
employment-right language retained
+ balanced growth / anti-inflation / structural / countercyclical architecture
but
- private judicial remedy
- Job Guarantee Office
- some direct controls / mandatory institutions
```

Researcher-side shorthand:

**problem broadening by additive layering before enforcement retreat**.

That is an analytical description, not actor vocabulary and not a schema verdict.

## 1. Why the March 1975 print matters

The 10 March 1976 Hawkins Subcommittee memorandum supplies a direct draft genealogy:

- August 1974: first Equal Opportunity and Full Employment proposal;
- January 1975: identical H.R.50 / S.50 reintroduction;
- **March 1975: “major broadening,” embodied in a House Subcommittee Print dated 20 March 1975**;
- 1976: new substitute retaining some features, adding many, and changing or eliminating older ones.

Primary scan / provenance:

- Ford Presidential Library / NARA, `Unemployment (1)`, Hawkins Subcommittee memo, 10 March 1976:
  https://s3.amazonaws.com/NARAprodstorage/lz/presidential-libraries/ford/grf-0039/635863/1515960.pdf

Evidence strength: **very high / actor-proximate retrospective version genealogy produced only one year after the intermediate print**.

The phrase `major broadening` is valuable but underdetermined by itself. It does not tell us whether the 1975 change:

- replaced the job-right formulation;
- weakened enforceability;
- merely added macroeconomic machinery;
- changed the harmed subject;
- changed the first-resort / last-resort ordering of instruments;
- or did several of these at once.

The point of this packet is to recover enough contemporaneous evidence to constrain those alternatives.

## 3. The March broadening occurred during a live hearing sequence

Contemporary catalog records identify the 1975 H.R.50 hearings as a multi-part sequence beginning in February and continuing through March–April. The March 20 Subcommittee Print therefore falls **inside**, not before or after, the hearing process.

This chronology is useful because it means the intermediate print cannot safely be described as merely the unchanged January bill that happened to be discussed in later hearings.

But chronology alone does not establish which witness or coalition caused the broadening.

Method fixture:

```text
hearing precedes / overlaps draft revision
!=
witness caused draft revision
```

A future influence claim requires staff memoranda, correspondence, markups, or an explicit actor statement connecting testimony to a changed provision.

## 4. June 1975: Hawkins directly confirms that judicial enforceability survived the broadening

The strongest corrective to a premature `job right -> macro planning` transition comes from Augustus Hawkins himself.

On 11 June 1975 Hawkins inserted into the *Congressional Record* a UAW Legal Department statement on H.R.50 Section 5(h). Hawkins's own introduction says that H.R.50 provides administrative relief and ultimately Federal-court appeal for persons denied the job right and that the sponsors considered this provision important to the effectiveness of the bill.

Official source:

- *Congressional Record*, Extensions of Remarks, 11 June 1975, p.18513 ff.:
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt14/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt14-4-3.pdf

The UAW statement identifies:

- Section 5(h): individual right to bring suit in Federal court after alleged violation of rights under the Act;
- an administrative determination and appeals structure before litigation;
- Section 5(g): a presumption that a person presenting at the Full Employment Office is prima facie willing and able to work;
- a short administrative timetable;
- a `suitable and comparable job` standard;
- wage / compensation standards.

Evidence strength:

- **very high** for Hawkins's statement that sponsors still treated Federal-court appeal as an important H.R.50 mechanism in June 1975;
- **high / actor-specific** for the UAW Legal Department's interpretation of Sections 5(g) and 5(h);
- not evidence that every member of Congress agreed with the UAW's legal or policy assessment.

This materially changes the version history.

The March 1975 broadening did **not** already produce the enforcement retreat documented in the March 1976 Hawkins staff memo.

At least through June 1975:

```text
macro-planning broadening
+
private / individual enforceability
coexisted
```

Therefore:

```text
problem broadening
!=
remedy weakening
```

at this stage.

The later elimination of judicial appeal must be dated **after** the March broadening, not treated as part of it.

## 5. A hostile February 1976 witness independently preserves March-print language

A useful adversarial source appears in the Joint Economic Committee hearing *Jobs and Prices in Boston*, held 16 February 1976.

Lawrence Sherman, identified in the hearing as a National Committee member of the U.S. Labor Party, attacks Humphrey–Hawkins. Because the witness is hostile, his political interpretation should **not** be normalized into neutral description. But his testimony is valuable because he explicitly attributes wording to the **20 March 1975 version of H.R.50**.

Official source:

- Joint Economic Committee, *Jobs and Prices in Boston*, hearing of 16 February 1976, printed 1977, pp.141–142 area:
  https://www.jec.senate.gov/reports/94th%20Congress/Hearings/Jobs%20and%20Prices%20in%20Boston%20%28844%29.pdf

The relevant scanned pages were image-checked in this research pass.

Sherman attributes to the March 20 version:

- a U.S. Full Employment Service with powers to seek out people not seeking work but able to work;
- a presumption that a job seeker presenting in person is prima facie willing to work;
- district-court appeal;
- employment counseling and local planning / community-board machinery.

Evidence strength: **medium-high for draft-text persistence because the witness names the exact version and reproduces clause-like language; low for his hostile causal and political characterizations**.

This source should be used asymmetrically:

```text
hostile witness may preserve a text accurately
!=
hostile witness supplies neutral meaning of the text
```

It independently supports the June Hawkins/UAW evidence that the March print preserved an administratively cognizable individual claimant and a judicial remedy.

It also helps date the next transformation: Sherman says in February 1976 that Humphrey–Hawkins is **now being revised**. That is consistent with the Hawkins staff's 10 March 1976 memorandum introducing a new substitute.

## 6. The National Purposes Budget layer was already thick by mid-1975

The March broadening was not merely a retention of the 1974 job guarantee. By mid-1975, multiple actor-specific sources describe a much more developed macro / national-planning structure around H.R.50.

### 6.1 Congressional Black Caucus: full employment + periodic national planning + residual job reservoirs

A Congressional Black Caucus 1975 account describes its full-employment legislative initiative around Hawkins's H.R.50. It summarizes:

- an annual **Full Employment and National Purposes Budget** sent by the President to Congress;
- a goal of reducing unemployment to **3 percent or less within 18 months**;
- an interim budget option for immediate programs;
- periodic Executive reporting on economic performance, trends, accomplishments, and projections;
- congressional review and development of supportive or alternative recommendations / legislation;
- immediate supplementary reservoirs of public and private service jobs;
- an expectation that those supplementary reservoirs decline as productivity and private-sector growth rise;
- a requirement that Federal agencies, including the Federal Reserve, act consistently with the National Purposes Budget.

Primary source:

- Congressional Black Caucus 1975 publication / Avoice scan:
  https://avoice.cbcfinc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/dhu-cbc-nb-1975.pdf

Evidence strength: **high as contemporaneous Congressional Black Caucus formulation / reception of H.R.50; not identical to Hawkins Subcommittee legislative intent**.

The source is especially valuable because it makes the ordering of instruments visible:

```text
national economic / resource plan
        ↓
private-sector and general-policy employment creation
        ↓ if insufficient
supplementary public / private service-job reservoirs
```

That is different from reading H.R.50 as simply a direct public-employment bill.

### 6.2 Coalition of Black Trade Unionists: Section 3 as the core, public jobs as residual

The Coalition of Black Trade Unionists adopted a jobs resolution at its Atlanta convention on 23 May 1975. The resolution was inserted into the *Congressional Record* on 16 June 1975.

Official source:

- *Congressional Record*, Extensions of Remarks, 16 June 1975, p.19140 ff.:
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt15/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt15-1-3.pdf

The CBTU resolution supports H.R.50/S.50 and explicitly interprets **Section 3** as the most important part of the bill because it moves the proposal beyond a simple public-jobs program.

Its formulation emphasizes:

- explicit national goals for full employment plus seventeen other national needs;
- analysis of resources and their allocation;
- the ordinary Federal budget as only one component of the larger National Purposes Budget;
- Federal-budget consistency with those national goals;
- Federal Reserve consistency with those goals;
- public jobs for the **residual unemployed** after the broader planning / full-employment mechanism operates.

The CBTU resolution states that if Section 3 works, the later public-jobs apparatus should be smaller than existing or proposed public-service employment programs.

Evidence strength: **high as a named contemporaneous Black labor actor's interpretation of the bill; not proof that Hawkins, Reuss, Humphrey, or all sponsors ranked Section 3 identically**.

This source yields a particularly useful actor-side causal ordering:

```text
macro / social planning = first-resort employment strategy
public job machinery = residual backstop
```

That is not a present-day inference imposed on the bill. At least one important supporting coalition stated the hierarchy explicitly.

## 7. October 1975: Hawkins and Reuss themselves describe the revised March print as a hybrid

On 3 October 1975 Hawkins inserted a joint Hawkins–Reuss response to Treasury and Labor Department objections to H.R.50 into the *Congressional Record*.

This source is unusually valuable because the sponsors explicitly distinguish the **original** H.R.50 from the **updated and revised Subcommittee Print of March 20, 1975**, noting that some executive objections addressed the original rather than the revised print then in wide use.

Official source:

- *Congressional Record*, Extensions of Remarks, 3 October 1975, p.31754 ff.:
  https://www.congress.gov/94/crecb/1975/10/03/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt24-6-3.pdf

In the sponsors' own description, the revised Hawkins–Reuss program still contains:

- a right to equal opportunities for useful paid employment at fair compensation;
- a **Full Employment and National Purposes Budget** specifying national priorities and resources;
- a **Job Guarantee Office**;
- a **Standby Job Corps**;
- local reservoirs of public and private employment projects;
- Federal employer-of-last-resort responsibility;
- demand management plus targeting employment where most needed;
- productivity / anti-inflation arrangements with private employers.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct sponsor explanation that explicitly identifies the March 20 revised print as the operative updated version**.

This source makes the central finding of this packet difficult to avoid:

```text
March 1975 broadening
!=
replacement of job guarantee by macro planning
```

Instead the revised formulation joins them.

The governed problem is simultaneously visible at several levels:

```text
individual:
person able and willing to work but lacking a suitable job

administrative:
can ordinary placement satisfy the claimant?
if not, can the guarantee / project reservoir do so?

aggregate:
is total labor demand sufficient for full employment?

allocative / planning:
what national goals and resources should be coordinated
so that full employment is generated through useful production?

monetary-fiscal:
are the Federal budget and Federal Reserve acting consistently
with the full-employment plan?
```

That is a **compound problem package**, not a single metric with a single instrument.

## 8. The March version should be treated as additive layering, not a linear substitution

The earlier 1974–76 packet was right to identify a trajectory toward broader macro-governance and later enforcement retreat, but the recovered intermediate evidence permits a more precise sequence.

### 8.1 What is already present in 1974

Do not retroactively make March 1975 the birth of all macroeconomic planning.

The 1974 Hawkins explanation already acknowledged:

- an annual Full Employment and Production Program;
- fiscal and monetary policy;
- price stability;
- balance-of-payments and business-policy dimensions.

The March print therefore **broadens / thickens / re-specifies** the planning layer rather than inventing it ex nihilo.

### 8.2 What becomes sharper in March 1975

The intermediate evidence supports a stronger planning / observatory package:

- named Full Employment and National Purposes Budget;
- quantified 3-percent unemployment target;
- short 18-month target horizon;
- integration of full employment with a broad set of national purposes;
- explicit resource accounting / allocation;
- Executive projection and reporting;
- congressional review;
- cross-agency consistency;
- explicit Federal Reserve consistency;
- supplementary job reservoirs framed as residual to broader economic success.

### 8.3 What does **not** disappear yet

At least through June–October 1975, evidence still supports:

- employment-right language;
- an identifiable individual claimant;
- administrative appeal;
- Federal judicial appeal;
- Job Guarantee Office;
- Standby Job Corps;
- Federal employer of last resort;
- public/private employment reservoirs.

Therefore the March edge is better represented as:

```text
1974
rights/remedy + planning

        ↓ broadening

March 1975
rights/remedy
+ stronger quantified macro plan
+ national-purposes/resource-allocation layer
+ stronger cross-government consistency
```

rather than:

```text
job right
→
macro planning
```

## 9. A problem can broaden before a remedy narrows

This is the strongest methodological contribution of the new evidence.

Historical change in a problem specification need not be a one-step substitution.

Here the sequence appears to be:

```text
Stage A:
individual right + job guarantee + macro responsibility

Stage B:
add national-purpose planning, quantitative targets,
resource allocation and stronger policy coordination
while preserving the enforceable claimant architecture

Stage C:
retain right language and a last-resort employment layer
while deleting private judicial enforcement and reorganizing institutions
```

This yields real-source fixtures:

```text
problem broadening
!=
remedy weakening

broader scale
!=
less visible harmed subject

macro planning
can coexist with
individual enforceability

later enforcement retreat
must not be retrojected
into an earlier broadening
```

The case therefore warns against a common historian's compression:

```text
1974 radical job guarantee
→
1976 moderate planning bill
```

The intermediate form was itself a **rights + planning hybrid**.

## 10. Actor plurality inside the same H.R.50 coalition

The recovered evidence also reinforces that one bill does not imply one actor problem.

### Hawkins / Reuss

Sponsor-side formulation in October 1975:

- continuous full-employment guarantee;
- National Purposes Budget;
- Job Guarantee Office / Standby Job Corps;
- employer of last resort;
- targeted labor demand;
- productivity / inflation concern.

### UAW Legal Department

Legal / labor formulation in May–June 1975:

- statutory right is meaningful only if administratively and judicially enforceable;
- focus on claimant procedure, standards, and Federal-court access.

### Coalition of Black Trade Unionists

Black-labor formulation in May–June 1975:

- mass unemployment is especially destructive of Black / minority employment gains;
- Section 3 planning and resource allocation is the key first-resort architecture;
- public jobs should become residual if national planning succeeds.

### Congressional Black Caucus

CBC formulation:

- nationwide full-employment / equal-opportunity structure;
- explicit quantitative goal;
- reporting / congressional review;
- direct job reservoirs;
- cross-agency / Federal Reserve consistency.

### U.S. Labor Party / Lawrence Sherman

Hostile formulation in February 1976:

- attacks the same administrative / claimant machinery as coercive labor control;
- nevertheless preserves useful text-level evidence of March-print provisions.

These formulations cannot be averaged into an invented consensus.

Method fixture:

```text
same bill coalition
!=
same causal model
!=
same valued mechanism
!=
same diagnosis of harm
```

## 11. Competing criticism is about causes and acceptable answers, not merely the goal

The October 1975 Hawkins–Reuss statement reproduces / answers executive criticism of H.R.50. Treasury and Labor objections focus on, among other things:

- inflation risk;
- costs;
- Federal control;
- CETA implications;
- labor-market pressure.

Hawkins and Reuss respond that full employment need not be sacrificed to inflation control and portray the bill as combining demand, targeted employment, productivity, social priorities, and anti-inflation measures.

This supplies another Problem History warning:

```text
shared concern about unemployment
!=
shared problem
```

because disagreement concerns:

- whether labor-market slack is necessary for price stability;
- whether the guarantee itself generates inflation;
- whether public action corrects or creates labor-market disorder;
- whether Federal coordination is a solution or an unacceptable control mechanism.

The disagreement is therefore causal and institutional, not only distributive or partisan.

## 12. Relation judgment: 1974 -> March 1975

Do **not** freeze the relation yet because the complete March 20 Subcommittee Print still needs direct standalone recovery and clause-level comparison with H.R.15476 / January H.R.50.

But the candidate space can now be narrowed.

### Evidence for `continuous`

- identical January 1975 reintroduction follows the 1974 bill;
- employment-right architecture persists;
- claimant / remedy machinery persists;
- Job Guarantee Office and Standby Job Corps persist;
- employer-of-last-resort architecture persists;
- full-employment normative target persists.

### Evidence against simple `continuous`

The Hawkins staff itself calls March 1975 a **major broadening**; contemporary descriptions show a more elaborated:

- target;
- time horizon;
- national-purposes frame;
- resource-allocation apparatus;
- reporting / observatory;
- congressional review;
- Federal Reserve / cross-agency consistency requirement.

### Current best candidate

`reformulated` now looks stronger than `transformed_successor` for the **1974 -> March 1975** edge, because the directly enforceable harmed subject and job-guarantee answer architecture remain highly visible while the scale / observatory / planning layer broadens around them.

That is **not** yet a frozen schema edge.

The **March 1975 -> 1976** edge is likely more transformative because the later staff memo explicitly records elimination of judicial appeal and institutional replacement while further broadening the macro / anti-inflation / balanced-growth field.

## 14. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1: reading March 1975 through the 1976 title

`Full Employment and Balanced Growth` is a later redesign. Do not back-project the 1976 institutional arrangement into the March 1975 print.

### Risk 2: treating national planning as replacing the job guarantee

The new primary evidence shows co-existence.

### Risk 3: treating the private right to sue as a 1974-only radical feature

Hawkins still publicly defended it in June 1975, after the March broadening.

### Risk 4: reading public jobs as the entire bill

CBTU explicitly ranks the National Purposes Budget / Section 3 above the residual public-jobs apparatus.

### Risk 5: reading the National Purposes Budget as the entire bill

UAW / Hawkins evidence shows that an individual enforceable employment right remained simultaneously central.

### Risk 6: using a hostile witness as a neutral interpreter

Sherman is valuable for version-specific textual evidence, not for neutral semantics or accepted causal claims.

### Risk 7: treating `3% unemployment` as the whole problem

The target sits inside a compound architecture of rights, resource allocation, national goals, demand management, placement, direct jobs, anti-discrimination, productivity and Federal monetary/fiscal coordination.

### Risk 8: treating archive location as authorship

Keyserling files are a recovery map, not proof of who drafted each clause.

## 16. What remains uncertain

1. The complete March 20 print has still not been read as a standalone clause-by-clause document.
2. We do not yet know which exact clauses were added on March 20 versus already present in the January reintroduction without direct text diff.
3. The causal role of February–March hearing witnesses in producing the broadening remains unproved.
4. Exact drafting authorship among Hawkins staff, Reuss, Keyserling, Browne, labor allies, and other advisers remains open.
5. We do not yet know the exact date between June/October 1975 and March 1976 when private judicial enforcement was removed.
6. We do not yet know whether all sponsor-side descriptions in October map perfectly onto the March print or include informal later adjustments while continuing to call the March print the operative version.
7. Federal Reserve provisions need clause-level comparison across January 1975, March 1975, March 1976, and 1978.

These uncertainties matter because the existing evidence is sufficient to reject a simple substitution narrative, but not sufficient to freeze every draft edge.

## 19. Current research judgment

The March 1975 evidence makes the internal Humphrey–Hawkins genealogy more granular and less linear.

The safest current reconstruction is:

> **The March 20, 1975 H.R.50 print broadened the governing scale and observatory around full employment—quantitative target, National Purposes Budget, national priorities, resource allocation, cross-agency and Federal Reserve consistency—without yet abandoning the individual employment-right claimant, private judicial remedy, or Federal employer-of-last-resort machinery. The later 1976 redesign then combined further macro/structural broadening with a real retreat in enforceability and institutional guarantee.**

This is stronger than a generic `radical bill watered down` story because it separates at least two distinct transformations:

1. **1974 → March 1975:** additive broadening / layered hybrid;
2. **March 1975 → 1976:** broadening plus enforceability/institutional retreat.

No final schema relation is frozen until the complete March 20 print is recovered and directly diffed.
