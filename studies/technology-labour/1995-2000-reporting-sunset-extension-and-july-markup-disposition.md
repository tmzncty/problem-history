# 1995–2000: reporting-sunset extension and the July H.R. 3046 markup status

Status: **M2 source-control / legislative-process genealogy / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet extends:

- `1994-2000-humphrey-hawkins-monetary-ranges-to-reporting-afterlife.md`;
- `2000-humphrey-hawkins-selective-reenactment-and-accountability-split.md`;

The immediately preceding packet recovered an important legislative genealogy:

```text
Oct 1999 House-passed H.R. 3046
preserve old §2A reporting architecture from sunset

        ↓

Jul 2000 Senate Banking
committee-print substitute amendment to H.R. 3046
centered on semiannual Fed testimony / replacement of lapsed HH reporting

        ↓

Dec 2000 Public Law 106-569 §1003
new §2B reporting / testimony architecture
without old monetary-growth ranges
```

It deliberately left two process questions unresolved:

1. did the July 13, 2000 H.R. 3046 substitute merely appear on a schedule, or did it actually enter the committee markup session?;
2. why is **15 May 2000** the operative statutory sunset date when the 1995 Act originally spoke in relative terms?

Fresh primary evidence resolves both questions substantially, while preserving an important remaining boundary: the July 13 substitute's **exact disposition** is still not recovered.

The strongest new reconstruction is:

```text
21 Dec 1995
PL 104-66 §3003(a)(1): listed periodic reports sunset
4 years after enactment
        ↓
original date = 21 Dec 1999

19 Oct 1999
House passes H.R. 3046:
selectively preserve old §2A and other reports

29 Nov 1999
PL 106-113 §236:
replace the relative 4-year deadline
with the fixed date 15 May 2000
        ↓
Congress buys a bounded extension window
without itself deciding that every listed report survives

15 May 2000
extended sunset arrives

13 Jul 2000
Senate Banking final calendar records H.R. 3046 substitute
inside the day's MARK-UPS
        ↓
so it was an actual markup object,
not merely a scheduled possibility

BUT
same final calendar's H.R. 3046 bill chronology records
no post-referral formal advancement
        ↓
markup occurrence != committee approval / report

Dec 2000
Congress enacts a redesigned Fed reporting architecture elsewhere
```

Researcher-side shorthand below—**`deadline extension as decision deferral`**, **`markup-without-advancement`**, and **`procedural narrowing of uncertainty`**—is analytical vocabulary only. It is not actor language and not a schema proposal.

## 1. Source control

### 1.1 Public Law 104-66, 21 Dec 1995: the original sunset rule

Official GovInfo record:

- https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-104publ66
- PDF: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ66/pdf/PLAW-104publ66.pdf

Relevant location:

- §3003(a)(1), 109 Stat. 734;
- §3003(c), 109 Stat. 735.

Evidence strength: **very high / enacted primary source / page-audited**.

Section 3003(a)(1) does **not** originally say `May 15, 2000`. It says that, subject to exceptions, each provision requiring a regular periodic report listed in House Document No. 103-7 shall cease to be effective with respect to that reporting requirement **four years after enactment**.

Because Public Law 104-66 was enacted on **21 Dec 1995**, the original general sunset date was therefore **21 Dec 1999**.

Section 3003(c) also matters for evidence discipline: it identifies the governing list as the Clerk of the House's list for the first session of the 103d Congress, **House Document No. 103-7**.

This corrects a source-control ambiguity in the earlier packets. `May 15, 2000` should not be silently projected backward into the 1995 enactment.

---

### 1.2 Public Law 106-113, 29 Nov 1999: Congress moves the date to 15 May 2000

Official GovInfo text:

- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-106publ113/html/PLAW-106publ113.htm

Relevant location:

- §236, `Postponement of Date of Termination of Federal Agency Reporting Requirements`.

Evidence strength: **very high / enacted primary source**.

Section 236 expressly amends §3003(a)(1) of the 1995 Act by striking the phrase equivalent to `4 years after enactment` and inserting the fixed date **May 15, 2000**.

So the legal chain is now direct:

```text
PL 104-66 (21 Dec 1995)
4 years after enactment
        ↓
21 Dec 1999

PL 106-113 §236 (29 Nov 1999)
strikes that formulation
and substitutes
15 May 2000
```

This is not an inferred effective date and not a later codifier's convenience. It is an enacted amendment.

Boundary:

The statute proves **what Congress changed**. It does not by itself prove why every participant supported the extension, nor that §236 was enacted specifically because of H.R. 3046 or the Federal Reserve report. Do not infer a Fed-specific causal story from chronology alone.

### 1.3 H.R. 3046, House passage before the deadline extension

Official text and prior packet source control:

- https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-106hr3046eh
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-106hr3046eh/html/BILLS-106hr3046eh.htm

Evidence strength: **very high / House-passed legislative text**.

Chronology now matters more sharply:

- **7 Oct 1999** — H.R. 3046 introduced;
- **19 Oct 1999** — House passes it;
- **20 Oct 1999** — received in Senate and referred to Senate Banking;
- **29 Nov 1999** — Congress separately enacts the general deadline extension to 15 May 2000.

Therefore H.R. 3046's House preservation answer was developed under an impending **21 Dec 1999** sunset, not under an already-set May deadline.

The later omnibus amendment then changed the temporal environment in which Senate Banking could deal with the preservation proposal.

Safe statement:

> the general sunset deadline was postponed after the House had passed H.R. 3046 and before the original December deadline arrived.

Unsafe without additional transmission evidence:

> Congress postponed the deadline in order to give H.R. 3046 more time.

That causal link remains unproved.

### 1.4 Senate Banking final Legislative Calendar, 106th Congress: July 13 was actually a markup object

Official Senate Banking Committee final calendar:

- `Legislative Calendar, Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs, 106th Congress, Final Calendar, S. Prt. 106-87`
- https://www.banking.senate.gov/download/106th-congress&download=1

Relevant locations:

- `MARK-UPS`, printed p. 5;
- `HOUSE BILLS`, H.R. 3046 entry, printed p. 43.

Evidence strength: **very high / committee's own final legislative-process record / page-audited**.

The `MARK-UPS` chronology for **13 Jul 2000** includes, alongside the day's other bills, a committee print of a substitute amendment to H.R. 3046 providing for semiannual Federal Reserve testimony before Congress.

This materially upgrades the earlier process finding.

Previous evidence consisted of:

- July 5 committee announcement of a planned markup;
- July 10/12 Daily Digest schedules;
- July 13 Senate floor authorization for the committee to meet.

Those sources proved that the substitute was **scheduled and authorized** for markup.

The committee's own **final calendar**, compiled as the Congress's completed activity record, places the H.R. 3046 substitute under `MARK-UPS` for that date. The safest inference is therefore:

```text
scheduled markup
        ↓
actual committee markup object
```

This closes the earlier possibility that the H.R. 3046 item simply vanished before the business meeting began.

Boundary:

`appears in the committee's final markup chronology`

still does **not** mean:

`the committee approved the substitute`.

### 1.5 The same final calendar records no formal H.R. 3046 advancement after referral

The `HOUSE BILLS` section of the same final Senate Banking calendar has a separate H.R. 3046 history. It records:

- introduction / House referral;
- House passage on 19 Oct 1999;
- Senate receipt and Banking referral on 20 Oct 1999.

It does **not** record a July 2000 `ordered reported`, `reported`, or comparable post-markup committee action for H.R. 3046.

Evidence strength: **very high for the committee's own recorded formal bill history**.

This matters because it narrows the disposition question substantially.

Combined with the `MARK-UPS` page:

```text
H.R. 3046 substitute
was actually a July 13 markup object

BUT

H.R. 3046 has no later formal advancement
in the committee's final bill chronology
```

This supports:

> **markup occurred without a recorded formal advancement of the bill.**

It does not distinguish among several still-live possibilities:

- item discussed and deferred;
- markup begun but not completed;
- no vote taken;
- substitute withdrawn;
- substitute failed;
- other procedural disposition not captured by the public summary.

Those alternatives require the business-meeting minutes, transcript, committee print annotations, vote record, or staff correspondence.

Do **not** convert `no recorded advancement` into `defeated`.

## 2. Fresh finding A: the May 2000 sunset was itself a second-stage intervention

Earlier packets treated May 15, 2000 as the relevant sunset date, but the primary legal sequence adds a missing temporal layer.

The original 1995 architecture was:

```text
periodic report appears in House Doc. 103-7
        ↓
no specific exemption
        ↓
reporting requirement terminates
4 years after 21 Dec 1995
        ↓
21 Dec 1999
```

In November 1999 Congress intervened again:

```text
impending 21 Dec 1999 termination
        ↓
PL 106-113 §236
        ↓
new common terminal date:
15 May 2000
```

This changes the problem genealogy in two ways.

First, **sunset is no longer one event**. For reports such as §2A, there is an original legislated endpoint and a later legislated postponement.

Second, postponement is analytically distinct from preservation.

H.R. 3046 proposed:

```text
this selected reporting requirement should not sunset
```

Section 236 did:

```text
this class of listed reporting requirements will sunset later
```

So:

```text
postponement != exemption
extension != preservation
```

The extension created more decision time while leaving the selection problem unresolved.

Researcher-side shorthand: **deadline extension as decision deferral**.

This is not a claim that legislators themselves used that phrase or that §236's only purpose was to permit report-by-report review. That rationale needs separate legislative-history evidence.

## 3. Fresh finding B: H.R. 3046 moved through three distinct procedural statuses

With the final calendar, H.R. 3046's lineage can now be decomposed more carefully.

### Status 1 — House-approved preservation proposal

By 19 Oct 1999:

```text
House answer:
selected reports are useful enough to exempt from sunset
        ↓
keep old legal requirements alive
```

### Status 2 — Senate redesign object that actually entered markup

By 13 Jul 2000:

```text
old deadline has passed
        +
practice / oversight concern survives
        +
Senate uses H.R. 3046 as legislative vehicle
        ↓
committee-print substitute
for semiannual Fed testimony
```

The word `markup` now rests on the final committee calendar rather than schedule alone.

### Status 3 — markup object without recorded formal advancement

After the July meeting:

```text
committee final bill history
records no H.R. 3046 post-referral advancement
```

This is the important new procedural state.

A proposal can therefore be:

```text
historically consequential
+ substantively part of a genealogy
+ actually taken up in markup

while

not becoming a reported / enacted bill in that form
```

That matters for Problem History because the relevant historical answer-space is wider than enacted law.

## 4. The disappearance test becomes more precise

This case now supplies at least four distinct kinds of institutional non-survival that should not be collapsed.

### 4.1 Deadline postponement

The old requirement remains legally alive for a bounded additional period.

Not disappearance yet.

### 4.2 Sunset

The old statutory reporting requirement lapses.

This is formal legal disappearance of that requirement, but not necessarily disappearance of practice, actor concern, or the wider problem.

### 4.3 Practical afterlife

Fed testimony/reporting continues during the lapse.

This blocks the equation:

```text
legal requirement disappears
=
practice disappears
```

### 4.4 Failed or non-advanced redesign vehicle

A committee substitute can materially change the proposed answer architecture yet fail to progress formally.

This blocks another equation:

```text
proposal fails to advance
=
proposal is historically irrelevant
```

The final December legislation can inherit or parallel parts of an earlier non-advanced redesign even when the intermediate vehicle itself never becomes law.

Direct transmission still needs clause-level evidence.

## 5. Actor problem vs researcher reconstruction

### 5.1 What actors directly establish

The following are actor / institutional facts supported directly by primary sources:

- 1995 law: a broad class of listed periodic reporting requirements was to terminate after four years;
- 1999 House H.R. 3046: selected banking/housing reports, including §2A, should be protected from that sunset;
- Nov 1999 law: the general terminal date was changed to 15 May 2000;
- July 2000 Senate Banking: a substitute amendment to H.R. 3046 centered on semiannual Fed testimony was an actual markup object;
- final Senate Banking calendar: H.R. 3046 has no recorded formal post-referral advancement;
- Dec 2000 law: Congress enacted a new semiannual Fed reporting/testimony architecture without restoring the old monetary-range machinery.

### 5.2 What remains researcher-side reconstruction

The following are useful but analytical descriptions:

- `deadline extension as decision deferral`;
- `preservation-to-redesign shift`;
- `markup-without-advancement`;
- `procedural narrowing of uncertainty`;
- `selective observatory reconstruction`.

Do not cite these as historical actors' categories.

### 5.3 What must still not be claimed

Do not yet write:

- Senate Banking **rejected** the July substitute;
- Gramm **withdrew** it;
- the July committee print was textually identical to final §2B;
- Federal Reserve staff **drafted** the substitute;
- §236's November deadline extension was enacted **because of H.R. 3046**;
- the disappearance of old §2A means the full-employment / price-stability problem disappeared.

All six claims exceed current evidence.

## 8. Hindsight risks

### 8.1 Treating the May deadline as if it had existed since 1995

This erases a real 1999 intervention and compresses two temporal decisions into one.

### 8.2 Reading the November extension as proof Congress wanted all listed reports preserved

The amendment postponed the general endpoint. It did not exempt every listed report permanently.

### 8.3 Reading the final December 2000 statute backward into July

The July committee print remains unrecovered. The final §2B architecture cannot be assigned clause-for-clause to the July substitute merely because the overall direction is similar.

### 8.4 Treating a committee markup entry as an approval entry

The final calendar itself separates `MARK-UPS` from bill-action chronology. Use that separation as evidence discipline.

### 8.5 Treating non-advancement as substantive repudiation

A bill can fail to advance for scheduling, bargaining, jurisdictional, vehicle-selection, or substantive reasons. Exact cause requires direct process evidence.
