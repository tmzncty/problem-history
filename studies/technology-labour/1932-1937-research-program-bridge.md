# 1932–1937: from displacement estimates to a federal research programme

> **Status:** M2 Episode B institutional / methodological bridge.
>
> **Purpose:** test whether the 1928–29 technological-unemployment problem field merely resembles the 1937 federal planning/measurement formulation, or whether recoverable personnel and institutional transmission exists.
>
> **Boundary:** this packet establishes a strong **1932→1937 research-program lineage around David Weintraub and cooperating institutions**. It does **not** prove that the 1929 Senate committee directly caused the 1937 National Resources Committee formulation, and it does not collapse all actors into one continuous problem.

## Executive finding

A direct bridge can now be recovered inside the research infrastructure, even though the broader 1928–29→1937 problem relation should remain unfrozen.

David Weintraub's 1932 article on worker displacement was not an isolated retrospective publication. NBER's contemporary annual-report material identifies it as a by-product of Leo Wolman's labour-market research. By 1937 Weintraub was director of the WPA National Research Project on Reemployment Opportunities and Recent Changes in Industrial Techniques. The Project explicitly cooperated with the Bureau of Labor Statistics and the National Bureau of Economic Research, continued some earlier NBER industry surveys, and prepared productivity/employment work in cooperation with BLS. Weintraub and Harold Posner's `Unemployment and Increasing Productivity` was in turn prepared for the National Resources Committee and incorporated into *Technological Trends and National Policy*.

More important than the personnel chain, the Project's own 1937 research statement explicitly rejects treating `technological unemployment` as a self-contained `machines versus men` problem. It argues that the category cannot be isolated from output, demand, market structure, labour supply, industry conditions, and worker trajectories. The Project therefore turns the inherited label into an object that itself requires decomposition and validation.

The strongest current relation is therefore not:

```text
1929 Senate technological-unemployment problem
        ↓ continuous
1937 NRC technological-unemployment problem
```

but:

```text
NBER labour-market research / Weintraub, 1932
        ↓ direct personnel continuity
WPA National Research Project, 1936–37
        ↓ explicit cooperation / continuation of studies
BLS + NBER + other agencies
        ↓
Weintraub/Posner report prepared for NRC
        ↓ direct document incorporation
NRC, Technological Trends and National Policy, 1937
```

This is a **research-program genealogy**. Whether the historical *problem* is `continuous`, `reformulated`, or a `transformed_successor` still requires a separate `IDENTITY-CHECK`.

# 2. 1929: Lubin and the Senate inquiry form an early worker-trajectory evidence base

The 1929 Senate Committee report does more than discuss technological unemployment abstractly. In its treatment of unemployment it repeatedly directs readers to work by **Isador Lubin** in the Institute of Economics / Brookings context.

Primary container:

- U.S. Senate Committee on Education and Labor, *Unemployment in the United States*, 1929, with Senate Report No. 2072:
  - https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/unemployment-united-states-98/fulltext

The report explicitly tells readers to consult Lubin's report for a fuller treatment of cyclical unemployment and later cites his evidence on employment exchanges. This establishes that Lubin's research was part of the committee's evidential environment, even though the committee did not simply adopt one Lubin formulation.

Lubin's related 1929 publication was:

- Isador Lubin, *The Absorption of the Unemployed by American Industry* (Brookings Institution, 1929), 42 pp.
  - Google Books bibliographic/preview record: https://books.google.com/books?id=ffNAAAAAIAAJ

A later BLS advisory report, itself a contemporary federal document, describes the Lubin study as covering roughly 750 workers who had been laid off because of technical improvements in Baltimore, Worcester, and Chicago. The same report places it alongside Robert J. Myers's study of displaced skilled clothing cutters and Clague/Couper's study of workers displaced by plant shutdowns.

Primary federal source:

- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, *Report of the Advisory Committee on Employment Statistics*, Bulletin No. 542, 1931:
  - https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/report-advisory-committee-employment-statistics-4086/fulltext

### What this establishes

By the beginning of the 1930s, one recoverable evidence tradition was already tracking **what happens to particular displaced workers after separation** rather than treating technological unemployment only as an aggregate stock.

This supports a bounded continuity claim at the level of research object:

```text
displaced worker
→ time out of work
→ occupational movement
→ new job / lower-skilled job / no reemployment
```

It does **not** yet establish a direct institutional chain from Lubin's 1929 work to the 1937 WPA National Research Project.

**Evidence strength:**

- high for Senate use of Lubin's research as part of its evidential field;
- high for the BLS advisory report's description of the study;
- medium for any stronger claim that Lubin's 1929 project directly shaped the later WPA programme without further archival evidence.

# 3. 1932–33 BLS: technological change becomes a repeatable sector-study object

Before the WPA National Research Project, BLS was already publishing industry-specific studies that explicitly linked technological change, productivity, and employment opportunity.

## 3.1 Postal Service, 1932

Witt Bowden, *Technological Changes and Employment in the United States Postal Service*, BLS Bulletin No. 574, December 1932:

- https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/technological-changes-employment-united-states-postal-service-4104
- searchable full text: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/technological-changes-employment-united-states-postal-service-4104/fulltext

The study states that its primary purpose is to estimate changes in labour productivity and the effects of those changes on the volume of employment, requiring analysis of the technological changes affecting both.

## 3.2 Electric-lamp industry, 1933

Witt Bowden, *Technological Changes and Employment in the Electric-Lamp Industry*, BLS Bulletin No. 593, October 1933:

- https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/technological-changes-employment-electric-lamp-industry-4114
- searchable full text: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/technological-changes-employment-electric-lamp-industry-4114/fulltext

In the letter of transmittal, Commissioner Isador Lubin identifies the lamp study as **one of a series** undertaken to determine how technological changes in industry affect output per worker and opportunities for employment.

### Problem-structure consequence

This BLS programme is not merely asking a binary question:

```text
Does machinery destroy jobs?
```

It operationalizes a more specific empirical relation:

```text
identified technical change
+ output per worker
+ industry output
+ employment volume
+ occupational composition
```

The object is therefore becoming repeatable across sectors and measurable through production/employment records.

**Evidence strength:** high for document identity and institutional purpose; exact quotations should still be scan-bound before final freeze because FRASER full text is OCR.

# 4. 1932 Weintraub: a direct personnel antecedent to the 1937 Project

David Weintraub published:

- `The Displacement of Workers through Increases in Efficiency and Their Absorption by Industry, 1920–1931`, *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 27, no. 180 (December 1932): 383–400, DOI `10.1080/01621459.1932.10502241`.

Publisher issue record:

- https://www.tandfonline.com/toc/uasa20/27/180

NBER's contemporary annual-report material identifies the article as a **by-product of the labour-market study** under Leo Wolman and describes Weintraub as Wolman's assistant.

NBER historical annual-report index:

- https://www.nber.org/about-nber/history/historical-archives/annual-reports-director-research

NBER chapter/PDF surfaced from the annual-report archive:

- https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c4151/c4151.pdf

The key point here is not merely topical resemblance. The same named researcher who was producing quantitative work on efficiency, displacement, and absorption in 1932 becomes director of the federal National Research Project a few years later.

### Current relation claim

```text
Weintraub / NBER labour-market work, 1932
        ↓
Weintraub / WPA National Research Project, 1937
```

has **strong personnel continuity**.

But personnel continuity alone does not prove unchanged problem identity. The 1937 Project statement in fact shows explicit methodological reformulation.

**Evidence strength:**

- high for Weintraub authorship, date, and publication identity;
- high for NBER's contemporary statement that the paper arose from its labour-market research;
- high for Weintraub's later directorship of the WPA Project;
- insufficient by itself for a `continuous` Problem History relation.

# 5. The WPA National Research Project: institutional integration, not an isolated study

Primary source:

- Irving Kaplan, *The Research Program of the National Research Project* (WPA National Research Project, August 1937):
  - FRASER record: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/research-program-national-research-project-6639
  - searchable text: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/research-program-national-research-project-6639/fulltext

The publication identifies **David Weintraub** as Project Director and **Irving Kaplan** as Associate Director.

The Project statement says the programme was established under WPA authority to collect and analyse evidence bearing on employment, unemployment, and relief. It names as cooperating federal agencies the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Bureau of Mines, Railroad Retirement Board, Social Security Board, Department of Commerce, Federal Trade Commission, and others. It also names private cooperating institutions including the **National Bureau of Economic Research** and the Industrial Research Department of the University of Pennsylvania.

The programme reports more than 700 manufacturing plants surveyed and more than 20,000 worker employment histories collected.

### Direct evidence of study continuation

The programme states that some manufacturing studies were being prepared **in cooperation with BLS**. It also says that, under a cooperative arrangement with the **NBER**, the National Research Project was preparing reports on brick, cement, lumber, flour, and beet-sugar industries for which NBER had undertaken earlier surveys.

This is stronger than thematic similarity:

```text
previous NBER surveys
        ↓ explicit cooperative arrangement
WPA National Research Project continuation / extension
```

and:

```text
BLS technological/productivity study programme
        ↔ cooperation
WPA National Research Project
```

The federal research field was therefore institutionally networked rather than newly invented in one 1937 report.

### Date-control warning

FRASER's searchable OCR of the 1937 programme says the National Research Program was established in October 1936 and the specific Project organized in December 1936. Some catalogue/series metadata elsewhere gives a different December year. Until the scan itself is checked at image level, this packet avoids making the exact organizational month/year do argumentative work. The secure claim is that the Project existed and was operating before the August 1937 programme statement.

# 6. Actor-explicit category critique: `technological unemployment` is itself declared analytically dangerous

This is the most important new formulation evidence.

Kaplan's 1937 programme statement opens by saying that the Project's general problem had often been approached through the special categories `technological unemployment` or `displacement of labor` by machine techniques. It immediately argues that those labels can pull unemployment out of its proper economic context and make technological development look self-generating and employment effects mechanically determined.

The statement then works through cases where a loading machine is installed but the counterfactual might be mine closure; where another mine may suffer indirect displacement; where production expands after mechanization; and where the workers initially separated later follow different employment trajectories.

It concludes that isolating `technologically unemployed` workers from the wider employed/unemployed population is no easier than isolating a special technological-unemployment problem from its social and economic context. It explicitly rejects the familiar `machines versus men` framing and insists on a comprehensive economic approach.

Primary passage location in FRASER OCR:

- *Research Program*, opening discussion, searchable lines corresponding to printed pp. 1–3:
  - https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/research-program-national-research-project-6639/fulltext

### Actor problem, reconstructed conservatively

A safe actor-constrained paraphrase is:

> Given a measurable change in labour requirements associated with a technical change, how do output, market conditions, industry structure, labour supply, and worker movement jointly determine whether employment opportunities actually shrink, for whom, and for how long?

This paraphrase is ours; it is not a quotation.

### Why this is a genuine reformulation

The Project is not merely choosing one answer to a settled question. It challenges the adequacy of the question category itself.

```text
special problem:
"technological unemployment"
        ↓ critique
observable machine displacement is causally ambiguous
        ↓
reformulated research object:
industrial technique
× unit labour requirement
× output
× economic conditions
× labour-market incidence
× worker trajectory
```

This is a much stronger historical transformation than simple vocabulary drift.

It also supplies a real Problem History case of:

```text
actor retains inherited vocabulary
while explicitly rejecting the inherited problem decomposition
```

That should be preserved for later `IDENTITY-CHECK` fixtures.

**Evidence strength:** high for actor/institutional formulation, with the standard OCR caveat for exact quotation.

# 7. The Project makes `observability` a methodological problem before the NRC synthesis

The 1937 Project statement is more explicit than the current Episode B packet about why the category is difficult to observe.

Its examples show that:

- a machine-associated layoff may be confounded with an alternative plant closure;
- technological change in one firm can displace workers indirectly elsewhere;
- mechanization can coincide with increased production rather than reduced employment;
- identifying a group as initially displaced does not determine which individuals remain unemployed after occupational and geographic movement;
- few job seekers can simply be labelled by the original source of their unemployment.

Therefore `administrative observability` is not merely our later summary of the 1937 NRC report. The WPA Project itself treats classification and causal attribution as part of the research problem.

### Method consequence for Problem History

This separates three layers that later histories can too easily collapse:

```text
technical change occurred
!=
a worker was separated because of it
!=
the worker remained technologically unemployed
```

and:

```text
historian can reconstruct a causal chain
!=
local actor could observe or name that causal chain
```

# 8. Direct bridge into the National Resources Committee

David Weintraub and Harold L. Posner, *Unemployment and Increasing Productivity* (1937), was issued under the National Research Project and explicitly **prepared for the National Resources Committee** as a report on technological trends and their social implications.

Bibliographic record:

- Eduskunnan kirjasto / Finna: https://eduskunnankirjasto.finna.fi/Record/ekk.993057144006250

The work was then incorporated as Section V, `Unemployment and Increasing Productivity`, in:

- U.S. National Resources Committee, *Technological Trends and National Policy, Including the Social Implications of New Inventions* (1937).

Thus the following edge is documentary, not inferred:

```text
WPA National Research Project
→ Weintraub/Posner report prepared for NRC
→ NRC technology-policy synthesis
```

This substantially strengthens the institutional genealogy behind the 1937 formulation already documented in `1928-1937-technological-unemployment.md`.

# 10. What changed in the answer space?

This bridge shows a change not only in causal description but in what a legitimate answer could look like.

## 10.1 1929 congressional / labour formulations

Prominent answers include:

- relief and prevention;
- employment exchanges;
- stabilization;
- reserve funds;
- burden-sharing for displaced workers;
- joint employer/labour/public action.

## 10.2 1932–33 sector-study formulation

A legitimate answer increasingly requires:

- plant or industry data;
- production/output measures;
- labour-time and productivity measures;
- tracing occupational effects;
- separating output change from technical change.

## 10.3 1937 National Research Project formulation

A legitimate answer must also handle:

- counterfactuals such as mechanize-or-close;
- indirect displacement;
- worker mobility after initial separation;
- industry and economy-wide output relations;
- labour supply;
- historical economic relationships;
- validity of analytical categories themselves.

Thus `technological unemployment` partly changes from a named social condition into a **research problem about how not to misclassify causal relations**.

# 11. Hindsight / failure risks exposed by this bridge

## 11.1 `Same researcher = same problem`

Unsafe. Weintraub supplies direct personnel continuity, but the 1937 Project explicitly reformulates the analytical object.

## 11.2 `Institutional cooperation = intellectual agreement`

Unsafe. BLS, NBER, WPA, and NRC cooperation shows research infrastructure, not consensus on causal theory or policy.

## 11.3 `A federal project called technological unemployment a problem, therefore it accepted the category`

Unsafe. The Project explicitly treats the isolated category as analytically misleading unless embedded in a broader economic context.

## 11.4 `Industry case study proves aggregate employment effect`

Unsafe. BLS and WPA repeatedly distinguish productivity, output, employment volume, and worker trajectories.

## 11.5 `Initial displacement = continuing unemployment`

Unsafe. The Project follows the later trajectories of displaced workers and treats the label as unstable across time.

## 11.6 `1929 Senate inquiry directly caused the 1937 NRC report`

Not established.

## 11.7 `Lubin at BLS proves his 1929 study became BLS policy`

Too strong without administrative records, correspondence, or explicit programme statements.

## 11.8 `NBER→WPA continuation means every earlier NBER problem survived unchanged`

Unsafe. The Project can continue data collections while changing the problem decomposition.

## 11.9 `OCR date = frozen institutional chronology`

Unsafe where FRASER metadata and OCR disagree. Use the scan before exact organizational dates become argumentative evidence.

## Bottom line

The 1937 federal formulation is no longer only a later endpoint that resembles the 1928–29 debate. A recoverable research infrastructure connects **Weintraub's 1932 NBER labour-market work, BLS technological-employment studies, the WPA National Research Project, and the National Resources Committee**.

But the most historically important finding is that this infrastructure did not simply preserve a question. It **made the inherited category itself problematic**. By 1937, the Project was asking not merely whether machines displaced workers, but whether `technological unemployment` could even be isolated from output, market conditions, industry structure, labour supply, and post-displacement worker trajectories without generating an artificial `machines versus men` problem.

That is strong evidence for **problem reformulation inside an institutionally continuous research field**—exactly the distinction this repository needs to preserve.
