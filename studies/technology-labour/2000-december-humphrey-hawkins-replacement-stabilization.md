# December 2000: post–Humphrey-Hawkins replacement text stabilizes

Status: **M2 implementation / disappearance genealogy / late-stage legislative-text recovery**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet extends the existing 1994–2001 Humphrey–Hawkins implementation/disappearance chain. Earlier work recovered a 13 Jul 2000 Senate Banking redesign markup object but not its text. The new evidence narrows the unresolved transmission gap from the other end: by **5 Dec 2000**, the replacement architecture had stabilized in parallel House and Senate legislative texts.

Researcher-side shorthand below—`late-stage text stabilization` and `continuity-language / mechanism-change split`—is analytical vocabulary, not actor terminology.

## 1. Primary-source control

### S. 3274, introduced 5 Dec 2000

Official GPO text:
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-106s3274is/html/BILLS-106s3274is.htm

S.3274 was introduced by Senator Wayne Allard for himself and Senators Phil Gramm, Paul Sarbanes, John Kerry, Richard Shelby, Rick Santorum, Rod Grams, Ben Nighthorse Campbell, and Daniel Inouye.

Relevant §1003, GovInfo HTML lines 5032–5073:

- Federal Reserve Act §2A is cut back to its first sentence;
- a new §2B is inserted;
- the Chair appears at semiannual hearings;
- routine House and Senate appearances alternate by year/date;
- the other committee may request a follow-up appearance;
- the Board sends the written report to both banking committees;
- the report covers monetary policy and economic developments/prospects, taking account of employment, unemployment, production, investment, real income, productivity, exchange rates, trade/payments, and prices;
- mandatory money/credit growth ranges and their old revision/deviation machinery are not restored.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct legislative text**.

Boundary: this establishes the 5 Dec Senate text, not the unrecovered 13 Jul committee print.

### H.R. 5640, House-passed 5 Dec 2000

Official GPO text:
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-106hr5640eh/html/BILLS-106hr5640eh.htm

Relevant §1003, GovInfo HTML lines 5051–5093.

The material Federal Reserve architecture is the same as S.3274 §1003. Parallel House and Senate texts therefore existed on the same date before Senate passage of the House bill.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct House-passed text**.

### House debate, 5 Dec 2000

Official Congressional Record:
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2000-12-05/pdf/CREC-2000-12-05-pt1-PgH11960.pdf

Relevant printed pages: H11985–H11986, H11989, H11991–H11992.

Chairman James Leach says the House is bringing the package back after consultation with the Senate and expects enactment. He describes roughly 45 Executive/independent-regulator reporting requirements as being renewed, **some with slight changes**, including the Fed semiannual monetary-policy report. He also treats congressional review of Fed monetary policy as a particularly important oversight responsibility.

Ranking Member John LaFalce describes the package as containing bipartisan language **reauthorizing the Humphrey-Hawkins report**.

But the section-by-section analysis printed in the same Record describes §1003 as a **new reporting requirement to replace the expired provisions** relating to the semiannual Humphrey-Hawkins reports.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct contemporaneous actor and legislative explanatory material / page-image audited**.

### Senate passage, 7 Dec 2000

Official Congressional Record, printed S11681:
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2000-12-07/pdf/CREC-2000-12-07.pdf

Senate Banking Chairman Phil Gramm states that H.R.5640 had passed the House on 5 Dec; companion Senate legislation had been introduced the same day; the package was the product of bipartisan negotiations in both chambers; it renewed the Fed monetary-policy report; and it removed older indicators, explicitly naming money-supply measures as indicators he considered no longer useful.

The Senate then passed H.R.5640.

Evidence strength: **very high / direct principal legislative-manager statement**.

Boundary: Gramm’s judgment about the usefulness of money-supply indicators is an actor formulation, not an objective historical verdict.

### Final statute

Public Law 106-569, approved 27 Dec 2000:
https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-106publ569

The enacted §1003 preserves the same core structure already present in the 5 Dec texts.

## 3. Fresh finding B — “renew / reauthorize / replace” coexist in actor language

The same legislative event is described contemporaneously as:

```text
Leach:
renew reporting requirements,
some with slight changes

LaFalce:
reauthorize the Humphrey-Hawkins report

section-by-section:
new reporting requirement
to replace expired Humphrey-Hawkins provisions
```

This is not noise. The formulations operate at different institutional resolutions.

Actors can recognize continuity in the semiannual reporting rhythm, congressional monetary-policy oversight, a recognizable “Humphrey-Hawkins report” practice, and Fed–Congress/public dialogue, while the clauses simultaneously remove mandatory money/credit growth ranges and old range revision/deviation explanation machinery and create a new §2B.

Therefore:

```text
continuity language
!= proof of mechanism continuity

replacement language
!= proof of problem disappearance
```

Actor labels alone cannot settle episode identity.

## 4. Fresh finding C — accountability changes are asymmetric

The replacement system separates personal testimony from written-report symmetry:

```text
routine personal Chair testimony:
one scheduled committee per semiannual cycle,
alternating by year/date

written report:
both committees every semiannual cycle

other chamber:
may request a follow-up appearance
```

So the change cannot be represented on one “accountability stronger/weaker” scale.

```text
routine duplicated personal appearances
        ↓ reduced

written bicameral information flow
        ↓ preserved

optional second-chamber appearance
        ↓ preserved

mandatory monetary-range accountability
        ↓ removed
```

This reinforces the earlier researcher-side concept of `accountability recomposition`, now with late-stage clause-level bicameral text.

## 5. Later institutional reconstruction

David E. Lindsey, *A Modern History of FOMC Communication: 1975–2002* (Federal Reserve Board, 2003):

https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/a-modern-history-fomc-communication-1975-2002-180/fulltext

Lindsey describes monetary ranges as having become operationally unimportant by the 1990s, notes their 1994 reinterpretation as benchmarks, and states that the mandatory ranges ended when the Humphrey-Hawkins reporting requirements expired in 2000.

Evidence strength: **high-quality later institutional reconstruction**.

Use: supports the long-run functional-hollowing chronology.

Boundary: it does not substitute for contemporaneous actors when reconstructing what Congress and the Fed thought they were doing in 2000.

## 6. Evidence and actor-layer summary

### Historical actors directly establish

- Leach: monetary-policy oversight remains highly important; reporting is renewed with some changes.
- LaFalce: the Humphrey-Hawkins report is being reauthorized.
- House/Senate explanatory material: §1003 is a new requirement replacing expired provisions.
- S.3274/H.R.5640: old §2A machinery is not restored; new §2B is substituted.
- Gramm: the compromise preserves reporting while dropping money-supply indicators he calls outdated.
- Congress: House text passes Senate and becomes law.

### Later institutional reconstruction

- Lindsey 2003: ranges had lost operational importance before statutory expiry.

## 8. Hindsight risks

1. Do not choose “renew,” “reauthorize,” or “replace” as the single correct historical verb; they track different levels.
2. Do not treat “Humphrey-Hawkins report” as a fixed legal object across versions.
3. Do not back-project the 5 Dec text into the unrecovered July print.
4. Do not infer all S.3274 sponsors shared Gramm’s monetary-aggregate rationale.
5. Do not convert Gramm’s “no longer useful” into settled economic fact.
6. Do not summarize reduced routine appearances as uniform accountability decline.
