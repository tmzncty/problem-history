# 1962–1963 Manpower Administration: from manpower planning to integrated federal program

Status: **M2 transition/source packet / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet fills one of the explicit source debts in `1962-1963-oam-omat-manpower-program-transition.md`: **Secretary's Order No. 3-63**, signed 19 February 1963 and later printed in the *Federal Register*.

The document matters because it supplies the missing administrative layer between:

```text
15 Jun 1962
Secretary's Order 17-62 establishes OMAT

        ↓

19 Feb 1963
Secretary's Order 3-63 establishes the Manpower Administration,
keeps Order 17-62 in force but subordinates its delegated authority
to the new Manpower Administrator,
and places OMAT inside the new administration

        ↓

Apr 1963
Secretary's Order 4-63 cancels Order 17-62,
keeps the offices created under earlier orders,
and explicitly modifies their functions/responsibilities
```

This is stronger than an office-name chronology. It provides direct actor-side evidence that **the same office and the same earlier establishing order could persist while authority, institutional role, and program context were already being changed**.

The main methodological increment is therefore:

```text
document/order continuity
!=
authority continuity
!=
institutional-role continuity
!=
problem continuity
```

It also sharpens the historical transformation from a bounded `automation/employment` administrative object toward a coordinated federal `manpower` field without claiming that automation disappeared or that all actors accepted one manpower formulation.

---

## 1. What this packet adds to the existing bridge

Existing repository work has already established:

- April 1961: the Department of Labor publicly/operationally creates the Office of Automation and Manpower (OAM), with a second October 1961 establishment instrument still unresolved;
- March 1962: OAM remains an explicit Department unit;
- June 1962: contemporary Department evidence says OAM is abolished and its functions assigned to the new Office of Manpower, Automation, and Training (OMAT);
- April 1963: Secretary's Order 4-63 embeds the office in a comprehensive Manpower Program and explicitly modifies functions/responsibilities.

What remained missing was the administrative event that created the **Manpower Administration itself** and specified where OMAT sat inside it.

Secretary's Order 3-63 provides that missing layer.

It also prevents an overly simple chronology in which the Department jumps directly from:

```text
OMAT
→ comprehensive Manpower Program
```

The contemporaneous sequence is more structured:

```text
OMAT
→ OMAT subordinated inside a new Manpower Administration
→ detailed functional allocation under the comprehensive Manpower Program
```

The intermediate step is historically meaningful because it changes **who controls the office, what the office is institutionally for, and what other policy streams are integrated with it**.

## 2. Primary source: Secretary's Order No. 3-63

### Source

U.S. Department of Labor, **Secretary's Order No. 3-63, "Manpower Administration—Establishment and Delegation of Authority."**

- signed at Washington, D.C.: **19 February 1963**;
- published: *Federal Register*, vol. 28, no. 77, **19 April 1963**;
- printed pp. **3899–3900**.

Public scan:

<https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/48/Federal_Register_1963-04-19-_Vol_28_Iss_77_%28IA_sim_federal-register-find_1963-04-19_28_77%29.pdf>

Public official-style issue access is also discoverable through Federal Register/GovInfo mirrors; the page-image claims below were checked directly against printed pp. 3899–3900 on 15 September 2026 (UTC+8).

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound primary administrative source.**

The order is an administrative instrument. It is excellent evidence for organizational authority, statutory responsibility, institutional role, and the Department's formal program categories. It is **not** a transparent record of how displaced workers, unions, employers, or individual researchers formulated their own problems.

## 3. Order 3-63 does not cancel Order 17-62; it subordinates it

This is the most important source-critical detail in the new material.

Paragraph 2(b) of Order 3-63 distinguishes several earlier orders. Some are cancelled. But **Secretary's Order 17-62 is not cancelled at this point**.

Instead, Order 17-62 and several other instruments are modified so that authority delegated under them becomes **subordinate and subject to the direction of the Manpower Administrator**.

That gives a direct contemporaneous administrative relation:

```text
Order 17-62
still operative
        +
its delegated authority is recontextualized
under a new superior authority
```

This matters because a later historical summary could easily represent the February 1963 reorganization as either:

- a clean institutional break; or
- simple continuation until Order 4-63 finally cancels 17-62.

Neither description is precise enough.

The order itself records a third condition:

> an earlier authority remains legally/administratively in force while the hierarchy in which that authority operates has changed.

For Problem History, that means:

```text
same governing document still active
!=
same practical/institutional role
```

and therefore certainly does not establish:

```text
same problem continues unchanged
```

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound.**

Boundary: this establishes a change in authority relation. It does not by itself tell us which substantive OMAT activities changed on 19 February 1963.

## 4. The new Manpower Administration explicitly contains OMAT, BES, and BAT

Order 3-63 formally establishes a **Manpower Administration** in the Department of Labor.

It states that the Administration is composed of:

- the **Office of Manpower, Automation and Training (OMAT)**;
- the **Bureau of Employment Security (BES)**;
- the **Bureau of Apprenticeship and Training (BAT)**.

This is not just a change of umbrella name.

The three components place several previously separable administrative problems into one superior structure:

- planning/research/evaluation;
- employment-service and program operations;
- apprenticeship/training;
- statutory training and adjustment responsibilities;
- wider employment-security and manpower functions.

The order therefore gives direct institutional evidence for **integration of previously distinct responsibility streams**.

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound.**

Boundary: integration into one administration is evidence of institutional coordination, not proof that the historical problems handled by all three components became one problem.

---

## 5. OMAT's new institutional role becomes unusually visible

Order 3-63 does not merely list OMAT as one of three component organizations.

It defines a dual office:

- the **Deputy Manpower Administrator for Planning, Research and Evaluation**;
- who is also the **Director of OMAT**.

Meanwhile the Deputy Manpower Administrator for **Program Operations** is also the Administrator of BES.

This structural division is extremely useful.

At minimum, it supports the following actor-side institutional reconstruction:

```text
Manpower Administration

├─ planning / research / evaluation
│     ↳ OMAT director is also the deputy responsible for this domain
│
├─ program operations
│     ↳ BES administrator is also the deputy responsible for this domain
│
└─ apprenticeship / training
      ↳ BAT remains a component bureau
```

That is stronger evidence than the later archival shorthand that OMAT was a "nonprogrammatic" component, because the contemporaneous order itself assigns OMAT's director the planning/research/evaluation deputyship.

### What this changes in the current hypothesis

Earlier repository work correctly treated the addition of `Training` in the OMAT name as insufficient evidence of substantive change.

Order 3-63 now supplies a different kind of evidence: by February 1963, OMAT is formally situated as the **planning/research/evaluation node inside a larger manpower apparatus**.

This supports a more precise `problem-role migration` hypothesis:

```text
automation/manpower office
→ planning/research/evaluation component
inside a broader manpower administration
```

But it still does **not** tell us exactly when that role first emerged. Order 17-62 remains necessary to determine whether this planning/research function was already explicit in June 1962.

### Evidence strength

**High for formal institutional role; medium for any claim about problem transformation.**

The institutional role is explicit. The problem-history interpretation remains a researcher reconstruction that must be tested against programme records and actor formulations.

## 6. The Manpower Administration aggregates multiple statutory problem streams

Paragraph 4 delegates to the Manpower Administrator responsibility for Department manpower programs and activities under a strikingly broad set of statutes and authorities.

Among the directly named responsibilities are:

- worker adjustment provisions under the **Trade Expansion Act of 1962**;
- the **Manpower Development and Training Act of 1962**;
- the **Area Redevelopment Act of 1961**;
- the **Public Works Acceleration Act of 1962**;
- agricultural and migrant-labor authorities;
- the **Wagner-Peyser Act**;
- relevant Social Security Act titles;
- the Federal Unemployment Tax Act;
- immigration/nationality responsibilities;
- servicemen's readjustment;
- national and D.C. apprenticeship statutes.

The order then directs the Manpower Administrator to coordinate manpower activities across bureaus and offices so as to ensure integration in a **"comprehensive Manpower Program."**

### Why this matters for problem identity

The administrative object visible here cannot be reduced to:

> unemployment caused by automation.

Nor can it be reduced to:

> retraining workers whose skills became obsolete.

The formal program context now combines difficulties arising from:

- technological change;
- regional redevelopment;
- trade adjustment;
- unemployment and employment services;
- apprenticeship/training;
- migration and labor supply;
- public works;
- occupational adjustment;
- multiple statutory benefit and labor-market systems.

A cautious researcher-level reconstruction is therefore:

```text
How can the Department coordinate multiple policy instruments,
knowledge systems, and operating bureaus around national manpower
planning, adjustment, development, and utilization?
```

This is **not** presented as an actor quotation. It is an analytic summary of the responsibilities the order explicitly aggregates.

### Evidence strength

**High for the aggregation of formal responsibilities.**

**Medium for the claim that this constitutes one transformed historical problem**, because a bureaucratic integration may coordinate several still-distinct problems rather than merge them conceptually.

## 7. The Manpower Administrator becomes a coordinating and evaluative authority

Order 3-63 does more than place statutes under one name.

The Manpower Administrator is assigned responsibility for:

- directing and supervising;
- setting objectives;
- reviewing and evaluating performance;
- functionally supervising and coordinating manpower activities across Department bureaus and offices;
- integrating those activities into the comprehensive Manpower Program;
- reviewing approvals/disapprovals of training programs and projects when requested by government agencies, labor groups, or employer groups.

This makes **coordination and evaluation themselves part of the administrative answer space**.

The Department is not only administering training places or responding after displacement. It is building a higher-order capacity for:

```text
program integration
+ objective setting
+ cross-office supervision
+ evaluation
+ review of training projects
```

That is a different administrative object from a simple machinery-displacement inquiry.

### Method implication

A historical problem can change because the **required knowledge and administrative capacity needed to answer it change**.

Here the answer space increasingly presupposes that the federal state must be able to coordinate heterogeneous programs and evaluate their performance.

That is evidence of transformation in **problem responsibility** even if older concerns remain visible.

## 8. September 1962: "sound manpower planning based on research" is already actor-explicit

Order 3-63 should not be treated as the first moment when a broad manpower-planning formulation appears.

A useful pre-order checkpoint is President John F. Kennedy's statement to the new **National Advisory Committee on Manpower Development and Training**, **27 September 1962**.

Public text:

<https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/statement-the-president-members-the-new-national-advisory-committee-manpower-development>

Kennedy presents the MDTA simultaneously as a measure that:

- fosters technological development;
- strengthens domestic prosperity;
- addresses a basic cause of long-term unemployment;
- encourages **sound manpower planning based on research**;
- reduces waste of national human resources.

This is important because it prevents another false chronology:

```text
1962 = only displaced-worker retraining
1963 = suddenly manpower planning
```

By September 1962, a broader planning/research rationale is already explicit at the presidential level.

But the source also prevents the opposite simplification. Kennedy still links the programme to long-term unemployment and technological development. The older difficulty has not vanished; it is being placed in a larger rationale.

### Evidence strength

**High / direct actor statement.**

Boundary: a presidential public statement does not establish the internal administrative division of OMAT or prove that all Department officials/committee members shared one formulation.

## 9. A two-stage 1963 reorganization is now visible

The existing packet relied heavily on Secretary's Order 4-63 because that order explicitly says earlier orders may be cancelled while offices remain and functions are modified.

Order 3-63 now shows that the 1963 reorganization is better treated as at least a **two-stage documentary process**.

### Stage 1 — Order 3-63, 19 February 1963

- creates the Manpower Administration;
- retains OMAT/BES/BAT as components;
- modifies Order 17-62 by subordinating delegated authority;
- makes OMAT's director the deputy responsible for planning, research, and evaluation;
- integrates broad statutory/program responsibilities under the Manpower Administrator.

### Stage 2 — Order 4-63, April 1963

- cancels Order 17-62;
- explicitly keeps the offices established under cancelled orders;
- modifies their functions/responsibilities;
- elaborates the comprehensive Manpower Program and detailed functional allocations.

### Why this distinction matters

If only Order 4-63 is read, the transformation can look instantaneous:

```text
old order cancelled
→ functions modified
```

Order 3-63 instead shows an intermediate state in which:

```text
old order still survives
+
old office still survives
+
new superior structure already exists
+
authority has already been subordinated/recontextualized
```

So the repository should not assign a single clean date to `the` transformation from OMAT to the broader manpower field.

The evidence supports **layered administrative transformation**, not a single switch.

## 10. Contemporary implementation evidence: FY1964 Department report

A useful near-contemporary implementation source is the Department of Labor's FY1964 annual-report material for the Manpower Administration.

GovInfo carrier:

<https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-L-59a2c307a5cd8a4f0cd97b3c8bd64abc/pdf/GOVPUB-L-59a2c307a5cd8a4f0cd97b3c8bd64abc.pdf>

The searchable official record describes the Manpower Administration as established in early 1963 to better carry out Congress's intent in the MDTA and says it directs and coordinates the efforts of:

- BES;
- OMAT;
- BAT;

while also seeking greater cohesion across other Department manpower activities.

It further frames the MDTA mandate in terms of:

- determining skill requirements;
- occupational development;
- maximum utilization of worker skills;
- diversified training programs;
- expanding manpower research.

### Why this source is useful

Order 3-63 is authority/organization evidence.

The FY1964 report is evidence that the integrated structure was being **operationally narrated** one fiscal year later as a capacity for:

```text
coordination
+ training expansion
+ skill-requirement knowledge
+ worker utilization
+ manpower research
```

This supports the claim that the broader manpower object was not merely a paper reorganization.

### Evidence boundary

**High as an official Department source, but not yet page-bound in this packet.**

The current web carrier is large and was not stably rendered page-by-page in this run. Use the searchable official record for source mapping and claim support, but do not freeze exact quotations until the scan pages are bound.

## 14. Hindsight and source-critical risks

1. **Publication date = effective date.** Order 3-63 was signed 19 February but printed in the *Federal Register* on 19 April. Do not date the administrative event solely by publication.
2. **Same order still active = same authority.** Order 17-62 survived Order 3-63 but its delegated authority was subordinated.
3. **Same office name = same institutional role.** OMAT persists while becoming structurally tied to planning/research/evaluation inside a larger administration.
4. **One administration = one problem.** Manpower Administration coordinates multiple statutory/problem streams; integration does not erase their distinctions.
5. **Manpower = neutral synonym for workers.** `Manpower` here is a federal administrative/knowledge object involving supply, requirements, utilization, training, mobility, program coordination, and evaluation.
6. **Automation disappears in 1963.** OMAT retains `Automation` in its name and Order 4-63 still explicitly names automation as a research/problem source.
7. **Automation remains named = old problem unchanged.** Its institutional role is increasingly nested in a wider planning/evaluation apparatus.
8. **MDTA alone explains the Manpower Administration.** Order 3-63 explicitly aggregates MDTA with ARA, trade adjustment, Wagner-Peyser, apprenticeship, employment-security, migration, and other authorities.
9. **The federal administrative formulation represents all workers/labor.** This packet concerns one institutional actor layer; union and worker formulations must remain separately reconstructed.
10. **Later archival language = contemporaneous actor language.** NARA's `nonprogrammatic component` description is useful, but the strongest contemporaneous evidence is the Order 3-63 dual-role structure itself.
11. **One reorganization date = one conceptual transformation date.** The February and April 1963 orders reveal a layered transition rather than a clean single switch.

## 16. What remains uncertain

### Secretary's Order 17-62 is still the main missing document

Order 3-63 tells us that 17-62 existed, remained operative, and had authority delegated under it.

It still does not tell us:

- OMAT's exact June 1962 functional clauses;
- how much planning/research/evaluation language already existed in 17-62;
- whether the 1963 dual-role arrangement was genuinely new or an organizational restatement;
- exactly how MDTA, ARA, training, research, and employment-service responsibilities were divided in June 1962.

### Secretary's Order 5-61 remains necessary

The April/October 1961 OAM establishment discrepancy is still unresolved.

### The federal administrative field is not the whole historical field

The next mature Episode must still include competing actor formulations from:

- organized labor;
- displaced/at-risk workers where recoverable;
- employers;
- congressional critics;
- vocational educators;
- economists/automation researchers.

Otherwise the repository would risk mistaking the state-created governable object for the entire social problem.

## 18. Current provisional judgement

The new evidence does **not** show that the old automation problem simply became the manpower problem.

It establishes something more historically precise:

> By September 1962, the Administration publicly connected technological development and long-term unemployment to research-based manpower planning. On 19 February 1963, Secretary's Order 3-63 then established a Manpower Administration that incorporated OMAT, BES, and BAT; retained but subordinated authority under OMAT's existing Order 17-62; made OMAT's director simultaneously responsible for planning, research, and evaluation; and aggregated a wide range of training, employment-security, redevelopment, trade-adjustment, apprenticeship, and related authorities into a comprehensive Manpower Program. The later Order 4-63 further modified functions. The administrative genealogy is therefore increasingly direct, while a claim of unchanged problem identity becomes less plausible.

For Problem History, this is a particularly strong case of **genealogy becoming more certain at the same time that identity becomes more doubtful**.
