# 1965–1966 OMAT → OMPER: office-title disappearance without problem disappearance

Status: **M2 transition/source packet / disappearance test / not a frozen Problem Episode**

Pilot: technology–labour

This packet follows the repository's 1961–1963 OAM/OMAT/manpower chain into the next institutional transition:

```text
14 Dec 1965
Secretary's Order 35-65
OMAT abolished
        ↓
Office of Manpower Policy, Evaluation, and Research (OMPER)
        ↓
"Automation" disappears from the office title
```

That naming change is an unusually useful test for a core Problem History question:

> When a historically salient problem-word disappears from the name of the institution that had carried it, has the problem itself disappeared?

The new evidence strongly says **no**.

The best-supported reconstruction is not simple persistence and not disappearance. It is a **change in institutional role and problem salience**:

```text
OMAT-era administrative identity
automation + manpower + training
        ↓
Dec 1965 reorganization
        ↓
OMPER
policy + planning + research + evaluation
        ↓

automation / technological change
remains an explicit research and adjustment object
inside a much wider manpower field

while

the foreground action problem increasingly becomes
hard-core unemployment + disadvantage + employability
+ skill shortage + human-resource development
```

Provisional researcher-side relation language:

- **institutional succession:** direct / strong;
- **problem disappearance:** rejected by direct evidence;
- **simple problem continuity:** also too strong;
- **best current description:** `problem-role migration`, `nesting`, or `decentering` inside a broader manpower/human-resources field;
- **formal relation type:** keep provisional until the text of Secretary's Order 35-65 and internal reorganization memoranda are recovered.

The central methodological increment is:

```text
term disappears from office name
!=
problem disappears

problem remains in research portfolio
!=
problem remains the dominant action problem

administrative succession
!=
unchanged problem identity
```

## 1. Repository state and why this is new

Existing technology–labour packets already establish:

- 1961: an Office of Automation and Manpower (OAM) becomes operational;
- 1962: OAM is replaced by the Office of Manpower, Automation, and Training (OMAT);
- 1963: OMAT is placed inside the new Manpower Administration as a planning/research/evaluation node;
- 1963: the Department defines a comprehensive Manpower Program in which automation is one causal/research input among many;
- 1964–1966: automation/cybernation remains a contested public and federal problem field, including the National Commission on Technology, Automation, and Economic Progress.

The unresolved disappearance test was later in the same administrative genealogy:

```text
OMAT
Office of Manpower, Automation, and Training
        ↓
OMPER
Office of Manpower Policy, Evaluation, and Research
```

A title-only chronology creates a tempting but unsafe story:

```text
1961–65: federal automation problem
1965–66: "automation" vanishes from office name
therefore automation problem fades/disappears
```

The FY1966 Department of Labor record makes that inference untenable.

## 2. Administrative genealogy: Order 35-65 is a real succession edge

### Source

National Archives and Records Administration, **Record Group 369: Records of the Employment and Training Administration**, section 369.7, "Records of the Office of Policy, Evaluation, and Research, 1963–67."

Public administrative history:

<https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/369.html>

NARA records the following succession:

```text
Office of Automation and Manpower (OAM)
Secretary's Order 5-61, 25 Oct 1961
        ↓
Office of Manpower, Automation, and Training (OMAT)
Secretary's Order 17-62, 15 Jun 1962
        ↓
OMAT placed in Manpower Administration
Secretary's Order 3-63, 19 Feb 1963
        ↓
OMAT abolished
Secretary's Order 35-65, 14 Dec 1965
        ↓
Office of Manpower Policy, Evaluation, and Research (OMPER)
```

NARA further records that OMPER was itself abolished by Secretary's Order 14-67 on 23 October 1967 and its functions vested in the Office of the Associate Manpower Administrator.

### Evidence strength

**High for archival administrative genealogy; later reconstruction, not 1965 actor wording.**

This is authoritative evidence that Order 35-65 formally superseded OMAT with OMPER. It is not sufficient by itself to reconstruct:

- the exact wording of Order 35-65;
- which OMAT functions were transferred unchanged;
- which were added, removed, or regrouped;
- why the word `Automation` was removed from the office title;
- whether contemporaries understood the change as a conceptual rejection of automation as a problem.

Therefore the full text of Order 35-65 remains an explicit source debt.

## 3. Contemporary Department explanation: the reorganization was about analytical depth and program/staff separation

### Primary source

U.S. Department of Labor, **Fifty-Fourth Annual Report, Fiscal Year 1966** (Washington: U.S. Government Printing Office, 1967).

Official GovInfo HTML with printed-page markers:

<https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-L-31a527c58f3b148646b3ef983acb693b/html/GOVPUB-L-31a527c58f3b148646b3ef983acb693b.htm>

Printed p. 11 states that the Department continually reviewed its organizational structure as programs changed. It then identifies the OMPER transition directly:

- OMPER was established **replacing OMAT**;
- the change was intended to give the Manpower Administrator an **analytical staff capability of greater depth**;
- it was the culmination of modifications designed to place **operating program responsibility within a limited number of organizations** in the Manpower Administration.

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound contemporaneous departmental source.**

This is substantially stronger than inferring meaning from the office names. The Department's own near-contemporaneous administrative rationale is about:

```text
staff analysis depth
+
clarifying / concentrating program responsibility
```

not an announced conclusion that automation had ceased to matter.

### Boundary

The annual report is retrospective for FY1966. It is not the text of Order 35-65 itself. We should therefore write:

> the Department's FY1966 account explains the reorganization in these terms

not:

> Order 35-65 itself declared these exact motives.

## 4. OMPER's actor-explicit institutional problem is broader than automation

The FY1966 annual report's OMPER section begins at printed p. 66.

It describes OMPER as the Manpower Administrator's staff arm and primary adviser for:

- policy;
- planning;
- research;
- evaluation.

Its assigned work includes:

- maintaining plans for a comprehensive Department manpower program;
- research through staff, grants, and contracts;
- experimental and demonstration projects;
- converting project results into operational programs;
- program evaluation;
- manpower information;
- assistance to foreign manpower planning.

The report defines the fundamental purpose of OMPER's research program as providing information and ideas to improve action programs that:

1. reduce unemployment; and
2. anticipate and prepare for the manpower needs of a changing economy.

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound official source.**

### Problem-History significance

This is not merely OMAT with a shorter name.

The actor-explicit governed field is now organized around a meta-capacity:

```text
observe
→ research
→ plan
→ experiment
→ evaluate
→ feed results back into programs
```

That differs from a bounded question such as:

```text
Does automation displace workers?
```

or even:

```text
How should workers displaced by automation be retrained?
```

The change is therefore at least an institutional **recontextualization** of the automation problem.

But broader remit does not establish that the older problem vanished.

## 5. Direct disappearance test: automation and technological change remain inside the FY1966 research field

The same official report gives unusually direct evidence against a disappearance claim.

### 5.1 Manpower Administration summary, printed pp. 37–38

The Manpower Administration reports that completed contract research during FY1966 included:

- techniques for projecting local manpower requirements;
- **reasons for management decisions to automate**;
- **the impact of technological change on occupational skill requirements**;
- job-seeking behavior of blue-collar and white-collar workers.

Official HTML, printed pp. 37–38:

<https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-L-31a527c58f3b148646b3ef983acb693b/html/GOVPUB-L-31a527c58f3b148646b3ef983acb693b.htm>

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound for the FY1966 research portfolio.**

Boundary: because FY1966 began before the December 1965 reorganization, not every project listed here can automatically be attributed to OMPER after its formal creation. The source proves **research-program continuity across the transition**, but individual project start dates must be checked before claiming post-14-Dec initiation.

That distinction matters.

## 6. Strong post-transition evidence: OMPER is explicitly receiving technological-change research in October 1966

A later source removes the fiscal-year timing ambiguity.

### Source lead

Edward R. F. W. Crossman, Stephen Laner, Louis E. Davis, and Stanley H. Caplan,
**Evaluation of Changes in Skill-Profile and Job-Content Due to Technological Change: Methodology and Pilot Results from the Banking, Steel and Aerospace Industries** (University of California, Berkeley, October 1966), U.S. Department of Labor Contract No. 81-04-05.

A later BLS annotated bibliography identifies the report as submitted to the **Director, Office of Manpower Policy, Evaluation and Research, U.S. Department of Labor**, in October 1966 and summarizes it as research on automation, technological change, employment, and skill levels.

BLS bibliography full text:

<https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/productivity-a-selected-annotated-bibliography-4574/productivity-a-selected-annotated-bibliography-1965-71-498999/fulltext>

The original report is also catalogued/digitized through ERIC and library systems; its title page identifies the same Labor Department contract and OMPER recipient.

### Evidence strength

**High for bibliographic identity and post-transition institutional recipient; primary report page-image verification still pending for exact wording.**

This is chronologically decisive for the disappearance test:

```text
14 Dec 1965
"Automation" disappears from office name

        ↓

Oct 1966
OMPER is still the recipient/sponsor-side office for research explicitly titled
"... Due to Technological Change"
```

So the claim

```text
office-name deletion
→ research problem disappears
```

is directly falsified.

## 7. Another post-transition OMPER publication still explicitly frames automation as a manpower problem

The U.S. Government's Monthly Catalog records a 1966 publication:

Robert L. Heilbroner, **Automation in the Perspective of Long-Term Technological Change**.

It records the work as:

- a Seminar on Manpower Policy and Program;
- based on a Washington seminar dated 16 December 1965;
- published December 1966;
- issued under the Manpower Administration / **Office of Manpower Policy, Evaluation, and Research**.

GovInfo Monthly Catalog extract is discoverable under the February 1967 catalog; the Online Books Page also records the 1966 Labor Department publication and library page-image holdings.

Catalog lead:

<https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/browse?c=x&key=Business+communication+--+Automation+--+Government+policy+--+United+States&type=lcsubc>

### Evidence strength

**High bibliographic evidence for a post-transition OMPER-associated automation publication; content analysis pending direct page reading.**

This is especially useful because it is not merely an inherited FY1965 publication whose production began before OMPER existed.

### Important date boundary

The seminar itself occurred **16 December 1965**, only two days after the NARA date for Order 35-65. Publication occurred in 1966 under the successor office.

That timing makes it a particularly good source candidate for studying whether the new office treated automation as:

- an inherited old topic;
- a subordinate research stream;
- a still-central policy problem;
- or a historical phenomenon being reframed inside long-run technological change.

Do not decide among these possibilities from the title alone. Read the text next.

## 8. The word `automation` also survives in the research/publication ecology after the office-title change

The FY1966 annual report's information section lists **Manpower Implications of Automation** among Department publications handled in the manpower information program.

The underlying papers originated at the OECD North American Regional Conference on Manpower Implications of Automation (Washington, December 1964), and a 1965 edition was issued under OMAT.

That provenance creates an important source-critical caution:

```text
publication appears in OMPER-era annual report
!=
OMPER initiated the publication after Dec 1965
```

The item therefore supports continuity of the information/research corpus across the reorganization, but should **not** be used by itself as proof that OMPER independently launched a new automation project.

The October 1966 Crossman contract report and December 1966 Heilbroner publication are better post-transition tests.

### Method fixture

```text
successor office reports inherited publication
!=
successor office originated the research
```

## 9. What actually moves to the foreground in 1966: hard-core unemployment, disadvantage, and human-resource development

The most important result is not just that automation survives.

The same Department source shows that its **relative problem role changes**.

### 9.1 Secretary's FY1966 formulation, printed pp. 3–4

The Secretary's report emphasizes that aggregate employment was rising and unemployment had fallen to around 4 percent, yet a persistent body of "hard-core" unemployed people and poverty remained.

The document identifies major barriers including:

- inadequate education;
- lack of skills;
- age;
- physical or mental handicaps;
- bigotry.

It then explicitly rejects a previous approach that began with jobs and worked backward to people and announces a **human resources development** approach that begins with people, using individualized combinations of:

- counseling;
- job referral;
- basic education;
- occupational training;
- medical, psychological, or legal assistance.

The report directly ties this problem to civil-rights outcomes and socially produced disadvantage.

Official GovInfo HTML, printed pp. 3–4:

<https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-L-31a527c58f3b148646b3ef983acb693b/html/GOVPUB-L-31a527c58f3b148646b3ef983acb693b.htm>

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound actor formulation.**

Boundary: this is the Secretary's Department-wide formulation. It does not prove that all OMPER staff, BLS researchers, unions, employers, civil-rights organizations, or displaced workers used the same problem definition.

## 10. Manpower Administration's own summary confirms the salience shift

At printed p. 37, the Manpower Administration says its main efforts in FY1966 were directed toward training and employment of the unemployed and underemployed, with emphasis on **disadvantaged, hard-core unemployed individuals** needing special preparation and assistance.

The same summary also reports:

- a major skilled and semiskilled worker shortage;
- human-resources-development pilot programs;
- training and apprenticeship expansion;
- research on automation and technological skill change;
- new research on youth, Black participation in apprenticeship, older workers, farm workers, and low-income migration.

### Evidence strength

**High / direct / page-bound.**

### Reconstruction

This creates a stronger historical picture than either of the following:

```text
A. automation disappeared after 1965

B. nothing changed; automation simply continued
```

The evidence instead supports:

```text
research field:
automation / technological change remains askable and funded

        while

action-program foreground:
hard-core unemployment / disadvantage / employability /
skill shortage / human-resource development rises in salience
```

That is a **change in relative problem position**.

## 11. Actor problem vs researcher reconstruction

### Historical actors directly support

The Department directly states that:

- OMPER replaced OMAT;
- the reorganization was to deepen analytical staff capability and reorganize operating responsibility;
- OMPER's institutional remit was policy/planning/research/evaluation for a comprehensive manpower program;
- FY1966 research still included automation decisions and technological-change skill effects;
- the action agenda emphasized hard-core unemployment and disadvantaged groups;
- the Secretary promoted a people-first human-resources-development approach.

### Later administrative reconstruction supports

NARA supplies:

- the order numbers and formal succession dates;
- the statement that OMAT was abolished by Order 35-65 and superseded by OMPER;
- later archival record-group lineage.

This is authoritative later administrative reconstruction, not 1965 participant language.

### Researcher reconstruction only

The following terms are **ours**, not actor quotations:

- `problem-role migration`;
- `nested persistence`;
- `decentering`;
- `problem-position change`.

They are analytical labels for the observed combination:

```text
continued research askability
+
changed institutional naming/remit
+
shifted action-program salience
```

They must remain visibly marked as researcher categories unless direct actor language matching them is found.

## 12. Why `disappearance` should be rejected here

A strong disappearance claim would require evidence that the older problem became:

- no longer askable;
- no longer institutionally actionable;
- no longer worth investigating;
- explicitly rejected as a false or obsolete category;
- or displaced so thoroughly that its previous causal/responsibility structure ceased to operate.

The 1965–66 evidence shows none of those conditions.

Instead, after `Automation` disappears from the office title:

- technological-change research continues;
- automation remains a research subject;
- OMPER-associated publications continue to address automation;
- BLS continues statutory technological-change studies in the same Department;
- the problem is embedded in a much broader manpower research/planning/evaluation apparatus.

Therefore:

```text
name disappearance
!=
problem disappearance
```

At most, current evidence supports **loss of institutional-title prominence / relative decentering**.

---

## 13. Why `simple continuity` should also be rejected

The inverse error would be to say that because automation research continues, the 1961 OAM problem simply persists unchanged.

By 1966 the institutional setting now contains:

- comprehensive manpower planning;
- evaluation of action programs;
- experimental/demonstration projects;
- local manpower projection;
- hard-core unemployment;
- racial discrimination and equal opportunity;
- youth employment;
- skill shortages;
- older-worker problems;
- geographic mobility;
- human-resource development;
- policy feedback and evaluation.

Automation is one input into this larger field.

The unit of concern has also expanded from a visible displaced worker toward:

```text
individual worker
+
worker group
+
local labor market
+
national manpower requirements
+
program performance
+
socially produced disadvantage
```

So:

```text
continued automation research
!=
unchanged automation problem
```

## 14. A useful new relation pattern for the repository

The source combination supplies a real fixture for a relation that is easy to lose in a binary continuity/disappearance model:

```text
PROBLEM A
is institutionally named and salient
        ↓
A is absorbed into a wider administrative/research field
        ↓
A loses naming prominence
        ↓
A remains a valid causal/research object
but is no longer the dominant action-program foreground
```

Possible schema vocabulary to consider later:

- `nested`;
- `decentered`;
- `reclassified`;
- `problem_role_changed`;
- or a separate attribute tracking **salience/role** rather than identity.

Do **not** add a new relation enum solely from this one case before checking other episodes.

A safer immediate representation is:

```text
relation: undetermined between continuous / reformulated / transformed_successor
notes:
  - institutional succession direct
  - substantive research persistence direct
  - action-program salience shifts direct
  - simple disappearance rejected
  - simple continuity not supported
```

## 15. Source criticism: fiscal-year documents can blur pre- and post-reorganization work

This packet exposes a source problem worth preserving as a methodological fixture.

The FY1966 report covers a year that straddles the December 1965 OMAT → OMPER transition. The successor-office section can summarize work begun before OMPER existed.

Therefore:

```text
listed in FY1966 OMPER-era report
!=
initiated by OMPER after 14 Dec 1965
```

For every project used to establish post-transition persistence, record:

- project start date if known;
- contract date if known;
- report completion date;
- named recipient office;
- whether the research originated under OMAT and was inherited by OMPER.

The October 1966 Crossman report is especially useful because it explicitly names OMPER as recipient well after the transition.

## 16. Later institutional history: useful context, not actor wording

The Department of Labor's later historical chapter on the New Frontier and Great Society says OMPER supplanted OMAT and describes its mission as supplying information to policymakers, planning employment/training programs, conducting research and demonstration projects, including work concerning urban ghetto residents and special groups, and feeding evaluation results back into employment/training programs.

DOL historical chapter:

<https://www.dol.gov/general/aboutdol/history/dolchp06>

### Evidence strength

**High-quality later institutional reconstruction / source map.**

Use it to locate administrative changes and research programs. Do not substitute its retrospective language for 1965–66 actor formulations when the annual report is available.

## 17. High-quality scholarship: Great Society employment policy context

Anaïs Miodek Bowring, **"The Ideological Boundary Condition on Great Society Employment Policy," Journal of Policy History** (2018), argues that Great Society employment policy opened substantial new areas of intervention while remaining bounded by assumptions that generally reinforced existing labor-market mechanisms rather than replacing them.

Publisher abstract:

<https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-policy-history/article/abs/ideological-boundary-condition-on-great-society-employment-policy/A2D4608BD06F484218803829C4DDACD9E>

### Use in this packet

This scholarship is valuable for a literature map because it helps prevent a triumphalist story in which `human resources development` automatically means the federal government had solved or transcended earlier technology–labour problems.

It should **not** be used to rewrite the Department's 1966 actors as if they consciously possessed Bowring's later analytical category.

The primary-source finding remains narrower:

> the Department's action foreground shifts toward disadvantage and human-resource development while technological-change research remains institutionally active.

## 20. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1 — office-name essentialism

Bad:

```text
"Automation" disappears from office title in 1965
→ government stopped treating automation as a problem
```

Better:

```text
title prominence disappears
while research persistence is directly observable
```

### Risk 2 — research persistence = unchanged problem

Bad:

```text
OMPER still studied technological change
→ same automation problem continued
```

Better:

```text
research object persists
inside a broader policy/planning/evaluation field
whose foreground action problems have changed
```

### Risk 3 — successor office inherits fiscal-year work = successor originated it

Bad:

```text
FY1966 annual report lists an automation publication
→ OMPER launched it after Dec 1965
```

Better:

```text
trace start / contract / publication / recipient dates separately
```

### Risk 4 — `human resources` as a timeless synonym for `manpower`

Do not normalize the vocabulary. The 1966 Secretary's text marks a specific attempt to reorganize unemployment intervention around the individual, disadvantage, service bundles, and civil results.

### Risk 5 — macro unemployment decline = technology–labour problem solved

The Department simultaneously reports low aggregate unemployment, persistent hard-core unemployment, skill shortages, and continued automation/technological-change research. These are different problem structures.

### Risk 6 — federal administrative formulation = whole social problem field

Labor, civil-rights groups, employers, Commission members, displaced workers, and researchers may formulate the same material difficulty differently. This packet only establishes the Department/Manpower Administration/OMPER side.

## 21. Uncertainties still open

1. **Secretary's Order 35-65 full text remains unrecovered.**
   - We know the formal succession from NARA.
   - We do not yet know the exact authority clauses, transferred functions, or stated rationale in the order itself.

2. **Did OMPER inherit all OMAT research functions or selectively restructure them?**
   - FY1966 reporting suggests strong staff/research continuity.
   - Direct line-by-line functional comparison requires Order 35-65 and the last OMAT organization statement.

3. **When did `automation` lose organizational-title salience?**
   - The formal title changes in December 1965.
   - The conceptual or budgetary shift may precede or follow that date.

4. **Was the people-first human-resources agenda causally connected to the OMAT → OMPER reorganization?**
   - Both are visible in the FY1966 Department.
   - Do not infer a direct causal relation without memoranda/drafts.

5. **How did OMPER itself classify automation internally?**
   - research theme;
   - technological-change subset;
   - adjustment problem;
   - forecasting input;
   - or residual legacy category?
   - Need OMPER research plans, budget justifications, and internal program memoranda.

6. **Did National Commission work feed OMPER research priorities?**
   - Chronology and subject overlap are strong.
   - Direct transmission remains unproven.

## 24. Provisional conclusion

The OMAT → OMPER transition is a strong negative test against simplistic disappearance histories.

A historian looking only at organization names could write:

```text
1961 OAM
1962 OMAT
1965 OMPER
→ automation disappears
```

The contemporary record instead shows:

```text
"Automation" disappears from the office title
        ↓
Department reorganizes staff capacity toward
policy / planning / research / evaluation
        ↓
technological-change and automation research continues
        ↓
meanwhile the action-program foreground expands toward
hard-core unemployment / disadvantage / skill shortage /
human-resource development
```

The historically defensible conclusion is therefore not `disappearance`.

It is a **change in the role and location of the problem**.
