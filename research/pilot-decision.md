# M1 Pilot Decision

> Status: **M1 candidate survey complete; M2 pilot selected.**
>
> Selected pilot: **“技术是否替代劳动？”** — understood only as a present researcher umbrella. The M2 source pack must reconstruct historically specific actor problems rather than assume one timeless technology-versus-work question.

## 1. Decision rule

The ROADMAP asks for the candidate that best tests the method, not the grandest topic. The decision therefore weighs:

- recoverable actor-side formulations;
- at least 50 years of primary material;
- visible change in formulation, stakes, presuppositions, and answer space;
- contemporaneous competing frames;
- a plausible opportunity to study emergence, split, displacement, or disappearance;
- hindsight risk strong enough to test the method but not so overwhelming that the candidate is only a modern umbrella;
- prior-art overlap low enough that Problem History can add more than a renamed intellectual/concept history;
- repository duplication.

No candidate is treated as one continuous lineage merely because it passes M1.

## 3. Why technology/labour wins

The candidate already supplies several historically different problem structures without requiring the repository to invent them:

1. **Ricardo 1821** — machinery and the interests of different social classes; labour demand is embedded in distribution, gross/net produce, and class-specific effects.
2. **Keynes 1930** — `technological unemployment` as labour-saving change outrunning new uses for labour, framed as a temporary maladjustment within a longer abundance problem.
3. **1964 Triple Revolution** — cybernation as a structural break in the jobs–income relation; productive abundance plus employment-based distribution becomes the central contradiction.
4. **Johnson / Bell / 1966 National Commission** — technology produces real worker displacement, but aggregate unemployment is framed through demand, adjustment, mobility, and policy rather than an inevitable collapse of employment.

The 1964–66 cluster is especially valuable because contemporaries dispute **what the problem is**, not merely which answer is correct.

The candidate also offers a rare M2 opportunity that the other four currently do not offer as cleanly: the nineteenth-century generalized **Machinery Question may cease to organize later conflicts even while machinery and industry-specific displacement continue**. That hypothesis is not yet proven; it is precisely the kind of disappearance/de-generalisation claim M2 should test with contemporaneous 1840s–1870s evidence.

This gives the pilot a useful combination:

```text
emergence candidate
+
reformulation
+
contemporaneous split
+
possible de-generalisation / displacement
```

without relying on one stable term.

## 4. Why the other candidates are not selected

### Literature / use

The source record is excellent, but `poesy`, `poetry`, `art`, books, and institutional `Literature` are too easily normalized into one modern object. Histories of literary criticism and of the idea/uses of literature are already mature. A narrow Peacock–Shelley exchange remains attractive for later method testing.

### Knowledge / identity

This is one of the strongest adversarial candidates because the modern word `identity` can silently collapse class, social existence, social location, gender, race, standpoint, marginality, and credibility. The broad chain is therefore excellent for testing object naturalization but weak as a first pilot unless narrowed to a more demonstrable historical question such as when social position becomes relevant to epistemic authority.

### Machine / thinking

This candidate contains perhaps the cleanest actor-side reformulation in all five surveys: Turing explicitly opens with “Can machines think?” and then replaces/rejects the ordinary formulation in favor of the imitation game. But external historiography is exceptionally mature, and this repository already contains a 1950–2023 M0 calibration corpus plus a deep 1972–1990 AI field-coherence study. Choosing it now would make repository density masquerade as research priority. Its better role is M3 failure cases and real identity fixtures.

### Modern state / modernization

The candidate title already fuses multiple problem families: legitimate domination/state form, civilization/national independence, development/growth policy, and sovereignty/economic autonomy. The materials are valuable precisely because they break the umbrella apart. A broad M2 pilot would be too close to mature modernization/development intellectual history unless radically narrowed.

## 5. M2 scope — selected pilot

The M2 pilot should **not** be titled as though the actors all asked “Will technology replace labour?” The working umbrella remains useful for retrieval only.

### Proposed three-episode architecture

#### Episode A — Machinery, class interests, and the generalized Machinery Question, ca. 1815–1848

Minimum tasks:

- establish actor-side formulations beyond Ricardo alone;
- recover competing labour / political-economy / parliamentary formulations;
- identify institutions and responsibility structures;
- test whether “machinery” itself aggregates otherwise different distress claims;
- collect positive evidence for the conditions under which a generalized Machinery Question emerged.

#### Episode B — Technological unemployment as maladjustment, ca. 1929–1939

Minimum tasks:

- keep Keynes from standing in for the whole Depression debate;
- recover U.S./British labour, business/engineering, economist, and government formulations;
- determine whether `technological unemployment` is one shared problem or a contested label;
- identify answer-space differences: shorter hours, demand, retraining, planning, anti-mechanization, abundance, etc.

#### Episode C — Automation/cybernation split, 1964–1966

Minimum tasks:

- retain the Triple Revolution’s jobs–income/distribution frame;
- retain Johnson’s adjustment/planning frame;
- retain Bell / National Commission distinction between particular displacement and aggregate unemployment;
- demonstrate direct controversy/transmission wherever possible;
- run pairwise `IDENTITY-CHECK` rather than merging all three into “automation anxiety.”

### Required transition test between A and B

Before freezing any A→B edge, investigate **1845–1870** for positive transition evidence. The pilot must determine whether the generalized Machinery Question:

- was answered;
- fragmented into industry-specific labour disputes;
- was displaced by other political-economic problem frames;
- lost salience/askability because institutions and economic conditions changed;
- or merely becomes hard to observe because the source survey is incomplete.

Absence of the earlier vocabulary is not enough.

## 6. M2 evidence priorities

Highest-value next source work:

- contemporaneous 1840s–1870s political economy, parliamentary, trade-union, employer, and press materials on machinery and labour;
- primary-source recovery from the 1929–39 U.S. technological-unemployment debate using Amy Sue Bix as a source map, not as actor evidence;
- page-stable / archival versions of the 1964–66 Triple Revolution, presidential, Bell, and National Commission material already identified in the M1 survey;
- direct transmission evidence between competing actors where available;
- negative-evidence design for the proposed mid-nineteenth-century de-generalisation.

## 7. Stop conditions for M2

Stop or narrow the pilot if:

- nineteenth-century “Machinery Question” cannot be demonstrated at actor/institutional level beyond later historiography;
- 1930s and 1960s materials prove to be only analogous anxieties with no useful transformation relation;
- the de-generalisation/disappearance claim rests only on silence or keyword decline;
- the work becomes a summary of Berg/Bix/Resnikoff rather than an independent problem-identity analysis;
- actor disagreement is flattened into a single technology-versus-jobs variable.

## 8. M1 completion conclusion

The M1 comparison supports selecting **technology/labour** because it is neither the least studied nor the simplest candidate. It is selected because it best exposes the project’s core claim to falsifiable historical work:

> the same broad material difficulty can be organized as different historical problems, and an older problem may lose its organizing form without the underlying difficulty disappearing.

That proposition remains a hypothesis for M2 to test, not a conclusion granted by the pilot-selection stage.
