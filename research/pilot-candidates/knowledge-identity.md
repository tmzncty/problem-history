# M1 candidate packet — “知识与身份的关系”

> Status: **surveyed as an M1 candidate, not selected as the final pilot**.
>
> Branch context: companion evidence packet for `research/pilot-selection.md`.
>
> Hard boundary: **“知识与身份的关系” is a present researcher umbrella, not a stable historical actor question.** The historical materials below speak instead about `social existence`, `social position`, `group life`, `class`, `gender`, `standpoint`, `marginality`, `situated knowledge`, `credibility`, and `identity power`. Those terms must not be normalized into one timeless variable called `identity`.

## 0. Provisional verdict

**Methodologically rich, source-rich, but too broad in its current wording and heavily occupied by mature fields.**

The candidate is excellent for testing one of Problem History's hardest rules:

```text
same modern umbrella
!= same historical object
!= same epistemic problem
```

A primary/source chain from Marx (1859) to Fricker (2007) spans **148 years**, but the chain repeatedly changes what is being related to what:

- material/social existence -> forms of consciousness;
- social location/group life -> perspectives and the sociology of knowledge;
- gendered/material location -> standpoint and claims about objectivity;
- racialized/gendered marginality -> an `outsider within` standpoint;
- partial embodiment/location -> situated objectivity;
- identity prejudice -> credibility deficits and interpretive exclusion.

This is not yet one lineage. Some links have strong intellectual transmission; some are rival or parallel genealogies; some may be only `analogy_only` until source work proves recognition.

The broad title also hides a major category problem: `identity` in contemporary philosophy often means race/gender/class/social identity, while earlier actors may be discussing class position, material relations, social groups, consciousness, or selfhood without an equivalent category.

**Current pilot assessment:** high value as an adversarial method case, but weaker than the technology/labour candidate as a broad M2 pilot unless narrowed. A stronger narrow formulation would be something like:

> **When and how does social position become epistemically relevant to who can know, what can be known, or whose word counts?**

Even that narrower title remains a researcher analytic question and would require pairwise identity checks.

## 1. Object instability: what does “knowledge + identity” conceal?

A present-day reader can easily merge the following into one story:

```text
Marx -> Mannheim -> feminist standpoint -> Black feminist thought
     -> situated knowledge -> epistemic injustice
```

The source record does not yet license that graph.

### Historical objects that must remain separate

| Source / moment | Actor-side object or relation | Do not silently rewrite as |
|---|---|---|
| Marx 1859 | material/social existence and forms of social consciousness | personal identity determines truth |
| Mannheim 1929/1936 | social situation/group life and perspectives/styles of thought | modern identity politics |
| Hartsock / Harding 1980s | material gender relations, feminist standpoint, science/objectivity | women automatically know better by essence |
| Collins 1986 | Black women intellectuals' outsider-within position and Black feminist thought | all members of an identity group share one epistemic view |
| Haraway 1988 | embodied, partial, situated knowledge and accountable objectivity | identity is a truth-token |
| Fricker 2007 | identity power, credibility economy, testimonial/hermeneutical injustice | marginalized identity confers epistemic privilege |

The candidate is therefore unusually good at exposing **object naturalization**. A repository that stores only `identity -> knowledge` would destroy the history it is trying to recover.

## 2. Existing scholarship / duplication map

### 2.1 Current feminist social epistemology map

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, **“Feminist Social Epistemology”** (first published 2006; substantive revision 2018):

- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminist-social-epistemology/

**Layer:** later scholarly synthesis / current field map.

The entry is useful because it distinguishes several projects that a broad `knowledge + identity` title would otherwise conflate:

- socially differentiated/situated knowers;
- standpoint theory;
- objectivity as social or situated;
- epistemic injustice;
- epistemologies of ignorance;
- community and relational models of knowing.

It also stresses that social epistemology is not simply the descriptive sociology of knowledge: feminist social epistemologists make normative claims about better/worse knowledge practices.

**Overlap warning:** very high. A pilot that merely summarizes these positions would be a renamed philosophy survey, not Problem History.

### 2.2 Current feminist epistemology / philosophy-of-science map

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, **“Feminist Epistemology and Philosophy of Science”** (substantive revision 1 Aug 2024):

- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-epistemology/

**Layer:** later scholarly synthesis.

The entry identifies situated knowledge as central and reviews how gender and other relations can affect knowledge, knowers, inquiry, justification, authority, and objectivity. It also explicitly distinguishes feminist empiricism, standpoint approaches, and other positions rather than treating feminist epistemology as one answer.

**Problem-History use:** strong map of later distinctions; not evidence that earlier actors were asking a single perennial question.

### 2.3 Mannheim scholarship: the sociology-of-knowledge problem was not simply “all knowledge is relative”

Martyn Hammersley, “Karl Mannheim's *Ideology and Utopia* and the public role of sociology,” *Qualitative Research* (2022):

- https://doi.org/10.1177/1468795X20986382

Alan Scott, “Politics and Method in Mannheim's `Ideology and Utopia',” *Sociology* 21.1 (1987):

- https://doi.org/10.1177/0038038587021001004

**Layer:** later scholarly reconstruction.

These studies are important because they resist a common retrospective compression. Mannheim's problem was not merely to assert social conditioning and stop at relativism; his sociology of knowledge was also an attempt to understand ideological division, relationism, rationality, and the public role of sociological inquiry under modern conditions.

### 2.4 Mature feminist standpoint literature

Nancy Hartsock, “The Feminist Standpoint: Developing the Ground for a Specifically Feminist Historical Materialism” (1983; later reprint):

- Oxford reprint record: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192892706.003.0025

Sandra Harding, *The Science Question in Feminism* (Cornell University Press, 1986):

- publisher record: https://www.cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801418808/the-/
- bibliographic/open preview record: https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2696320W/The_science_question_in_feminism

Lorraine Code, *What Can She Know? Feminist Theory and the Construction of Knowledge* (Cornell University Press, 1991):

- https://cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801497209/what-can-she-know/

**Layer:** these are historical theoretical interventions and also evidence that the candidate's later portion is already a mature intellectual field.

The overlap is therefore not incidental. If this project simply re-labels standpoint epistemology as a “knowledge/identity problem,” it fails the repository stop condition.

### Duplication verdict

**Very high.** The potential Problem-History increment is narrower:

- test whether the historical *problem* shifts when `social location` changes from a causal/explanatory condition of consciousness to a possible source of epistemic advantage;
- test when the problem becomes one of **objectivity** rather than merely social determination;
- test when a claim about social location becomes a claim about **credibility distribution / injustice**;
- preserve competing and non-genealogical strands instead of forcing one lineage.

## 3. Primary/source chain

## Episode lead A — Marx 1859: social existence and forms of consciousness

### Primary source

Karl Marx, Preface to *A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy* (1859).

Open text with source metadata:

- https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/preface.htm

Related manuscript background:

- *The German Ideology* (written 1845–46; publication history is much later): https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1845/german-ideology/ch01a.htm

### Actor-explicit move

In the 1859 Preface, Marx states that the mode of production of material life conditions social, political, and intellectual life, and contrasts consciousness determining existence with social existence determining consciousness. He also distinguishes material transformations from the ideological forms in which people become conscious of conflicts and fight them out.

### Safe problem reconstruction

This is not safely paraphrased as:

> “Does a person's identity determine what they know?”

It is closer to a historical-materialist explanatory problem:

> how should forms of social consciousness and ideological struggle be explained in relation to material production and social relations?

### Problem structure

```yaml
target:
  - forms of social consciousness
  - ideological forms in which social conflict becomes conscious
social_condition:
  - relations and mode of production
stakes:
  - explanatory adequacy of a materialist account of historical change
  - critique of accounts that treat ideas as autonomous drivers
not_yet_established:
  - a theory of modern social identity
  - a general rule that every belief is reducible to group membership
  - marginalized epistemic privilege
```

### Evidence strength

**High** for the actor wording in the 1859 published Preface.

### Hindsight risk

The modern terms `identity`, `positionality`, `standpoint`, and `epistemic authority` should not be inserted into Marx's actor vocabulary without a separate researcher layer.

## Episode lead B — Mannheim 1929 / 1936: social location becomes a sociology-of-knowledge problem

### Source/version control

Karl Mannheim, *Ideologie und Utopie* (German, 1929); English expanded version, *Ideology and Utopia: An Introduction to the Sociology of Knowledge* (1936).

Digitized scan lead for the English edition:

- https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.187027

Important version warning:

- **1929 German and 1936 English are not identical containers.** The English edition adds material. Exact quotations and page locators must record which version is being used.

### Actor-side problem lead

Mannheim generalizes the problem of ideological perspective: not only an opponent's isolated claim but styles/worldviews of thought can be related to social location and group life. The 1936 English text famously describes knowing as a cooperative process of group life rather than the achievement of an isolated individual.

But the historical stakes are not exhausted by `knowledge is socially conditioned`. Later scholarship shows that Mannheim is also confronting a problem of ideological division, relativism/relationism, and whether sociology can produce a broader understanding without pretending to occupy a position outside history.

### Current safe reconstruction

```yaml
target:
  - perspectives / styles of thought
  - political worldviews
  - sociology of knowledge itself
social_condition:
  - social position / group life / historical situation
stakes:
  - explain conflicting worldviews
  - avoid collapsing social conditioning into arbitrary relativism
  - define a public/intellectual role for sociological synthesis
answer_space:
  - sociology of knowledge
  - relationism rather than simple absolutism or relativism
```

### Evidence strength

- **High** for publication history and the central sociology-of-knowledge orientation.
- **Medium-high** for exact page-level actor wording in this packet: the accessible scan is identified, but this run did not complete image-by-image facsimile verification. Before an M2 episode is frozen, quotations should be checked against the 1929 German or the specified 1936 edition.
- **High** for later interpretive warning from Hammersley and Scott that relativism was not simply Mannheim's final answer.

### Relation lead A -> B

There is genuine intellectual inheritance from Marxist ideology critique into Mannheim's sociology of knowledge, but that does **not** automatically settle `continuous`.

Important discontinuities include:

- Marx's central explanatory target is material production/social relations and historical consciousness;
- Mannheim generalizes social perspectivism beyond a simple ruling/oppressed class dyad and turns the observer's own position into a methodological problem;
- Mannheim's answer space includes relationism and a reflexive sociology of knowledge.

Current candidate label: **`transformed_successor` worth testing, not frozen**.

## Adversarial side lead — Du Bois 1903: “second-sight” is not automatically standpoint epistemology

### Primary source

W. E. B. Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903), ch. I, “Of Our Spiritual Strivings.”

- Project Gutenberg full text: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/408/408-h/408-h.htm

### Actor wording

Du Bois describes the Veil, double-consciousness, and a Black subject being “gifted with second-sight” in an American world that forces self-perception through the eyes of others. The immediate problem is racialized self-consciousness, divided identity, recognition, and the possibility of being both Black and American without exclusion.

### Why this is a deliberate negative control

Later scholars sometimes draw epistemic implications from Du Boisian `second sight`. For example:

José Itzigsohn and Karida Brown, “Sociology and the Theory of Double Consciousness: W. E. B. Du Bois's Phenomenology of Racialized Subjectivity,” *Du Bois Review* (2015):

- https://doi.org/10.1017/S1742058X15000107

Their later reconstruction highlights the potential awareness produced by second sight. That is useful scholarship, but it cannot silently convert the 1903 episode into an actor-explicit theory of `epistemic privilege of marginalized identities`.

### Method value

This is an excellent future failure-case pair:

```text
later epistemic reinterpretation
!= historical actor formulation
```

No Du Bois -> Hartsock/Harding/Collins lineage should be asserted without transmission evidence.

## Episode lead C — 1983–1991: gendered social location, standpoint, and the problem of objectivity

This is probably not one episode. It is a compact field of competing formulations and should be tested as an exchange/debate cluster.

### C1. Hartsock 1983 — feminist standpoint as feminist historical materialism

Nancy Hartsock, “The Feminist Standpoint: Developing the Ground for a Specifically Feminist Historical Materialism,” in Harding & Hintikka (eds.), *Discovering Reality* (1983), pp. 283–310.

- later Oxford reprint metadata: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192892706.003.0025

Hartsock explicitly adapts Marxian historical-materialist resources for feminist purposes. A key later historiographic point, also reflected in current SEP summaries, is that standpoint is not simply `a woman has experiences, therefore she knows better`; influential standpoint theories distinguish a social location from an achieved critical standpoint.

**Evidence strength:** medium-high for source identity and broad actor project; page-level quotations should be verified against the 1983 text before M2 use.

### C2. Harding 1986 — the “science question” changes the stakes

Sandra Harding, *The Science Question in Feminism* (1986).

- Cornell bibliographic record: https://www.cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801418808/the-/
- Open Library bibliographic/preview record: https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2696320W/The_science_question_in_feminism

Harding organizes a controversy among feminist empiricism, feminist standpoint, and feminist postmodernism. The central stakes include whether sciences formed within Western, masculine, bourgeois institutions can be used for emancipatory ends and how feminist critiques should understand objectivity and bias.

This is already a substantial shift from Mannheim:

```text
social determination of thought
        ->
which social locations / research starting points improve or distort inquiry?
        ->
what should count as objectivity in feminist science?
```

The arrows above are **researcher analytic transformations**, not historical identity verdicts.

### C3. Lorraine Code 1991 — the identity of the knower becomes explicitly epistemological

Lorraine Code, *What Can She Know? Feminist Theory and the Construction of Knowledge* (Cornell University Press, 1991).

- https://cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801497209/what-can-she-know/
- bibliographic record: https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3748869W/What_can_she_know

Publisher descriptions explicitly frame the issue as whether the **sex of the knower is epistemologically significant**, while challenging the idea of a value-free, ideologically neutral generic knower.

This is one of the cleanest pieces of evidence that a formulation close to the modern candidate becomes actor-explicit by the early 1990s. It must not be used to back-project `identity of the knower` as the wording of Marx or Mannheim.

### Evidence strength

- **High** for publication/bibliographic identity of the 1983–91 sources.
- **High** for the fact that feminist epistemology had become a self-conscious debate over standpoint, situated knowers, and objectivity by this period.
- **Medium-high** for exact Hartsock/Harding formulation until page-level primary text is verified.
- **High** for Code's publisher-level framing; book text should still be page-located before exact quotation.

## Episode lead D — 1986–1988: marginality and partial perspective complicate a single “identity standpoint”

## D1. Patricia Hill Collins 1986 — `outsider within`

Patricia Hill Collins, “Learning from the Outsider Within: The Sociological Significance of Black Feminist Thought,” *Social Problems* 33.6 (1986), S14–S32.

- Oxford Academic record and abstract: https://academic.oup.com/socpro/article-abstract/33/6/s14/1610242
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/800672

### Actor-explicit formulation

Collins argues that many Black women intellectuals used their marginal `outsider within` position to produce Black feminist thought with a distinctive standpoint on self, family, and society. The article emphasizes self-definition/self-valuation, interlocking oppression, Afro-American women's culture, and the possible sociological value of personal/cultural biography.

### Important boundary

This is **not** equivalent to:

```text
Black identity -> automatic true knowledge
```

The actor problem concerns marginal institutional position, lived social relations, intellectual work, and what those can disclose or challenge in sociological paradigms.

### Evidence strength

**High** for the actor formulation: the original journal abstract and bibliographic record are stable and directly identify the argument.

## D2. Donna Haraway 1988 — situated knowledges and accountable objectivity

Donna Haraway, “Situated Knowledges: The Science Question in Feminism and the Privilege of Partial Perspective,” *Feminist Studies* 14.3 (1988), 575–599.

- bibliographic / DOI record: https://philpapers.org/rec/HARSKT
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/3178066
- University of Illinois teaching/research page with page-located excerpts from the article: https://seeingsystems.illinois.edu/situated-knowledges-collective-resource-mapping/

### Actor-explicit problem

Haraway states her problem as finding a way to hold together:

- radical historical contingency of knowledge claims and knowers;
- reflexive criticism of meaning-making technologies;
- commitment to faithful accounts of a real world.

She rejects a disembodied `view from nowhere` model of objectivity and argues for particular, embodied, partial location together with accountability.

### Why this matters for Problem History

Haraway's formulation complicates a simple story in which marginal identity yields a single privileged standpoint. The problem becomes how to retain objectivity and real-world accountability **after** accepting partial, situated perspectives.

Potential relation to Hartsock/Harding/Collins:

- clear shared debate field and vocabulary;
- important changes in what `objectivity`, `standpoint`, and `location` are doing;
- likely a `split / reformulated` cluster rather than one linear succession.

**Do not freeze an edge yet.**

### Evidence strength

**High** for source identity and page metadata; **medium-high** for the page-located quotation access path because the convenient public text used here is a university-hosted instructional reproduction rather than the journal facsimile. Exact M2 quotations should be checked against a canonical copy.

## Episode lead E — Fricker 2007: identity power as an injustice in credibility and interpretive resources

### Primary/theoretical source

Miranda Fricker, *Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing* (Oxford University Press, 2007).

- book DOI / record: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198237907.001.0001
- ch. 1, “Testimonial Injustice”: https://academic.oup.com/book/32817/chapter-abstract/274999720
- ch. 7, “Hermeneutical Injustice”: https://academic.oup.com/book/32817/chapter-abstract/275001188

### Actor-explicit formulation

Fricker explicitly introduces **identity power** and analyzes a form of epistemic injustice in which prejudice causes a speaker to receive a credibility deficit. She separately analyzes hermeneutical injustice, where structural prejudice in shared interpretive resources can prevent someone from making sense of important social experience.

### Problem structure

```yaml
target:
  - credibility judgments
  - shared interpretive resources
social_relation:
  - identity power / structural identity prejudice
stakes:
  - being wronged specifically in one's capacity as a knower
answer_space:
  - testimonial justice / corrective epistemic virtue
  - hermeneutical justice
not_equivalent_to:
  - standpoint privilege
  - social-location determinism
  - the claim that identity fixes truth
```

### Historical transformation lead

A present researcher might describe a shift:

```text
Whose position helps produce knowledge?
        ->
How does social identity affect objectivity?
        ->
How does identity prejudice unfairly distribute credibility
and interpretive capacity?
```

But the final arrow cannot be treated as a simple answer to standpoint theory. Fricker's object, wrong, and answer space differ significantly.

### Evidence strength

**High** for the actor formulation because Oxford's own chapter abstracts explicitly identify `identity power`, credibility deficit, and hermeneutical marginalization.

## 4. At least three plausible historical breaks

### Break 1 — 1859 -> 1929/36

```text
material social existence conditions consciousness
        ->
reflexive sociology of socially located perspectives
```

**Why plausible:** Mannheim turns the observer's and competing groups' positions into a methodological problem and attempts to manage relationism/ideological division.

**What must still be proved:** exact reception/transmission, which Marxian claims Mannheim takes over, and whether the relevant problem is continuous or only an intellectual descendant.

### Break 2 — 1929/36 -> 1983–91

```text
social location explains perspective
        ->
social location may be epistemically consequential for objectivity,
critique, and the starting point of inquiry
```

**Why plausible:** feminist standpoint theory explicitly takes over Marxian/historical-materialist resources; modern feminist epistemology makes the differentiated identity/location of the knower an epistemic issue.

**Major discontinuity:** Hartsock's explicit genealogy is Marxian. A neat `Mannheim -> feminist standpoint` arrow is therefore **not established**. The repository must allow parallel inheritance or `analogy_only` rather than fabricate direct transmission.

### Break 3 — mid/late 1980s

```text
one feminist standpoint / gendered social location
        ->
Black feminist outsider-within / interlocking oppression
        +
partial, embodied, situated objectivity
```

**Why plausible:** Collins and Haraway are direct contemporaries in a debate where the generic category `women` and simple identity-to-knowledge formulas are under pressure.

**Relation candidate:** probably `split / reformulated`, but not yet adjudicated.

### Break 4 — 2007

```text
social location / standpoint / objectivity
        ->
identity power, credibility economy, and hermeneutical injustice
```

**Why plausible:** the epistemic wrong is now formulated around exclusion or devaluation in practices of knowing, not around whether one social position inherently yields a superior standpoint.

**Relation candidate:** `transformed_successor` or `reformulated` may be testable, but `undetermined` remains legitimate without stronger genealogy/reception evidence.

## 5. Emergence, transformation, and disappearance

### What can currently be said

The broad difficulty — people in different social locations perceive and interpret the world differently — is not enough to date a Problem Episode.

The source chain supports several more specific emergences:

1. **mid-19th century materialist explanation:** social consciousness is explicitly subordinated to material/social existence in Marx's historical explanation;
2. **interwar sociology-of-knowledge problem:** Mannheim makes social perspectivism and reflexive knowledge a dedicated methodological/public-intellectual problem;
3. **1980s–1990s feminist epistemological problem:** the situated/differentiated knower becomes explicitly central to objectivity, science, and epistemic authority;
4. **2007 identity-power/injustice problem:** credibility and interpretive participation are framed as sites of specifically epistemic injustice.

### What cannot currently be said

- that an earlier `knowledge and identity problem` existed under the same identity;
- that Mannheim's problem disappeared when feminist standpoint appeared;
- that standpoint theory was solved or replaced by situated knowledge;
- that epistemic injustice superseded standpoint theory;
- that the abstract individual knower became historically `unaskable`.

The evidence shows **proliferation, splitting, and reframing**, not clean extinction.

This candidate is therefore less useful than the technology/labour candidate for testing `problem disappearance`, but very strong for testing **problem multiplication and object instability**.

## 7. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1 — `identity` back-projection

Do not make `identity` a universal actor term. Earlier sources often organize the relation through class, material existence, group life, social position, gender relations, race, or institutional marginality.

### Risk 2 — `social position -> truth`

Neither sociology of knowledge nor feminist standpoint can be safely reduced to `group membership automatically determines truth`.

### Risk 3 — essentializing standpoint

Do not turn `women`, `Black women`, or any other category into a homogeneous knowing subject. Influential standpoint theories distinguish social location from an achieved standpoint; Collins and Haraway further complicate any unitary identity-to-knowledge mapping.

### Risk 4 — Du Bois as automatic precursor

`second-sight` is a tempting modern epistemic metaphor. Historical actor evidence must remain distinct from later scholarship that redeploys Du Bois for standpoint/social epistemology.

### Risk 5 — Mannheim as the missing middle by narrative convenience

Because the chronology looks neat, an Agent may fabricate:

```text
Marx -> Mannheim -> Hartsock -> Haraway -> Fricker
```

Actual actor-side genealogy may bypass Mannheim, branch through Marxism, Black feminist thought, philosophy of science, sociology, or independent problem fields.

### Risk 6 — normative/descriptive collapse

`social location influences belief` is not the same claim as `a social location yields epistemic advantage`, which is not the same problem as `identity prejudice causes credibility injustice`.

### Risk 7 — “objectivity was rejected”

Haraway and Harding do not simply discard objectivity. Their interventions attempt to reconstruct conditions for stronger/accountable objectivity under situated knowledge.

### Risk 8 — later category victory

Because `epistemic injustice` is now a productive research programme, do not use it as the master category for every earlier case of exclusion, silencing, misrecognition, or biased credibility.

### Risk 9 — canonical theory history replacing episode history

A sequence of famous texts is not itself a historical episode chain. For M2, the project needs exchange, reception, institutional uptake, contemporary rivals, and evidence that actors recognized or transformed the relevant problem field.

## 8. What would make this candidate distinctively Problem History?

A weak project would ask:

> what did Marx, Mannheim, Hartsock, Haraway, Collins, and Fricker say about situated knowledge?

That is an intellectual-history / theory-survey question already well served by existing scholarship.

A stronger Problem-History pilot would instead test claims such as:

1. **When does social conditioning become an epistemic problem rather than only a causal explanation of consciousness?**
2. **When does location become a possible epistemic advantage rather than merely a source of limitation/distortion?**
3. **When does `who knows?` split into questions of objectivity, standpoint achievement, institutional marginality, credibility, and interpretive resources?**
4. **Can two theories share the claim that knowledge is socially situated while nevertheless instantiate different historical problems?**
5. **Which later genealogies are actor-recognized, and which are retrospective constructions by feminist/social epistemology?**

That would genuinely stress `IDENTITY-CHECK` rather than restating a known philosophy syllabus.

## 10. Stop conditions

Do **not** choose this broad candidate if:

- the usable graph turns out to be only a canonical bibliography of famous epistemologists;
- `identity` remains a present-day umbrella with no actor-language control;
- direct transmission among episodes cannot be distinguished from later retrospective genealogy;
- the project treats every social influence on thought as one problem;
- the contribution duplicates mature histories of standpoint/social epistemology without a distinct identity/transformation argument.

Continue only if primary work can establish at least three episodes where **target, stakes, presuppositions, and answer space change in historically documentable ways**.

- **Competing formulations:** very strong in the 1980s feminist epistemology cluster.

### Weaknesses

- **Object instability:** extreme (`social being / class / social location / gender / race / standpoint / identity power` are not interchangeable).
- **Prior-art overlap:** extremely high.
- **Broad candidate coherence:** low unless narrowed.
- **Problem-disappearance evidence:** currently weak; the history looks more like proliferation/splitting than clean disappearance.

### Provisional ranking

**Strong method case, but not currently the strongest M2 pilot.**

Compared with the already-surveyed technology/labour candidate, this candidate offers even better tests of object instability and retrospective genealogy, but worse boundaries and heavier mature-literature overlap. If all five candidates are eventually compared, the broad “知识与身份” umbrella should probably rank below “技术是否替代劳动？” unless the next source pass reveals a compact, actor-linked sub-lineage.
